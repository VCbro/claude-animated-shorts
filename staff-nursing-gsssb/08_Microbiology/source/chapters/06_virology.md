## 6.1 General Properties of Viruses

- **Smallest infectious agents (20–300 nm)**; **acellular**, **obligate intracellular parasites**; contain **only one type of nucleic acid (DNA or RNA)**; **no ribosomes, no energy system**; **not affected by antibiotics**; pass through bacterial filters; seen by **electron microscope** (**largest – poxvirus ~300 nm, visible by light microscopy – Paschen bodies; smallest – parvovirus ~20 nm, picornaviruses ~25–30 nm**).
- **Structure** – **virion** = **nucleic acid core + capsid (protein coat made of capsomeres)** = **nucleocapsid**; ± **envelope** (lipid, from host membrane – **enveloped viruses sensitive to ether, detergents, alcohol, drying**); **peplomers/ spikes** (glycoproteins – haemagglutinin, neuraminidase).
- **Symmetry** – **icosahedral (cubic – adenovirus, polio, herpes)**, **helical (influenza, measles, rabies – bullet-shaped)**, **complex (poxvirus – brick-shaped, bacteriophage – tadpole)**.
- **Replication steps** – **adsorption (attachment) → penetration → uncoating → biosynthesis (eclipse phase – no infectious virus detectable) → assembly (maturation) → release (lysis or budding)**.
- **Retroviruses (HIV)** – **reverse transcriptase (RNA → DNA)**, integrates into host genome (provirus).
- **Cultivation** – **animal inoculation (suckling mice)**, **embryonated hen's egg** (chorioallantoic membrane – pox "pocks", amniotic & allantoic cavity – influenza, yolk sac – Chlamydia/ Rickettsia), **tissue/ cell culture (most common – cytopathic effect CPE)**.
- **Inclusion bodies** (diagnostic) – **Negri bodies (rabies – hippocampus, Purkinje cells – intracytoplasmic)**, **Guarnieri/ Paschen bodies (vaccinia/ smallpox)**, **owl's eye (CMV – intranuclear)**, **Cowdry type A (herpes – intranuclear)**, **molluscum bodies**, **Warthin-Finkeldey giant cells (measles)**.
- **Interferons** – host proteins (**IFN-α, β, γ**) produced by virus-infected cells – **non-specific antiviral** (block replication in neighbouring cells); used therapeutically (hepatitis B/C – older, MS).
- **Oncogenic viruses** – **HPV (cervical cancer – types 16, 18)**, **HBV & HCV (hepatocellular carcinoma)**, **EBV (Burkitt's lymphoma, nasopharyngeal carcinoma)**, **HHV-8 (Kaposi's sarcoma)**, **HTLV-1 (adult T-cell leukaemia)**.
- **Bacteriophage** – virus infecting bacteria (phage typing, transduction).

## 6.3 RNA Viruses

| Virus | Disease & points |
|---|---|
| **Poliovirus (Picornaviridae – Enterovirus; types 1, 2, 3)** | **Faeco-oral**; multiplies in gut → viraemia → **anterior horn cells of spinal cord** → **acute flaccid paralysis (asymmetric, lower limbs, no sensory loss)**; **~90–95 % inapparent, < 1 % paralytic**; **provocation polio (IM injections)**; **India – last wild case 13 January 2011 (Howrah, WB); WHO certified polio-free 27 March 2014**; **WPV2 eradicated 2015, WPV3 2019**; **cVDPV** risk; **vaccines – OPV (live, Sabin – bOPV types 1 & 3; at birth, 6, 10, 14 wks, 16–24 months), IPV (killed, Salk – fIPV intradermal 0.1 ml at 6 & 14 wks + 9 months)**; **AFP surveillance – any child < 15 yrs with AFP – 2 stool samples 24 hrs apart within 14 days, sent in reverse cold chain**; **Pulse Polio (1995)** |
| **Coxsackie, echovirus, enterovirus 71** | **Hand-foot-mouth disease (Coxsackie A16, EV71)**, **herpangina**, **myocarditis/ pericarditis, Bornholm disease (pleurodynia – Coxsackie B)**, aseptic meningitis |
| **Hepatitis A virus (HAV – Picornavirus)** | **Faeco-oral (water, food)**; **incubation 15–45 days (avg ~30)**; children – mild/ anicteric; **no chronicity/ carrier state**; **anti-HAV IgM – diagnosis**; vaccine (killed/ live) |
| **Hepatitis E virus (HEV)** | **Faeco-oral (water-borne epidemics – Delhi 1955–56)**; **incubation 2–9 weeks (~40 days)**; **high mortality in pregnancy (~20–25 %, 3rd trimester – fulminant hepatitis)**; no chronicity (except immunocompromised) |
| **Hepatitis C virus (HCV – Flaviviridae)** | **Blood-borne (transfusion, IV drug use, dialysis, unsafe injections)**; **most likely to become chronic (~70–85 %)** → cirrhosis, HCC; **no vaccine**; **curable with DAAs (sofosbuvir + velpatasvir/ daclatasvir, 12 weeks)**; **National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme (2018)** |
| **Hepatitis D virus (Delta)** | **Defective RNA virus – needs HBsAg (HBV) to replicate**; **co-infection or super-infection (more severe)**; HBV vaccine protects |
| **Rotavirus (Reoviridae – double-stranded RNA, "wheel-like")** | **Commonest cause of severe diarrhoea (dehydrating gastroenteritis) in infants & young children (6–24 months)**, winter; **faeco-oral**; **Rotavac (indigenous) vaccine – oral, 6, 10, 14 wks (UIP since 2016)** |
| **Norovirus (Caliciviridae)** | **Outbreaks of gastroenteritis (cruise ships, hospitals, "winter vomiting disease")**; hand washing with soap |
| **Measles (rubeola – Paramyxoviridae, Morbillivirus)** | **Airborne, highly infectious (R₀ 12–18)**; **incubation ~10 days (to fever), 14 days to rash**; **prodrome – 3 Cs: cough, coryza, conjunctivitis + high fever**; **Koplik's spots (buccal mucosa opposite lower molars – pathognomonic, appear 1–2 days before rash)**; **maculopapular rash starts behind ears/ hairline → face → trunk → limbs (day 4), fades with brownish staining & branny desquamation**; infectious **4 days before to 4 days after rash**; complications – **pneumonia (commonest cause of death)**, **diarrhoea**, otitis media, encephalitis, **SSPE (years later)**, **corneal ulceration & blindness (vitamin A deficiency)**, malnutrition; **vitamin A supplementation** (age-based doses × 2 days); **MR vaccine – 9–12 months & 16–24 months (UIP)**; **live attenuated (Edmonston-Zagreb)**; reconstitute with diluent, **use within 4 hrs**, protect from light |
| **Mumps (Paramyxovirus)** | **Droplet & saliva**; **incubation ~16–18 days**; **parotitis (bilateral, painful – ear lobe lifted)**; complications – **orchitis (post-pubertal males – sterility rare)**, oophoritis, **aseptic meningitis (commonest extra-salivary)**, pancreatitis, deafness; MMR vaccine |
| **Rubella (German measles – Togaviridae)** | **Mild fever, rash (face → body, 3 days – "3-day measles"), post-auricular & suboccipital lymphadenopathy (characteristic)**; **congenital rubella syndrome (CRS) – 1st trimester infection: classic triad – cataract (eye), PDA/ cardiac defects, sensorineural deafness (most common)** + microcephaly, IUGR, "blueberry muffin" rash; **MR/ MMR vaccine; avoid pregnancy for 1 month after vaccination** |
| **Influenza (Orthomyxoviridae – segmented RNA)** | **Haemagglutinin (H – attachment) & neuraminidase (N – release)**; **antigenic drift (minor – seasonal epidemics, A & B)** vs **antigenic shift (major – reassortment, only type A → pandemics: 1918 H1N1 Spanish flu, 2009 H1N1 swine flu)**; **avian flu H5N1**; droplet; **Rx – oseltamivir (within 48 hrs)**; annual vaccine |
| **RSV (respiratory syncytial virus – Paramyxoviridae)** | **Commonest cause of bronchiolitis & pneumonia in infants < 1–2 yrs**; syncytia; nirsevimab/ palivizumab (prophylaxis) |
| **Rabies virus (Rhabdoviridae – bullet-shaped, Lyssavirus)** | **Zoonosis – dog bite (~96–97 % in India)**, cats, monkeys, mongoose, jackals, bats; **travels along nerves (retrograde axonal) to CNS**; **incubation 1–3 months (variable – shorter in face/ head/ hand bites)**; **hydrophobia (fear of water – spasm of pharyngeal muscles), aerophobia, hypersalivation, agitation → paralysis → death (~100 % fatal once symptomatic)**; **Negri bodies**; **diagnosis – corneal impression smear (IFA), skin biopsy (nape of neck), saliva PCR**; **WHO categories – I (licks on intact skin – no PEP), II (minor scratches, nibbling – vaccine), III (transdermal bites, licks on broken skin/ mucosa, bat exposure – vaccine + RIG)**; **wound wash with soap & running water for 15 min (most important first aid)**, antiseptic, **don't suture immediately**; **ARV – intramuscular Essen (days 0, 3, 7, 14, 28) or **Zagreb 2-1-1** (2 doses on day 0, 1 each on days 7 & 21)/ intradermal Updated Thai Red Cross (0.1 ml at 2 sites on days 0, 3, 7, 28)**; **RIG (ERIG 40 IU/kg, HRIG 20 IU/kg) infiltrated into & around wound**; **deltoid/ anterolateral thigh – never gluteal**; **pre-exposure – days 0, 7 (21/28)**; **World Rabies Day – 28 September (Pasteur's death anniversary)**; **NRCP – Zero by 30** (dog-mediated rabies elimination by 2030) |
| **Dengue (Flaviviridae – 4 serotypes DENV 1–4)** | **Vector – Aedes aegypti (day-biter, breeds in clean stagnant water – coolers, tyres, pots; "tiger mosquito")**; **incubation 4–7 days**; **dengue fever – "breakbone fever": high fever, retro-orbital pain, myalgia, arthralgia, rash**, **thrombocytopenia**; **warning signs (abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleed, lethargy, liver enlargement, rising haematocrit with falling platelets)**; **severe dengue (DHF/ DSS) – plasma leakage – secondary infection with different serotype (antibody-dependent enhancement)**; **critical phase – defervescence (day 3–7)**; **tourniquet test**; **NS1 antigen (day 1–5), IgM ELISA (after day 5)**; **Rx – paracetamol, fluids; avoid aspirin/ NSAIDs/ steroids**; **National Dengue Day – 16 May**; no vaccine in UIP (Qdenga/ Dengvaxia abroad) |
| **Chikungunya (Togaviridae – Alphavirus)** | **Aedes**; **severe, prolonged polyarthralgia ("that which bends up")**, fever, rash; rarely fatal |
| **Japanese encephalitis (Flavivirus)** | **Culex (tritaeniorhynchus) – rice fields**; **pigs – amplifier hosts**, ardeid birds – reservoir; **man – dead-end host**; children; high mortality & neurological sequelae; **JE vaccine (live SA 14-14-2) – endemic districts at 9 & 16–24 months** |
| **Zika (Flavivirus)** | Aedes; **microcephaly (congenital)**, GBS |
| **Yellow fever (Flavivirus)** | Aedes; Africa & S. America; **not in India**; **vaccine (17D) – valid lifelong; certificate for travellers – 10 days after vaccination**; India requires certificate/ quarantine (6 days) for arrivals from endemic areas |
| **KFD (Kyasanur forest disease – Flavivirus)** | **Ticks (Haemaphysalis); monkeys; Karnataka ("monkey fever")** |
| **Nipah virus (Paramyxoviridae, Henipavirus)** | **Fruit bats (Pteropus) – reservoir**; pigs; **date palm sap**; **encephalitis & respiratory illness, high fatality (40–75 %)**; outbreaks – **Kerala (2018, 2019, 2021, 2023…), West Bengal (2001, 2007)**; no vaccine |
| **SARS-CoV-2 (Coronaviridae – crown-like spikes)** | **COVID-19 – pandemic (declared 11 March 2020; PHEIC ended May 2023)**; **droplet/ aerosol & contact**; **spike protein – ACE2 receptor**; **incubation 2–14 days (~5)**; anosmia, ageusia, fever, cough; **RT-PCR (gold standard), rapid antigen test**; variants – Delta, Omicron; India vaccines – **Covishield (viral vector), Covaxin (inactivated, indigenous), ZyCoV-D (DNA)**; SARS (2002), MERS (camels, 2012) |
| **HIV (Retroviridae – Lentivirus; ssRNA, reverse transcriptase)** | See 6.4 |
| **Ebola, Marburg (Filoviridae)** | **Viral haemorrhagic fever**; contact with body fluids; very high mortality |
| **Hantavirus** | Rodents; HFRS, pulmonary syndrome |

## 6.4 HIV/ AIDS

- **HIV-1 (worldwide, more virulent; most common in India) & HIV-2 (West Africa)**; **retrovirus** – 2 copies of ssRNA, **reverse transcriptase, integrase, protease**; envelope glycoproteins **gp120 (binds CD4) & gp41**; core **p24**.
- **Target cells – CD4+ T-helper lymphocytes** (also macrophages, dendritic cells, microglia) via **CD4 + co-receptors CCR5/ CXCR4**.
- **Transmission** – **sexual (commonest – ~85–90 % in India)**, **blood & blood products**, **sharing needles (IDU)**, **mother-to-child (transplacental, intrapartum – maximum, breastfeeding)** – **MTCT risk ~30–45 % without intervention, < 2 % with ART**; **NOT spread by** casual contact, hugging, sharing utensils, mosquitoes, swimming pools.
- **Inactivated by** – heat (56 °C × 30 min), 70 % alcohol, 1 % hypochlorite (enveloped – fragile outside body).
- **Natural history** – **acute HIV (2–4 wks – seroconversion illness, flu-like; high viral load, infectious)** → **window period (infected but antibody −ve – ~3–12 weeks; 4th gen tests reduce it)** → **clinical latency (asymptomatic, 8–10 yrs)** → **symptomatic** → **AIDS (CD4 < 200 cells/µl or AIDS-defining illness)**; **normal CD4 – 500–1500/µl**.
- **Opportunistic infections (OIs)** – **TB (commonest OI & commonest cause of death in India)**, **oral candidiasis (commonest fungal)**, **Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia (PCP – CD4 < 200; cotrimoxazole prophylaxis)**, **cryptococcal meningitis (CD4 < 100)**, **toxoplasmosis (brain abscess)**, **CMV retinitis (CD4 < 50)**, MAC, cryptosporidiosis/ isosporiasis (chronic diarrhoea), herpes zoster, **Kaposi's sarcoma**, lymphoma, HIV wasting, PML.
- **Diagnosis (NACO)** – **ELISA/ rapid tests (3 different antigens/ principles – "strategy III" for diagnosis)**; **p24 antigen (early)**; **HIV DNA PCR – infants < 18 months (maternal antibodies interfere – Early Infant Diagnosis at 6 weeks)**; **Western blot** (older confirmatory); **viral load (RNA PCR) – monitoring**; **CD4 count**.
- **ART** – **"Test & Treat" (all HIV+ regardless of CD4, since 2017)**; first-line **TLD (tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir)**; adherence > 95 %; **PPTCT – lifelong ART to pregnant women + infant nevirapine prophylaxis (6–12 weeks)**; **PEP – within 72 hrs, 28 days**.
- **National programmes** – **NACO (1992)**, **NACP-V (2021–26)**; **ICTC** (Integrated Counselling & Testing Centre); **HIV/ AIDS (Prevention & Control) Act 2017**; **World AIDS Day – 1 December**; **red ribbon**; **UNAIDS 95-95-95 targets**.
