## 7.1 Fungi – General

- **Eukaryotic**, **cell wall of chitin**, cell membrane contains **ergosterol** (target of **amphotericin B – binds ergosterol; azoles – inhibit its synthesis**); heterotrophic; reproduce by **spores (sexual & asexual)**.
- **Morphological types** – **yeasts (unicellular, budding – Cryptococcus)**, **yeast-like (pseudohyphae – Candida)**, **moulds/ filamentous (hyphae → mycelium – Aspergillus, Mucor, dermatophytes)**, **dimorphic (yeast at 37 °C in tissue, mould at 25 °C – Histoplasma, Blastomyces, Sporothrix, Talaromyces/ Penicillium marneffei)** ("**Mould in the cold, yeast in the heat**").
- **Hyphae** – **septate (Aspergillus – acute-angle/ 45° dichotomous branching)** vs **aseptate/ pauciseptate, broad, ribbon-like, right-angle branching (Mucor/ Rhizopus – mucormycosis)**.
- **Lab diagnosis** – **KOH mount (10–20 % – skin, nail, hair)**, **India ink (Cryptococcus capsule – CSF)**, **Gram stain (Candida – Gram +ve budding yeast)**, **culture – Sabouraud's dextrose agar (SDA, with chloramphenicol/ cycloheximide)**, **lactophenol cotton blue mount**, **germ tube test (Candida albicans – within 2 hrs)**, **Wood's lamp (UV – Microsporum fluoresces green; tinea versicolor yellow-gold)**, serology (cryptococcal antigen, galactomannan – Aspergillus, β-D-glucan).

## 7.3 Parasitology – General

- **Parasite** – lives on/ in host, deriving benefit; **ectoparasite** (lice, mites – scabies) vs **endoparasite**.
- **Definitive host** – harbours **adult/ sexual stage**; **intermediate host** – **larval/ asexual stage**; **reservoir host**; **paratenic**; **man as definitive host** for most helminths; **man as intermediate host – malaria (mosquito is definitive), hydatid disease (dog definitive), Toxoplasma (cat definitive), cysticercosis (Taenia solium larva)**.
- **Protozoa** – unicellular; **trophozoite (active, feeding, motile) & cyst (resistant, infective form – most intestinal protozoa)**; **helminths** – multicellular worms – **nematodes (roundworms), cestodes (tapeworms), trematodes (flukes)**.
- **Lab** – **stool examination – saline mount (trophozoites, eggs) & iodine mount (cysts – nuclei)**; **formol-ether concentration**; **3 consecutive samples**; **stool for trophozoites – warm & fresh, within 30 min**; **peripheral blood smear (thick – detection, thin – species)**, rapid antigen tests.

## 7.5 Malaria

- **Species** – **P. vivax (commonest in India overall ~ half, relapses)**, **P. falciparum (most dangerous – cerebral malaria, severe/ complicated; ~ half in India, dominant in Odisha, NE, tribal/ forest areas)**, P. malariae (quartan, nephrotic syndrome), P. ovale (relapses; Africa), **P. knowlesi (monkeys, SE Asia)**.
- **Vector – female Anopheles mosquito** (bites at **night/ dusk–dawn**; breeds in **clean water**); **India – An. culicifacies (rural – main vector ~65 %), An. stephensi (urban – overhead tanks, wells), An. fluviatilis (hills/ foothills – streams), An. minimus & An. dirus (NE – forest), An. sundaicus (coastal – brackish water)**.
- **Life cycle** – **mosquito = definitive host (sexual cycle – sporogony)**; **man = intermediate host (asexual cycle – schizogony)**; **infective form to man – sporozoites (mosquito saliva)**; **infective form to mosquito – gametocytes**; **exo-erythrocytic (liver) cycle** → **merozoites** → **erythrocytic cycle** (ring forms → trophozoites → schizonts → RBC rupture → fever paroxysm); **hypnozoites (dormant liver forms) – P. vivax & P. ovale → relapses (primaquine kills)**; **extrinsic incubation period in mosquito ~10–14 days** (temperature dependent); **intrinsic incubation (man) – vivax ~14 days, falciparum ~12 days**.
- **Fever periodicity** – **tertian (every 48 hrs) – vivax, ovale ("benign tertian"), falciparum ("malignant tertian" – often irregular)**; **quartan (72 hrs) – malariae**; **quotidian (24 hrs) – knowlesi/ mixed**.
- **Paroxysm stages** – **cold stage (chills, rigors – 15–60 min) → hot stage (high fever 2–6 hrs) → sweating stage (2–4 hrs)**.
- **Falciparum features** – **all ages of RBCs infected (high parasitaemia), multiple ring forms per RBC, accolé/ appliqué forms, banana/ crescent-shaped gametocytes (diagnostic), schizonts not seen in peripheral blood (sequestration in capillaries – cytoadherence)**; **complications – cerebral malaria (coma), severe anaemia, hypoglycaemia, ARDS, AKI (blackwater fever – haemoglobinuria), shock (algid malaria), jaundice, DIC**; **vivax – Schüffner's dots, enlarged RBCs, amoeboid trophozoites**.
- **Diagnosis** – **peripheral blood smear (gold standard) – thick smear (detection, more sensitive), thin smear (species identification)** – **Giemsa/ JSB/ Leishman stain**; **RDT (bivalent – HRP-2 for Pf, pLDH for Pv) – ASHA level, 15–20 min**; QBC; PCR; **slide collection before antimalarials, from finger prick (3rd/ 4th finger) of all fever cases**.
- **Treatment (NCVBDC/ NVBDCP, 2013 & updates)** – **vivax: chloroquine 25 mg/kg over 3 days (10-10-5) + primaquine 0.25 mg/kg × 14 days (radical cure; G6PD; not in pregnancy & infants)**; **falciparum: ACT – artesunate (3 days) + sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine (day 1) [AS + SP] – rest of India; artemether-lumefantrine (AL) – North-East states** + **primaquine 0.75 mg/kg single dose (gametocidal, day 2)**; **severe malaria – injectable artesunate (IV/ IM)**, quinine (alternative); **1st trimester pregnancy Pf – quinine** (ACT now also allowed per WHO).
- **Prevention** – **LLINs (long-lasting insecticidal nets)**, **IRS (indoor residual spraying – DDT/ synthetic pyrethroids/ malathion)**, larvicides (temephos/ Abate), **larvivorous fish (Gambusia, guppy)**, source reduction, repellents; chemoprophylaxis (travellers – doxycycline 100 mg daily/ mefloquine weekly); **RTS,S & R21 vaccines (WHO – Africa)**.
- **National Framework for Malaria Elimination (2016–2030)** – **zero indigenous cases by 2027, elimination by 2030**; **World Malaria Day – 25 April**; **Anti-Malaria Month – June**.
- **Indicators** – **API (annual parasite incidence = confirmed cases/ population × 1000)**, **ABER (annual blood examination rate ≥ 10 %)**, SPR, SFR.

## 7.6 Helminths

### Nematodes (roundworms)
| Worm | Points |
|---|---|
| **Ascaris lumbricoides (roundworm)** | **Largest intestinal nematode (female 20–35 cm)**; **faeco-oral (embryonated eggs – soil)**; **larval lung migration → Loeffler's syndrome (cough, eosinophilia)**; **intestinal obstruction (worm bolus) in children**, biliary obstruction, malnutrition; **albendazole 400 mg** |
| **Hookworm (Ancylostoma duodenale, Necator americanus)** | **Filariform larva penetrates skin (walking barefoot – feet) – "ground itch"**; attach to **jejunum** – suck blood (**~0.2 ml/day per worm – A. duodenale**) → **iron deficiency (microcytic hypochromic) anaemia**, hypoproteinaemia; **eosinophilia**; prevention – **wear footwear, sanitary latrines**; albendazole + iron |
| **Enterobius vermicularis (pinworm/ threadworm)** | **Commonest in children**; **perianal itching at night (female lays eggs at anus)**; **autoinfection** (scratching – fingernails); **NIH swab/ cellophane (Scotch) tape test – early morning before bath** (eggs D-shaped/ planoconvex); **treat whole family**; albendazole repeat after 2 weeks; cut nails, wash hands |
| **Trichuris trichiura (whipworm)** | **Barrel/ lemon-shaped eggs with bipolar plugs**; caecum; **rectal prolapse**, dysentery, anaemia |
| **Strongyloides stercoralis** | **Skin penetration; autoinfection → hyperinfection syndrome (steroids, HTLV-1)**; **larvae (rhabditiform) in stool**; ivermectin |
| **Wuchereria bancrofti, Brugia malayi (filaria)** | **Lymphatic filariasis** – **vector: Culex quinquefasciatus (W. bancrofti – urban, dirty water)**, **Mansonia (Brugia – water plants, Kerala)**; **adult worms in lymphatics** → **lymphoedema, elephantiasis (legs), hydrocele (commonest chronic manifestation in men)**, chyluria, **tropical pulmonary eosinophilia**; **microfilariae – nocturnal periodicity (10 PM–2 AM – night blood smear)**; **ICT card test (antigen)**; **Rx/ MDA – DEC 6 mg/kg + albendazole (DA); IDA (ivermectin + DEC + albendazole – triple drug) in selected districts**; **MMDP (morbidity management) – foot hygiene for lymphoedema**; elimination target **2027** |
| **Dracunculus medinensis (Guinea worm)** | **Drinking water from step-wells containing Cyclops (water flea)**; worm emerges from blister on leg; **India certified free in 2000**; prevention – filtering water through cloth |
| **Toxocara** | Visceral larva migrans (dog/ cat) |
| **Trichinella spiralis** | Undercooked pork – myositis, periorbital oedema, eosinophilia |
| **Ancylostoma braziliense** | **Cutaneous larva migrans (creeping eruption)** – dog/ cat hookworm |

### Cestodes (tapeworms) – flat, segmented, hermaphrodite
| Worm | Points |
|---|---|
| **Taenia solium (pork tapeworm)** | **Undercooked pork (cysticerci) → intestinal taeniasis**; **ingestion of eggs (faeco-oral/ autoinfection) → cysticercosis – neurocysticercosis (commonest cause of acquired epilepsy in developing countries; "starry sky" calcifications)** – man acts as intermediate host; **pig – intermediate host**; Rx – **albendazole/ praziquantel + steroids** |
| **Taenia saginata (beef tapeworm)** | Undercooked beef; **longest tapeworm (up to 10 m)**; no cysticercosis in man |
| **Echinococcus granulosus (dog tapeworm)** | **Hydatid disease** – **dog = definitive host; sheep/ man = intermediate host (accidental)**; **liver (commonest ~60–70 %, right lobe) > lungs**; **hydatid cyst – "hydatid sand", daughter cysts**; **rupture → anaphylaxis**; **Casoni test (old)**, USG/ CT, serology; **Rx – surgery (PAIR) + albendazole** |
| **Hymenolepis nana (dwarf tapeworm)** | **Smallest tapeworm; commonest tapeworm in humans (children)**; no intermediate host needed (direct, autoinfection) |
| **Diphyllobothrium latum (fish tapeworm)** | **Largest tapeworm** (up to 10–25 m); **vitamin B12 deficiency (megaloblastic anaemia)**; raw fish |

### Trematodes (flukes) – flat, unsegmented, leaf-shaped; snail intermediate host
- **Schistosoma haematobium** – **urinary schistosomiasis (terminal haematuria, bladder cancer – squamous)**; **S. mansoni, S. japonicum** – intestinal/ hepatic (portal hypertension); **cercariae penetrate skin in fresh water (swimmer's itch)**; praziquantel.
- **Fasciola hepatica (sheep liver fluke)**, **Fasciolopsis buski (largest intestinal fluke – water plants)**, **Clonorchis sinensis (Chinese liver fluke – cholangiocarcinoma, raw fish)**, **Paragonimus westermani (lung fluke – crabs; haemoptysis mimics TB – NE India)**.
