## 1.1 Definition & Branches

:::def Definitions
- **Microbiology** – study of **microorganisms (microbes)** too small to be seen by naked eye (< 0.1 mm) – **bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, helminths (parasitology), algae**.
- **Pathogen** – microbe capable of causing disease; **opportunistic pathogen** – causes disease only when host immunity is low (Candida, Pneumocystis); **commensal** – lives on host without harm (normal flora).
- **Virulence** – degree of pathogenicity; **infectivity**, **invasiveness**, **toxigenicity**.
- **Branches** – bacteriology, virology, mycology (fungi), parasitology (protozoa & helminths), immunology, medical/ clinical microbiology.
:::

### Koch's postulates
1. The organism must be present in **every case** of the disease (absent in healthy).
2. It must be **isolated in pure culture**.
3. Pure culture **inoculated into a healthy susceptible animal** must produce the **same disease**.
4. The organism must be **re-isolated** from the experimental animal.
- **Exceptions** – organisms that **can't be cultured (M. leprae, T. pallidum)**, carriers, viruses; **molecular Koch's postulates (Falkow)**.

## 1.3 Classification of Microorganisms

| Feature | **Prokaryotes** (bacteria) | **Eukaryotes** (fungi, protozoa, helminths, human cells) |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | **No nuclear membrane** (nucleoid), single circular chromosome | **True nucleus with membrane**, multiple chromosomes |
| Organelles | **No mitochondria, ER, Golgi** | Present |
| Ribosomes | **70S (30S + 50S)** – antibiotic target | **80S (40S + 60S)** |
| Cell wall | **Peptidoglycan** (except Mycoplasma – no wall) | Fungi – **chitin**; animal cells – none |
| Sterols in membrane | Absent (except Mycoplasma) | Present (fungi – **ergosterol**, target of azoles & amphotericin) |
| Division | **Binary fission** | Mitosis/ meiosis |
| Size | 0.5–5 µm | > 5 µm |

- **Viruses** – **acellular**, **obligate intracellular parasites**, contain **either DNA or RNA (never both)**, no metabolism/ ribosomes, **not affected by antibiotics**; **prions** – infectious proteins (no nucleic acid – CJD, kuru, mad cow) – **most resistant to sterilisation**; **viroids** – naked RNA (plants).
- **Chlamydia & Rickettsia** – **obligate intracellular bacteria**; **Mycoplasma** – **smallest free-living bacteria, no cell wall** (penicillin-resistant).
- **Whittaker's 5 kingdoms** – Monera (bacteria), Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia; **3 domains** – Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya.
- **Binomial nomenclature (Linnaeus)** – genus (capital) + species (small), italics – e.g. ***Staphylococcus aureus***.

## 1.4 Microscopy

| Microscope | Principle / use |
|---|---|
| **Light (bright-field/ compound) microscope** | Most common; stained smears; **magnification up to ~1000× (oil immersion – 100× objective × 10× eyepiece)**; **resolving power ~0.2 µm**; **cedar wood oil/ immersion oil** ↑ resolution |
| **Dark-ground (dark-field)** | **Unstained thin organisms – Treponema pallidum (syphilis), Leptospira**; motility |
| **Phase-contrast** | Living unstained cells, internal structures |
| **Fluorescence** | **Fluorescent dyes – auramine-rhodamine (TB – LED microscopy)**, immunofluorescence (rabies – corneal impression) |
| **Electron microscope (TEM/ SEM)** | **Viruses**; **resolution ~0.1–1 nm**; magnification up to ~1,000,000×; **Ernst Ruska & Knoll (1931)** |

- **Units** – **1 mm = 1000 µm (micrometre/ micron)**; **1 µm = 1000 nm**; bacteria measured in **µm**, viruses in **nm**.
- **Parts** – eyepiece (ocular), objectives (10×, 40×, **100× oil**), **condenser** (focuses light), iris diaphragm, coarse & fine adjustment, stage; **total magnification = objective × eyepiece**.

## 1.5 Normal Flora (Microbiota)

| Site | Common normal flora |
|---|---|
| **Skin** | **Staphylococcus epidermidis (most common)**, S. aureus (nose – 30 % carriers), diphtheroids, Propionibacterium (acne) |
| **Nose & throat** | **S. aureus (anterior nares)**, viridans streptococci, Neisseria, H. influenzae |
| **Mouth** | **Viridans streptococci (S. mutans – dental caries)**, anaerobes |
| **Stomach** | Almost sterile (acid) – H. pylori in some |
| **Large intestine** | **Most populated (~10¹¹/g) – Bacteroides (most common, anaerobes), E. coli, Enterococcus, Clostridium, Bifidobacteria, Lactobacilli** |
| **Vagina** | **Lactobacilli (Döderlein's bacilli – acidic pH)**, Candida (small numbers) |
| **Urethra** | Distal part – S. epidermidis, diphtheroids |
| **Sterile sites** | **Blood, CSF, bladder urine, lower respiratory tract, internal organs, peritoneal & pleural cavities** |

- **Benefits** – **colonisation resistance (prevent pathogens)**, **vitamin K & B synthesis (gut)**, immune stimulation; **disturbance by antibiotics → superinfection – C. difficile (pseudomembranous colitis), oral/ vaginal candidiasis**.
- **Newborn** – sterile at birth (largely), colonised during delivery & feeding; **breast-fed baby – Lactobacillus bifidus** predominate.

## 1.6 Importance of Microbiology in Nursing

- **Prevention & control of hospital-acquired infection (HAI)**; asepsis, sterilisation, disinfection.
- **Correct specimen collection, labelling & transport**; interpretation of reports.
- **Safe handling of biomedical waste**; standard precautions; **needle-stick injury management**.
- Immunisation, health education (hand washing, safe water, food hygiene).
- Antibiotic stewardship & AMR awareness; patient isolation.
