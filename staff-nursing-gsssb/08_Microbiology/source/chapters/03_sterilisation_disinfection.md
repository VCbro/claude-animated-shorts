# Sterilisation & Disinfection
<!-- desc: Definitions, physical methods (heat, radiation, filtration), autoclave, hot air oven, chemical disinfectants, Spaulding classification, sterilisation indicators, CSSD, care of instruments, fumigation, spill management -->

## 3.1 Definitions

:::def Definitions
- **Sterilisation** – process that **destroys/ removes ALL microorganisms including bacterial spores**.
- **Disinfection** – destruction of **pathogenic** microbes on **inanimate objects** (not necessarily spores).
- **Antisepsis** – disinfection of **living tissue/ skin** (antiseptic – povidone-iodine, chlorhexidine, alcohol).
- **Asepsis** – absence of pathogenic organisms; **medical asepsis (clean technique)** – reduce number & spread; **surgical asepsis (sterile technique)** – eliminate all microbes.
- **Decontamination** – making items safe to handle (removing/ inactivating pathogens) – **first step**; **cleaning** – physical removal of dirt & organic matter (**must precede disinfection/ sterilisation**).
- **Bactericidal** (kills) vs **bacteriostatic** (inhibits growth); **sporicidal, virucidal, fungicidal**.
- **Sanitisation** – reducing microbes to safe public-health levels (utensils).
- **Fumigation** – disinfection of rooms/ OT with gas/ vapour.
:::

- **Order of resistance (most → least)** – **prions > bacterial spores > mycobacteria (TB) > non-enveloped (small) viruses (polio, hepatitis A) > fungi > vegetative bacteria > enveloped (lipid) viruses (HIV, HBV, influenza)**.

## 3.2 Physical Methods

@fig sterilisation.svg | Methods of sterilisation & disinfection with Spaulding classification

### Heat (most reliable)
- **Moist heat** – kills by **coagulation & denaturation of proteins**; **more efficient than dry heat** (penetrates better, lower temp & time).
- **Dry heat** – kills by **oxidation**, protein denaturation.

| Method | Details | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Autoclave (steam under pressure)** | **121 °C, 15 lb/in² (psi), 15–20 min** (most common); **126 °C – 20 psi – 10 min**; **134 °C, 30 psi, 3–5 min (flash/ prevacuum)**; principle – **water boils at higher temp under pressure**; air must be removed (air pockets prevent sterilisation); **most effective method for heat-resistant items** | **Surgical instruments, linen, gowns, dressings, gloves, culture media, lab waste, rubber** |
| **Hot air oven (dry heat)** | **160 °C × 2 hrs** (holding time 1 hr in some texts) / **170 °C × 1 hr / 180 °C × 30 min**; fan for circulation; don't overload; **cool before opening (glass breaks)** | **Glassware (syringes, Petri dishes, pipettes), powders (talc), oils, greases, paraffin, sharp metal instruments (no blunting/ rust)** |
| **Incineration** | Burning to ash (≥ 800–1050 °C) | **Anatomical waste, soiled dressings, BMW (yellow)** |
| **Red heat/ flaming** | Holding in flame till red hot | **Inoculating loops, needles, forceps tips** |
| **Boiling** | **100 °C for 10–30 min** – **disinfection, NOT sterilisation (spores & some viruses survive)**; add 2 % sodium bicarbonate ↑ efficacy | Feeding bottles, utensils |
| **Pasteurisation** | **Holder method – 63 °C × 30 min**; **flash (HTST) – 72 °C × 15–20 s**; (UHT – 135–140 °C × 1–2 s) | **Milk** (kills Brucella, M. bovis, Coxiella, Salmonella) |
| **Tyndallisation (intermittent sterilisation)** | **100 °C (steam) × 20 min on 3 successive days** (spores germinate between) | Sugar/ egg/ serum media |
| **Inspissation** | **80–85 °C × 30 min on 3 days** | **LJ medium, Loeffler's serum slope** |
| **Vaccine bath** | 60 °C × 1 hr | Vaccines (killed) |

### Radiation
- **Ionising (gamma rays – Cobalt-60)** – **"cold sterilisation"** – **disposable syringes, catheters, sutures, gloves, IV sets, bone grafts** (industrial).
- **Non-ionising (UV, 260 nm)** – **poor penetration – surface & air disinfection** (OT, labs, biosafety cabinets); damages eyes & skin.

### Filtration
- **Heat-labile fluids** – **sera, antibiotics, vaccines, IV solutions, enzymes**; **membrane filters (0.22 µm)**, Seitz (asbestos), sintered glass, candle filters; **HEPA filters (air – OT, laminar air flow, ICU; remove particles ≥ 0.3 µm with 99.97 % efficiency)**; viruses & mycoplasma may pass.

### Others
- **Drying, freeze-drying (lyophilisation – preservation of cultures, vaccines)**, ultrasonic cleaning (instruments), plasma sterilisation (**hydrogen peroxide gas plasma – heat-sensitive items, low temp**).

## 3.3 Chemical Methods

| Agent | Concentration / features | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Alcohols (ethyl, isopropyl)** | **60–90 % (70 % best – water needed for protein denaturation)**; **not sporicidal**; quick evaporation | **Skin antisepsis, hand rub, thermometers, surfaces, injection site** |
| **Aldehydes – glutaraldehyde 2 % (Cidex)** | **High-level disinfectant (20 min) & sterilant (~10 hrs)**; **sporicidal**; non-corrosive; activated (alkaline) – use within **14 days**; toxic fumes – rinse with sterile water | **Endoscopes, cystoscopes, bronchoscopes, anaesthetic equipment, laparoscopes** |
| **Formaldehyde (formalin – 40 % gas in water)** | **10 % formalin – tissue preservation (histopathology)**; gas – **fumigation of OT/ rooms** (with KMnO₄ – 500 ml formalin + 250 g KMnO₄ per 1000 ft³; ~6 hrs/ overnight; neutralise with ammonia); carcinogenic, irritant | Fumigation, specimen preservation, toxoid preparation |
| **Ethylene oxide (ETO)** | Gas; **alkylating agent – sterilant at low temp (~50–60 °C)**; **toxic, flammable, carcinogenic – needs aeration (12–24 hrs)** | **Heat-sensitive – plastics, catheters, heart-lung machine, cardiac pacemakers, sutures, rubber, optical instruments** |
| **Halogens – chlorine (sodium hypochlorite/ bleach)** | **Hypochlorite – 1 % (10,000 ppm available chlorine) for blood/ body-fluid spills & BMW disinfection**; **0.5 % (5000 ppm) – surfaces, linen soaking**; 0.1 % – general; corrosive to metals, inactivated by organic matter; **freshly prepare daily**; **bleaching powder (33 % chlorine)** | Spills, floors, water chlorination (residual 0.5 mg/L), linen |
| **Iodine – povidone-iodine (Betadine 5–10 %), tincture iodine** | Iodophor – slow release; **sporicidal (prolonged)**, broad; **allow to dry (2 min)** | **Pre-operative skin prep, wounds, surgical scrub (7.5 %)** |
| **Chlorhexidine** | **0.5 % in 70 % alcohol (skin prep – preferred for CVC insertion)**, **2–4 % (hand scrub)**, **0.12–0.2 % mouthwash (VAP prevention)**, **4 % umbilical cord care gel (7.1 % chlorhexidine digluconate)**; **residual action**; not sporicidal | Hand hygiene, skin prep, oral care, catheter care |
| **Phenol & derivatives** | **Phenol (carbolic acid) – Lister**; **phenol coefficient (Rideal-Walker, Chick-Martin tests – efficiency vs phenol)**; **cresol (Lysol), chloroxylenol (Dettol), hexachlorophene** | Floors, surfaces, faeces; Dettol – skin |
| **Hydrogen peroxide** | **3 % – wound cleaning (effervescence)**; 6–7.5 % – HLD; gas plasma | Wounds, contact lens |
| **Quaternary ammonium compounds (QACs)** | **Cetrimide, benzalkonium chloride** – detergent; low-level; inactivated by soap; Savlon = **cetrimide + chlorhexidine** | Surfaces, skin cleaning |
| **Dyes** | **Gentian violet (crystal violet)**, acriflavine, brilliant green | Skin, wounds, media (selective) |
| **Heavy metals** | **Silver nitrate (1 % – Credé's method for ophthalmia neonatorum – now tetracycline/ erythromycin ointment)**, silver sulfadiazine (burns), mercurials (thiomersal – vaccine preservative) | – |
| **Peracetic acid** | Sporicidal, low temp | Endoscopes, dialysers |
| **Potassium permanganate (KMnO₄ – 1 : 10,000)** | Oxidising; **sitz bath, wound wash, gastric lavage (some poisonings)**, water disinfection of wells (old) | – |

- **Factors affecting disinfectant action** – **concentration, contact time, temperature, pH, presence of organic matter (blood, pus ↓ efficacy – clean first!)**, type & number of organisms, nature of surface (biofilm).
- **Ideal disinfectant** – broad-spectrum, fast, not inactivated by organic matter, non-toxic, non-corrosive, stable, cheap, odourless, residual effect.

## 3.4 Spaulding Classification

| Category | Contact | Examples | Process required |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | Enter **sterile tissue/ vascular system** | **Surgical instruments, needles, urinary catheters, implants, IV cannula, biopsy forceps** | **Sterilisation** |
| **Semi-critical** | **Mucous membranes/ non-intact skin** | **Endoscopes (GI), laryngoscope blades, ET tubes, anaesthesia circuits, vaginal speculum, thermometers (oral/ rectal)** | **High-level disinfection (HLD)** – glutaraldehyde, OPA, peracetic acid, hydrogen peroxide; (boiling/ pasteurisation) |
| **Non-critical** | **Intact skin** | **BP cuff, stethoscope, bedpans, crutches, bed rails, linen, furniture** | **Low/ intermediate-level disinfection** – 70 % alcohol, hypochlorite, QACs, phenolics; detergent & water |

## 3.5 Monitoring Sterilisation (Indicators)

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Mechanical/ physical** | Pressure gauge, thermometer, timer, printouts |
| **Chemical indicators** | **Autoclave tape (diagonal stripes turn black)**, **Bowie-Dick test (air removal in pre-vacuum autoclave – daily first run)**, Browne's tubes (red → green), internal indicator strips (class 4–6) |
| **Biological indicators (most reliable – gold standard)** | **Autoclave – Geobacillus stearothermophilus spores**; **hot air oven – Bacillus atrophaeus (formerly B. subtilis var. niger) / Clostridium tetani (non-toxigenic) spores**; **ETO – Bacillus atrophaeus**; **ionising radiation – Bacillus pumilus**; filtration – Brevundimonas diminuta / Serratia marcescens |

## 3.6 CSSD & Care of Equipment

- **CSSD (Central Sterile Supply Department)** – zones: **dirty (receiving & decontamination) → clean (inspection, packing) → sterile (storage & issue)** – **unidirectional flow**.
- **Steps for reprocessing** – **decontamination (soaking in 0.5 % hypochlorite × 10 min) → cleaning (detergent, brushing, ultrasonic; disassemble) → drying → inspection → packing (double-wrap, indicator tape, label with date) → sterilisation → storage → issue**.
- **Sterile packs** – shelf life **event-related** (intact, dry, unopened – generally re-sterilise after ~7–30 days per policy in cloth wraps); **wet/ torn/ dropped pack = unsterile**.
- **Sterile field rules** – only sterile touches sterile; **edges (1 inch border) of sterile field are unsterile**; below waist/ table level unsterile; **never turn back on sterile field**; **if in doubt, consider it contaminated**; pour fluids from height without splashing.
- **Rubber goods** – autoclave at lower temp/ time; **gloves – powder before autoclaving**; **catheters – disposable (gamma/ ETO)**.
- **Thermometers** – wash with soap & cold water, wipe with 70 % alcohol **from stem (cleaner) towards bulb (dirtier)**, store dry.
- **Linen** – soiled/ infected – **red bag/ bin**, soak in 0.5–1 % hypochlorite, wash hot (≥ 71 °C × 25 min).
- **Blood/ body fluid spill management** – **wear gloves (PPE) → cover spill with absorbent paper → pour 1 % hypochlorite (10,000 ppm) over it → leave 10–20 min → wipe (outside → inside) → discard in yellow bag → clean with detergent**; spill kit.
