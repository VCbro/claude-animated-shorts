## 4.1 Infection – Basic Terms

- **Infection** – entry & multiplication of microorganisms in host tissues; **colonisation** – presence & multiplication **without** tissue invasion/ illness; **contamination** – presence on surface/ object.
- **Types** – **primary, secondary** (on top of primary), **cross-infection** (from another patient/ source), **nosocomial (hospital-acquired)**, **iatrogenic** (due to medical intervention), **subclinical (inapparent)**, **latent** (dormant – HSV, TB), **opportunistic**, **endogenous** (own flora) vs **exogenous**, **zoonosis** (animals → humans – rabies, plague, leptospirosis, brucellosis, anthrax), **focal**, **local/ systemic**.
- **Carrier** – harbours pathogen without disease & can spread it (**typhoid Mary**); **incubatory, convalescent, healthy, chronic (> 1 yr – typhoid in gall bladder)**.
- **Stages of infectious disease** – **incubation period** (entry → first symptoms) → **prodromal** (vague, non-specific) → **illness (acute/ fulminant)** → **decline** → **convalescence**.
- **Epidemic** – sudden ↑ cases above expected in community; **endemic** – constantly present; **pandemic** – worldwide (COVID-19, 1918 flu); **sporadic** – scattered cases; **outbreak** – localised epidemic.

| **Infectious agent** | Bacteria, virus, fungi, parasites | Early diagnosis, treatment, disinfection, sterilisation |

| **Portal of exit** | Respiratory, GI, blood, skin, urine | Cover cough, dressing wounds, safe disposal of excreta |

| Mode | Details | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact – direct** | Person-to-person (touch, sexual, kissing) | **MRSA, scabies, STIs, impetigo** |
| **Contact – indirect** | Via **fomites** (inanimate objects – bed rails, stethoscopes, towels) | **MRSA, C. difficile, rotavirus** |

| **Vertical** | Mother → baby (transplacental, during birth, breastfeeding) | **HIV, HBV, syphilis, rubella, toxoplasma, CMV (TORCH)** |

## 4.3 Hospital-Acquired Infections (HAI/ HCAI/ Nosocomial)

- **Definition** – infection **not present or incubating at admission**, appearing **≥ 48 hrs after admission** (or within 3 days of discharge/ **30 days of surgery**, **90 days if implant**).
- **Prevalence** – ~5–10 % of hospitalised patients (higher in ICU).
- **Common organisms** – **Gram-negative bacilli (E. coli, Klebsiella, Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter)**, **S. aureus (MRSA)**, Enterococcus (VRE), CoNS, **Candida**, C. difficile.

| HAI | Rank / key prevention ("bundle") |
|---|---|
| **CAUTI (catheter-associated UTI)** | **Commonest HAI (~30–40 %)**; **E. coli** commonest; prevention – **avoid unnecessary catheterisation, aseptic insertion, closed drainage system, bag below bladder level (off floor), maintain unobstructed flow, daily review & early removal, perineal hygiene**; don't routinely change catheter/ irrigate |
| **SSI (surgical site infection)** | **S. aureus** commonest; **pre-op bath, clip (don't shave) hair just before surgery, antibiotic prophylaxis within 60 min before incision, glycaemic control, normothermia, OT discipline, aseptic dressing** |
| **VAP (ventilator-associated pneumonia)** | **Head end elevation 30–45°**, **daily sedation vacation & readiness-to-wean assessment**, **oral care with chlorhexidine**, subglottic suction, **cuff pressure 20–30 cmH₂O**, DVT & peptic ulcer prophylaxis, hand hygiene |
| **CLABSI (central line-associated BSI)** | **Hand hygiene, maximal sterile barrier precautions, chlorhexidine-alcohol skin prep, avoid femoral site (prefer subclavian), daily review & prompt removal**, scrub the hub, transparent dressing changed 7 days (gauze – 2 days) |
| **C. difficile infection** | Contact precautions, **soap & water hand wash (alcohol doesn't kill spores)**, hypochlorite cleaning, antibiotic stewardship |

## 4.4 Standard & Transmission-Based Precautions

### Standard precautions (CDC 1996) – for ALL patients, all the time
- **Treat all blood, body fluids, secretions, excretions (except sweat), non-intact skin & mucous membranes as potentially infectious**.
- Components – **hand hygiene**, **PPE** (gloves, gown, mask, eye protection as per risk), **respiratory hygiene/ cough etiquette**, **safe injection practices (one needle, one syringe, one time; no recapping)**, **sharps safety**, patient placement, **environmental cleaning**, care of equipment & linen, **waste management**, safe lumbar puncture (mask).
- **Universal precautions (1987 – blood-borne)** were expanded into standard precautions.

| Transmission-based | Precautions | Diseases |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact precautions** | **Single room/ cohort**, **gloves & gown on entry**, dedicated equipment (stethoscope, BP cuff) | **MRSA, VRE, C. difficile, scabies, rotavirus, herpes zoster (localised), wound infections, impetigo, RSV** |
| **Droplet precautions** | **Single room (or ≥ 1 m/ 3 ft spacing)**, **surgical mask within 1–2 m**, patient wears mask during transport | **Influenza, pertussis, diphtheria (pharyngeal), mumps, rubella, meningococcal meningitis (first 24 hrs of therapy), plague (pneumonic), adenovirus** |
| **Airborne precautions** | **AIIR (airborne infection isolation room) – negative pressure, ≥ 6–12 air changes/ hr, door closed**, **N95 respirator (fit-tested)** for staff, **surgical mask for patient** during transport; non-immune staff avoid (measles/ varicella) | **Pulmonary/ laryngeal TB, measles, varicella, disseminated zoster**; (COVID-19 – aerosol-generating procedures) |
| **Protective (reverse) isolation** | **Positive-pressure room**, HEPA; protects **immunocompromised** patient (neutropenia, transplant, burns) | – |

## 4.5 Hand Hygiene

- **Single most important & effective measure to prevent HAI**; **"Clean care is safer care" (WHO 2005)**; **World Hand Hygiene Day – 5 May**; **Global Handwashing Day – 15 October**.
- **WHO "My 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene"** – **1. Before touching a patient**; **2. Before clean/ aseptic procedure**; **3. After body fluid exposure risk**; **4. After touching a patient**; **5. After touching patient surroundings**.
- **Alcohol-based hand rub (ABHR)** – **preferred method when hands not visibly soiled**; **20–30 seconds**; ~3 ml.
- **Soap & water hand wash** – **40–60 seconds**; when **hands visibly soiled**, after **toilet**, **C. difficile/ spore-forming organisms, norovirus**, before eating.
- **WHO steps (6–7)** – 1. palm to palm; 2. right palm over left dorsum with interlaced fingers & vice versa; 3. palm to palm fingers interlaced; 4. backs of fingers to opposing palms (fingers interlocked); 5. rotational rubbing of thumbs; 6. rotational rubbing of fingertips in palm; (7. wrists) – then rinse, dry with single-use towel, **turn off tap with towel**.
- **Surgical hand scrub** – **2–5 min (traditionally 5 min first scrub)** with antiseptic (chlorhexidine 4 %/ povidone-iodine 7.5 %) or alcohol-based surgical rub; **hands held above elbows**; fingertips → elbows; **nails short, no rings/ watches/ artificial nails**.
- **Gloves don't replace hand hygiene** – perform before donning & after removing.
- **Most commonly missed areas** – **thumbs, fingertips, between fingers**.

## 4.6 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

- **Donning sequence (CDC)** – **hand hygiene → gown → mask/ respirator → goggles/ face shield → gloves** (gloves over gown cuffs).
- **Doffing sequence** – **gloves (most contaminated – first) → goggles/ face shield → gown → mask/ respirator (last, outside room for N95) → hand hygiene** (alternatively gown + gloves together); **hand hygiene between steps** if contaminated.
- **N95** – filters **≥ 95 % of particles ≥ 0.3 µm**; **fit test & seal check** each use; **surgical mask** – protects from droplets & protects patient from wearer.
- **Gloves** – sterile (surgical procedures), clean/ examination (contact with body fluids); **double gloving** in high-risk surgery; latex allergy – nitrile.

## 4.7 Needle-Stick Injury (NSI) & Post-Exposure Prophylaxis

- **Risk of transmission after percutaneous exposure** – **HBV ~6–30 % ("rule of 3s": HBV 30 %, HCV 3 %, HIV 0.3 %)**; **HIV via mucous membrane ~0.09 %**.
- **Immediate steps** – **wash wound with soap & running water** (don't scrub, **don't squeeze/ suck**, don't use bleach/ strong antiseptics); **mucosa – flush with water; eye – irrigate with clean water/ saline**; **report immediately to supervisor/ HICC**; document; assess source patient (HIV, HBsAg, HCV status).
- **HIV PEP** – **start as early as possible – ideally within 2 hrs, not later than 72 hrs**; **28 days**; regimen – **TLD (tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir)** (NACO); baseline & follow-up HIV tests (**6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months**).
- **HBV PEP** – if unvaccinated/ non-responder: **HBIG (0.06 ml/kg IM, within 24 hrs, ≤ 7 days) + HBV vaccine series**; vaccinated responder (anti-HBs ≥ 10 mIU/ml) – no treatment.
- **HCV** – no PEP; follow-up testing.
- **Prevention** – **never recap needles (if must – one-handed scoop technique)**, **dispose immediately in puncture-proof sharps container (fill only ¾)**, needle cutters/ destroyers, safety-engineered devices, **HBV vaccination of all HCWs (3 doses – 0, 1, 6 months)**.

## 4.8 Biomedical Waste Management Rules 2016 (amended 2018, 2019)

| Category (bag/ container) | Waste | Treatment/ disposal |
|---|---|---|
| **Yellow** (non-chlorinated plastic bags) | **Human anatomical waste (tissues, organs, body parts, placenta), animal waste, soiled waste (dressings, cotton, plaster casts, bags with blood/ body fluids), expired/ discarded medicines, cytotoxic drugs, chemical waste, discarded linen/ mattresses with body fluids, microbiology lab waste (pre-treat – autoclave on-site), chemical liquid waste** | **Incineration / plasma pyrolysis / deep burial** (rural, where allowed) |
| **Red** (non-chlorinated plastic bags/ containers) | **Contaminated recyclable waste – tubing, bottles, IV sets, catheters, urine bags, syringes (without needles & fixed needle syringes with cut needles), vacutainers, gloves** | **Autoclave/ microwave/ hydroclave → shredding → recycling** (not incinerated/ landfilled) |
| **White (translucent)** – **puncture-proof, leak-proof, tamper-proof container** | **Sharps – needles, syringes with fixed needles, needles from needle tip cutter/ burner, scalpels, blades**, other contaminated sharp metal | **Autoclave/ dry heat sterilisation → shredding/ encapsulation → iron foundry/ sanitary landfill** |
| **Blue** (cardboard boxes with blue marking/ puncture-proof containers) | **Glassware – broken or discarded & contaminated glass, medicine vials & ampoules (except cytotoxic), metallic body implants** | **Disinfection (soaking in hypochlorite)/ autoclave → recycling** |

- **Segregation at source (point of generation)** – most important step; **untreated BMW not stored beyond 48 hrs**; **barcoding of bags**; **biohazard & cytotoxic symbols**; **liquid waste – pre-treated (1 % hypochlorite) before drain**; **general (non-infectious) waste – black/ green bags (municipal)**; HCF must have **phase-out of chlorinated plastic bags**; records & annual report to **State Pollution Control Board**.
- **Pre-treatment of lab waste & blood bags – on-site autoclave/ disinfection (as per WHO/ NACO guidelines)** before sending.
- **Mercury spills** – collect with syringe/ cards into airtight container with water (no vacuum cleaner, no broom).

## 4.9 Infection Control Programme

- **Hospital Infection Control Committee (HICC)** – chairperson (medical superintendent), **infection control officer (microbiologist)**, **infection control nurse (ICN – key person; ~1 per 100–250 beds)**, members from medicine, surgery, nursing, CSSD, housekeeping, pharmacy; meets regularly; policies, surveillance, outbreak investigation, training, antibiotic policy.
- **Surveillance** – HAI rates (per 1000 device-days), **hand hygiene audits**, environmental sampling (OT – air settle plates as indicated), outbreak investigation.
- **Kayakalp** (2015) – award scheme for cleanliness & infection control in public health facilities; **Swachh Bharat**; **NQAS**.
- **Health care worker immunisation** – **hepatitis B (all)**, influenza (annual), varicella/ MMR (non-immune), tetanus, COVID-19.
