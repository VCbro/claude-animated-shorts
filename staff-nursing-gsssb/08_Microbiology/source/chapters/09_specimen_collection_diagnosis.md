## 9.1 General Principles

- **Collect before starting antibiotics/ antimicrobials** (or before the next dose – trough).
- **Collect from the actual site of infection**, avoiding contamination with normal flora.
- **Adequate quantity**, at the **right time** (e.g. **early morning sputum/ urine**, **blood culture during/ before fever spike**, **stool in acute phase**).
- **Sterile, leak-proof, wide-mouthed, screw-capped containers**; **aseptic technique**; **standard precautions (gloves)**.
- **Correct labelling** – **patient's name, age/ sex, IPD/ UHID number, ward, specimen type & site, date & time of collection**, collector's name; **label the container (not the lid)**; **fill requisition form** – clinical details, provisional diagnosis, **antibiotics already given**, investigation required; **biohazard label** for high-risk (HIV/ HBV).
- **Transport promptly – ideally within 1–2 hrs**; if delay – **refrigerate at 4 °C (urine, sputum, stool – most)** **EXCEPT**: **CSF (meningococcus – keep at 37 °C/ room temp, never refrigerate)**, **blood culture bottles (room temp/ incubator)**, **specimens for gonococcus, anaerobes**, genital specimens.
- **Transport media** for swabs – **Stuart's/ Amies**; **Cary-Blair/ VR medium (cholera)**; **viral transport medium (VTM)** for viral swabs (COVID, influenza); **triple packaging** for infectious substances (primary leak-proof → secondary leak-proof with absorbent → outer rigid package).
- **Rejection criteria** – unlabelled/ mislabelled specimens, leaking/ broken container, insufficient quantity, wrong container/ preservative, **delayed transport**, **24-hr urine incomplete**, saliva instead of sputum, dry swabs, formalin-fixed tissue for culture.

## 9.2 Blood Culture

- **Indications** – fever of unknown origin, **sepsis/ septic shock**, endocarditis, typhoid (1st week), neonatal sepsis, pneumonia, meningitis.
- **Timing** – **before antibiotics**, **ideally at onset of fever/ chills**; **2 sets (aerobic + anaerobic bottles) from 2 different sites** (to distinguish contaminants); **endocarditis – 3 sets** over hrs.
- **Volume** – **adults 8–10 ml per bottle (20 ml/ set)**; **children 1–5 ml (paediatric bottles)**; neonates 0.5–1 ml; **blood : broth ratio 1 : 5 to 1 : 10**.
- **Technique** – hand hygiene, gloves; **disinfect skin with 70 % alcohol then chlorhexidine-alcohol (or povidone-iodine – allow to dry 1–2 min)**; **don't palpate site after disinfection**; disinfect bottle tops with alcohol; **venepuncture, not from existing IV line** (except catheter-related BSI with paired peripheral sample); **inoculate aerobic bottle first (if using syringe – anaerobic first if butterfly to avoid air)**; label; **don't refrigerate** – send immediately/ incubate at 37 °C.
- **Automated systems (BACTEC, BacT/ALERT)** – continuous monitoring; most positives in 24–48 hrs; final negative at 5 days.
- **Common contaminants** – **CoNS (S. epidermidis), diphtheroids, Bacillus, Propionibacterium** (skin flora).

## 9.3 Urine

| Specimen | Method |
|---|---|
| **Clean-catch midstream urine (MSU) – commonest** | **Early morning sample (concentrated) preferred**; **wash genitals with soap & water (no antiseptic)** – female: separate labia, clean front to back; male: retract foreskin; **discard first part, collect middle portion (~10–20 ml) in sterile container**, don't touch inside of container |
| **Catheter specimen (CSU)** | **Never from drainage bag**; **clamp tubing below sampling port for 15–30 min**, **disinfect sampling port with alcohol**, **aspirate with sterile needle/ syringe from port** (or proximal catheter wall) |
| **Suprapubic aspiration** | **Gold standard (sterile) – infants**; any growth significant |
| **Infants** | Sterile adhesive urine bag (screening only), clean-catch, catheter |
| **24-hour urine** | **Discard first morning void, note time, collect all subsequent urine for 24 hrs including next morning's first void at same time**; preservative as per test; refrigerate; **if a sample is missed – restart** |
| **For AFB (genitourinary TB)** | **Early morning urine × 3–5 consecutive days** (entire first void) |

- **Transport** within **1–2 hrs** or **refrigerate at 4 °C (up to 24 hrs)** – bacteria multiply at room temp → false +ve.
- **Kass criteria – significant bacteriuria ≥ 10⁵ CFU/ml (100,000/ml)** in MSU; **10³–10⁵ – doubtful (repeat)**; **< 10³ – contamination/ insignificant**; any count in SPA significant.
- **Urine routine** – **pus cells > 5/HPF – pyuria**; **nitrite test (dipstick) – Gram-negative bacteria (E. coli)**; leukocyte esterase.

## 9.4 Stool

- **Fresh stool (~2–5 g or walnut size; 5–10 ml liquid)** in **clean, dry, wide-mouthed, screw-capped container**; **not mixed with urine, water or toilet disinfectant**; **from portion with blood/ mucus**.
- **For trophozoites (amoeba, Giardia) – examine within 30 min–1 hr (warm)**; **for ova & cysts – 3 samples on alternate/ consecutive days**.
- **Rectal swab** – when stool not available (cholera, shigella in infants).
- **Transport** – **Cary-Blair (cholera, Salmonella, Shigella)**, **VR medium/ alkaline peptone water (cholera)**, **buffered glycerol saline (Shigella)**; **AFP surveillance stool – 2 samples (≥ 8 g each) 24 hrs apart within 14 days of paralysis, reverse cold chain (2–8 °C)**.
- **Occult blood** – avoid red meat, iron, vitamin C, NSAIDs before test (guaiac).
- **Pinworm – cellophane tape (NIH swab), early morning before bathing/ defecation**.

## 9.5 Respiratory Specimens

- **Sputum** – **early morning, deep cough specimen (not saliva/ nasal secretion)**; **rinse mouth with plain water (no antiseptic/ toothpaste)**; take deep breaths, cough forcefully; **2–5 ml (purulent/ mucopurulent)** in **sterile wide-mouthed container**; **collect outdoors/ well-ventilated area** (TB); **TB – 2 samples (spot + early morning) for microscopy; 1 for CBNAAT/ TrueNat (NTEP)**; **induced sputum (3 % hypertonic saline nebulisation)** if unable; **gastric aspirate/ lavage – children (early morning, empty stomach)**; **quality – < 10 squamous epithelial cells & > 25 pus cells/ LPF (Bartlett's/ Murray-Washington)**.
- **Throat swab** – **tongue depressed, swab both tonsils & posterior pharyngeal wall (avoid touching tongue, cheeks, teeth)** – **diphtheria (both cotton & from edge of membrane)**, **streptococcal pharyngitis**.
- **Nasopharyngeal swab** – **flexible flocked swab through nostril parallel to palate to nasopharynx (depth ≈ nostril to ear tragus), rotate & hold few seconds** – **influenza, COVID-19 RT-PCR, pertussis (pernasal swab)**, RSV; place in **VTM, 2–8 °C**.
- **Nasal swab** – MRSA carrier screening (anterior nares).
- **BAL/ endotracheal aspirate** – ventilated patients (VAP).

## 9.6 Other Specimens

| Specimen | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Pus & wound swab** | **Aspirated pus (syringe) better than swab** (more organisms, anaerobes); **clean wound surface with sterile saline first (remove surface debris & exudate)**, **swab from base/ advancing edge using Levine technique (rotate over 1 cm² with pressure)**; 2 swabs (Gram stain + culture); anaerobes – **transport in syringe/ anaerobic medium without air**; deep tissue biopsy best |
| **CSF** | **Lumbar puncture by doctor (L3–L4/ L4–L5), strict asepsis**; **3 sterile tubes – 1st biochemistry (glucose, protein), 2nd microbiology (Gram stain, culture – most important), 3rd cytology/ cell count**; **~1 ml each (3–5 ml)**; **transport immediately, never refrigerate (keep at 37 °C – meningococcus & H. influenzae are fragile)**; India ink (Cryptococcus), CBNAAT (TB) |
| **High vaginal swab (HVS)/ endocervical swab** | **Speculum (no lubricant except water)**; HVS – Candida, Trichomonas (wet mount – examine immediately/ warm), bacterial vaginosis (**clue cells, whiff test – fishy odour with KOH, Amsel criteria**); **endocervical – gonococcus (Stuart's/ Amies, room temp, not refrigerated), Chlamydia (NAAT)** |
| **Urethral swab** | Gonococcus – **Gram stain (intracellular Gram −ve diplococci)**, culture |
| **Eye swab/ conjunctival** | Before instilling drops; ophthalmia neonatorum |
| **Ear swab** | Otitis externa/ media discharge |
| **Skin scrapings, nail, hair** | **Clean with 70 % alcohol, scrape advancing edge with scalpel, collect in black paper/ envelope (not closed container – moisture)** – fungi (KOH) |
| **Blood for serology** | **3–5 ml in plain tube (red top – no anticoagulant) → serum**; paired sera 10–14 days apart (rising titre) |
| **Blood smear for malaria** | **Finger prick**; thick & thin smears **on same slide/ separate**, **air-dry, label**; collect at fever/ before antimalarial; **thick smear – not fixed (dehaemoglobinised); thin smear – fixed with methanol** |
| **Microfilaria** | **Night blood (10 PM–2 AM)** – thick smear |
| **Tissue/ biopsy** | **Culture – in sterile saline (not formalin)**; histopathology – **10 % formalin (10 × volume)** |
| **Blood – anticoagulants (vacutainer colour)** | **Purple/ lavender – EDTA (CBC, blood group, HbA1c)**; **light blue – sodium citrate 3.2 % (PT/ INR, aPTT – 1:9 ratio, fill exactly)**; **red – plain/ clot activator (serum – serology, biochemistry)**; **yellow/ gold – SST gel (serum chemistry)**; **green – heparin (electrolytes, ABG – heparinised syringe)**; **grey – sodium fluoride + potassium oxalate (blood glucose – inhibits glycolysis)**; **ESR – black (citrate 1:4)** |
| **Order of draw (CLSI)** | **Blood culture → light blue (citrate) → red/ gold (serum) → green (heparin) → lavender (EDTA) → grey (fluoride)** |

## 9.7 Laboratory Safety & Biosafety

- **Biosafety levels (BSL)** – **BSL-1** (non-pathogenic – B. subtilis, E. coli K-12; teaching labs); **BSL-2** (moderate risk – **most clinical labs – S. aureus, HBV, HIV (specimens), Salmonella**; class II BSC for aerosols); **BSL-3** (serious airborne – **M. tuberculosis cultures, SARS-CoV-2 culture, Brucella, Yersinia pestis**; negative pressure, BSC class II/ III); **BSL-4** (life-threatening, no treatment – **Ebola, Marburg, Nipah, smallpox, Lassa**; positive-pressure suits; **NIV Pune – India's BSL-4 lab**).
- **Biological safety cabinets** – **class I** (protects user & environment), **class II (protects user, product & environment – most common in clinical labs, TB lab)**, **class III** (glove box – BSL-4).
- **Lab rules** – no eating/ drinking/ mouth pipetting; lab coat & gloves; hand wash; disinfect bench (start & end of day); report spills & accidents; **sharps safety**; vaccinated against HBV.
- **ICMR** – apex body for biomedical research; **NIV Pune (virology), NICD → NCDC Delhi (surveillance), NIRT Chennai (TB), NIMR Delhi (malaria)**, **Central Research Institute Kasauli (vaccines)**, **BCG Lab Guindy (Chennai)**, **Pasteur Institute Coonoor (rabies)**, **Haffkine Institute Mumbai (plague vaccine – W. M. Haffkine)**; **IDSP (Integrated Disease Surveillance Programme, 2004) – S, P, L forms (Syndromic, Presumptive, Laboratory)**; **IHIP portal**.

## 9.8 Nurse's Role in Microbiology

1. **Explain procedure** to patient, obtain cooperation/ consent.
2. **Collect correct specimen** at correct time, **before antibiotics**, using correct container & technique.
3. **Aseptic technique & standard precautions**; avoid contamination.
4. **Label correctly** & complete requisition form; **mention antibiotics**.
5. **Transport immediately** under appropriate conditions; **maintain chain of custody**.
6. **Record** collection in nurses' notes; **follow up & communicate reports** (critical values) to doctor.
7. Infection control – isolation, hand hygiene, waste disposal, **report notifiable diseases**.
8. **Health education** – hand washing, safe water & food, immunisation, completion of antibiotics.
