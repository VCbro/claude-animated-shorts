## 14.1 Basics

- **Hypoxaemia** – ↓ O₂ in **blood** (PaO₂ < 80; < 60 = respiratory failure). **Hypoxia** – ↓ O₂ at **tissue** level.
- **Types of hypoxia**: **Hypoxic (hypoxaemic)** – ↓ PaO₂ (altitude, lung disease); **Anaemic** – ↓ Hb (anaemia, **CO poisoning**); **Stagnant (circulatory/ ischaemic)** – ↓ blood flow (shock, HF); **Histotoxic** – cells can't use O₂ (**cyanide**).
- **Early signs of hypoxia**: **restlessness, anxiety, confusion (earliest)**, **tachycardia, tachypnoea**, ↑ BP, dyspnoea, use of accessory muscles, nasal flaring. **Late signs**: **cyanosis**, bradycardia, hypotension, arrhythmias, lethargy, coma.
- **Cyanosis** – bluish colour when **reduced (deoxygenated) Hb > 5 g/dL** – **late sign**; **central** (lips, tongue, mucosa – lung/ heart) vs **peripheral** (nail beds, extremities – cold, poor circulation). **Anaemic patients may not show cyanosis**; polycythaemia shows early.
- **Hypercapnia** – ↑ CO₂ (> 45) – headache, drowsiness, flushed warm skin, confusion.

## 14.2 Oxygen Therapy

:::key Oxygen – basic facts
- O₂ is a **drug** – needs **prescription** (except emergency) – dose (flow L/min or FiO₂), device, duration, target SpO₂.
- Room air FiO₂ = **21 %**. Each **1 L/min via nasal cannula ↑ FiO₂ by ~4 %**.
- **Target SpO₂**: most patients **94–98 %**; **COPD/ CO₂ retainers 88–92 %**.
- **O₂ cylinder colour (India – IS 3933)**: **Black body with white shoulder (neck)**; **Medical air** – grey body, black & white shoulder; **N₂O – French blue**; **CO₂ – grey**; **Entonox (N₂O + O₂) – blue body, blue-white shoulder**; Cyclopropane – orange; **Helium – brown**; Nitrogen – grey with black shoulder.
- Portable cylinders: **D (~340–400 L)**, **E (~680 L)**; full cylinder pressure ~**2000 psi (~137 bar)**.
- **Duration (min) = (Cylinder pressure psi − 200 safe residual) × cylinder factor ÷ flow (L/min)** (D = 0.16, E = 0.28).
- **Liquid O₂** – central supply (−183 °C); **O₂ concentrator** – home (extracts O₂ from air, 90–95 %).
:::

### O₂ delivery devices
| Device | Flow (L/min) | FiO₂ | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nasal cannula (prongs)** | **1–6** (> 6 – drying, no added benefit) | **24–44 %** | **Most common, comfortable**, can eat & talk; **humidify if > 4 L/min**; mouth breathing still gets some O₂ |
| **Simple face mask** | **5–10** (**minimum 5** – else CO₂ rebreathing) | **35–55/60 %** | Short-term |
| **Partial rebreather mask** | 6–11 (8–12) | **60–75 %** | Reservoir bag **⅓–½ full on inspiration** |
| **Non-rebreather mask (NRBM)** | **10–15** | **80–95 % (up to ~100 %)** | One-way valves; **bag must not collapse** (fill before applying); **highest concentration without intubation** – emergencies, CO poisoning |
| **Venturi mask (air-entrainment)** | 4–12 (as per colour adaptor) | **Precise 24–60 %** | **Most accurate/ fixed FiO₂ – COPD** |
| **Face tent** | 8–12 | 30–55 % | Facial trauma/ burns, claustrophobia |
| **Tracheostomy collar/ T-piece (Briggs)** | 8–10 | 24–100 % | Tracheostomy/ ET tube (humidified) |
| **Oxygen hood/ head box** | 7–12 | 30–90 % | **Infants** |
| **Oxygen tent** | 10–15 | ~30–50 % | Children (croup) – historic; humidified, cool |
| **High-flow nasal cannula (HFNC)** | **up to 60** | 21–100 % | Heated humidified – ICU |
| **CPAP/ BiPAP (NIV)** | | | Sleep apnoea, COPD exacerbation, pulmonary oedema |
| **Bag-valve-mask (Ambu bag)** | 10–15 with reservoir | ~100 % | Resuscitation |

:::warn Oxygen safety
- **No smoking / no open flame** – "**No Smoking – Oxygen in Use**" sign; O₂ **supports combustion** (not flammable itself).
- **No oil/ grease** on cylinder valves/ fittings (spontaneous combustion); no petroleum jelly on lips/ nose → use **water-soluble** lubricant.
- **Cotton** clothing & linen (static electricity – avoid wool/ synthetic).
- Electrical equipment grounded; cylinder **secured upright/ chained** on stand/ trolley (falls → valve breaks → "rocket"); store in cool place, away from heat.
- **Open cylinder valve slightly first ("cracking") to blow out dust** – **away from patient**.
- **Check flow meter** (ball/ float – read at **centre of ball**), humidifier water level (**sterile/ distilled water**, change every 24 hrs).
- **O₂ toxicity** – FiO₂ > **50–60 % for > 24–48 hrs** → substernal pain, dry cough, dyspnoea, **atelectasis**, ARDS-like; **Retinopathy of prematurity (ROP – retrolental fibroplasia)** in preterm infants → monitor SpO₂ (90–95 %).
- **COPD (CO₂ retainers)** – high O₂ → **↓ hypoxic drive → hypoventilation, CO₂ narcosis** → low flow, Venturi; but **never withhold O₂ from hypoxic patient**.
- Skin care – **behind ears, nares, bridge of nose** (pressure from tubing); oral care (drying).
:::

- **Humidification** – O₂ is **dry** → dries mucosa → humidify with **sterile/ distilled water** (bubble humidifier) for > 4 L/min; heated humidifier for tracheostomy/ ventilator (bypass upper airway).

## 14.3 Airway Clearance & Lung Expansion

### Deep breathing, coughing & incentive spirometry
- **Diaphragmatic (abdominal) breathing** – hand on abdomen rises on inhalation through nose, exhale through pursed lips.
- **Pursed-lip breathing** (COPD) – **inhale through nose (count 2), exhale slowly through pursed lips (count 4)** → keeps airways open (back pressure), **CO₂ out**, ↓ air trapping.
- **Controlled/ huff cough** – deep breath, hold 2–3 sec, 2–3 forceful coughs; **splint incision with pillow** (post-op).
- **Incentive spirometer** – **atelectasis & pneumonia prevention** (post-op): **sit upright**, exhale normally, **seal lips around mouthpiece**, **inhale slowly & deeply** (raise ball/ piston to target), **hold breath 3–5 sec**, exhale slowly; **10 times every 1–2 hrs while awake**; then cough. (**Volume-oriented** – piston; flow-oriented – balls.)

### Steam inhalation
- Purposes: **loosen secretions, soothe mucosa, relieve congestion** (common cold, sinusitis, bronchitis, croup). Medications – **tincture benzoin (Friar's balsam)**, menthol, eucalyptus.
- **Nelson's inhaler** – jug with spout; water **~ 60–70 °C (just below boiling, 140–160 °F)**; mouthpiece/ spout **wrapped with gauze**; distance **~ 23–30 cm (9–12 inch)** from face; cover head with towel (tent); **15–20 min**; after – avoid going out in cold air for **30 min**; **eyes closed**.
- Precautions: **scalds/ burns** (children, elderly – supervise), don't fill above level of spout.

### Nebulization
- Converts liquid medicine into **fine mist/ aerosol** (particles **1–5 µm** reach lower airways) – **bronchodilators (salbutamol, ipratropium), steroids (budesonide), NS, mucolytics (acetylcysteine), antibiotics**.
- Types: **jet (compressor)**, **ultrasonic**, mesh.
- Procedure: **sitting upright (Fowler's)**, medicine in cup (diluted with **NS 2–4 ml**), flow **6–8 L/min** (O₂ or air), breathe **slowly & deeply through mouth**, until mist stops (**10–15 min**), cough after; check pulse (salbutamol – **tachycardia, tremors**); **rinse mouth after steroids** (oral thrush); clean & dry equipment.
- **MDI (metered dose inhaler)** – shake, exhale, inhale slowly with actuation, **hold breath 10 sec**, wait **1 min** between puffs; **spacer** improves delivery; **bronchodilator first, steroid after (5 min gap)**.
- **DPI (dry powder inhaler)** – **rapid, forceful** inhalation; **don't shake**, don't exhale into device.

### Chest physiotherapy (CPT)
- **Postural drainage** – positioning so **gravity drains secretions from specific lung segments** into larger airways; done **before meals (1 hr) or 2–3 hrs after meals** (vomiting/ aspiration ↓), **early morning (secretions accumulate overnight) & bedtime**; each position **5–15 min** (total 20–30 min); **after bronchodilator nebulization** (open airways first).
 - **Upper lobes** – **sitting upright / semi-Fowler's**
 - **Middle lobe (R)/ lingula (L)** – **side-lying, head down ~15°** (quarter turn back)
 - **Lower lobes** – **Trendelenburg (head down 30–45°)**, prone/ lateral as per segment
- **Percussion (clapping)** – **cupped hands**, rhythmic over affected segment on **thin cloth (towel)**, **1–2 min (3–5 min)**; **avoid spine, sternum, breasts, kidneys, liver, spleen, below ribs**.
- **Vibration** – flat hand, tensed arm, **during exhalation**.
- **Contraindications to CPT**: **haemoptysis, pulmonary embolism, ↑ICP, unstable cardiac, rib fractures, osteoporosis, recent spinal/ eye/ head surgery, bleeding disorders, pneumothorax (untreated), empyema**, immediately after meals; head-down contraindicated in ↑ICP, GERD, late pregnancy, severe HTN.

## 14.4 Suctioning

| Type | Route | Technique |
|---|---|---|
| **Oropharyngeal (Yankauer – tonsil tip)** | Mouth | **Clean (medical asepsis)** |
| **Nasopharyngeal/ Nasotracheal** | Nose | **Sterile** |
| **Endotracheal/ Tracheostomy** | ET tube/ trach | **Sterile (open) or closed in-line system** |

:::key Suctioning – must-remember rules
- **Indications (as needed – not routine)**: audible/ visible secretions, **gurgling**, crackles/ rhonchi, ↓ SpO₂, ↑ peak airway pressure (ventilator), restlessness.
- **Position**: conscious – **semi-Fowler's**; unconscious – **lateral (facing nurse)**.
- **Hyperoxygenate with 100 % O₂ for 30–60 sec before & after** (hypoxia prevention).
- **Suction pressure**: **adult 100–150 mmHg (wall)** (some texts 80–120); **child 80–100 (100–120)**; **infant 60–80 (50–100)**; portable – adult 10–15 cmHg.
- **Catheter size**: **≤ half the internal diameter of artificial airway**; adult **12–16 Fr** (12–14 for trach), child 8–10, infant 5–8.
- **Insertion length**: nasopharyngeal – **tip of nose to earlobe (~13–15 cm adult)**; trach – until resistance felt (carina), then **withdraw 1 cm** before suction.
- **Insert WITHOUT suction** (thumb off port) → **apply intermittent suction while withdrawing with rotating motion**.
- **Each suction pass max 10–15 seconds** (time = 1 breath hold for nurse); **max 3 passes**; **rest 20–30 sec (1 min)** between passes.
- **Suction order**: **trachea first, then nose/ mouth** (same catheter: sterile → clean, never reverse; clean area to dirty).
- **Saline instillation – not routinely recommended** (pushes bacteria, ↓ SpO₂).
- Flush catheter with sterile NS between passes.
- **Complications**: **hypoxia (most common)**, **bradycardia/ arrhythmias (vagal)**, trauma/ bleeding, infection, atelectasis, laryngospasm, ↑ICP. **Stop if SpO₂ falls / HR drops** → reoxygenate.
:::

## 14.5 Tracheostomy Care

- **Tracheostomy** – surgical opening in trachea (**2nd–3rd/ 3rd–4th tracheal ring**); tube – **outer cannula, inner cannula, obturator** (+ cuff in cuffed tubes).
- **Bedside always**: **obturator, spare tube (same size & one size smaller)**, tracheal dilator, suction, O₂, Ambu bag, sterile gloves.
- **Care** (every 8 hrs/ PRN): sterile technique (fresh trach), **clean inner cannula** (disposable or with **H₂O₂ then rinse with NS**), stoma cleaned with **NS** (H₂O₂ half-strength if crusted, rinse), **sterile drain/ split gauze (pre-cut keyhole – never cut gauze – fibres aspirated)**, change ties – **2 persons / new tie before removing old**; **one finger (1–2 finger) space** under ties.
- **Cuff pressure 20–25 mmHg (25–30 cmH₂O)** – check every 8 hrs (manometer); high → **tracheal necrosis/ stenosis/ fistula**; low → aspiration, air leak.
- **Humidification** essential (upper airway bypassed); fluids.
- **Accidental decannulation** – fresh (< 72 hrs–7 days, tract not formed): **call help, keep stoma open with tracheal dilator/ hemostat**, O₂, bag-mask over mouth & cover stoma; mature tract – **insert spare tube with obturator, then remove obturator**.
- First tube change – by **surgeon/ ENT after 5–7 days**.
- Communication – **writing board, picture cards, speaking valve (Passy-Muir – cuff must be deflated)**.
- **Fenestrated tube** – allows speaking/ weaning. **Decannulation** – after capping trials (24 hrs).

:::exam PYQ style
- Earliest sign of hypoxia – **restlessness/ confusion**; late – **cyanosis**.
- O₂ cylinder colour (India) – **black body, white shoulder**.
- Nasal cannula flow – **1–6 L/min**; FiO₂ – **24–44 %**.
- Highest O₂ without intubation – **non-rebreather mask**.
- Precise FiO₂/ COPD – **Venturi mask**.
- Simple mask minimum flow – **5 L/min**.
- Humidifier water – **sterile/ distilled**.
- Suction each pass – **10–15 sec**; adult pressure – **100–150 mmHg**.
- Suction applied – **while withdrawing**, not inserting.
- Incentive spirometer – **inhale slowly & deeply, hold 3–5 sec**.
- Postural drainage timing – **before meals / early morning**.
- Steam inhalation distance – **~23–30 cm (9–12 inch)**.
- Tracheostomy cuff pressure – **20–25 mmHg**.
- Tracheostomy bedside – **obturator & spare tube**.
:::
