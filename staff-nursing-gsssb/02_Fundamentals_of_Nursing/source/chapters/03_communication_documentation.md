## 3.1 Communication

:::def Communication
Information, ideas, feelings ka **do ya zyada logon ke beech exchange**. Latin "**communis**" = common. Components: **Sender → Message → Channel/ Medium → Receiver → Feedback** (+ context/ environment, noise).
:::

- **Verbal** – spoken/ written words. **Non-verbal** – body language, facial expression, eye contact, touch, posture, gestures, tone (paralanguage), personal space (**proxemics**). **~65–93 % of communication is non-verbal**.
- **Proxemics (Edward Hall) – zones**: **Intimate 0–18 inch (0–45 cm)** – physical care; **Personal 18 inch–4 ft** – interview/ history; **Social 4–12 ft** – group; **Public > 12 ft** – lecture.
- **SOLER** (Egan) – active listening posture: **S**it squarely, **O**pen posture, **L**ean forward, **E**ye contact, **R**elaxed.
- Levels: intrapersonal (self-talk), interpersonal (2 persons), small group, public, transpersonal.
- **Therapeutic communication** – patient-centred, goal-directed, helps patient express feelings.

### Therapeutic vs Non-therapeutic techniques
| Therapeutic ✔ | Example | Non-therapeutic ✘ | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Active listening** | Attention, nodding | **False reassurance** | "Sab theek ho jayega" |
| **Open-ended questions** | "Aap kaisa mehsoos kar rahe hain?" | **Closed questions (yes/no) excessively** | "Dard hai?" |
| **Silence** | Patient ko sochne ka time | **Giving advice** | "Aapko surgery karwani chahiye" |
| **Restating / Paraphrasing** | "Aap keh rahe hain ki neend nahi aati?" | **Asking "Why"** | "Aapne dawa kyun nahi li?" (defensive banata hai) |
| **Reflecting** (feelings wapas) | "Aap pareshan lag rahe hain" | **Changing subject** | |
| **Clarifying** | "Aapka matlab kya hai?" | **Approval/ disapproval, judging** | "Ye galat hai" |
| **Focusing** | Main issue pe laana | **Stereotyping / clichés** | "Har koi aisa feel karta hai" |
| **Summarizing** | End me recap | **Belittling, defending** | "Doctor bahut ache hain" |
| **Offering self** | "Main aapke saath baithti hoon" | **Probing/ excessive questioning** | |
| **Sharing observation, Giving information, Exploring, Validating** | | **Literal responses, Disagreeing, Arguing** | |

:::tip Exam trick – therapeutic answer kaise pehchane?
Jis option me nurse **patient ki feelings ko accept/ explore** kare (open-ended, reflection, "Tell me more…") wo **therapeutic** hai. "Don't worry", "Why…", advice, doctor par baat taal dena – **non-therapeutic**.
:::

### Barriers to communication
Physical (noise, pain, hearing loss), language/ cultural, psychological (anxiety, fear, anger), **semantic (medical jargon)**, environmental, status difference, information overload, poor listening.

- **Communication with special patients**: hearing impaired – face the patient, good light, speak slowly, don't shout, written/ sign; visually impaired – announce yourself before touching, explain surroundings (clock positions for food), don't move furniture; aphasia – simple yes/no, picture boards; unconscious – **assume patient can hear** (hearing last sense lost) – talk, explain before touching; children – age appropriate, play; elderly – patience, low-pitch.

## 3.2 Nurse-Patient Relationship (NPR)

- **Therapeutic relationship** – **patient-centred, goal-directed, time-limited**, professional (not social).
- **Peplau's phases**: **Orientation** (meet, trust, contract, assess – patient seeks help) → **Identification** (patient identifies helper) → **Exploitation (working)** (patient uses services) → **Resolution (termination)** (goals achieved, independence).
- Standard phases (Stuart): **Pre-interaction** (nurse self-preparation, read records) → **Orientation (introductory)** → **Working** → **Termination**.
- **Trust** – foundation; **empathy, genuineness, respect, unconditional positive regard (Carl Rogers)**.
- **Transference** (patient apni purani feelings nurse par daale), **Counter-transference** (nurse apni feelings patient par).
- **Boundaries** – no gifts, no personal relationships, no self-disclosure overuse.

## 3.3 Health Education

:::def Health Education
"Health education is a process that informs, motivates and helps people to **adopt and maintain healthy practices and lifestyles**" (WHO). Aim – **behaviour change**.
:::

- **Principles**: interest, participation, known to unknown, **simple to complex**, motivation, comprehension, reinforcement, learning by doing, credibility, good human relations, **feedback**, setting an example.
- **Domains of learning (Bloom's taxonomy)**: **Cognitive** (knowledge – thinking), **Affective** (attitude/ feelings – values), **Psychomotor** (skills – e.g. insulin injection practice → **return demonstration**).
- Teaching methods: **lecture, demonstration & return demonstration (best for skills)**, discussion, role play, **group discussion, panel, symposium, workshop, seminar**, counselling (individual).
- **AV aids**: **auditory** (radio, tape), **visual** (charts, posters, flash cards, models, flannel graph, slides), **audio-visual** (TV, film, video). "**Cone of experience**" (Edgar Dale) – direct purposeful experience most effective; verbal symbols least.
- **Approaches**: regulatory, service, **educational**, primary health care.
- **Communication for change**: Knowledge → Attitude → Practice (**KAP**). **Stages of adoption**: Awareness → Interest → Evaluation → Trial → Adoption.
- Readiness to learn – first assess (pain, anxiety reduce karo pehle).

## 3.4 Records & Reports

:::def Record vs Report
**Record** – permanent **written** document of patient's care (legal document). **Report** – **oral or written** exchange of information among health team (e.g. shift handover, incident report).
:::

### Purposes of records
**Communication**, legal evidence, **quality assurance/ audit**, education, research, statistics, billing/ reimbursement, planning care.

### Principles of good documentation (ABC + more)
- **Accurate, Brief, Complete**, **Current/ timely** (immediately after care), **Factual (objective)**, **Legible**, **organized/ chronological**, **confidential**.
- **Blue/ black permanent ink**; date, time, **signature with designation** after each entry.
- **Error**: draw **single line**, write "**error/ mistaken entry**", initial/ sign – **never erase, white-out, overwrite, or tear page**.
- **No blank lines** – draw line through empty space.
- Chart **only your own care** (never for others); **never chart in advance**.
- Use **approved abbreviations** only; avoid "**U**" (write units), "**QD**" (write daily), trailing zero (**1.0 mg ✘ → 1 mg**), leading zero needed (**.5 ✘ → 0.5 mg**).
- Quote patient's words for subjective data ("…").
- Avoid vague terms ("good", "normal", "adequate", "seems").

### Methods of documentation
| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Narrative** | Story-like, chronological (traditional) |
| **SOAP / SOAPIE / SOAPIER** | **S**ubjective, **O**bjective, **A**ssessment, **P**lan, **I**ntervention, **E**valuation, **R**evision – **POMR** (Problem-Oriented Medical Record – **Lawrence Weed**) |
| **PIE** | **P**roblem, **I**ntervention, **E**valuation |
| **Focus charting / DAR** | **D**ata, **A**ction, **R**esponse |
| **Charting by exception (CBE)** | Only abnormal findings documented |
| **Kardex** | Card file – quick summary of care plan (not permanent record) |
| **Computerized/ EHR** | Electronic; password confidentiality |

### Common records & reports
- **Temperature/ TPR chart**, **I/O chart**, nurses' notes, medication administration record (**MAR**), doctor's order sheet, **consent form**, **incident report** (**NOT kept in patient file**, not mentioned in notes), **diet sheet**, **Kardex**, **24-hr report/ census** (admissions, discharges, deaths, transfers, births), **change-of-shift report (handover)** – bedside preferred, **SBAR**.
- **Anecdotal record** – teacher's record of student's behaviour; **Cumulative record** – student's overall record.
- **Retention of records** – inpatient records usually **3 years** (minimum, MCI regulation); medico-legal cases – longer/ permanent; birth & death – permanent.

:::mnemo SBAR – handover/ communication with doctor
**S**ituation (abhi kya ho raha hai) · **B**ackground (history) · **A**ssessment (aapka analysis) · **R**ecommendation (kya chahiye) – **ISBAR** me pehle **I**dentify yourself.
:::

:::exam PYQ style
- Problem-oriented record (SOAP) introduced by – **Lawrence Weed (1969)**.
- Incident report kahan rakhte hain – **hospital administration/ risk management – patient chart me nahi**.
- Charting me galti hone par – **single line strike, "error", sign** (erase nahi).
- Best method to teach a skill – **demonstration & return demonstration**.
- Patient "mujhe surgery se darr lag raha hai" → best response – "**Aap kis baat se darr rahe hain? Mujhe batayiye.**" (exploring).
- Intimate distance – **0–18 inches**.
:::
