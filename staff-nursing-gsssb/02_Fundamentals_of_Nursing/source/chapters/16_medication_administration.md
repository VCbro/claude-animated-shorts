# Medication Administration & Drug Calculations

## 16.1 Basics & Rights

- **Pharmacology** – study of drugs. **Pharmacokinetics** – what body does to drug: **ADME – Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism (liver), Excretion (kidney)**. **Pharmacodynamics** – what drug does to body.
- **Half-life (t½)** – time for drug concentration to fall by 50 %. **Steady state** – after ~4–5 half-lives. **Therapeutic index** – safety margin (narrow – digoxin, lithium, warfarin, phenytoin, theophylline, aminoglycosides – **monitor levels**).
- **Peak level** – highest concentration (draw ~30–60 min after IV dose); **Trough level** – lowest (**draw just before next dose**).
- Drug names: **chemical**, **generic (non-proprietary – official)**, **trade/ brand (proprietary)**. Prescribe & use **generic names**.
- **Side effect** (predictable, mild, unintended), **adverse effect** (harmful), **toxic effect**, **allergic reaction**, **idiosyncrasy** (unexpected individual response), **tolerance**, **dependence**, **cumulative effect**, **synergism** (combined effect > sum), **antagonism**, **potentiation**.
- **Teratogenic** (fetal malformation), **iatrogenic** (treatment caused).

:::key Rights of medication administration
**Classic 5 rights**: **Right Patient, Right Drug, Right Dose, Right Route, Right Time**.
**Extended (10 rights)**: + **Right Documentation**, **Right Reason/ indication**, **Right Response (evaluation)**, **Right to Refuse**, **Right Education/ information**, (+ Right assessment, Right expiry, Right form/ approach).
**3 checks**: (1) when **taking from shelf/ drawer**, (2) when **preparing/ pouring**, (3) **before administering (at bedside)** / returning container.
**2 patient identifiers** (name + DOB/ UHID) – ask patient to state name; check ID band; **never use room/ bed number**.
:::

- **Medication errors** – most common: wrong dose, wrong time, omission; **report immediately** – **first assess patient**, notify doctor, document, incident report.
- **Never give medicine prepared by someone else**; **document only after giving**; **don't leave medicines at bedside**; patient refuses → document & inform; **stay till patient swallows**.
- **Verbal/ telephone orders** – emergency only; **write down & read back**, doctor co-sign within 24 hrs.
- **Order types**: **Stat** (immediately, once), **PRN** (as needed), **Standing/ routine** (until discontinued), **Single/ one-time**, **Now** (within 90 min).

## 16.2 Routes of Administration

- **Enteral**: oral, sublingual, buccal, NG/ PEG, rectal. **Parenteral**: ID, SC, IM, IV, intra-arterial, intrathecal, intraosseous, epidural, intracardiac, intra-articular. **Topical/ local**: skin, eye, ear, nose, vaginal, inhalation, transdermal.
- **Fastest action**: **IV > inhalation > sublingual > IM > SC > oral > rectal > transdermal** (approx). **IV – 100 % bioavailability**.
- **First-pass metabolism** (liver) – oral drugs; avoided by **sublingual, IV, rectal (partial), transdermal**.

### Oral medications
- Position – **sitting upright** (aspiration); give with **full glass of water (60–100 ml)**; check **swallowing/ gag reflex**.
- **Don't crush**: **enteric-coated (EC), sustained/ extended-release (SR, ER, XL, CR, LA), sublingual, buccal, capsules with beads** – breaking destroys action/ causes irritation.
- **Liquid medicine** – shake; pour with **label towards palm**; read at **bottom of meniscus at eye level**; use oral syringe for accuracy.
- **Iron syrup** – **through straw** (teeth staining); **cough syrup – last, no water after** (soothing effect).
- **Sublingual (NTG)** – under tongue, don't swallow/ chew/ drink till dissolved; **Buccal** – between cheek & gum.
- **Antacids** – 1–3 hrs after meals & HS; separate from other drugs by 1–2 hrs.
- **Pulse/ BP check before**: **digoxin (apical 1 min – hold if < 60 adult)**, beta-blockers, antihypertensives; **RR before opioids**; **blood sugar before insulin/ OHA**; **K⁺ before diuretics/ digoxin**.

## 16.3 Injections – Parenteral

@fig injection_angles.svg | Injection routes – angle aur depth

| Route | Angle | Needle gauge & length | Max volume | Sites |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Intradermal (ID)** | **10–15°**, **bevel UP** | **26–27 G, ⅜–½ inch** (tuberculin syringe 1 ml) | **0.01–0.1 ml** | **Inner (ventral) forearm** (Mantoux, sensitivity tests), **upper arm (BCG – left deltoid insertion)**, upper back |
| **Subcutaneous (SC)** | **45°** (thin) – **90°** (normal/ obese; pinch 2.5 cm/ 1 inch skin) | **25–27 G, ½–⅝ inch** | **≤ 1 ml (max 1.5–2 ml)** | **Abdomen** (2 inch/ 5 cm away from umbilicus – **fastest SC absorption**), **outer upper arm**, anterior thigh, scapular area, upper buttock |
| **Intramuscular (IM)** | **90°** | **21–23 G (19–25), 1–1.5 inch** (child ⅝–1 inch) | **Deltoid ≤ 1 ml (0.5–1)**; **ventrogluteal/ vastus lateralis 2–3 ml (up to 5 ml adult)**; infant vastus ≤ 0.5–1 ml | Deltoid, **ventrogluteal**, vastus lateralis, dorsogluteal, rectus femoris |
| **Intravenous (IV)** | **15–30° (≈ 25°)**, bevel up | 18–22 G | Any (as ordered) | Peripheral veins |

- **Mantoux/ sensitivity test** – **don't massage**, mark circle; read **48–72 hrs**; **bleb/ wheal** formation confirms correct ID injection.
- **Heparin & insulin (SC)**: **don't aspirate, don't massage** (haematoma / ↑ absorption); heparin – abdomen; **enoxaparin** – don't expel air bubble (pre-filled), inject in **"love handles"**, don't rub.
- **Aspiration** before IM – **not recommended for vaccines** (WHO/ CDC) & SC; for other IM drugs – per policy (dorsogluteal – yes).

### IM injection sites

@fig im_injection_sites.svg | IM sites – deltoid, ventrogluteal, vastus lateralis, dorsogluteal

| Site | Landmarks | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Deltoid** | **2.5–5 cm (2–3 finger-widths) below acromion process**, inverted triangle (acromion base – axillary line apex) | Small volume (**≤ 1 ml**); vaccines (Hep B, Td, COVID, rabies adult); **radial & axillary nerve, brachial artery** risk |
| **Ventrogluteal** | **Palm on greater trochanter**, **index finger on ASIS**, **middle finger along iliac crest** → inject in **V/ triangle** between fingers (left hand for right hip) | **Safest site** for adults & children (> 7 months) – **no major nerves/ vessels**, thick muscle, less fat |
| **Vastus lateralis** | **Middle third of anterolateral thigh** (hand's breadth below greater trochanter & above knee) | **Site of choice for infants (< 1 yr/ 12 months)** – vaccines (Pentavalent, PCV, IPV) |
| **Dorsogluteal** | **Upper outer quadrant** of buttock (imaginary line from **PSIS to greater trochanter** – inject above & lateral) | **Sciatic nerve injury & superior gluteal artery** risk – **avoid / last option**; **never < 3 yrs** (muscle undeveloped) |
| **Rectus femoris** | Anterior middle third of thigh | Self-injection |

:::key Z-track technique
- Purpose – **prevent leakage (tracking) of irritating/ staining drugs into subcutaneous tissue** – **iron dextran (Imferon)**, hydroxyzine, some antipsychotics (haloperidol decanoate).
- **Change needle after drawing up** (drug on outside of needle stains); **air-lock 0.2–0.5 ml** (optional).
- **Pull skin & SC tissue laterally 2.5–3.5 cm (1–1.5 inch)** → inject at 90° slowly (**10 sec/ml**) → **wait 10 sec** → withdraw needle → **release skin** (track sealed) → **don't massage**; preferred site – **ventrogluteal**/ dorsogluteal; walk to ↑ absorption.
:::

### Needles & syringes
- **Gauge** – diameter (**bigger number = thinner needle**); **length** – choose by site & patient size.
- Parts – **syringe: tip, barrel, plunger, flange**; **needle: hub, shaft, bevel** (**bevel up** for ID/ SC/ IV).
- **Insulin syringe – U-100 (100 units/ml)**; **tuberculin syringe – 1 ml (0.01 ml markings)**.
- **Ampoule** – **glass**: flick to bring drug down, **break away from you with gauze**, use **filter needle** (glass particles), no air injection. **Vial** – rubber stopper; **swab with alcohol**, **inject equal volume of air** (positive pressure) before withdrawing; **multi-dose vial – label date/ time opened, discard after 28 days** (or as per manufacturer).
- **Reconstitution** – diluent (sterile water/ NS) as per label; roll (don't shake proteins).

### Insulin administration
- **Mixing two insulins – "Clear before Cloudy"** (Regular → NPH): inject air into **NPH (cloudy)** first → air into **Regular (clear)** → withdraw **Regular** → withdraw **NPH**. ("**RN**" – Regular, NPH). **Glargine/ detemir – never mix**.
- **Only Regular insulin (clear) can be given IV**.
- Roll cloudy insulin gently between palms (**don't shake**).
- **Abdomen – fastest absorption**; rotate sites **within same region** (lipohypertrophy prevention); **2 inch (5 cm) from umbilicus**; don't inject into exercising limb.
- Storage: unopened – **refrigerate 2–8 °C**; in-use vial/ pen – **room temp ≤ 28–30 days**; never freeze/ direct sunlight.

## 16.4 Other Routes

- **Eye drops**: patient **head tilted back, looks up**; **pull lower lid down** → drop into **lower conjunctival sac (centre/ outer third)** – **never on cornea**; dropper **1–2 cm** above (no touch); **close eye gently (not squeeze) & press inner canthus (nasolacrimal duct/ punctum) 1–2 min** (systemic absorption ↓); **multiple drops – wait 3–5 min**; **drops before ointment** (wait ~10 min); **ointment – thin ribbon from inner to outer canthus**.
- **Ear drops**: warm to **body temperature** (cold → **vertigo**, nausea); side-lying affected ear up; **adult – pinna UP & BACK**; **child < 3 yrs – DOWN & BACK**; drops on canal wall (not directly on TM); **remain lying 5–10 min**; cotton loosely (optional).
- **Nasal drops**: **head tilted back** (posterior pharynx) – **Proetz position** (head over bed edge – ethmoid/ sphenoid sinuses), **Parkinson position** (head turned to side – maxillary/ frontal sinuses); remain **5 min**; **decongestant drops max 3–5 days** (rebound congestion – rhinitis medicamentosa).
- **Inhalers (MDI)**: shake, exhale fully, inhale slowly with actuation, **hold breath 10 sec**, wait **1 min** between puffs; **bronchodilator before steroid**; **rinse mouth after steroid** (thrush); spacer. **DPI** – don't shake, fast deep inhalation.
- **Transdermal patch** (NTG, fentanyl, nicotine): **hairless, clean, dry** area; **rotate sites**; **remove old patch first**; gloves; don't cut; fentanyl – no heat pads (↑ absorption).
- **Rectal suppository** – Sims' (left lateral), beyond internal sphincter (adult 7.5–10 cm), against rectal wall.
- **Vaginal** – lithotomy/ dorsal recumbent, applicator, **at bedtime**, lie **10–15 min**.
- **Topical** – gloves, clean area, thin layer; **steroid creams – thin, don't cover** unless ordered.

## 16.5 Storage, Controlled Drugs & Safety

- **Narcotics/ controlled drugs (NDPS Act 1985 – India)**: **double-locked cupboard**, **separate register**, **count at every shift change by 2 nurses**, **wastage witnessed & signed by 2 nurses**, keys with in-charge; **empty ampoules returned** to pharmacy.
- **High-alert medications** (ISMP) – **concentrated KCl** (never IV push – dilute; stored separately with red label), insulin, heparin/ anticoagulants, opioids, chemotherapy, neuromuscular blockers, magnesium sulphate, hypertonic saline → **independent double-check**.
- **LASA (Look-Alike Sound-Alike)** drugs – Tall Man lettering (e.g. hydrOXYzine/ hydrALAZINE); separate storage.
- Storage: **cool, dry, dark** place (most **< 25 °C**); **refrigerator 2–8 °C** – **insulin, vaccines (cold chain), oxytocin, some antibiotics (reconstituted), suppositories, eye drops (some)**; light-sensitive – **nitroprusside, amber bottles**; check **expiry date** (FEFO – First Expiry First Out).
- **Cold chain** (vaccines) – **ILR (ice-lined refrigerator) +2 to +8 °C**; **deep freezer −15 to −25 °C**; **VVM (vaccine vial monitor)** – inner square lighter than outer circle = usable; **Shake test** – frozen DPT/ TT/ Hep B.
- **Paediatric doses** – Clark's rule & Young's rule (below).

## 16.6 Drug Calculations

:::key Formulas
- **Dose required (tablet/ liquid) = (Desired dose ÷ Dose on hand) × Quantity (vehicle)** → **D/H × Q**
- **Young's rule** (child **1–12 yrs**): **Child dose = [Age (yrs) ÷ (Age + 12)] × Adult dose**
- **Clark's rule** (by weight): **Child dose = [Weight (lb) ÷ 150] × Adult dose** (or **wt kg ÷ 70**)
- **Fried's rule** (infant < 1–2 yr): **[Age (months) ÷ 150] × Adult dose**
- **BSA (Body surface area) rule** – most accurate: **(BSA m² ÷ 1.73) × Adult dose**
- **Percentage solution**: **1 % = 1 g in 100 ml** (w/v); **1 : 1000 = 1 g in 1000 ml = 1 mg/ml**; 1 : 10,000 = 0.1 mg/ml.
- **Dilution: C₁V₁ = C₂V₂**.
- **Units**: 1 g = 1000 mg; 1 mg = 1000 mcg; 1 kg = 2.2 lb; 1 L = 1000 ml; **1 tsp = 5 ml**, **1 tbsp = 15 ml**, **1 oz = 30 ml**, 1 cup = 240 ml; **1 grain (gr) = 60–65 mg**; 1 inch = 2.54 cm.
:::

:::exam Solved calculations
1. Order **500 mg**, available **250 mg/tab** → 500/250 × 1 = **2 tablets**.
2. Order **75 mg**, available **50 mg in 2 ml** → 75/50 × 2 = **3 ml**.
3. Order **Inj. Gentamicin 60 mg**, vial **80 mg/2 ml** → 60/80 × 2 = **1.5 ml**.
4. **Paracetamol 15 mg/kg** for 20 kg child = **300 mg**; syrup 250 mg/5 ml → 300/250 × 5 = **6 ml**.
5. **Young's**: adult dose 500 mg, child 6 yrs → 6/(6+12) × 500 = **166.6 mg**.
6. **Clark's**: adult 300 mg, child 50 lb → 50/150 × 300 = **100 mg**.
7. Prepare **500 ml of 5 % solution** → 5 g/100 ml → **25 g in 500 ml**.
8. **Adrenaline 1 : 1000** – 0.5 ml = **0.5 mg**.
9. **Heparin 5000 units** ordered; vial **25,000 units/5 ml** → 5000/25000 × 5 = **1 ml**.
10. **Insulin 30 units** from U-100 → **0.3 ml**.
11. **Crystalline penicillin 10 lakh units** vial – add **5 ml** → 2 lakh units/ml; order 4 lakh → **2 ml**.
:::
