## 15.1 Body Fluids – Quick Facts

- Body water **~60 % adult** (male 60 %, female 50–55 %), **newborn 75–80 %**, elderly 45–50 %. **ICF ⅔ (40 %) – K⁺ main**; **ECF ⅓ (20 %) – Na⁺ main** (interstitial 15 % + plasma 5 %).
- **Daily intake ≈ output ≈ 2500 ml** (water 1500 + food 800 + metabolism 200 | urine 1500 + insensible skin 500 + lungs 300–400 + faeces 100–200).
- **Thirst centre – hypothalamus**. Hormones: **ADH** (water retain), **aldosterone** (Na retain, K excrete), **ANP** (Na & water excrete).
- Electrolytes normal: **Na 135–145**, **K 3.5–5.0**, **Ca 8.5–10.5 mg/dL**, **Cl 96–106**, **HCO₃ 22–26**, **Mg 1.5–2.5**.

| Fluid volume deficit (Dehydration) | Fluid volume excess (Overload) |
|---|---|
| Thirst, **dry mucosa**, **poor skin turgor**, sunken eyes, **sunken fontanelle (infant)** | **Oedema**, puffy eyelids, **weight gain** |
| **Tachycardia, weak thready pulse, hypotension**, orthostatic | **Bounding pulse, hypertension**, **JVD** |
| **↓ urine, concentrated (SG > 1.030)** | **Crackles**, dyspnoea, cough (pulmonary oedema) |
| ↑ Hct, ↑ BUN, weight loss | ↓ Hct (dilution) |
| Fever, confusion, flat neck veins | S3 sound |

- **Best indicator of fluid balance – daily weight** (**1 kg = 1 L**), same time, scale, clothes.
- **ORS (WHO low osmolarity – 245 mOsm/L)**: per litre – **NaCl 2.6 g, glucose (anhydrous) 13.5 g, KCl 1.5 g, trisodium citrate 2.9 g**. Home-made – **6 level tsp sugar + ½ tsp salt in 1 L clean water**. **29 July = ORS Day**.

## 15.2 IV Fluids

| Fluid | Tonicity | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **0.9 % NaCl (Normal saline)** | **Isotonic** (308 mOsm) | Shock, hypovolaemia, **blood transfusion (only compatible fluid)**, hyponatraemia, DKA (first) |
| **Ringer's Lactate (Hartmann's)** | Isotonic | **Burns (Parkland)**, surgery, trauma; contains K & Ca – not with blood; avoid in liver failure (lactate), lactic acidosis |
| **5 % Dextrose (D5W)** | Isotonic in bag, **physiologically hypotonic** | Free water, hypernatraemia; **avoid in ↑ICP/ head injury** |
| **0.45 % NaCl (½ NS)** | **Hypotonic** | Cellular dehydration, hypernatraemia, DKA (later) |
| **DNS (5 % dextrose in 0.9 % NaCl)** | **Hypertonic** | Maintenance, fluid + calories |
| **10 % Dextrose** | Hypertonic | **Hypoglycaemia**, TPN stop/ transition |
| **25 %/ 50 % Dextrose (D25/ D50)** | Hypertonic | **Severe hypoglycaemia** (bolus) |
| **3 % NaCl** | Hypertonic | **Severe hyponatraemia, cerebral oedema** – slowly, ICU |
| **Isolyte-P / Isolyte-M** | | Paediatric / maintenance |
| **Mannitol 20 %** | Hypertonic (osmotic diuretic) | **↑ICP**, cerebral oedema – **use filter** |
| **Colloids** – albumin 5 %/ 25 %, Haemaccel (gelatin), dextran, hetastarch | Plasma expanders | Hypovolaemia, hypoalbuminaemia |

## 15.3 IV Cannulation

| Gauge (G) | Colour | Flow (approx) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| **14 G** | **Orange** | ~270–300 ml/min | **Trauma, massive blood loss** – largest |
| **16 G** | **Grey** | ~200 ml/min | Major surgery, rapid fluids, trauma |
| **18 G** | **Green** | ~90–100 ml/min | **Blood transfusion**, surgery, CT contrast |
| **20 G** | **Pink** | ~60 ml/min | **Most adult IV therapy**, blood (acceptable) |
| **22 G** | **Blue** | ~35 ml/min | Children, elderly, small veins |
| **24 G** | **Yellow** | ~20 ml/min | **Neonates**, infants, fragile veins |
| **26 G** | Violet | ~13 ml/min | Neonates |

- **Smaller gauge number = larger diameter**.
- **Site selection**: **distal first (hand – metacarpal/ dorsal veins), then move proximal** (forearm – **cephalic, basilic, median antebrachial**); **non-dominant arm**; **avoid**: joints/ flexion areas (antecubital – except emergency/ short-term), **arm with AV fistula, mastectomy side, injured/ infected/ oedematous/ burned limb, phlebitis, legs in adults (DVT)**; infants – **scalp veins**, hand, foot.
- Tourniquet **10–15 cm (4–6 inch) above** site, **< 1–2 min**; skin antisepsis **chlorhexidine 2 % in 70 % alcohol** (or povidone-iodine) – **let dry 30 sec**; insert **bevel up at 10–30° (15–30°)**; look for **flashback**; advance cannula, withdraw stylet; **remove tourniquet before connecting**; secure with transparent dressing; label (date, time, gauge, initials).
- **Change peripheral cannula every 72–96 hrs** (or when clinically indicated, policy); **IV administration set** – continuous **every 72–96 hrs**; **blood set – after each unit / every 4 hrs**; **TPN/ lipid set – 24 hrs (lipids 12 hrs)**; IV fluid bag – **hang max 24 hrs**.
- **Phlebitis scale (VIP – Visual Infusion Phlebitis, Jackson)** – 0–5; score ≥ 2 → remove cannula.

## 15.4 IV Flow Rate Calculation

:::key Formulas (exam favourite)
- **Drops/min (gtt/min) = Total volume (ml) × Drop factor (gtt/ml) ÷ Time (minutes)**
- **ml/hr = Total volume (ml) ÷ Time (hours)**
- **Drop factor**: **Macro-drip (adult) set = 10, 15 or 20 gtt/ml** (Indian sets commonly **15 or 20**); **Micro-drip (paediatric/ burette) = 60 gtt/ml** → **micro-drops/min = ml/hr**.
- **Blood set = 15 gtt/ml** (10–15).
- **Infusion time (hrs) = Volume ÷ ml/hr**.
:::

:::exam Solved calculations
1. 1000 ml NS in 8 hrs, drop factor 15 → (1000 × 15) ÷ 480 = **31.25 ≈ 31 drops/min**.
2. 500 ml in 4 hrs, drop factor 20 → (500 × 20) ÷ 240 = **41.6 ≈ 42 drops/min**.
3. 1500 ml in 12 hrs → ml/hr = 1500 ÷ 12 = **125 ml/hr**.
4. Micro-drip 60 gtt/ml, 50 ml/hr → **50 microdrops/min**.
5. 1000 ml at 20 drops/min, DF 20 → 1 ml/min → **1000 min ≈ 16 hrs 40 min**.
6. 2 pints (1 pint ≈ 500 ml) blood – 1000 ml.
:::

## 15.5 IV Complications

| Complication | Signs | Nursing action |
|---|---|---|
| **Infiltration** (non-vesicant fluid into tissue) | **Swelling, coolness, pallor, taut skin**, ↓/ no flow, discomfort | **Stop, remove cannula, elevate limb**, warm/ cold compress (as per fluid), restart other limb |
| **Extravasation** (vesicant – chemo, dopamine, noradrenaline, calcium, KCl conc, dextrose 50 %) | Pain, burning, blistering → necrosis | **Stop, don't flush, aspirate**, antidote (**phentolamine** for vasopressors; hyaluronidase), mark area, notify |
| **Phlebitis** (vein inflammation) | **Redness, warmth, pain, red streak/ cord along vein** | Remove cannula, **warm moist compress**, elevate, re-site |
| **Thrombophlebitis** | Phlebitis + clot (hard cord) | Same; no massage |
| **Local infection/ cellulitis** | Pus, redness | Remove, culture tip, antibiotics |
| **Septicaemia** | Fever, chills, hypotension | Remove, blood culture, antibiotics |
| **Fluid overload** (circulatory) | **Dyspnoea, crackles, JVD, ↑BP, bounding pulse, cough (frothy)** | **Slow IV to KVO, high Fowler's, O₂**, inform, diuretic |
| **Air embolism** | **Sudden dyspnoea, chest pain, hypotension, cyanosis, churning murmur**, loss of consciousness | **Clamp line, turn patient to LEFT side in Trendelenburg (head down)** – air trapped in right atrium, O₂, inform |
| **Speed shock** (drug given too fast) | Flushing, headache, chest tightness, syncope, shock | Stop, inform; prevent – infusion pump |
| **Haematoma** | Bruising | Pressure, remove |
| **Catheter embolism** | Piece of catheter breaks | Tourniquet above site, X-ray |
| **Nerve injury** | Tingling | Remove |

- **KVO (Keep Vein Open)/ TKO** – ~10–20 ml/hr (or 5–20 drops/min micro).
- Prevention of air embolism – **prime tubing fully**, all connections secure, never let bag run dry, **Luer-locks**.

## 15.6 Blood Transfusion – Nursing Basics

- **Pre-transfusion**: **written order & informed consent**; **blood group & cross-match**; check **2 nurses at bedside – patient ID, blood group, Rh, unit number, expiry, compatibility label**; **baseline vitals**; **18–20 G cannula**; **blood set with filter (170–200 µm)**; **prime with 0.9 % NS only**.
- Collect blood from bank **just before use**; start **within 30 min**; **complete within 4 hrs** (whole blood/ PRBC); **don't warm in hot water/ microwave** (blood warmer only); **no drugs in blood line**.
- **Rate**: **first 15 min slow (~2 ml/min / 15–20 drops/min) & nurse stays with patient** (most reactions occur in **first 15 min/ first 50 ml**); then increase.
- **Vitals: before, 15 min after start, every 30 min–1 hr, at end** (and 1 hr after).
- **Storage**: whole blood/ PRBC **2–6 °C** (CPDA-1: **35 days**; SAGM: 42 days); **platelets 20–24 °C with agitation – 5 days**; **FFP −30 °C / ≤ −18 °C – 1 year**.
- **Reaction → STOP transfusion immediately → keep vein open with NS (new tubing) → inform doctor & blood bank → vitals → send blood bag + set + patient's blood & urine sample → document**.
- Reactions: **febrile non-haemolytic (most common)**, **acute haemolytic (most dangerous – ABO mismatch – clerical error; fever, chills, flank/ back pain, haemoglobinuria, hypotension)**, allergic (urticaria – antihistamine), anaphylaxis, **TACO (circulatory overload)**, TRALI, bacterial, hypocalcaemia (citrate – massive), hyperkalaemia, hypothermia.
- **Universal donor O –ve**, **universal recipient AB +ve**. **1 unit PRBC ↑ Hb ~1 g/dL**.

:::exam PYQ style
- Fluid compatible with blood – **0.9 % NaCl**.
- Largest IV cannula – **14 G (orange)**; blood transfusion – **18 G (green)**; neonates – **24 G (yellow)**.
- Drops/min = **volume × drop factor ÷ time (min)**.
- Micro-drip drop factor – **60**.
- Infiltration – **cool, pale, swollen** site; phlebitis – **red, warm, tender** along vein.
- Air embolism position – **left lateral + Trendelenburg**.
- Fluid overload – **high Fowler's, slow IV**.
- Blood transfusion complete within – **4 hrs**; first **15 min** stay with patient.
- Transfusion reaction – **first stop transfusion**.
- Tourniquet placed – **10–15 cm above** site.
- IV site selection – **distal to proximal**.
:::
