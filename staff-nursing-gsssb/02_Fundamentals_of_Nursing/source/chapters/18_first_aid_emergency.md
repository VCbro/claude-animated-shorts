## 18.1 Principles of First Aid

:::def First aid
**Immediate & temporary care** given to an injured or suddenly ill person **before arrival of medical help**, using available resources. Aims – **3 P's: Preserve life, Prevent deterioration (worsening), Promote recovery**.
:::

- **Golden rules**: **ensure scene safety first (self-safety)** → **DR ABC** – **D**anger, **R**esponse, **A**irway, **B**reathing, **C**irculation; call for help (**108 ambulance, 112 emergency**); **don't move casualty** unless danger (spinal injury suspicion); treat **life-threatening conditions first** (airway → breathing → bleeding → shock); reassure; **don't give anything by mouth** to unconscious/ abdominal injury/ surgery likely; keep warm; record & hand over.
- **Priority in casualty**: **(1) Airway obstruction/ not breathing**, **(2) severe bleeding**, **(3) shock**, (4) unconsciousness, (5) fractures, burns.
- **Recovery position (lateral/ semi-prone)** – **unconscious but breathing** casualty (no spinal injury) – keeps **airway open, drains vomit/ secretions**.
- **St. John Ambulance** (first aid training organization), **Indian Red Cross Society (1920)**. **World First Aid Day – 2nd Saturday of September**.

## 18.2 CPR / Basic Life Support (AHA)

:::key Adult BLS – key numbers (AHA 2020/ 2025)
- **Check responsiveness** (tap & shout) & **breathing (look at chest) + carotid pulse simultaneously ≤ 10 sec**.
- **Call for help / 108, get AED**.
- Sequence **C-A-B** (Compressions → Airway → Breathing).
- **Compression**: **lower half of sternum (centre of chest, between nipples)**, **heel of one hand + other hand on top**, **arms straight (locked elbows)**, shoulders over hands.
- **Rate 100–120 /min**; **depth 5–6 cm (2–2.4 inch)**; **full chest recoil**; minimize interruptions (**< 10 sec**).
- **Ratio 30 : 2** (adult – 1 or 2 rescuers); **child/ infant 2 rescuers – 15 : 2**; 1 rescuer – 30 : 2.
- **Airway**: **head tilt–chin lift**; **jaw thrust** if spinal injury.
- **Rescue breath** – 1 sec each, visible chest rise (avoid excessive ventilation). With advanced airway – **1 breath every 6 sec (10/min)**.
- **AED** – as soon as available; **pads: right upper chest (below clavicle) & left side (below armpit)**; "**stand clear**" during analysis & shock; resume CPR immediately after shock.
- Rotate compressor **every 2 min (5 cycles)**.
- **Respiratory arrest only (pulse present)** – **1 breath every 6 sec (10/min)** adult; child/ infant – **every 2–3 sec (20–30/min)**.
:::

| | Adult | Child (1 yr – puberty) | Infant (< 1 yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pulse check | **Carotid** | Carotid/ femoral | **Brachial** |
| Compression | 2 hands | 1 or 2 hands | **2 fingers / 2-thumb encircling** (just below nipple line) |
| Depth | **5–6 cm** | **~5 cm (⅓ AP diameter)** | **~4 cm (1.5 inch; ⅓ AP)** |
| Ratio (2 rescuers) | 30 : 2 | **15 : 2** | **15 : 2** |
| Lone rescuer – no phone | Call first then CPR | **CPR 2 min first then call** | **CPR 2 min first then call** |

- **Chain of survival (OHCA)**: activate EMS → **high-quality CPR** → **defibrillation** → advanced resuscitation → post-arrest care → recovery.
- **Hands-only CPR** for untrained lay rescuers.
- Stop CPR when: **ROSC**, rescuer exhausted, scene unsafe, **qualified help takes over**, DNR/ death declared.

## 18.3 Choking (Foreign Body Airway Obstruction)

- **Universal choking sign** – **hands clutching throat**. **Mild** (can cough, speak) – **encourage coughing**, don't interfere. **Severe** (can't speak/ breathe/ cough, cyanosis, silent cough) – act.
- **Conscious adult/ child (> 1 yr)**: **Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre)** – stand behind, **fist (thumb side) above navel, below xiphoid**, quick **inward & upward** thrusts until object expelled or unconscious. (Some guidelines: 5 back blows + 5 abdominal thrusts.)
- **Pregnant/ obese**: **chest thrusts** (lower sternum).
- **Infant (< 1 yr)**: **5 back blows** (head lower, face down on forearm, between shoulder blades) **+ 5 chest thrusts** (2 fingers, lower sternum) – **NO abdominal thrusts** (liver injury); **no blind finger sweep**.
- **Unconscious** → lower to ground, **start CPR** (compressions); **look in mouth before breaths** – remove only if visible.
- **Self-Heimlich** – own fist or over back of chair.

## 18.4 Bleeding (Haemorrhage)

- Types: **Arterial** (**bright red, spurting/ pulsatile**, most dangerous), **Venous** (**dark red, steady flow**), **Capillary** (oozing, slow). **External/ internal**; **primary, reactionary (within 24 hrs), secondary (7–14 days)**.
- **Control of external bleeding**:
 1. **Direct pressure** with clean pad/ gloved hand – **most effective first step** (10–15 min continuous; **don't remove soaked pad – add more on top**)
 2. **Elevation** of injured part above heart (if no fracture)
 3. **Pressure points** (indirect – brachial, femoral) – adjunct
 4. **Tourniquet** – **life-threatening limb bleeding not controlled by direct pressure** – **5–7.5 cm (2–3 inch) above wound** (not on joint), **note time of application**, don't cover it, don't loosen (by first aider)
 5. **Haemostatic dressing**, pressure bandage
- **Embedded object – don't remove**; pad around & bandage (pressure around).
- **Epistaxis (nose bleed)**: **sit up & lean FORWARD** (not back), **pinch soft part of nose 10–15 min**, breathe through mouth, cold compress on bridge; don't blow nose.
- **Internal bleeding** signs – shock, pain, swelling, **haematemesis (coffee-ground), melena, haemoptysis (frothy bright red)**; treat for shock, urgent hospital.

## 18.5 Shock

- **Inadequate tissue perfusion**. Types: **hypovolaemic (haemorrhage, burns, dehydration – most common)**, cardiogenic, **distributive (septic, anaphylactic, neurogenic)**, obstructive.
- Signs: **restlessness/ anxiety (earliest)**, **pale, cold, clammy skin**, **rapid weak thready pulse**, rapid shallow breathing, thirst, **↓ BP (late)**, ↓ urine, confusion, unconsciousness.
- First aid: **lay flat, raise legs 20–30 cm (~12 inch) – shock position** (unless head/ chest/ spinal injury/ fracture of leg), **keep warm (blanket – not overheat)**, **loosen tight clothing**, control bleeding, **nothing by mouth** (moisten lips), reassure, monitor, **108**.
- **Anaphylaxis** – **adrenaline 1:1000 IM (0.5 mg adult, 0.3 mg child) in anterolateral thigh** – EpiPen; position – breathing difficulty → sitting; faint → lying with legs raised.
- **Fainting (syncope)** – temporary loss of consciousness (↓ brain blood flow): **lay flat, raise legs**, loosen clothes, fresh air; **don't sit up quickly**; if feeling faint – sit & put head between knees.

## 18.6 Fractures, Dislocations & Spinal Injury

- **Signs of fracture**: pain, **tenderness**, swelling, **deformity**, **loss of function**, **abnormal mobility**, **crepitus**, shortening, bruising.
- **First aid**: **immobilize "as it lies"** – **splint including joint above & below**; pad splint; check **CSM (circulation, sensation, movement) distal before & after**; **open fracture – cover with sterile dressing, don't push bone back**, control bleeding around (no direct pressure on bone end); elevate (after splinting); ice; treat shock; no food (surgery).
- **Splints** – rigid (wooden, SAM, inflatable/ air splint), soft (pillow, blanket), **anatomical splint (body part – injured leg tied to good leg)**, traction splint (femur – Thomas splint).
- **Specific**: **clavicle** – arm sling/ figure-of-8; **forearm/ wrist** – splint + arm sling; **humerus** – collar & cuff/ splint + sling; **ribs** – support arm on injured side, **no tight strapping**; **pelvis** – lie flat, legs together, padding; **femur** – traction/ long splint (heavy blood loss – shock); **skull** – head injury care.
- **Spinal injury** suspicion (fall from height, diving, RTA, neck pain, tingling/ weakness): **don't move**, **manual in-line stabilization of head**, **cervical collar**, **log roll** with 4 persons, **spine board**, **jaw thrust** for airway.
- **Dislocation** – **don't try to reduce**; immobilize in position found; ice.
- **Sprain/ strain** – **RICE** (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation) – first 24–48 hrs.

## 18.7 Burns, Scalds & Electric Shock

- **First aid for burns**: **Stop the burning** (**Stop, Drop & Roll**; smother with blanket), remove from source; **cool under cool running water for 20 min** (within 3 hrs) – **no ice, no butter/ oil/ toothpaste/ ghee/ ink**; **remove jewellery, watches, tight clothing early** (before swelling) – **don't pull off stuck clothes**; **don't break blisters**; **cover with clean non-fluffy material / cling film**; keep warm (hypothermia); fluids if conscious & minor; refer if large/ face/ hands/ genitals/ inhalation.
- **Chemical burns** – brush off dry chemical, **flush with copious water 20–30 min**; eyes – **irrigate 15–30 min (inner → outer canthus)**; remove contaminated clothing.
- **Electric shock** – **switch off power / break contact with dry non-conducting object (wood, rubber)** – **don't touch victim directly**; check ABC, **CPR**; look for **entry & exit burns**; **ECG monitoring** (arrhythmias); all electric injuries → hospital.
- **Lightning** – victim can be touched safely; "**reverse triage**" – treat apparently dead first.
- **Rule of nines & Parkland** – see MSN notes.

## 18.8 Poisoning, Bites & Stings

- **General**: ABC, **identify poison** (container, history) – save container/ vomitus, call **Poison Control/ 108**, **don't induce vomiting** (especially **corrosives – acids/ alkalis, kerosene/ petroleum – aspiration**), unconscious → recovery position; **activated charcoal** (hospital, within 1 hr – not for corrosives, hydrocarbons, iron, alcohol); skin contamination – remove clothes, wash; inhaled (CO/ gas) – **fresh air**, O₂.
- **Corrosive ingestion** – conscious → **sips of water/ milk** to dilute (some guidelines); no emesis.
- **Snake bite** – "**Do it R.I.G.H.T.**": **R**eassure, **I**mmobilize limb (splint like fracture, below heart level), **G**et to **H**ospital, **T**ell doctor symptoms; **DON'T: tourniquet, cut, suck, ice, electric shock, herbal remedy, alcohol**; remove rings/ tight items; **ASV (anti-snake venom) – polyvalent**.
- **Dog bite** – **wash wound with soap & running water 15 min** (most important), antiseptic (povidone-iodine), **don't suture immediately**, **anti-rabies vaccine ± RIG**, tetanus.
- **Bee/ wasp sting** – **scrape sting out** (credit card) – **don't squeeze with tweezers** (injects venom); ice, antihistamine; anaphylaxis watch.
- **Scorpion sting** – ice, immobilize, hospital (prazosin).
- **Insect in ear** – **tilt head, pour warm oil/ water** (floats out) – **not for TM perforation**; don't probe.
- **Foreign body in eye** – don't rub, **flush with water**, lift upper lid over lower; **embedded/ penetrating object – don't remove, cover both eyes** (paper cup over object).

## 18.9 Other Emergencies

- **Drowning** – remove safely (don't become victim), **5 initial rescue breaths** then CPR (**hypoxia main problem** – breaths important), don't try to drain water from lungs, keep warm, hospital (secondary drowning).
- **Heat stroke** – **core temp > 40 °C**, **hot dry skin**, confusion/ unconscious → **EMERGENCY: rapid cooling** (shade, remove clothes, **cold water immersion/ wet sheets + fan**, ice packs to **neck, axillae, groin**), **no antipyretics**. **Heat exhaustion** – sweating, weakness, cool clammy skin → rest in cool place, **ORS/ salt water**. **Heat cramps** – salt-water/ ORS, stretching.
- **Hypothermia** – remove wet clothes, **gradual rewarming** (blankets, warm drinks if conscious – **no alcohol**), handle gently. **Frostbite** – **rewarm in warm water 37–40 °C**, **don't rub/ massage**, don't rewarm if risk of refreezing.
- **Seizure** – **protect from injury**, cushion head, loosen clothing, **don't restrain**, **don't put anything in mouth**, time the seizure, **recovery position after**; > 5 min → emergency.
- **Hypoglycaemia** – conscious → **sugar/ glucose/ juice (15 g)**; unconscious → nothing by mouth, 108 (glucagon/ IV dextrose).
- **Asthma attack** – sit upright, reliever inhaler (salbutamol), calm.
- **Heart attack** – rest (W position/ half-sitting), loosen clothes, **aspirin 300–325 mg chewed** (if not allergic), call 108, CPR if arrest.
- **Stroke** – **BE FAST**, note time, 108, nothing by mouth.
- **Head injury** – observe consciousness (GCS/ AVPU), **assume neck injury**, recovery position if unconscious (with spinal care), bleeding from ear – **don't plug** (cover loosely – CSF), lean towards injured side.

## 18.10 Triage (Mass Casualty)

| Colour | Priority | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Red** | **Immediate (I)** – life-threatening but salvageable | Airway obstruction, severe bleeding, shock, tension pneumothorax |
| **Yellow** | **Delayed/ Urgent (II)** – serious but can wait | Fractures (stable), moderate burns |
| **Green** | **Minor (III) – "walking wounded"** | Sprains, minor cuts |
| **Black** | **Dead / expectant (IV)** | No breathing after airway opened, unsurvivable |

- **START triage** – **R**espirations (> 30 → red), **P**erfusion (cap refill > 2 sec/ no radial pulse → red), **M**ental status (can't follow commands → red); walking → green. "**Do the greatest good for the greatest number**".
