## 7.1 Infection – Basics

- **Infection** – pathogen ka body me entry + multiplication → host response. **Colonization** – present but no disease. **Contamination** – object/ surface par microbes.
- **Stages of infection**: **Incubation** (entry → first symptoms) → **Prodromal** (non-specific symptoms – most infectious) → **Illness (acute)** → **Convalescence** (recovery).
- **Local** (redness, swelling, pain, heat, loss of function) vs **Systemic** (fever, malaise, ↑ WBC, ↑ ESR).
- Body defences: **1st line** – skin, mucosa, cilia, tears (lysozyme), stomach acid, normal flora; **2nd line** – inflammation, phagocytosis, fever, complement; **3rd line** – immune response (antibodies, T cells).
- **Normal WBC 4,000–11,000**; **neutrophils ↑ – bacterial**; **lymphocytes ↑ – viral/ TB**; **eosinophils ↑ – allergy/ parasites**.

- **Modes of transmission**: **Contact** (direct – touch; indirect – **fomites** = contaminated objects), **Droplet** (> 5 µm, travels **≤ 1 m/ 3 ft** – cough, sneeze), **Airborne** (< 5 µm droplet nuclei, long distance), **Vehicle** (water, food, blood), **Vector** (mosquito, fly – mechanical/ biological).

## 7.2 Health Care-Associated Infection (HAI / Nosocomial)

- Infection acquired in hospital – **not present/ incubating at admission**, appears **≥ 48 hrs after admission** (or within 3 days of discharge / 30 days of surgery).
- **Most common HAI – UTI (CAUTI)** (~30–40 %) → then **surgical site infection (SSI)**, **pneumonia (VAP)**, **bloodstream (CLABSI)**.
- Common organisms: **E. coli, Staph aureus (MRSA), Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, Enterococcus (VRE), Candida, C. difficile, Acinetobacter**.
- **Iatrogenic infection** – due to treatment/ procedure. **Exogenous** – from outside (other patient, staff); **Endogenous** – patient's own flora. **Cross infection** – patient to patient.
- **Single most important measure to prevent HAI = HAND HYGIENE** (Ignaz **Semmelweis** – 1847, Vienna – handwashing reduced puerperal fever – "**Father of hand hygiene/ infection control**"). **Joseph Lister** – antiseptic surgery (**carbolic acid/ phenol**, 1867) – "Father of antiseptic surgery".
- **Hospital Infection Control Committee (HICC)** – **Infection control nurse (ICN)** – surveillance, training, audit.

## 7.3 Hand Hygiene

:::key Hand hygiene – WHO numbers
- **5 moments** (WHO 2009): (1) Before touching patient, (2) Before clean/ aseptic procedure, (3) After body fluid exposure risk, (4) After touching patient, (5) After touching patient surroundings.
- **Soap & water (handwash)**: **40–60 sec**; when hands **visibly soiled**, after **C. difficile/ spore-formers**, norovirus, before eating, after toilet.
- **Alcohol-based hand rub (ABHR, 60–80 % alcohol)**: **20–30 sec**; **preferred for routine** when not visibly soiled; **3–5 ml** (palmful).
- **Surgical hand scrub/ antisepsis**: **2–6 min** (traditional 5–10 min) with chlorhexidine/ povidone-iodine; **fingertips → elbows**; hands **held above elbow** (water flows from clean to less clean – hands to elbow); dry with **sterile towel**.
- **Medical handwash**: hands **lower than elbows** (water flows to fingertips/ sink).
- **WHO 6 steps** (7 with wrists): palm to palm → back of hands (right over left & vice versa) → palm to palm fingers interlaced → backs of fingers (interlocked) → **rotational rubbing of thumbs** → rotational rubbing of fingertips in palm (+ wrists).
- Nails short (< 0.5 cm/ ¼ inch), **no artificial nails, no rings/ watches**, cover cuts. Dry with **paper towel**; **turn off tap with towel** (elbow/ foot taps best).
- **5 May = World Hand Hygiene Day**; **15 October = Global Handwashing Day**.
:::

## 7.4 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

- **Gloves**: **clean (examination)** – contact with body fluids; **sterile** – sterile procedures. Remove gloves – **glove-to-glove, skin-to-skin** technique; hand hygiene after removal. Gloves **don't replace hand hygiene**.
- **Gown** – fluid-resistant, back-tied; **Mask** – surgical (droplet, 3-ply); **N95 respirator** (filters ≥ **95 %** of particles ≥ **0.3 µm**; **fit-test**/ seal check; airborne – TB, measles, chickenpox, COVID aerosol); **goggles/ face shield**; cap, shoe cover.
- **Donning order (CDC)**: **Gown → Mask/ respirator → Goggles/ face shield → Gloves** ("**G-M-G-G**"; gloves last, over gown cuffs).
- **Doffing order (CDC)**: **Gloves → Goggles/ face shield → Gown → Mask/ respirator** (mask last – after leaving room) → hand hygiene. (Alternate: gown & gloves together.)
- Mask change – when **moist** (surgical mask effective ~4–6 hrs/ till damp); don't touch front.

## 7.5 Isolation Precautions (CDC 2007)

| Precaution | Diseases | Key measures |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard precautions** (Universal 1985 + Body substance isolation) | **ALL patients – all blood, body fluids, secretions, excretions (except sweat), non-intact skin, mucosa** | Hand hygiene, PPE as needed, safe injection, respiratory hygiene/ cough etiquette, sharps safety |
| **Contact** | **MRSA, VRE, C. difficile, scabies, lice, impetigo, herpes simplex/ zoster (localized), wound infection, RSV**, diarrhoea (rotavirus) | Gloves + gown on entering, **dedicated equipment** (stethoscope, BP cuff), private/ cohort room |
| **Droplet** | **Influenza, pertussis, diphtheria (pharyngeal), mumps, rubella, meningococcal meningitis, Hib, streptococcal pharyngitis, adenovirus, mycoplasma pneumonia** | **Surgical mask** within 3–6 ft, private room/ cohort (beds > 3 ft apart), patient masked during transport |
| **Airborne** | **TB (pulmonary/ laryngeal), measles (rubeola), chickenpox (varicella), disseminated herpes zoster**, SARS, COVID (aerosol procedures) | **N95**, **AIIR – negative pressure room, 6–12 air changes/hr**, door closed, patient surgical mask during transport |
| **Protective (reverse) isolation** | **Neutropenic, burns, transplant, chemotherapy** patients | Protect **patient from outside**; positive pressure, HEPA, no fresh flowers/ plants, low-microbial diet |

:::mnemo Airborne – "My Chicken Hez TB"
**M**easles, **Chicken**pox, disseminated **He**rpes **z**oster, **TB** → Airborne + N95.
Droplet – "**SPIDERMAN**": **S**epsis/ **S**carlet fever/ **S**trep, **P**neumonia/ **P**ertussis, **I**nfluenza, **D**iphtheria, **E**pidemic parotitis (mumps), **R**ubella, **M**eningitis (meningococcal), **A**denovirus, **N** – Neisseria.

- **Cough etiquette** – cover mouth/ nose with tissue or **elbow (sleeve)**, dispose tissue, hand hygiene, mask, distance > 3 ft.

## 7.6 Asepsis

:::def Medical vs Surgical asepsis
**Medical asepsis (clean technique)** – microorganisms ki **sankhya kam karna & spread rokna** – handwashing, clean gloves, cleaning, disinfecting, isolation; e.g. **oral medication, bed bath, enema, NG feeding**.
**Surgical asepsis (sterile technique)** – **all microorganisms including spores ko destroy/ exclude** karna – **sterile field**; e.g. **catheterization, injections, IV cannulation, surgical dressing, OT, sterile specimen**.
:::

### Principles of sterile technique (sterile field rules)
- **Sterile touches sterile only** – sterile + unsterile = **unsterile**.
- Sterile objects **below waist level** or out of sight = **unsterile**.
- **1 inch (2.5 cm) border** of sterile field = **unsterile**.
- Sterile field **prolonged exposure to air** → contaminated; don't talk/ cough/ sneeze/ reach over sterile field.
- **Moisture → strike-through contamination** (wet sterile drape = contaminated).
- **Doubt = consider contaminated**.
- **Never turn back** to sterile field; face it always.
- **Sterile forceps (Cheattle forceps)** – tip always **down**; container with antiseptic **changed every 24 hrs** (replaced by individual sterile forceps now).
- **Pouring sterile solution**: label towards palm, **discard first few ml (lip cleaning)**, pour from **4–6 inches (10–15 cm)** height without splashing, don't touch bottle to container.
- Open sterile package: **first flap away from you**, then sides, **last flap towards you**.
- Sterile pack checks: **sterilization date/ expiry, indicator tape colour change**, intact, dry. **Event-related sterility** – sterile till package damaged/ wet (not fixed time) – old rule autoclaved packs ~**7 days (cloth wrap)**.
- **Sterile gloves**: **open gloving** (ward procedures) – **first glove picked by cuff (inside – skin to skin)**, second glove picked by **gloved fingers under the cuff (glove-to-glove)**. **Closed gloving** – OT with gown (hands inside sleeves).
- Hands **above waist & in front**.

## 7.7 Sterilization & Disinfection

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sterilization** | **All microorganisms + spores** destroyed |
| **Disinfection** | Pathogens destroyed **except spores** (on inanimate objects) |
| **Antisepsis** | Disinfection **on living tissue (skin)** – antiseptic |
| **Sanitization** | Microbe count reduced to safe level (public health) – cleaning |
| **Decontamination** | Removal of pathogens to make items safe to handle (first step – e.g. 0.5 % hypochlorite soak 10 min) |
| **Bacteriostatic** | Growth inhibit (not kill) |
| **Bactericidal** | Kill bacteria |
| **Fumigation** | Room disinfection by gas (formaldehyde) |

### Spaulding classification (1957)
| Category | Contact | Example | Processing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | **Sterile tissue/ blood** | Surgical instruments, **catheters, needles, implants** | **Sterilization** |
| **Semi-critical** | **Mucous membrane/ non-intact skin** | **Endoscopes**, laryngoscope blade, **thermometer (rectal/ oral)**, respiratory equipment | **High-level disinfection (HLD)** |
| **Non-critical** | **Intact skin** | **BP cuff, stethoscope, bedpan, bed rails, crutches** | **Low/ intermediate-level disinfection** |

### Methods of sterilization
| Method | Details | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Autoclave (moist heat under pressure – steam)** – **most common, most reliable/ efficient** | **121 °C, 15 psi (lb/inch²), 15–20 min** (wrapped 30 min); **134 °C, 30 psi, 3–4 min** (flash/ prevac) | **Surgical instruments, linen, dressings, glassware, culture media** – not for heat-sensitive/ oils/ powders |
| **Hot air oven (dry heat)** | **160 °C × 2 hrs** (or **170 °C × 1 hr**, **180 °C × 30 min**) | **Glassware, powders, oils, petroleum jelly, sharp instruments** (no rust/ blunt) |
| **Boiling** | **100 °C, 10–30 min** (min 10–20) – **NOT sterilization (spores survive)** – disinfection | Home use |
| **Incineration** | Burning to ashes (> 800–1000 °C) | **Anatomical waste**, dressings, infected material |
| **Flaming** | Direct flame | Inoculating loops, needle tips (lab) |
| **Ethylene oxide (ETO) gas** | Low temp (~37–55 °C), **aeration 8–12 hrs** (toxic, carcinogenic) | **Heat-sensitive – plastics, rubber, catheters, endoscopes, cardiac pacemakers** |
| **Formaldehyde gas** | Fumigation | Rooms, OT (now less) |
| **Plasma sterilization (H₂O₂ gas plasma)** | Low temp, short cycle | Heat & moisture sensitive |
| **Radiation – Gamma (ionizing)** | **Cold sterilization** | **Disposable syringes, needles, catheters, gloves, sutures** (industrial) |
| **UV radiation (non-ionizing)** | Surface disinfection only | OT/ lab air & surfaces |
| **Filtration** | Membrane filters (0.22 µm) | **Heat-sensitive liquids/ vaccines, sera**; HEPA (air) |
| **Glutaraldehyde 2 % (Cidex)** | **Sterilant: 10 hrs**; **HLD: 20 min** | **Endoscopes**, bronchoscopes |

### Sterilization indicators
- **Physical** – temperature, pressure, time gauges.
- **Chemical** – **autoclave indicator tape** (white lines → **black/ brown stripes**), **Bowie-Dick test** (daily air removal test, prevac autoclave).
- **Biological (most reliable)** – spores: **Geobacillus (Bacillus) stearothermophilus** – **autoclave**; **Bacillus atrophaeus (subtilis)** – **dry heat & ETO**.

### Common disinfectants & antiseptics
| Agent | Concentration / Use |
|---|---|
| **Sodium hypochlorite (bleach)** | **1 % – blood/ body fluid spills** (10 min contact); **0.5 %** – surface, decontamination of instruments/ gloves; 0.1 % – general surfaces |
| **Alcohol (ethyl/ isopropyl)** | **70 %** (most effective – water helps penetration) – skin antisepsis, thermometer, stethoscope; not sporicidal |
| **Povidone-iodine (Betadine)** | **5–10 %** – skin prep, wound; allergy (iodine) |
| **Chlorhexidine gluconate** | **2–4 %** skin prep, **0.12–0.2 % mouthwash** (VAP prevention), hand scrub – **longest residual action** |
| **Hydrogen peroxide** | **3 %** – wound cleaning (effervescence), 6 % disinfectant |
| **Phenol (carbolic acid)** | 1–5 % – floors, excreta; **Lister** used; Lysol, cresol |
| **Savlon** | Chlorhexidine + cetrimide |
| **Dettol** | Chloroxylenol |
| **Potassium permanganate (KMnO₄)** | **1 : 1000 – 1 : 10,000** – wound, sitz bath, gastric lavage (some poisons) |
| **Gentian violet** | 1 % – fungal, oral thrush |
| **Formalin** | 40 % formaldehyde – specimen preservation (**10 % formalin**), fumigation |
| **Lime (calcium oxide)** | Faeces/ excreta disinfection |
| **Bleaching powder** | 33 % available chlorine – water chlorination (**0.5 ppm residual chlorine** after 1 hr), latrines |
| **Glutaraldehyde 2 %** | Endoscopes (HLD) |
| **Ortho-phthalaldehyde (OPA) 0.55 %** | HLD – 12 min |

- **Terminal disinfection** – **after patient discharge/ death/ transfer** – room & unit cleaned. **Concurrent disinfection** – **during illness** (daily, immediately) – excreta, linen.
- **Linen**: soiled linen in **red/ yellow bag** (as per hospital), **don't shake**, hold away from uniform, don't put on floor.

## 7.8 Biomedical Waste Management

@fig bmw_bins.svg | BMW segregation – 4 colour categories (BMW Rules 2016)

- **BMW Management Rules 2016** (Ministry of Environment, Forest & Climate Change), **amended 2018 & 2019** – replaced 1998 rules.
- **Segregation at source** (point of generation) – **most important step**.
- **4 categories**: **Yellow** (anatomical, soiled waste, expired medicines, chemical, lab), **Red** (contaminated **recyclable plastic** – tubing, catheters, IV sets, syringes without needles, gloves), **White translucent** (**sharps** – puncture-proof, leak-proof, tamper-proof), **Blue** (**glassware** – cardboard box with blue marking; metallic implants).
- **Untreated waste** storage **max 48 hrs**; **bar-code** labelling; **chlorinated plastic bags banned** (dioxins on burning); **pre-treatment of lab/ highly infectious waste** on-site (autoclave) before disposal.
- **Liquid waste** – disinfect before draining. **Cytotoxic drugs** – yellow (incineration at > 1200 °C).
- **Biohazard symbol**; incinerator: primary chamber **800 ± 50 °C**, secondary **1050 ± 50 °C**.

### Needle-stick/ sharps injury
- Prevention: **NO recapping** (if must – **one-handed scoop technique**), dispose immediately in sharps container (**¾ full → seal**), **needle cutter/ destroyer**, safety-engineered devices, never bend/ break needles by hand, Hep B vaccination of staff.
- **Immediate action**: **wash with soap & running water** (do not scrub, **don't squeeze/ suck**, no bleach on skin), mucosa – flush with water, **report** to in-charge, test source & self (HIV, HBV, HCV), **PEP for HIV within 2 hrs (max 72 hrs) – 28 days**, HBV – HBIG + vaccine if non-immune.
- Risk of transmission after needle-stick: **HBV ~30 % (6–30 %)** > **HCV ~1.8 %** > **HIV ~0.3 %** ("**Rule of 3s: 30, 3, 0.3**").

- Most effective method to prevent cross infection – **hand washing**.
- Autoclave – **121 °C, 15 lb/inch², 15–20 min**.
- Biological indicator for autoclave – **Geobacillus stearothermophilus**.
- Hot air oven – **160 °C for 2 hrs** – glassware, oils, powders.
- Endoscope disinfection – **2 % glutaraldehyde (Cidex)**.
- Blood spill – **1 % sodium hypochlorite**.
- Disposable syringes sterilized by – **gamma radiation**.
- Most common HAI – **UTI**.
- Measles patient – **airborne** precautions; MRSA – **contact**; Influenza – **droplet**.
- PPE removal – **gloves first**; donning – **gloves last**.
- Ethylene oxide – **heat-sensitive (plastic, rubber) items**.
- First flap of sterile pack opened – **away from the nurse**.
