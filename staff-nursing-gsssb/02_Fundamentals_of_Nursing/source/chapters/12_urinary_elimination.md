## 12.1 Micturition – Basics

- Urinary system: kidneys → ureters (**25–30 cm**) → bladder → urethra (female **3–4 cm**, male **18–20 cm**).
- **Bladder capacity 300–600 ml** (avg 500); **urge to void at ~150–300 ml** (stretch receptors); can hold up to ~600–1000 ml (overdistension).
- **Micturition reflex** – **sacral spinal cord S2–S4** (parasympathetic – **pelvic nerve**) → detrusor contracts & internal sphincter relaxes; **external sphincter – voluntary (pudendal nerve)**. Micturition centre – **pons**; cortex – voluntary control.
- Normal adult output **1–2 L/day (1500 ml)**; **≥ 30 ml/hr (0.5 ml/kg/hr)**; child 1 ml/kg/hr; infant 2 ml/kg/hr.
- Normal voiding **5–6 times/day**, not at night.

### Normal urine
| Character | Normal | Abnormal |
|---|---|---|
| **Colour** | **Pale yellow / straw/ amber** (urochrome) | Dark amber (dehydration), **red/ pink (haematuria, beetroot, rifampicin-orange)**, **tea/ cola (bilirubin, AGN)**, **blue-green** (methylene blue, Pseudomonas), **milky (chyluria, pyuria)**, **black (alkaptonuria, melanoma)** |
| **Clarity** | **Clear/ transparent** | Cloudy (UTI, pus, phosphates) |
| **Odour** | **Faintly aromatic**; ammonia on standing | **Fruity/ acetone (ketoacidosis)**, foul (infection), **mousy (PKU)**, maple syrup (MSUD) |
| **pH** | **4.5–8 (avg 6, slightly acidic)** | |
| **Specific gravity** | **1.005–1.030 (1.010–1.025)** | ↑ dehydration, SIADH, glycosuria; ↓ (fixed 1.010) – renal failure, DI |
| **Constituents** | Water 95 %, urea, creatinine, uric acid, electrolytes | **Glucose, protein, ketones, blood, bilirubin, casts** = abnormal |

### Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Polyuria** | > **3 L/day** (DM, DI, diuretics) |
| **Oliguria** | < **400 ml/day** (< 0.5 ml/kg/hr) |
| **Anuria** | < **100 ml/day** (some < 50) |
| **Nocturia** | Waking at night to void |
| **Dysuria** | **Painful/ burning** urination |
| **Frequency** | Voiding often (small amounts) |
| **Urgency** | Sudden strong desire to void |
| **Hesitancy** | Difficulty in **starting** stream (BPH) |
| **Haematuria** | Blood in urine |
| **Pyuria** | Pus in urine |
| **Glycosuria** | Glucose (> renal threshold **180 mg/dL**) |
| **Proteinuria/ Albuminuria** | Protein |
| **Ketonuria** | Ketones (DKA, starvation) |
| **Enuresis** | Involuntary urination – **bedwetting (nocturnal) after age 5** |
| **Retention** | Unable to empty bladder (urine accumulates) |
| **Residual urine** | Urine left after voiding – **> 100 ml (some > 50–150) abnormal** – **PVR** |
| **Suppression** | Kidneys **not producing** urine (vs retention – producing but not voiding) |
| **Incontinence** | Involuntary loss of urine |

## 12.2 Urine Specimen Collection

| Specimen | Method |
|---|---|
| **Routine (random)** | Clean container, **first morning (most concentrated)** preferred, 10–50 ml; within 1 hr to lab (or refrigerate) |
| **Clean-catch midstream (MSU) – for culture** | Clean meatus (female – front to back; male – circular from meatus), **begin voiding into toilet → collect midstream in sterile container** → finish in toilet; don't touch inside of container; **send within 30 min / refrigerate (4 °C) max 24 hrs** |
| **From catheter (CSU)** | **Clamp tubing below port 15–30 min**, clean **sampling port with alcohol**, **aspirate with sterile syringe**; **never from drainage bag**; never disconnect catheter |
| **24-hour urine** | **Discard first morning void (note time)** → collect all urine for 24 hrs → **include final void at same time next day**; keep **on ice/ with preservative**; **if one sample missed/ discarded → restart** (creatinine clearance, protein, VMA, catecholamines) |
| **Timed (2 hr, 12 hr)** | Same principle |
| **Infant** | Adhesive **urine collection bag (U-bag)** |
| **Suprapubic aspiration** | Sterile – infants (most reliable culture) |

- **Urine for sugar (Benedict's test)** – blue → green → yellow → orange → **brick red (+ + + +)**. **Rothera's test** – ketones (purple ring). **Heat & acetic acid test / Sulfosalicylic acid** – protein (albumin). **Dipstick (reagent strip)** – read at exact time.
- Specific gravity – **urinometer** at room temp; refractometer.

## 12.3 Bedpan & Urinal

- **Fracture bedpan (slipper pan)** – flat end goes **under buttocks** – **hip fracture, spinal injury, casts, elderly, can't raise hips**.
- Warm the bedpan (metal – rinse with warm water); powder rim (not on skin – friction ↓); patient in **semi-Fowler's (normal voiding/ defecation position)** if allowed; privacy; **toilet tissue & call bell in reach**; don't leave patient on pan long (pressure).
- Male – **urinal**; **standing** position (if allowed) natural.
- **Commode** – bedside chair-toilet.

## 12.4 Urinary Catheterization

:::def Catheterization
Introduction of a catheter through the **urethra into the bladder** under **strict aseptic (sterile) technique** to drain urine. Types: **Intermittent (straight/ in-and-out – Nelaton/ Robinson)** & **Indwelling/ retention (Foley – self-retaining balloon)**; **Suprapubic** (through abdominal wall – long-term, urethral injury); **Condom (external – Texas) catheter** (male incontinence – change daily, leave **2.5–5 cm space** at tip, not too tight).
:::

@fig foley_catheter.svg | Foley catheter – balloon, ports aur closed drainage

### Indications
**Urinary retention** (acute), **accurate hourly urine output** (critically ill/ shock), **pre/ intra-op** (pelvic surgery, long surgery), **bladder irrigation**, urinary obstruction (BPH), sterile specimen, incontinence with **stage 3–4 sacral ulcer**, end-of-life comfort, instillation of drugs, neurogenic bladder (intermittent).

### Catheter facts
- Size in **French (Fr/ Ch)** – **1 Fr = ⅓ mm** (outer diameter). **Adult female 12–14 Fr, adult male 14–16 Fr**, children 8–10 Fr; haematuria/ clots – **18–24 Fr (3-way)**. **Smallest size that drains well** (urethral trauma ↓).
- **Balloon – 5 ml (10 ml) – fill with sterile water** (not saline – crystallizes & balloon may not deflate; not air – floats/ deflates); 30 ml balloon – post-TURP (haemostasis/ traction).
- Material: **latex** (short-term ≤ 2 weeks; latex allergy check), **silicone** (long-term – up to **12 weeks**), **silver-alloy/ hydrogel-coated** (↓ infection), PVC (intermittent).
- **2-way** (balloon + drainage), **3-way** (+ irrigation – **CBI after TURP**).

:::key Female catheterization – steps
- **Dorsal recumbent position** (or Sims' – lateral if can't flex) – knees flexed, feet apart; good light.
- Perineal care first; open sterile tray; **sterile gloves**; drape.
- **Non-dominant hand separates labia (becomes contaminated – stays in place till catheter inserted)**; dominant (sterile) hand cleans.
- Cleaning: **front to back** – far labia minora → near labia minora → **meatus (centre) last**; one swab one stroke.
- Lubricated catheter inserted **5–7.5 cm (2–3 inch)** until urine flows, then advance **2.5–5 cm (1–2 inch) more** → inflate balloon → **gently pull back until resistance** felt.
- **Catheter in vagina by mistake → leave it there** (landmark) & insert **new sterile catheter** into meatus above it; then remove the wrong one.
:::

:::key Male catheterization – steps
- **Supine**, legs slightly apart. Retract foreskin (uncircumcised).
- Clean **meatus in circular motion outward** (glans).
- **Instil lignocaine gel (2 %) into urethra** – wait 3–5 min (anaesthesia + lubrication).
- Hold penis **perpendicular (60–90°) to body**, slight traction (straightens urethra) → insert **17–22.5 cm (7–9 inch)** till urine flows, then advance to **bifurcation (Y-port)** → inflate balloon → pull back gently → lower penis.
- **Replace foreskin** (prevent **paraphimosis**).
- Resistance at **prostate** – ask patient to take deep breath/ bear down (relaxes sphincter); **never force** (false passage).
:::

- **Inflate balloon only after urine flows** (urethral trauma if inflated in urethra – pain → deflate, advance).
- **Drain max ~750–1000 ml at once** in acute retention (as per policy) → then clamp – **haematuria/ hypotension (decompression)** risk (evidence mixed; follow policy).
- **Secure catheter** – **female: inner thigh**; **male: upper thigh or lower abdomen (penis upward – prevents peno-scrotal fistula/ pressure)**; leave slack.

### Catheter care (CAUTI prevention bundle)
- **Insert only when necessary; remove as soon as possible (daily review)** – **most important**.
- **Closed drainage system** – never disconnect catheter from tubing.
- **Bag always below bladder level** – not on floor; hang on bed frame (not side rail); no loops/ kinks (dependent loops ↓).
- **Empty bag when ⅔ full** (or every 8 hrs/ before transfer) using **separate clean container**, outlet not touching container.
- **Meatal care daily with soap & water** (during bath) – antiseptics not recommended routinely.
- **Fluids 2–3 L/day** (if not restricted) – flushing; **routine catheter change not needed** (change if blocked/ infected/ as per manufacturer).
- Strict hand hygiene; no routine bladder irrigation; no antibiotic prophylaxis routinely.
- Signs of CAUTI – fever, cloudy foul urine, suprapubic/ flank pain, confusion (elderly).

### Catheter removal
- Check order; empty bag; **deflate balloon completely with syringe** (don't cut balloon port); ask patient to **breathe out/ relax**, withdraw gently; clean perineum.
- After removal: **first void expected within 6–8 hrs**; measure first 3 voids; encourage fluids; mild burning/ frequency normal initially; **can't void in 8 hrs/ distension → inform** (bladder scan).

### Bladder irrigation
- **Intermittent** (closed – via port with sterile syringe, 30–60 ml NS) or **Continuous bladder irrigation (CBI) – 3-way catheter** – post-TURP/ bladder surgery – NS (room temp) – adjust rate to keep **output light pink (rosé)**; clots/ bright red → ↑ rate; **actual urine output = total drainage − irrigation volume**.
- **Bladder training** – timed voiding (every 2 hrs → gradually increase), Kegel exercises, fluid schedule.
- **Clean intermittent self-catheterization (CISC)** – neurogenic bladder (spinal injury, MS) – **clean (not sterile) technique at home**, every 4–6 hrs.

## 12.5 Urinary Retention & Incontinence

- **Retention** causes: **post-op/ post-anaesthesia (spinal)**, **BPH**, urethral stricture, drugs (**anticholinergics – atropine, antihistamines, opioids**), anxiety, lack of privacy, spinal cord injury, faecal impaction.
- Signs: **bladder distension (palpable, dull above symphysis)**, discomfort, dribbling (**overflow**), small frequent voids, restlessness.
- **Nursing measures to induce voiding** (before catheter): **privacy**, **normal position** (sit/ stand), **running tap water sound**, **pour warm water over perineum**, **warm sitz bath**, place hands in warm water, **stroke inner thigh**, **Credé's manoeuvre** (suprapubic pressure – neurogenic bladder; not if obstruction), adequate fluids, analgesia, ambulation. If not voided **8 hrs post-op** (or distended) → catheterize (as ordered); **bladder scan** for PVR.

| Incontinence type | Cause / Feature | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Stress** | Weak pelvic floor – leak on **coughing, sneezing, laughing, lifting** (multiparous women, post-menopause) | **Kegel exercises**, weight loss, surgery (sling) |
| **Urge (overactive bladder)** | Sudden urgency, can't reach toilet (UTI, stroke) | Bladder training, **anticholinergics (oxybutynin)** |
| **Overflow** | Bladder over-distended → dribbling (BPH, neurogenic, drugs) | Catheter, treat cause |
| **Functional** | Normal bladder, but **can't reach toilet** – mobility/ cognitive (dementia) | Toileting schedule, commode near bed, easy clothing |
| **Reflex** | Spinal cord lesion above S2 – no sensation | CISC |
| **Total** | Continuous unpredictable leak (fistula) | Surgery, pads |
| **Mixed** | Stress + urge | |

- Care: skin care (barrier cream), **incontinence pads (not diapers routinely – dignity)**, timed voiding, adequate fluids (don't restrict – concentrated urine irritates bladder), avoid caffeine/ alcohol/ carbonated drinks, **external catheter (male)**, Kegel.
