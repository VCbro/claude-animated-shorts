## 9.1 Sleep – Physiology

- **Rest** – mental & physical calmness, relaxation (not necessarily sleep). **Sleep** – recurrent, altered state of consciousness; controlled by **RAS (reticular activating system)** & **BSR (bulbar synchronizing region)** in brainstem; **pineal gland – melatonin** (darkness ↑).
- **Circadian rhythm** – 24-hr biological clock (**suprachiasmatic nucleus of hypothalamus**); body temperature lowest early morning.

| Stage | Features |
|---|---|
| **NREM Stage 1 (N1)** | **Lightest**, transition, few minutes, easily aroused; hypnic jerks |
| **NREM Stage 2 (N2)** | Light sleep, **~50 % of total sleep**, sleep spindles & K-complexes; vitals ↓ |
| **NREM Stage 3 (N3) – deep/ slow-wave/ delta sleep** | **Deepest**, **hardest to arouse**, **tissue repair, growth hormone ↑**, muscles relaxed; **sleepwalking (somnambulism), night terrors, bedwetting (enuresis)** occur here |
| **REM (Rapid Eye Movement) – paradoxical sleep** | **Dreaming** (vivid), brain very active (EEG like awake), **muscle atonia**, irregular HR/ RR/ BP ↑, **memory consolidation & learning**; ~**20–25 %** of adult sleep; first REM **~90 min** after sleep onset; REM periods **lengthen toward morning** |

- **Sleep cycle ≈ 90–110 min**, **4–6 cycles/night**. Sequence: N1 → N2 → N3 → N2 → **REM** → N2…
- Newborn – **~50 % REM**; elderly – **↓ N3 (deep sleep)**, frequent awakening.

### Sleep requirements (approx.)
| Age | Hours/day |
|---|---|
| **Newborn (0–3 months)** | **14–17 (up to 16–18)** |
| Infant (4–11 months) | 12–15 |
| Toddler (1–2 yrs) | 11–14 |
| Preschool (3–5 yrs) | 10–13 |
| School age (6–13 yrs) | 9–11 |
| Adolescent (14–17 yrs) | 8–10 |
| **Adult (18–64)** | **7–9** |
| **Elderly (65+)** | **7–8** (but less deep sleep) |

### Sleep disorders
| Disorder | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Insomnia** | **Difficulty falling/ staying asleep** – most common sleep disorder |
| **Hypersomnia** | Excessive sleep |
| **Narcolepsy** | Sudden uncontrollable sleep attacks in daytime (goes directly into REM), **cataplexy** (sudden muscle weakness with emotion) – stimulants |
| **Sleep apnoea** | Breathing stops ≥ 10 sec during sleep; **obstructive (OSA – snoring, obesity) → CPAP**; central |
| **Somnambulism** | **Sleepwalking** (N3) – safety, don't wake forcefully |
| **Somniloquy** | Sleep talking |
| **Bruxism** | Teeth grinding (N2) |
| **Enuresis** | Bedwetting (children) |
| **Night terrors (pavor nocturnus)** | Child screams, can't be consoled, **no memory** (N3) vs **Nightmares** – frightening dreams (**REM**), remembers |
| **Restless leg syndrome** | Urge to move legs at night |
| **Sleep deprivation** | Irritability, fatigue, ↓ concentration, hallucinations; **ICU psychosis** |

### Promoting sleep – nursing measures
- **Sleep hygiene**: regular bedtime & wake time, **avoid caffeine (4–6 hrs before), alcohol, nicotine, heavy meals** near bedtime, **light snack/ warm milk (tryptophan)**, exercise (not 2–3 hrs before sleep), **avoid daytime naps** (if insomnia), bed only for sleep, dark-quiet-cool room, screens off.
- Hospital: **cluster nursing activities**, dim lights, reduce noise, back rub, **relieve pain**, toileting before sleep, comfortable position, bedtime rituals, **HS care**, relaxation.
- Sedatives/ hypnotics – last option (benzodiazepines – dependence; elderly – fall risk, confusion).

## 9.2 Pain

:::def Pain
**IASP (2020)**: "An unpleasant sensory and emotional experience associated with, or resembling that associated with, actual or potential tissue damage."
**Margo McCaffery (1968)**: "Pain is **whatever the experiencing person says it is**, existing **whenever he says it does**." → **Patient's self-report = most reliable indicator of pain**.
:::

- **Nociception** – 4 processes: **Transduction** (stimulus → impulse) → **Transmission** (A-delta – fast, sharp, myelinated; C fibres – slow, dull, unmyelinated) → **Perception** (cortex – conscious awareness) → **Modulation** (endorphins, enkephalins – body's natural opioids).
- **Gate Control Theory – Melzack & Wall (1965)**: substantia gelatinosa in dorsal horn acts as gate; **large-fibre stimulation (rubbing, massage, TENS)** closes gate → pain ↓.
- **Specificity theory** (von Frey), **Pattern theory** (Goldschneider).

### Types of pain
| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Acute** | Sudden, < 3–6 months, protective, **sympathetic signs (↑ HR, BP, RR, sweating, dilated pupils)** |
| **Chronic** | > 3–6 months, persistent; **no sympathetic signs** (adaptation), depression, fatigue |
| **Cutaneous (superficial)** | Skin – sharp, burning, localized |
| **Deep somatic** | Bone, muscle, tendons – diffuse |
| **Visceral** | Organs – cramping, poorly localized, may be referred |
| **Referred** | Felt at site distant from origin – **MI → left arm/ jaw**; **gallbladder → right shoulder**; **diaphragm → shoulder tip**; **kidney → groin**; **appendix → periumbilical (early)** |
| **Radiating** | Spreads from origin (sciatica) |
| **Phantom** | In amputated limb – **real pain** |
| **Neuropathic** | Nerve damage – burning, shooting, tingling (diabetic neuropathy, post-herpetic) – **gabapentin, pregabalin, amitriptyline** |
| **Intractable** | Resistant to treatment |
| **Psychogenic** | No physical cause found (still real to patient) |
| **Breakthrough pain** | Flare above controlled chronic pain (cancer) → rescue dose |

### Pain assessment
- **PQRST / OLDCART**: **P**rovokes/ palliates, **Q**uality, **R**egion/ radiation, **S**everity, **T**iming.
- Scales:

| Scale | Use |
|---|---|
| **Numeric rating scale (NRS) 0–10** | Adults (most common); **1–3 mild, 4–6 moderate, 7–10 severe** |
| **Visual analogue scale (VAS)** | **10 cm line** – no pain to worst pain |
| **Verbal descriptor scale** | None – mild – moderate – severe |
| **Wong-Baker FACES** | **Children ≥ 3 yrs**, low literacy, language barrier |
| **FLACC** (Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability) | **2 months–7 yrs / non-verbal** (0–10) |
| **CRIES / NIPS / PIPP** | **Neonates** |
| **Oucher scale** | Children 3–12 (photos) |
| **PAINAD** | **Advanced dementia** |
| **CPOT / BPS** | **Ventilated/ ICU** non-verbal |

- **Pain threshold** – minimum stimulus at which pain is felt (fairly same in all). **Pain tolerance** – maximum pain a person can bear (varies – culture, past experience, anxiety). **Anxiety ↑ → pain perception ↑**.
- **Reassess pain** after intervention: **IV opioid – 15–30 min**, **oral – 30–60 min**.
- Elderly/ cognitively impaired – pain **undertreated** – observe behaviour (grimacing, restlessness, guarding).

### Pain management
**Pharmacological**:
- **Non-opioids**: **Paracetamol** (max **4 g/day adult**; hepatotoxic), **NSAIDs** (ibuprofen, diclofenac – GI bleeding, renal; **give with food**), aspirin (**not in children < 16 – Reye's syndrome**).
- **Opioids**: **Morphine** (gold standard), fentanyl (patch – 72 hrs), tramadol, codeine, pethidine (meperidine – avoid – toxic metabolite), buprenorphine. Side effects – **respiratory depression (most serious – check RR; < 12 hold)**, **constipation (most common – does not develop tolerance; give laxative prophylactically)**, nausea, sedation, **pinpoint pupils**, urinary retention, pruritus. Antidote – **Naloxone**.
- **Adjuvants (co-analgesics)**: antidepressants, anticonvulsants, corticosteroids, muscle relaxants.
- **WHO analgesic ladder (1986)**: Step 1 mild – non-opioid ± adjuvant → Step 2 moderate – **weak opioid (codeine, tramadol)** + non-opioid → Step 3 severe – **strong opioid (morphine)** + non-opioid. Principles: **by mouth, by the clock, by the ladder, for the individual, attention to detail**.
- **PCA (Patient-Controlled Analgesia)** – **only patient presses button** (not family/ nurse) – "PCA by proxy" dangerous; lockout interval.
- **Epidural analgesia**, nerve blocks, local anaesthetics (lignocaine).
- **Placebo** – not used to "test" pain (unethical without consent).
- **Tolerance** (need higher dose), **physical dependence** (withdrawal on stopping) ≠ **addiction** (psychological craving – rare in pain patients). **Equianalgesia** – dose conversion between opioids/ routes.

**Non-pharmacological**:
- **Physical**: **cutaneous stimulation – massage, heat & cold, TENS (Transcutaneous Electrical Nerve Stimulation), acupressure, acupuncture**, positioning, immobilization, exercise, contralateral stimulation.
- **Cognitive-behavioural**: **distraction** (music, TV, conversation – best for **short, acute** procedural pain), **relaxation/ deep breathing**, **guided imagery**, **biofeedback**, hypnosis, meditation, prayer, **humour**, **reiki/ therapeutic touch**.
- **Environmental**: quiet, dim light, comfortable.
- Patient education, **prevent pain** (premedicate **30 min before** painful procedure like dressing/ ambulation).
