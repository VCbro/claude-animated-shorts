# Body Mechanics, Mobility, Positioning & Pressure Ulcers
<!-- desc: Body mechanics, ROM terms & exercises, hazards of immobility, positions, moving & transfer, ambulation aids, pressure ulcer staging, Braden & prevention -->

## 10.1 Body Mechanics

:::def Body mechanics
Body ka **coordinated, efficient & safe use** (musculoskeletal + nervous system) during movement, lifting, turning – **nurse ki back injury (most common occupational injury)** aur patient injury rokne ke liye.
:::

- **Principles**:
 - **Wide base of support** (feet apart, **shoulder width / ~30 cm**, one foot slightly forward) → **stability ↑**
 - **Lower centre of gravity** – **bend knees & hips, not the back** (squat)
 - Keep object **close to body** (centre of gravity) – lifting leverage
 - **Use large muscles of legs & thighs** (not back) – "**lift with legs**"
 - **Back straight**, avoid **twisting** (turn whole body – pivot on feet)
 - **Push, pull, slide or roll** rather than lift – **sliding/ rolling needs less energy than lifting**
 - Work at **waist height** (adjust bed)
 - Tighten **abdominal & gluteal muscles** before moving (internal girdle)
 - Get help/ **mechanical lift** for heavy patients; **friction ↓** (slide sheet)
 - Use body weight as counterbalance; move rhythmically
- **Centre of gravity** in standing adult – **2nd sacral vertebra (S2)**, centre of pelvis. **Line of gravity** must fall within base of support for balance.
- **Good posture (alignment)** – head erect, shoulders back, chest out, abdomen in, knees slightly flexed, feet parallel.

## 10.2 Range of Motion (ROM)

| Movement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Flexion** | Angle ↓ (bending) |
| **Extension** | Angle ↑ (straightening to 180°) |
| **Hyperextension** | Beyond 180° |
| **Abduction** | **Away from midline** |
| **Adduction** | **Towards midline** |
| **Internal/ external rotation** | Turning towards/ away from midline |
| **Circumduction** | Circular movement (combination) – shoulder, hip |
| **Pronation** | **Palm down** (posterior) |
| **Supination** | **Palm up** (anterior) – "**soup in palm**" |
| **Inversion / Eversion** | Sole **inward / outward** |
| **Dorsiflexion** | Toes **up** (towards shin) |
| **Plantar flexion** | Toes **down** (pointing) – **footdrop** = fixed plantar flexion |
| **Opposition** | Thumb touches fingertips |
| **Radial/ ulnar deviation** | Wrist sideways |

- **Active ROM** – patient khud karta hai; **Active-assisted** – nurse partial help; **Passive ROM** – **nurse moves joints** (unconscious, paralysed) – **maintains joint mobility only, not muscle strength**. **Resistive** – against resistance (strength ↑).
- Each movement **3–5 times**, **2–3 times/day**, slowly, **support joint above & below**, **stop at point of pain/ resistance**; never force.
- **Contraindications to ROM**: acute inflammation/ arthritis flare, **fractures (unhealed)**, **DVT in that limb**, recent MI (passive caution), joint dislocation, torn ligaments.
- **Isotonic exercise** – muscle shortens, movement (walking, swimming, ROM) – **↑ muscle tone, mass, strength & circulation**. **Isometric** – **muscle contracts without joint movement** (quadriceps setting, gluteal setting) – **↑ strength**; **↑ BP & HR – avoid in cardiac patients**; useful for patients **in cast/ traction**. **Isokinetic** – constant-speed resistance (machines). **Aerobic** – O₂ use ↑ (cardiac endurance).
- **Quadriceps setting** – press knee into bed, hold 5–10 sec – **prepare for ambulation**, prevent DVT.

## 10.3 Hazards of Immobility

| System | Complications |
|---|---|
| **Musculoskeletal** | **Disuse atrophy** (muscle mass ↓ ~3 %/day / 10–15 % per week), **contractures** (**footdrop**, hip flexion), **osteoporosis** (Ca loss from bones – disuse), **joint ankylosis** |
| **Cardiovascular** | **Orthostatic hypotension** (most common), **DVT/ thrombus** (**Virchow's triad – stasis, hypercoagulability, vessel injury**), ↑ cardiac workload, **dependent oedema** |
| **Respiratory** | **Hypostatic pneumonia**, **atelectasis** (secretions pool), ↓ chest expansion |
| **Integumentary** | **Pressure ulcers** |
| **Urinary** | **Urinary stasis**, **renal calculi** (hypercalciuria), **UTI**, retention/ incontinence |
| **GI** | **Constipation** (most common GI), anorexia, faecal impaction |
| **Metabolic** | ↓ BMR, **negative nitrogen balance** (protein catabolism), **hypercalcaemia**, ↓ appetite |
| **Psychosocial** | Depression, boredom, **sensory deprivation**, isolation, altered sleep, dependence |

- Prevention: **turn every 2 hrs**, **early ambulation (most important)**, ROM, deep breathing & coughing, incentive spirometry, fluids 2–3 L, high-fibre & high-protein diet, **compression stockings/ SCD**, positioning, footboard, skin care.

## 10.4 Positioning

@fig patient_positions.svg | Important patient positions

| Position | Description | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| **Supine/ Dorsal** | Flat on back | Post spinal anaesthesia, spinal injury, rest |
| **Dorsal recumbent** | Supine + knees flexed, feet flat | Perineal care, female catheterization, vaginal exam |
| **Prone** | On abdomen, head to side | Back care, **after tonsillectomy (semi-prone)**, ARDS (proning), spine surgery, prevents hip flexion contracture |
| **Lateral (side-lying)** | On side, upper leg flexed | Rest, pressure relief, back care |
| **Sims' (semi-prone)** | **Left lateral**, **right knee & thigh flexed**, left arm behind | **Enema, rectal temperature/ exam**, suppository, **unconscious (drain secretions)**, perineal exam |
| **Fowler's** | HOB **45–60°** (semi-Fowler's **30–45°**, high Fowler's **60–90°**, low Fowler's 15–30°) | **Dyspnoea**, cardiac, eating, NG feeding, post-thyroidectomy, ↑ICP (30°) |
| **Orthopnoeic** | Sitting up, leaning forward over **over-bed table with pillow** | **Severe dyspnoea, asthma, COPD**, thoracentesis |
| **Trendelenburg** | Whole bed tilted – **head low, feet high** (~15–30°) | Pelvic surgery, **postural drainage (lower lobes)**; shock (old practice) |
| **Reverse Trendelenburg** | **Head high, feet low** | Head & neck surgery, GERD, hiatal hernia |
| **Modified Trendelenburg** | Supine, only **legs raised 20–30°** | **Shock** |
| **Lithotomy** | Supine, hips & knees flexed, **legs in stirrups** | **Vaginal exam, delivery, cystoscopy, TURP**, Pap smear |
| **Knee-chest (genupectoral)** | Kneeling, chest on bed, buttocks up | **Sigmoidoscopy, rectal exam**, cord prolapse, retroverted uterus |
| **Jack-knife (Kraske)** | Prone, hips flexed over break of table | **Proctological (rectal) surgery**, haemorrhoidectomy |
| **Lateral kidney** | Lateral with bridge under flank | Nephrectomy |
| **Tripod** | Sitting, leaning forward on arms | COPD (accessory muscles) |

## 10.5 Moving, Lifting & Transfer

- **Moving up in bed**: lower HOB flat, patient bends knees & pushes with feet (on count of 3), **2 nurses + draw/ lift sheet** (avoid dragging – **shear**), trapeze.
- **Log rolling** – **spinal cord injury/ spinal surgery** – body moved as **one unit (like a log)**, **3–4 persons**, one person **controls head & neck** & gives commands, pillow between legs.
- **Dangling** – sit at bed edge with legs hanging **few minutes before standing** – **prevents orthostatic hypotension** (check dizziness, pulse).
- **Bed → wheelchair transfer**: wheelchair **at 45° angle to bed (parallel/ close), on patient's STRONGER (unaffected) side**, **wheels locked**, footrests up, patient wears non-skid shoes, **pivot on strong leg**, gait belt (transfer belt) at waist.
- **Bed → stretcher**: stretcher **parallel & touching bed**, both **locked**, same height, **3 persons (3-man lift)** or **slide board/ sheet**; stretcher par **side rails up & safety strap**; normal travel me **patient ke feet aage**, nurse **head end** par (airway observe).
- **Wheelchair down ramp** – **backwards** (nurse walks backwards); entering lift – **back in**.
- **Mechanical lift (Hoyer lift)** – heavy/ immobile patients.
- Hemiplegic – nurse stands on **affected (weak) side** while ambulating (support).

## 10.6 Ambulation Aids (Crutch, Cane, Walker)

:::key Crutches
- **Axillary crutch length** = patient's height − **40 cm (16 inches)**; or **2–3 finger-widths (~5 cm) below axilla**; handgrip so **elbow flexed 20–30°**; crutch tips **15 cm (6 inch) in front & 15 cm to side** of feet (tripod position – standing stance).
- **Body weight on hands/ palms – NOT axilla** (**crutch palsy – radial/ brachial plexus nerve damage**).
- **Gaits**:
 - **4-point gait** – **most stable/ safest, slowest**; partial weight-bearing **both legs** (R crutch → L foot → L crutch → R foot) – arthritis, cerebral palsy
 - **2-point gait** – faster version of 4-point (R crutch + L foot together, then L crutch + R foot)
 - **3-point gait** – **non-weight bearing on one leg** (both crutches + affected leg forward together, then unaffected leg) – fracture/ amputation of one leg
 - **Swing-to / Swing-through** – **paraplegia** (both legs paralysed, with braces) – swing-through fastest
- **Stairs**: **UP – "Up with the GOOD"** (unaffected leg first → then crutches + affected leg). **DOWN – "Down with the BAD"** (crutches + affected leg first → then good leg). ("**Good goes to heaven, bad goes to hell**")
- Sitting: back to chair, both crutches in hand on **affected side**, grip armrest with other hand, lower.
:::

- **Cane**: held in hand **opposite (unaffected/ stronger side)** to affected leg; height – top at **greater trochanter / wrist crease** (elbow 15–30° flexed); move **cane + affected leg together** → then unaffected leg. Quad cane (4 legs) – more stable.
- **Walker**: most stable aid; height – wrist crease (elbow 15–30° flexed); **lift walker & move 15–20 cm (6–8 inch) forward → step with affected (weak) leg → then unaffected leg**; **never on stairs**; wheeled walker – rolled.
- **Gait belt** – while assisting ambulation; if patient starts to fall → **ease patient gently to floor** (protect head) – don't try to hold up.

## 10.7 Pressure Ulcer / Pressure Injury (Bed Sore / Decubitus Ulcer)

:::def Pressure injury (NPIAP 2016)
**Localized damage to skin and/or underlying soft tissue, usually over a bony prominence**, as a result of **intense and/or prolonged pressure** or pressure in combination with **shear**. Capillary closing pressure ~**32 mmHg** – isse zyada pressure → ischaemia.
:::

@fig pressure_points.svg | Position ke hisaab se pressure points

- **Most common site – sacrum/ coccyx** (supine), then **heels**, ischial tuberosity (sitting), greater trochanter (lateral), occiput (children), elbows, scapula, ears.
- **Causes**: **pressure (main)**, **shear** (sliding down when **HOB > 30°** – skin stays, deep tissue moves → vessels stretch), **friction** (skin rubbing on sheet – abrasion), **moisture** (incontinence, sweat – maceration).
- **Risk factors**: **immobility (most important)**, ↓ sensation (paralysis, diabetes), malnutrition (**albumin < 3.5 g/dL**, protein deficiency), dehydration, anaemia, incontinence, elderly, ↓ LOC, obesity/ emaciation, sedation, fever, poor circulation, steroids.

@fig pressure_ulcer_stages.svg | Pressure ulcer stages – depth ke hisaab se

| Stage (NPIAP) | Description |
|---|---|
| **Stage 1** | **Intact skin, non-blanchable erythema** (redness persists after pressure removed > 30 min); dark skin – purple/ blue, warm/ cool, firm |
| **Stage 2** | **Partial-thickness skin loss** with exposed dermis – **shallow open ulcer, red-pink wound bed** or **intact/ ruptured serum-filled blister**; no slough |
| **Stage 3** | **Full-thickness skin loss** – **subcutaneous fat visible**; slough may be present; **undermining & tunnelling** may occur; **no bone/ tendon/ muscle** |
| **Stage 4** | **Full-thickness skin & tissue loss** – **exposed bone, tendon, muscle, cartilage**; osteomyelitis risk |
| **Unstageable** | Base covered by **slough (yellow) or eschar (black)** – depth can't be seen (stable dry heel eschar – **don't remove**) |
| **Deep tissue pressure injury (DTPI)** | Intact/ non-intact skin with **persistent deep red, maroon or purple discolouration** or blood-filled blister |
| Medical device-related; Mucosal membrane pressure injury | From devices (NG, O₂ tubing, catheter) |

- **Reverse staging is NOT done** – healing stage 4 ulcer is "healing stage 4", not stage 3.
- **Assessment tools**: **Braden scale** (most used) – **6 subscales**: **Sensory perception, Moisture, Activity, Mobility, Nutrition, Friction & shear**; score **6–23**; **lower score = higher risk**: **≤ 18 at risk**, 15–18 mild, 13–14 moderate, 10–12 high, **≤ 9 very high risk**. **Norton scale** (5 items – physical condition, mental, activity, mobility, incontinence; **5–20; ≤ 14 at risk**). **Waterlow** (UK). **Push tool** – healing assessment.

:::mnemo Braden – "SMAMN-F"
**S**ensory · **M**oisture · **A**ctivity · **M**obility · **N**utrition · **F**riction & shear → max **23**, min **6**; kam score = zyada khatra.
:::

### Prevention & management
- **Reposition every 2 hrs** (bed), **every 1 hr in chair** (patient shifts weight every 15 min); turning clock; **30° lateral tilt** (avoid direct trochanter pressure).
- **HOB ≤ 30°** (shear ↓) unless contraindicated; **lift (don't drag)** – lift sheet/ trapeze.
- **Pressure-redistribution surfaces** – alternating pressure air mattress, water bed, foam, gel; **heels floated** (pillow under calves).
- **Don't massage reddened areas**; **no donut/ ring cushions**.
- Skin **clean & dry**, mild cleanser, moisturize dry skin, **barrier cream** (incontinence); check skin each shift.
- **Nutrition: high protein (1.25–1.5 g/kg), calories, Vit C, zinc, fluids**.
- Wound care: **moist wound healing**; cleanse with **NS**; **debridement** of necrotic tissue – **sharp/ surgical, mechanical (wet-to-dry), enzymatic (collagenase), autolytic (hydrocolloid/ hydrogel – body's own enzymes), biological (maggot/ larval therapy)**; dressings – **transparent film** (stage 1–2), **hydrocolloid** (stage 2–3, light exudate), **foam**, **alginate** (heavy exudate – absorbs, from seaweed), **hydrogel** (dry wound – adds moisture), **NPWT – negative pressure (VAC) therapy** (stage 3–4).
