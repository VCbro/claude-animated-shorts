# Care of Unconscious, Peri-operative, Terminally Ill & Dead Patient

## 19.1 Care of the Unconscious Patient

- **Levels of consciousness**: **Alert/ conscious → Lethargic (drowsy) → Obtunded (arousable with stimulation) → Stupor/ semi-coma (only vigorous/ painful stimulus) → Coma (no response)**. **GCS 3–15** (≤ 8 = coma); **AVPU**.
- **Causes**: head injury, stroke, hypoglycaemia/ hyperglycaemia, poisoning, hypoxia, infections (meningitis), seizures, shock, uraemia, hepatic failure.

:::key Nursing care – systems approach
- **Airway (priority 1)**: **lateral/ semi-prone (recovery) position**, head turned to side, **oropharyngeal airway (Guedel)**, **suction**, O₂; **remove dentures**; **no pillow under head** (if airway risk); jaw thrust.
- **Breathing & circulation**: monitor vitals, SpO₂, GCS, pupils (neuro observation chart – hourly/ 2-hourly).
- **Eye care**: **no blink/ corneal reflex** → **NS cleaning, artificial tears/ lubricant ointment, eye pads/ taping lids closed** → **prevent corneal ulceration/ exposure keratitis**.
- **Oral care**: every **2–4 hrs**, **side-lying**, suction ready, lip lubricant, padded tongue blade (no fingers).
- **Skin/ pressure care**: **turn every 2 hrs**, pressure-relieving mattress, skin clean & dry.
- **Positioning & ROM**: **passive ROM 2–3 times/day**, **footboard (footdrop)**, **trochanter roll**, **hand rolls**, splints – **contracture prevention**.
- **Nutrition & fluids**: **NG/ PEG feeding**, IV fluids, I/O, daily weight.
- **Elimination**: **catheter / condom catheter**, bowel care (stool softeners, suppository), monitor constipation & retention.
- **Safety**: **side rails up**, bed low, **seizure precautions**, no hot water bottles (burns), restraint only if needed.
- **Communication/ sensory stimulation**: **talk to patient, call by name, explain every procedure** (**hearing is last sense lost & first to return**), orient (date, time), touch, music, family involvement.
- **Infection prevention**: chest (pneumonia – turning, suction, chest physio), UTI, VAP bundle.
- **Eye, ear, nose, perineal, hair care**; maintain dignity & privacy.
:::

## 19.2 Peri-operative Basics (FON level)

- **Pre-operative**: **informed consent** (before pre-medication), **NPO** (solids **6–8 hrs**, clear fluids **2 hrs**), **skin preparation (hair removal by clipper just before surgery)**, **bowel prep (enema)** as ordered, **bladder emptied** (void before OT), **remove jewellery, dentures, contact lenses, hairpins, nail polish, make-up, prosthesis**, hospital gown, **ID band**, allergy check, **vitals baseline**, investigations (CBC, BT/CT, blood grouping & cross-match, RFT, sugar, ECG, X-ray), **pre-medication (atropine/ glycopyrrolate, sedative)** – **side rails up after pre-med**; pre-op teaching (**deep breathing, coughing, leg exercises, incentive spirometry, early ambulation**); **pre-op checklist**.
- **Post-operative immediate**: **airway (lateral position till gag reflex returns)**, vitals **every 15 min** first hour, check **dressing & drains (bleeding)**, IV, pain, **first void within 6–8 hrs**, prevent complications – **atelectasis (first 24–48 hrs)**, shock/ haemorrhage, **DVT (early ambulation)**, paralytic ileus, wound infection (day 3–7), dehiscence. Post-spinal anaesthesia – **flat 6–8 hrs** (headache).
- **Post-op bed** – see Chapter 8.

## 19.3 Sensory Alterations

- **Sensory deprivation** (↓ stimuli – ICU isolation, blindness) – boredom, confusion, hallucinations. **Sensory overload** (excess stimuli – ICU noise, pain) – anxiety, confusion, **ICU psychosis/ delirium**.
- Nursing – meaningful stimulation (clock, calendar, radio, visitors), **reduce noise**, cluster care, **orientation**, glasses/ hearing aids.

## 19.4 Stress & Coping

- **Hans Selye – General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS)**: **(1) Alarm reaction** (fight or flight – sympathetic, adrenaline ↑) → **(2) Resistance** (adaptation) → **(3) Exhaustion** (if stress continues – illness/ death). **Local adaptation syndrome (LAS)** – inflammation, reflex pain response.
- **Eustress** (positive stress), **Distress** (negative).
- **Defence mechanisms** (Freud): denial, projection, rationalization, regression, repression (unconscious forgetting), suppression (conscious), displacement, sublimation (socially acceptable – healthiest), compensation, reaction formation, identification, intellectualization.

## 19.5 Loss, Grief & Dying

- **Loss** – actual/ perceived, maturational/ situational. **Grief** – emotional response to loss. **Bereavement** – state of having lost someone. **Mourning** – outward/ cultural expression.
- **Types of grief**: **Normal/ uncomplicated**, **Anticipatory** (before actual loss – terminal illness), **Complicated/ dysfunctional** (prolonged > 6–12 months, chronic, delayed, exaggerated, masked), **Disenfranchised** (not socially recognized – miscarriage, pet), **Ambiguous** (missing person).

:::key Kübler-Ross – 5 Stages of Grief/ Dying (1969, "On Death and Dying")
**D-A-B-D-A**: **1. Denial** ("Nahi, ye mere saath nahi ho sakta" – shock, isolation) → **2. Anger** ("Mere saath hi kyun?" – blames staff/ God – **don't take personally**) → **3. Bargaining** ("Bas beti ki shaadi tak jee lun…" – deals with God) → **4. Depression** (sadness, withdrawal, crying – allow to express) → **5. Acceptance** (calm, peace, planning). Stages not always in order.
**Nursing**: **active listening, presence, therapeutic silence, touch**, allow expression, don't argue in denial, honest information, spiritual support.
:::

- Other models: **Engel** (shock & disbelief → developing awareness → restitution), **Bowlby** (numbness → yearning & searching → disorganization & despair → reorganization), **Worden's 4 tasks** of mourning.

### Care of the dying patient
- **Palliative care (WHO)** – improves **quality of life** of patients & families facing life-threatening illness – **relief of pain & other symptoms, psychosocial & spiritual support**; neither hastens nor postpones death. **Hospice** – end-of-life (last ~6 months), home/ institution. **DNR (Do Not Resuscitate)** – documented; still give full comfort care.
- **Physical care**: pain relief (by the clock), mouth care (dry mouth), skin care, **position for comfort**, eye care, suction (secretions), elimination, nutrition (small, as desired – **don't force**), O₂ for comfort.
- **Psychosocial/ spiritual**: be present, allow family, **religious rituals** (Gangajal, Tulsi, Quran recitation, last rites – priest), honest answers, life review, fulfil wishes.
- **"Death rattle"** – noisy breathing from secretions → **side position, glycopyrrolate/ hyoscine**, gentle oral suction.

### Signs of approaching death (impending death)
- **↓ level of consciousness**, increasing sleep, confusion/ restlessness (terminal agitation)
- **Cheyne-Stokes respiration**, apnoea periods, **death rattle**, mouth breathing
- **Skin cold, mottled (knees & feet first), cyanosed, clammy**; **"Hippocratic facies"** (pinched nose, sunken eyes)
- **Pulse weak, rapid & irregular; BP falls**
- **↓ urine output**, incontinence; loss of swallowing & gag reflex
- **Pupils dilated, fixed**; blurred vision; jaw sags
- **Hearing is the last sense to go** – keep talking gently

### Signs of death (clinical)
- **Cessation of heartbeat (no pulse, no heart sounds)** & **respiration**, **fixed dilated pupils (no light reflex)**, **flat ECG (asystole)**, no reflexes, loss of muscle tone.
- **Brain death** – irreversible loss of all brain functions including **brainstem** (no brainstem reflexes, **apnoea test +ve**) – certified by **board of doctors** (India – **Transplantation of Human Organs & Tissues Act (THOTA) 1994** – **4 doctors** panel, tests repeated after 6 hrs).
- **Post-mortem changes**:
 - **Pallor mortis** – paleness (15–30 min)
 - **Algor mortis** – **cooling of body** (~1 °C/hr, ~1.5 °F/hr) till room temp
 - **Livor mortis (post-mortem lividity/ hypostasis)** – **purplish discolouration in dependent parts** (blood settles by gravity) – starts **20 min–2 hrs**, **fixed after 6–8 hrs (12 hrs)** → **place body supine with pillow under head** (prevent face discolouration)
 - **Rigor mortis** – **stiffening of muscles** (ATP depletion) – starts **2–4 hrs** after death, **begins in small muscles – eyelids/ jaw/ face/ neck** (involuntary first – **heart**), spreads downward, **complete in ~12 hrs**, disappears in **24–48 (36) hrs** (same order) → **position body before rigor sets in** (close eyes, mouth, limbs straight)
 - **Putrefaction/ decomposition** – after 24–48 hrs (first sign – **greenish discolouration of right iliac fossa**)

## 19.6 Last Office (Care of the Dead Body)

:::key Steps of last office
1. **Death declared & certified by doctor** (time noted); inform relatives (doctor) & nursing in-charge; **respect religious/ cultural beliefs** – ask family.
2. Hand hygiene, **standard precautions (gloves, apron)**; screen/ privacy.
3. **Place body supine, flat**, arms by sides (or across abdomen), **one pillow under head & shoulders** (prevent pooling of blood in face – **livor mortis**).
4. **Close eyes** (gently press lids **for 30 sec**; moist cotton pads if needed); **close mouth** – **insert dentures** (shape of face) – support jaw with **rolled towel/ bandage under chin**.
5. **Remove tubes, drains, catheters, IV lines** (cut & cap or as per policy) & dress wounds – **EXCEPT in medico-legal cases (MLC)/ post-mortem cases** – **leave all tubes in place**, cut close & tape.
6. **Plug orifices** (mouth, nose, ears, rectum, vagina) with **cotton** as per policy to prevent leakage (some policies now discourage – follow hospital).
7. **Clean the body** (sponge), comb hair, remove jewellery (**hand over valuables to relatives with witness & document**; wedding ring may be left – taped, as per family wish).
8. **Clean gown/ shroud**; **identification tags** – **wrist, ankle (big toe) & outside shroud** (name, age, sex, ID, date & time of death, ward).
9. Wrap in **shroud/ mortuary sheet**; **infectious cases – "biohazard/ danger of infection" label** & body bag.
10. Allow family to view & grieve before shifting; **shift to mortuary** (or hand over to relatives) **within 1–2 hrs** (before rigor complete); **death certificate (Form 4/ 4A – MCCD)**, **death report/ register**, documentation; **terminal cleaning of unit**.
:::

- **Medico-legal case (MLC)** – unnatural death (accident, poisoning, burns, suicide, assault, sudden unexplained death, unknown/ unidentified body) → **inform police**, **body for post-mortem (autopsy)**, **don't remove tubes/ lines**, preserve clothes/ evidence, no last office cleaning beyond minimal.
- **Autopsy (post-mortem)** – clinical (consent of relatives) vs medico-legal (no consent needed – police).
- **Organ & tissue donation**: **eye (cornea) donation – within 6 hrs** of death (keep **eyes closed with moist cotton, head raised on pillow, fans off, AC on**, ice packs on lids); **skin – within 6 hrs**; organs (heart, lungs, liver, kidneys) – only from **brain-dead (heart-beating) donors**; **NOTTO** (National Organ & Tissue Transplant Organisation); **3 August = Indian Organ Donation Day** (2023 se; pehle 27 November); **13 August = World Organ Donation Day**; eye donation fortnight **25 Aug – 8 Sept**.
- **Euthanasia** – active (illegal in India), passive (allowed with safeguards – SC 2011, 2018); **living will** valid.

:::exam PYQ style
- Unconscious patient position – **lateral/ semi-prone (recovery)**.
- Last sense to be lost in dying patient – **hearing**.
- First stage of Kübler-Ross – **denial**; last – **acceptance**; "if only…" – **bargaining**.
- Rigor mortis starts – **2–4 hrs** after death, begins in **jaw/ eyelids (small muscles)**.
- Livor mortis – **purple discolouration of dependent parts** → pillow under head.
- Algor mortis – **cooling**.
- Dentures in dead body – **insert (maintain face shape)**.
- MLC body – **tubes not removed**.
- Identification tags on dead body – **wrist, toe & shroud**.
- Eye donation – **within 6 hrs**.
- GAS – **Hans Selye**; stages – **alarm, resistance, exhaustion**.
:::
