## 17.1 Hot & Cold Applications

| | **Heat** | **Cold** |
|---|---|---|
| Blood vessels | **Vasodilation** → blood flow ↑ | **Vasoconstriction** → blood flow ↓ |
| Effects | ↑ metabolism, ↑ tissue O₂, **muscle relaxation**, ↓ joint stiffness, **promotes suppuration (pus localisation)**, healing, ↓ pain (spasm) | **↓ swelling/ oedema**, **↓ bleeding**, **↓ pain (local anaesthetic – slows nerve conduction)**, ↓ metabolism, ↓ inflammation, ↓ temperature |
| Uses | Muscle spasm, **joint stiffness (arthritis, chronic)**, **abscess (localize pus)**, **IV infiltration (non-vesicant) / phlebitis**, menstrual cramps, retention of urine (warm water on perineum), **sitz bath** | **Acute injury (first 24–48 hrs) – sprain, strain**, haemorrhage/ epistaxis, **fever (cold sponge)**, **post-tonsillectomy (ice collar)**, insect bites, headache, burns (cool water), **joint swelling (acute)** |
| Contraindications | **First 24 hrs of injury/ trauma (↑ bleeding & swelling)**, **active bleeding**, **acute inflammation – appendicitis (rupture)**, **non-inflammatory oedema**, **localized malignancy (spread)**, open wound, **testes (spermatogenesis ↓)**, pregnant abdomen, impaired sensation (burns), metal implants (diathermy) | **Open wounds (↓ healing)**, **impaired circulation (PVD, Raynaud's, diabetes)**, shivering, cold allergy, **elderly/ infants** (hypothermia), oedematous tissue, neuropathy |

- **Rebound phenomenon** – heat max effect at **20–30 min**; beyond **45–60 min** → **vasoconstriction (reverse)**; cold beyond ~**60 min** → **vasodilation**. So apply **20–30 min**, then rest.
- **Elderly, infants, diabetics, unconscious, paralysed, neuropathy** – **↓ sensation – burn/ frostbite risk** → extra care, lower temp.

### Methods & temperatures
| Application | Type | Temperature / time |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot water bag (bottle)** | Dry heat | **Adult 46–52 °C (115–125 °F)**; **children < 2 yrs, elderly, unconscious 40.5–46 °C**; fill **½–⅔**, **expel air**, check leak (invert), cover with flannel/ towel; never on unconscious/ diabetic; check skin every 5–10 min |
| **Electric heating pad** | Dry heat | Low setting; **don't lie on it**, no pins |
| **Infrared/ heat lamp** | Dry | **45–60 cm (18–24 inch)** distance, 20 min |
| **Hot moist compress/ fomentation** | Moist | **40.5–43 °C (105–110 °F)**; 15–20 min; change every 5 min; **moist heat penetrates deeper** |
| **Sitz bath** (hip bath) | Moist | **40–43 °C**, **15–20 min** – **perineal/ rectal**: haemorrhoids, episiotomy, fissure, post-delivery; watch for **faintness** (pooling of blood in pelvis) |
| **Hot soak / immersion** | Moist | 40–43 °C, 15–20 min |
| **Paraffin wax bath** | | Arthritis (hands) – ~52 °C |
| **Ice bag/ ice cap/ ice collar** | Dry cold | Fill **½–⅔ with small ice pieces**, expel air, cover; **20–30 min** |
| **Cold compress** | Moist cold | **15 °C**; 20 min |
| **Cold sponge/ tepid sponge** | Moist | **Tepid 27–34 °C**; cold 18–27 °C; **15–20 min (20–30)**; **stop if shivering** (heat production); sponge with **long strokes**, ice bags at axilla/ groin; **recheck temp after 30 min** |
| **Cooling blanket (hypothermia blanket)** | | Hyperthermia |
| **Chemical hot/ cold packs** | | Squeeze to activate |

## 17.2 Wounds

- **Wound** – break in continuity of skin/ tissue.
- **Classification**: **intentional** (surgical) vs **unintentional** (trauma); **open** vs **closed**; **acute** vs **chronic** (> 4–12 weeks – pressure ulcer, diabetic foot, venous ulcer).
- **Types**: **Incision** (clean cut – knife), **Laceration** (torn, irregular edges), **Abrasion** (scrape – superficial), **Puncture** (penetrating – nail; **tetanus risk**), **Contusion** (bruise – closed), **Avulsion** (tissue torn away), **Penetrating**, **Crush**.
- **Surgical wound classification (CDC)**: **Clean** (no inflamed/ GI/ GU/ respiratory tract entry – hernia, thyroid) – infection < 2 %; **Clean-contaminated** (GI/ respiratory entered under control – cholecystectomy); **Contaminated** (open fresh trauma, spillage); **Dirty/ infected** (old trauma, pus, perforated viscus).

### Wound healing
- **Phases**: **Haemostasis** (immediate – platelet plug, clot) → **Inflammatory** (**0–4/ 3–6 days** – redness, heat, swelling, pain; neutrophils, macrophages) → **Proliferative/ Reconstructive** (**4–21 days** – **granulation tissue (beefy red, granular)**, collagen, angiogenesis, epithelialization, wound contraction – myofibroblasts) → **Maturation/ Remodelling** (**21 days – 1–2 years** – scar strengthens to ~**70–80 %** of original).
- **Primary intention** – edges approximated (sutured surgical) – minimal scar, fast. **Secondary intention** – wound left open, heals by **granulation & contraction** from bottom up – larger scar (pressure ulcer, burns, infected). **Tertiary intention (delayed primary closure)** – left open few days (infection control/ oedema) then sutured.
- **Factors delaying healing**: **age (elderly)**, **malnutrition (protein, Vit C, zinc)**, **diabetes**, **infection**, **smoking**, **steroids/ immunosuppressants/ chemo**, obesity, poor circulation/ anaemia, stress, radiation, foreign body, **excess moisture or dryness**.
- **Wound bed colour (RYB)**: **Red** (granulation – healthy → protect, keep moist), **Yellow** (slough/ exudate → cleanse, debride), **Black** (necrotic eschar → debride).
- **Exudate types**: **Serous** (clear, watery – plasma), **Sanguineous** (bright red – fresh bleeding), **Serosanguineous** (pale pink – normal early post-op), **Purulent** (pus – thick, yellow/ green, foul – infection).

### Wound complications
- **Haemorrhage** (primary, reactionary – within 24 hrs, secondary – 7–14 days – infection).
- **Infection** – fever, ↑ pain, redness, warmth, swelling, pus, foul odour (usually **post-op day 3–7**).
- **Dehiscence** – **partial/ total separation of wound layers** (**post-op day 5–10**; obesity, coughing, malnutrition) – patient feels "**something gave way/ popped**", ↑ serosanguineous drainage.
- **Evisceration** – **protrusion of viscera through wound** → **EMERGENCY**: **stay with patient, call for help**, **cover with sterile towel/ dressing soaked in sterile NS (warm)**, **low Fowler's with knees flexed** (↓ tension), NPO, monitor shock, **never push organs back**.
- **Fistula**, **keloid** (excess scar beyond wound), **hypertrophic scar**, **adhesions**, **incisional hernia**.
- Prevention of dehiscence – **splint incision with pillow** when coughing, nutrition, abdominal binder.

## 17.3 Dressing

- **Purposes**: protect from contamination & trauma, **absorb drainage**, maintain **moist environment**, haemostasis (pressure), immobilize, debride, comfort, psychological.
- **Principles**: **surgical asepsis**, hand hygiene, **clean → dirty**: clean **from incision (cleanest) outward**, **top to bottom**, **one swab one stroke**; **drain site cleaned last (separately) in circular motion from drain outward**; old dressing removed with **clean gloves**, discard; **sterile gloves/ forceps** for new; **premedicate for pain 30 min before**; **assess wound** (size, colour, drainage, odour, approximation); date & initial on dressing; **dressing trolley – clean (upper) shelf for sterile, lower for unsterile**; infected wounds dressed **last** in ward.
- **Dry dressing** – clean, closed wounds; **wet-to-moist** – debridement; **wet-to-dry** (non-selective mechanical debridement – painful; less used); **pressure dressing** – bleeding.
- **Modern dressings**: **transparent film (Tegaderm)** – IV sites, stage 1–2 ulcers (see through, no absorb); **hydrocolloid (Duoderm)** – light–moderate exudate, autolytic debridement (change 3–5 days); **hydrogel** – **dry wounds – add moisture**; **alginate (seaweed)** – **heavy exudate**, haemostatic; **foam** – moderate–heavy; **silver/ antimicrobial**; **gauze**; **NPWT (VAC)** – **negative pressure (−125 mmHg)**.
- **Wound cleansing solution – Normal saline (best, physiological)**; avoid routine povidone-iodine, H₂O₂, Dakin's on granulating tissue (cytotoxic).

### Drains
- **Passive** – **Penrose (open, soft rubber)** – gravity/ capillary; **corrugated drain**. **Active (closed suction)** – **Hemovac** (spring), **Jackson-Pratt (JP – bulb/ grenade)** – **compress bulb before closing cap** (creates suction), empty when **½ full**, measure.
- **T-tube** (bile – CBD), **chest tube**, **NG**. Report sudden ↑ bright red drainage.
- **Shortening a drain** – pull out by prescribed length & cut, re-pin (safety pin) (Penrose).

### Sutures & staples
- **Absorbable** (catgut – plain/ chromic, **Vicryl (polyglactin)**, PDS) – internal; **non-absorbable** (**silk, nylon (Ethilon), polypropylene (Prolene)**, stainless steel, staples) – skin; removed.
- **Suture removal**: **face/ neck 3–5 days**, **scalp 7–10**, **trunk/ abdomen 7–10**, **arms 7–10**, **legs/ feet 10–14**, joints/ back 10–14. **Alternate sutures removed first** (check wound strength) → rest; **cut close to skin on one side, pull through from opposite (no outer part drawn through tissue)**; apply **Steri-strips**.
- **Staple removal** – staple remover (lower jaw under staple, squeeze).
- Suture types: **interrupted** (each separate – most common), **continuous**, **blanket (locking)**, **mattress (vertical/ horizontal)**, **subcuticular**, **retention** (deep – heavy).

## 17.4 Bandages & Binders

@fig bandage_turns.svg | Roller bandage turns – kaunsa turn kahan

- **Purposes**: hold dressing/ splint, **support**, **pressure (bleeding/ oedema)**, immobilize, limit movement, correct deformity.
- **Types**: **Roller bandage** (gauze, crepe/ elastic, cotton, plaster), **Triangular bandage** (first aid – **arm sling**, head, hand, foot), **Many-tailed bandage/ binder** (abdomen, chest – post-op, **scultetus**), **T-bandage/ binder** (**perineum** – hold perineal dressing; double-T for male), **Breast binder**, **Abdominal binder**, **Tubular (stockinette)**, **Elastic (crepe) bandage**, **Plaster of Paris**.
- **Parts of roller bandage**: **initial (free) end, body, terminal end (drum/ roll)**; **head** = rolled part.
- **Turns**: **Circular** (anchor – start & finish; wrist, forehead), **Spiral** (cylindrical – finger, upper arm, trunk), **Reverse spiral** (cone-shaped – forearm, leg), **Figure-of-eight** (joints – elbow, knee, ankle), **Spica** (figure-8 around a **joint + body part** – shoulder, hip, thumb, groin), **Recurrent** (**stump, head (capeline), fingertip**).

:::key Rules of bandaging
- Part in **functional/ natural position**, clean & dry, **pad bony prominences** & skin surfaces (axilla, between fingers).
- **Bandage roll held upwards (facing outward/ upwards)**, unroll **on the skin** (not in air) – even tension.
- **Start distal → proceed proximal** (venous return).
- **Begin & end with 2 circular turns** (anchor).
- **Each turn overlaps ⅔ (or ½) of previous** width.
- **Leave fingertips/ toes exposed** – check circulation (**CSM – colour, sensation, movement**; capillary refill) **every 1–2 hrs (15 min initially)**; numbness, tingling, pallor, cyanosis, coldness, pain → **loosen**.
- Secure with tape/ clip – **not over wound or bony prominence or inner aspect**; avoid knots on injured part.
- Elastic bandage – apply with **slight stretch**; remove & reapply every 8 hrs (or as ordered).
- Width: **finger 2.5 cm (1 inch)**, **hand/ arm 5 cm (2 inch)**, **leg 7.5–10 cm (3–4 inch)**, **trunk 10–15 cm**.
:::

- **Triangular bandage**: base, apex, two ends; **arm sling (large/ broad sling)** – forearm fracture (hand slightly higher than elbow, **~10 cm/ 4 inch**); **St. John's (elevation) sling** – hand/ clavicle injury, hand **elevated toward opposite shoulder**; **collar & cuff** – humerus fracture; **reef knot** (flat, doesn't slip) – first aid.
- **Binders** – abdominal (post-op support, splint incision – applied **from bottom up**), **breast binder** (lactation suppression – older), **scultetus binder (many-tailed)** – applied **from bottom upward**, overlapping.

## 17.5 Specimen Collection – General Rules

- **Correct container**, **label at bedside** (name, ID, date, time, specimen type, ward), **requisition form**, **send immediately** (or refrigerate as directed), **standard precautions (gloves)**, sterile technique for culture, **collect before antibiotics** (cultures), adequate quantity, avoid contamination.

| Specimen | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Blood (venous)** | Tourniquet **< 1 min**, cleanse, **order of draw: blood culture → citrate (light blue – PT/INR) → serum (red/ gold) → heparin (green) → EDTA (lavender/ purple – CBC, HbA1c) → fluoride/ oxalate (grey – glucose)**; **invert tubes gently** (don't shake – haemolysis) |
| **Blood culture** | **Before antibiotics**, **2 sets from 2 different sites**, during fever spike/ chills; **10 ml per bottle (adult)**; aerobic bottle first (with syringe); clean bottle top with alcohol |
| **Capillary blood (glucose)** | **Side of fingertip** (fewer nerve endings), **wipe first drop**; infant – **lateral/ medial heel** |
| **ABG** | **Radial artery**, **Allen's test** first, heparinized syringe, expel air bubbles, **ice**, **pressure 5 min (10 min if anticoagulants)** |
| **Sputum** | **Early morning**, **before breakfast**, **after rinsing mouth with plain water** (no toothpaste/ mouthwash), **deep cough** (not saliva), sterile container; **AFB – 2 samples (spot + early morning)**; 5–10 ml; send within 1–2 hrs |
| **Throat swab** | Before antibiotics/ meals; **tongue depressor**, swab **tonsils & posterior pharynx** without touching tongue/ cheeks |
| **Nasal/ nasopharyngeal swab** | Head tilted back, swab to posterior nasopharynx, rotate |
| **Wound swab** | **Clean wound with NS first** (remove surface exudate), swab **viable tissue (centre) – Levine technique** (rotate 1 cm² with pressure), not pus/ necrotic |
| **Urine** | See Chapter 12 (MSU, catheter port, 24-hr) |
| **Stool** | See Chapter 13 (ova & parasites – warm, fresh; occult blood – diet restriction) |
| **CSF** | Lumbar puncture – 3 sterile tubes (biochem, micro, cell count) – send immediately (cells lyse) |

:::exam PYQ style
- Heat causes – **vasodilation**; cold – **vasoconstriction**.
- Sprain first 24 hrs – **cold (ice)**; after 48 hrs – heat.
- Heat contraindicated – **appendicitis, first 24 hrs of injury, bleeding, malignancy**.
- Hot water bag temperature adult – **46–52 °C**; fill **⅔**, expel air.
- Sitz bath – **40–43 °C, 15–20 min**.
- Tepid sponge – **27–34 °C**; stop if **shivering**.
- Wound cleaning direction – **from incision outward / clean to dirty**.
- Evisceration – **cover with sterile saline-soaked dressing**, low Fowler's knees flexed.
- Granulation tissue colour – **beefy red**; pus – **purulent**.
- Suture removal face – **3–5 days**; abdomen – **7–10 days**.
- Bandage applied – **distal to proximal**, each turn covers **⅔** of previous.
- Joint bandage – **figure of 8**; stump/ head – **recurrent**; cone-shaped limb – **reverse spiral**; shoulder/ hip – **spica**.
- Perineal dressing hold – **T-binder**.
- Blood culture – **before antibiotics, 2 sites**.
- Sputum for AFB – **early morning**, mouth rinsed with plain water.
:::
