# Vital Signs – Temperature, Pulse, Respiration, BP, SpO₂ & Pain

:::key Vital signs – normal adult values (sabse important table)
| Vital sign | Normal | Ranges / terms |
|---|---|---|
| **Temperature (oral)** | **37 °C (98.6 °F)**; range 36.5–37.5 °C | Rectal +0.5 °C, Axillary −0.5 °C, Tympanic ≈ core |
| **Pulse** | **60–100 /min** | Newborn 120–160, infant 100–140, child 80–120 |
| **Respiration** | **12–20 /min** | Newborn 30–60, infant 25–40, child 20–30 |
| **BP** | **< 120/80 mmHg** | Newborn ~65/40 |
| **SpO₂** | **95–100 %** | < 90 % hypoxaemia |
| **Pain** | 0 (no pain) | 5th vital sign |

- Vital signs kab lete hain: **admission**, as ordered/ routine (4 hourly), **before & after surgery/ invasive procedure**, **before & after drugs affecting cardio-respiratory system** (digoxin, antihypertensives, opioids), **blood transfusion**, **condition change**, before & after nursing interventions affecting vitals (ambulation).
- **Order of checking** (child): **R → P → T** (rona shuru hone se pehle respiration). Adult: T, P, R, BP.
- Abnormal value → **recheck** first, then report.

## 5.1 Body Temperature

- **Thermoregulation centre – Hypothalamus** ("body's thermostat"). Anterior hypothalamus – heat loss; posterior – heat production/ conservation.
- **Heat production**: basal metabolism (BMR), muscle activity (**shivering** – heat ↑ up to 4–5 times), thyroxine, adrenaline, fever.
- **Heat loss mechanisms**:

| Mechanism | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Radiation** (most heat loss ~60 %) | Without contact – electromagnetic waves | Body heat to cold room |
| **Conduction** | **Direct contact** with cooler object | **Ice pack, cold bed**, cold stethoscope |
| **Convection** | **Air/ water current** | **Fan**, wind |
| **Evaporation** | Liquid → vapour (sweat, insensible) | **Tepid sponge**, sweating |

- **Factors**: age (newborn – poor regulation; elderly – lower temp), **circadian rhythm (lowest 4–6 am, highest 4–8 pm)**, exercise, hormones (**ovulation ↑ 0.3–0.6 °C – progesterone**), stress, environment.

### Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Afebrile** | Normal temperature |
| **Pyrexia/ Fever/ Febrile** | Above normal (> 37.5 °C oral / > 38 °C) |
| **Hyperpyrexia** | **> 41 °C (105.8 °F)** |
| **Hypothermia** | **< 35 °C (95 °F)** |
| **Heat stroke** | > 40 °C with CNS dysfunction, hot dry skin |

### Types of fever
| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Continuous/ sustained** | Remains above normal, fluctuation **< 1 °C** in 24 hr | **Typhoid (2nd week)**, lobar pneumonia |
| **Remittent** | Above normal throughout, fluctuation **> 1 °C (2 °C)**, never normal | **Typhoid (early)**, infective endocarditis |
| **Intermittent** | Fever present for some hours, **returns to normal** daily | **Malaria**, septicaemia, TB (pyaemia) |
| **Relapsing/ Recurrent** | Fever days alternating with afebrile days | **Brucellosis (undulant)**, Pel-Ebstein (Hodgkin's), Borrelia |
| **Quotidian** | Every 24 hrs | P. knowlesi |
| **Tertian** | Every **48 hrs** (alternate day) | **P. vivax, P. ovale**, falciparum (malignant tertian) |
| **Quartan** | Every **72 hrs** | **P. malariae** |
| **Step-ladder** | Gradual daily rise | **Typhoid (1st week)** |

- **Stages of fever**: **Onset/ chill (cold) stage** – shivering, pale cold skin, **rigors** (heat production ↑ to reach new set-point) → **Course/ plateau (flush) stage** – hot dry flushed skin, thirst → **Defervescence/ termination** – **sweating**, temp falls. Fall: **Crisis** (sudden – within hours) vs **Lysis** (gradual – days).
- **Fever care**: rest, **fluids ↑ (2.5–3 L)**, light clothing, **tepid sponge (water 27–34 °C / ~1 °C below body)**, antipyretics (paracetamol), oral care, high-calorie diet; during chill stage – **cover with blanket (warmth)**; record 4-hourly.
- **Cold sponge** – avoid ice/ alcohol rub (shivering, vasoconstriction).

### Temperature sites & thermometers
| Site | Time (clinical glass) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral** (sublingual pocket) | **3 min** (2–3) | Wait **15–30 min after eating, drinking hot/cold, smoking**; **contraindicated**: unconscious, infants/ children < 5–6 yrs, mouth breathers, oral surgery, seizures, confused, O₂ mask |
| **Axillary** | **5–10 min** (9 min) | **Safest**, least accurate; children; dry axilla |
| **Rectal** | **2–3 min** | **Most accurate (core)**; insert **adult 1.5 inch (3.5 cm)**, child 1 inch, infant 0.5 inch; **Sims' position**; lubricate; **contraindicated**: rectal surgery, diarrhoea, haemorrhoids, **MI/ cardiac patients (vagal stimulation → bradycardia)**, neutropenia, newborn (perforation risk) |
| **Tympanic** | 1–3 sec | Infrared; pull pinna **up & back (adult)**, **down & back (child < 3 yr)**; close to core |
| **Temporal artery** | Seconds | Forehead scan |
| **Groin** | | Rarely |

- **Clinical thermometer (mercury)** – range **35–42 °C (95–110 °F)**; **constriction** prevents mercury fall-back; **shake down below 35 °C (95 °F)** before use. Mercury thermometers being **phased out** (toxicity) → **digital**.
- **Celsius ↔ Fahrenheit**: **°F = (°C × 9/5) + 32**; **°C = (°F − 32) × 5/9**. e.g. 38 °C = 100.4 °F; 40 °C = 104 °F; 104 °F = 40 °C.
- Thermometer **cleaning**: soap & **cold water**, wipe **from stem (clean area) towards bulb (dirty area)**, then 70 % alcohol; **hot water nahi** (mercury expand → thermometer toot sakta hai).
- **Mercury spill** – don't touch/ vacuum; use mercury spill kit, gloves, collect with card/ syringe, airtight container.

## 5.2 Pulse

@fig pulse_sites.svg | Pulse sites – kaunsi site kab use karein

- **Pulse** = wave of blood created by **left ventricle contraction** felt over artery.
- Assess: **Rate, Rhythm, Volume (strength), Tension, Equality (bilateral), Character, Condition of arterial wall**.
- **Volume scale**: **0 = absent, 1+ = weak/ thready, 2+ = normal, 3+ = full/ bounding** (some 4-point scale: 3+ increased, 4+ bounding).
- Count: regular – 30 sec × 2; **irregular – full 1 minute**; apical always 1 min.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tachycardia** | > 100 /min |
| **Bradycardia** | < 60 /min |
| **Thready/ weak pulse** | Shock, haemorrhage, dehydration |
| **Bounding pulse** | Fever, fluid overload, anxiety, aortic regurgitation |
| **Water-hammer (Corrigan's) pulse** | **Aortic regurgitation** |
| **Pulsus paradoxus** | SBP falls > 10 mmHg on inspiration – **cardiac tamponade**, severe asthma |
| **Pulsus alternans** | Strong-weak alternate – **LV failure** |
| **Pulsus bisferiens** | Double peak – AR, HOCM |
| **Pulse deficit** | **Apical − Radial** (2 nurses, same time, 1 min) – **atrial fibrillation** |
| **Sinus arrhythmia** | Rate ↑ in inspiration, ↓ expiration – **normal in children** |
| **Dicrotic pulse** | Typhoid |

- **Apical pulse**: **5th ICS, left mid-clavicular line** (adult); **4th ICS, lateral to MCL in children < 7 yrs**. Use for **infants < 2 yrs**, irregular pulse, **before digoxin**.
- **Factors ↑ pulse**: exercise, fever, pain, anxiety, haemorrhage/ hypovolaemia, hypoxia, sympathomimetics (adrenaline, atropine), standing. **↓ pulse**: sleep, athletes, **digoxin, beta-blockers**, hypothermia, ↑ICP (late), vagal stimulation.

## 5.3 Respiration

- **External respiration** (lungs – gas exchange alveoli ↔ blood) & **internal respiration** (blood ↔ tissues). **Ventilation** = movement of air in/out.
- Count **without informing patient** (pretend pulse is being counted) – conscious control se pattern badal jata hai; **1 full minute** if irregular; one inspiration + expiration = 1 breath.
- **Inspiration – active** (diaphragm contracts), **expiration – passive** (normal).
- Respiratory centre: **medulla oblongata & pons**; main stimulus – **↑ CO₂ / H⁺** (chemoreceptors); COPD – **hypoxic drive**.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Eupnoea** | Normal breathing | |
| **Tachypnoea** | > 20 /min, fast shallow | Fever, pneumonia, anxiety |
| **Bradypnoea** | < 12 /min | **Opioid overdose**, ↑ICP |
| **Apnoea** | Absence of breathing | Cardiac arrest, sleep apnoea |
| **Dyspnoea** | Difficult/ laboured breathing (subjective) | HF, asthma |
| **Orthopnoea** | Breathing difficulty **lying flat**, eased sitting up | **Left HF** |
| **PND** | Sudden night breathlessness | Left HF |
| **Hyperventilation** | ↑ rate & depth → **↓ CO₂ (resp. alkalosis)** | Anxiety |
| **Hypoventilation** | ↓ → ↑ CO₂ | Sedation |
| **Kussmaul** | **Deep, rapid, regular** | **DKA / metabolic acidosis** |
| **Cheyne-Stokes** | **Waxing-waning depth + apnoea periods** (cyclic) | **Dying patient, HF, ↑ICP**, drug overdose |
| **Biot's (ataxic)** | Irregular, varying depth, sudden apnoea | **Meningitis**, brain damage (medulla) |
| **Apneustic** | Prolonged gasping inspiration | Pons lesion |
| **Stertorous** | **Snoring** sounds | Unconscious, airway obstruction |
| **Stridor** | High-pitched crowing **(inspiratory)** | **Upper airway obstruction** – laryngeal oedema, croup |
| **Wheeze** | Whistling **(expiratory)** | **Asthma** |
| **Sighing** | Deep inspiration + prolonged expiration | Anxiety, normal occasionally |

## 5.4 Blood Pressure

@fig bp_korotkoff.svg | BP lene ka sahi tareeka aur Korotkoff ki 5 sounds

- **BP = Cardiac output × Peripheral resistance**. **Systolic** – ventricular contraction; **Diastolic** – ventricular relaxation (minimum pressure).
- **Pulse pressure = SBP − DBP** (normal ~40); wide → aortic regurgitation, ↑ICP; narrow → shock, cardiac tamponade, aortic stenosis.
- **MAP = DBP + ⅓ pulse pressure = (SBP + 2 DBP) ÷ 3** (normal 70–100).
- Factors: age ↑, **exercise ↑**, stress ↑, **sex (women < men before menopause)**, **diurnal (lowest early morning, highest late afternoon)**, weight/ obesity ↑, **posture (lying > sitting > standing slightly)**, race, medications, smoking ↑, caffeine ↑.
- **Sphygmomanometer** – mercury (gold standard), aneroid, electronic. **Riva-Rocci** invented mercury sphygmomanometer (1896); **Korotkoff** described sounds (1905). Stephen Hales – first measured BP (horse, 1733).
- Cuff size: **bladder width = 40 % of arm circumference** (or 20 % wider than diameter); **length 80 %**. Adult standard 12–13 cm wide.
- Patient **seated 5 min rest**, **no caffeine/ smoking 30 min**, feet flat, legs uncrossed, **arm supported at heart level**, palm up; cuff **2.5 cm above antecubital fossa**; **palpatory method first** → auscultatory; deflate **2–3 mmHg/ sec**; wait **1–2 min** before repeat (venous congestion).
- **Thigh BP** – popliteal artery, **large cuff**, prone position; **SBP 10–40 mmHg higher** than arm.
- **Orthostatic (postural) hypotension**: SBP fall **≥ 20 mmHg** or DBP **≥ 10 mmHg** within 3 min of standing (lying → standing).

| Error | Result |
|---|---|
| **Cuff too narrow/ small** | **Falsely HIGH** |
| **Cuff too wide/ large** | **Falsely LOW** |
| **Cuff wrapped too loosely** | **Falsely HIGH** |
| **Arm below heart level** | **Falsely HIGH** |
| **Arm above heart level** | **Falsely LOW** |
| **Deflating too slowly** | Falsely high diastolic (venous congestion) |
| **Deflating too quickly** | **Falsely low systolic** & high diastolic |
| **Arm unsupported** (muscle contraction) | Falsely high |
| **Repeating too soon** | Falsely high |
| **Mercury not at eye level** (reading from above/ below) | Parallax error (above eye → low; below eye → high) |
| Failure to identify auscultatory gap | **Falsely low systolic** |

:::mnemo BP error trick
"**Small cuff, Loose cuff, Low arm → High BP**" · "**Big cuff, High arm → Low BP**"
:::

- **BP classification** (JNC-7): Normal < 120/80; **Prehypertension** 120–139/80–89; **Stage 1** 140–159/90–99; **Stage 2 ≥ 160/≥ 100**. **Hypotension** < 90/60.
- **Avoid BP in arm with**: IV infusion, **AV fistula/ shunt**, **mastectomy (lymph node dissection) side**, injury/ burns, cast, PICC line.

## 5.5 Pulse Oximetry (SpO₂) & Pain

- **Pulse oximeter** – non-invasive, uses **red & infrared light** (Beer-Lambert law), sites: finger, toe, ear lobe, forehead, bridge of nose; infants – foot/ palm.
- Normal **95–100 %**; COPD target **88–92 %**; < 90 % = hypoxaemia (PaO₂ ~60).
- **False readings**: **nail polish (dark), acrylic nails**, cold extremities/ poor perfusion, movement, bright light, **CO poisoning (falsely HIGH)**, severe anaemia, jaundice (bilirubin), methylene blue dye, dark skin (slight overestimation). Change finger probe site every **2–4 hrs** (pressure).
- **Pain – 5th vital sign** (American Pain Society, 1996). Scales: NRS 0–10, **VAS 10 cm**, **Wong-Baker FACES (≥ 3 yrs)**, **FLACC** (2 months–7 yrs/ non-verbal), CRIES (neonates), PAINAD (dementia). Detail – Chapter 9.

:::exam PYQ style
- Thermoregulation centre – **hypothalamus**.
- Most accurate route of temperature – **rectal**; safest – **axillary**.
- Rectal temp contraindicated in – **MI, diarrhoea, rectal surgery**.
- Cheyne-Stokes respiration – **dying/ HF/ ↑ICP**; Kussmaul – **DKA**.
- Pulse deficit – **atrial fibrillation**; counted by **2 nurses** simultaneously.
- Apical pulse site – **left 5th ICS mid-clavicular line**.
- Cuff too narrow → **falsely high** BP.
- 1st Korotkoff sound – **systolic**; 5th – **diastolic** (adult).
- Malaria fever – **intermittent**; Typhoid – **step-ladder (1st wk) / continuous**.
- 39 °C = **102.2 °F**; 100 °F = **37.8 °C**.
:::
