## 2.1 Health & Illness

- **WHO (1948)**: "Health is a state of complete **physical, mental and social well-being** and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." (Spiritual dimension – later suggested.) **7 April = World Health Day**.
- **Dimensions of health**: physical, mental, social, spiritual, emotional, vocational, environmental, intellectual.
- **Wellness** – active process of achieving full potential (Halbert Dunn – "high-level wellness").
- **Health-illness continuum** (Dunn/ Travis) – high-level wellness ←→ premature death.

| Health model | Key idea |
|---|---|
| **Health Belief Model** (Rosenstock, Becker) | Perceived susceptibility, seriousness, benefits, barriers → behaviour |
| **Health Promotion Model** (Pender) | Positive health behaviours |
| **Agent-Host-Environment (Epidemiological triad)** (Leavell) | Disease = interaction of agent + host + environment |
| **Holistic model** | Whole person – body, mind, spirit |
| **Maslow's model** | Hierarchy of needs |

- **Stages of illness behaviour (Suchman)**: (1) Symptom experience → (2) Assumption of sick role → (3) Medical care contact → (4) Dependent patient role → (5) **Recovery/ rehabilitation**.
- **Levels of prevention** – Primordial, Primary (health promotion + specific protection), Secondary (early diagnosis & treatment), Tertiary (disability limitation & rehabilitation).
- **Risk factors**: genetic, age, lifestyle, environment.

## 2.2 Health Care Delivery & Hospital

- **Levels of health care (India)**: **Primary** – Sub-centre/ Health & Wellness Centre (Ayushman Arogya Mandir – pop. 5000 plain/ 3000 hilly), **PHC** (30,000/ 20,000), **Secondary** – **CHC** (1,20,000/ 80,000 – 30 beds, 4 specialists), Sub-district & District hospital; **Tertiary** – Medical college hospitals, AIIMS, super-speciality.
- **Hospital** – types by ownership (government, private, trust), by function (general, speciality, teaching), by bed (small < 100, medium 100–300, large > 300).
- **Ward** – unit of hospital; nurse-patient ratio (INC): general ward **1 : 6** (per shift), ICU **1 : 1**, step-down 1 : 2–3.
- **Patient unit** – space & equipment for one patient (bed, side locker, over-bed table, chair, call bell, light, oxygen & suction outlets). Distance between beds – **at least 1–1.5 m (3–6 feet)** to prevent cross-infection.
- **Ideal ward temperature** – **20–24 °C (68–75 °F)**; humidity **30–60 %**; noise level – WHO **< 35–40 dB** (day) in wards.
- **Lighting** – natural light best; night light for safety.

## 2.3 Admission, Transfer & Discharge

### Admission
- Types: **Routine/ planned (elective)**, **Emergency**, **Day care**, **Transfer from other unit/hospital**.
- Steps: **Receive & welcome** (address by name), identify (**ID band – 2 identifiers: name + UHID/DOB**), orient to ward (call bell, toilet, routines, visiting hours), vitals, height & weight, **nursing admission assessment & history**, allergies (allergy band – **red**), valuables list (with witness), consent, inform doctor, **plan of care**.
- **Nurse's first responsibility** on admission – **make patient comfortable & safe**, orient, assess.
- **Admission bed** – prepared to receive patient (open bed / ether/ post-op bed as required).

### Transfer
- **Intra-hospital** (ward to ward/ ICU) or **inter-hospital** (referral). Prepare patient & family, transfer summary/ notes, belongings, medications, **nurse-to-nurse handover (SBAR)**, accompany critical patient with O₂, monitor.

### Discharge
- **Discharge planning starts on admission** (most important point!).
- Types: **Routine/ planned**, **LAMA/ DAMA** (Leave/ Discharge Against Medical Advice), **Absconding** (bina bataaye bhaag jaana – report immediately), **Transfer**, **Death**.
- Steps: doctor's written order, **discharge teaching (medicines, diet, activity, follow-up, warning signs)**, return valuables, discharge summary/ certificate, remove ID band (as per policy) at exit, escort to vehicle in **wheelchair**, after discharge – **terminal cleaning/ disinfection** of unit (closed bed).
- **LAMA** – patient ko risks samjhao, inform doctor, **signed LAMA form** (patient/ relative) with witness; refuse karne par document; can't be forced (autonomy) – but detaining = false imprisonment.
- **Medical/ dead body cannot be detained for unpaid bills** (Patients' Charter).

## 2.4 Patient Safety

- **WHO International Patient Safety Goals (IPSG – JCI)**:
 1. **Identify patients correctly** – **2 identifiers** (name + DOB/ UHID) – **room/ bed number is NOT an identifier**
 2. Improve effective communication (read-back of verbal orders, critical values)
 3. Improve safety of **high-alert medications** (KCl concentrated, insulin, heparin, opioids – double check)
 4. Ensure **correct site, correct procedure, correct patient surgery** (site marking, time-out)
 5. Reduce risk of **health care-associated infections** (**hand hygiene**)
 6. Reduce risk of **patient harm from falls**
- **17 September = World Patient Safety Day** (WHO, since 2019).
- **Root cause analysis (RCA)** – sentinel event ke baad; **Near miss** – error that didn't reach patient; **Sentinel event** – unexpected death/ serious injury.

### Fall prevention
- High-risk: **elderly (> 65)**, history of falls, sedatives/ diuretics/ antihypertensives, confusion, weakness, postural hypotension, impaired vision, urgency/ nocturia, IV poles/ tubes.
- Assessment tools: **Morse Fall Scale** (0–125; **≥ 45 high risk**), Hendrich II.
- Interventions: **bed in lowest position, wheels locked**, side rails (as per policy – not all 4 – entrapment), **call bell within reach**, non-slip footwear, adequate lighting/ **night light**, clear clutter, frequent rounds & toileting schedule, **dangle legs before standing**, yellow fall-risk band/ sign.
- **Patient found on floor** → **first assess** (ABC, injury, LOC) **before moving**, call for help, inform doctor, incident report.

### Restraints
- **Last resort** – only when other alternatives fail; to protect patient/ others; **doctor's written order** (not PRN), time-limited (adult **renew every 4 hrs**, children 2 hrs, < 9 yrs 1 hr – JC standard), **informed family**.
- Types: **physical** (wrist, ankle, vest/ jacket, mitten, belt, elbow restraint – infants), **chemical** (sedatives), environmental (side rails).
- **Check every 15–30 min**: circulation, skin, **2 fingers space** under restraint; **release every 2 hrs** for ROM, toileting, food, fluids.
- Tie to **bed frame (movable part that moves with head of bed) – NOT side rails**; **quick-release knot** (clove hitch for limb; **half-bow knot** to frame).
- Unnecessary restraint = **false imprisonment**. **Mummy restraint** – infants (procedures on head/neck); **elbow restraint** – after cleft lip repair (prevent touching).

### Fire safety
- **RACE**: **R**escue (patient ko hatao) → **A**larm (fire alarm, call) → **C**ontain/ confine (doors & windows band) → **E**xtinguish/ Evacuate.
- **PASS** (extinguisher): **P**ull pin → **A**im at base → **S**queeze → **S**weep.
- Evacuation order: **ambulatory patients first → wheelchair → bed-ridden** (horizontal evacuation first – same floor, then vertical). Don't use lifts.
- Fire classes: **A** – wood, paper, cloth (water, foam) · **B** – flammable liquids, oil, petrol (foam, CO₂, dry chemical) · **C** – electrical (**CO₂, dry chemical – NOT water**) · D – metals · K – kitchen oils.
- **Fire triangle** – heat + fuel + oxygen. O₂ in use → no smoking, no oil/ grease on fittings.
- **Electrical safety** – 3-pin plug (grounded), no frayed cords, don't overload, report sparks, **never touch patient & electrical equipment with wet hands**.

### Other hazards
- **Poisoning** (children – keep medicines locked), **burns** (hot water bottles – check temperature), **suffocation/ choking**, **radiation** (TDS principle), **needle stick injury**, **chemical (glutaraldehyde, cytotoxic)**, **back injury** of nurses (body mechanics).
- **Hospital codes (common)**: **Code Blue** – cardiac/ respiratory arrest · **Code Red** – fire · **Code Pink** – infant/ child abduction · **Code Black** – bomb threat · **Code Orange** – hazardous spill/ mass casualty · **Code Yellow** – disaster/ mass casualty (varies) · **Code Grey/ White** – violent person · **Code Silver** – person with weapon.

:::exam PYQ style
- Discharge planning starts – **at the time of admission**.
- Patient identification ke liye minimum – **2 identifiers**; bed number **not** an identifier.
- Restraint ko bandhte hain – **bed frame** se, side rail se nahi; check every **15–30 min**, release every **2 hrs**.
- Unnecessary restraint is – **false imprisonment**.
- Electrical fire – **CO₂** extinguisher; **water nahi**.
- Ideal distance between two beds – **3–6 feet (1–2 m)**.
