## 11.1 Nutrients – Basics

| Nutrient | Calories/g | Function | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Carbohydrate** | **4 kcal** | Main energy source (50–60 % of calories); fibre | Cereals, sugar, fruits |
| **Protein** | **4 kcal** | **Tissue building & repair**, enzymes, antibodies; adult **0.8–1 g/kg/day** (ICMR ~0.83 g/kg) | Egg, milk, pulses, meat, fish, soya |
| **Fat** | **9 kcal** (highest) | Energy store, fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K) absorption, insulation | Oil, ghee, butter, nuts |
| **Alcohol** | **7 kcal** | – | |
| Vitamins, minerals, water | 0 | Regulatory | |

- **Water** – ~60 % of body; intake **2–3 L/day**. **Fibre** – 25–40 g/day – constipation ↓.
- **Egg protein** – **reference protein** (biological value ~100). **Complete protein** – all essential amino acids (animal sources, soya). Cereal + pulse combination → complementary.
- **Essential amino acids** – 9 (body can't make). **Essential fatty acids** – linoleic, linolenic.
- **Calorie requirement (ICMR)**: sedentary man ~**2100–2300 kcal**, moderate ~2700–2800, heavy ~3500+; woman sedentary ~1700–1900; **pregnancy +350 kcal (2nd/3rd trimester)**, **lactation +500–600 kcal**.
- **BMR** – minimum energy for vital functions at rest (~1 kcal/kg/hr).

### Vitamins
| Vitamin | Deficiency | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| **A (Retinol)** | **Night blindness** (first), **Bitot's spots**, xerophthalmia, keratomalacia | Carrot, green leafy veg, liver, fish liver oil, mango, papaya |
| **D (Calciferol)** | **Rickets (children)**, **osteomalacia (adults)** | **Sunlight**, fish liver oil, egg yolk |
| **E (Tocopherol)** | Haemolytic anaemia, infertility (rats); antioxidant | Vegetable oils, nuts |
| **K (Phylloquinone)** | **Bleeding** (↓ prothrombin), haemorrhagic disease of newborn | Green leafy veg, **gut bacteria**; newborn – **1 mg IM at birth** |
| **B1 (Thiamine)** | **Beriberi** (wet – heart; dry – neuritis), **Wernicke-Korsakoff** (alcoholics) | Whole cereals, pulses (polishing rice removes) |
| **B2 (Riboflavin)** | **Angular stomatitis, cheilosis, glossitis (magenta tongue)** | Milk, eggs, green veg |
| **B3 (Niacin)** | **Pellagra – 3 D's: Dermatitis, Diarrhoea, Dementia** (+ Death) – **maize/ jowar eaters** | Meat, groundnut |
| **B6 (Pyridoxine)** | Peripheral neuritis (**INH therapy**), convulsions | Cereals, meat |
| **B9 (Folic acid)** | **Megaloblastic anaemia**, **neural tube defects** (pregnancy) | Green leafy veg, liver |
| **B12 (Cyanocobalamin)** | **Pernicious/ megaloblastic anaemia + neurological (subacute combined degeneration)** – vegans | **Only animal foods** – meat, milk, egg |
| **C (Ascorbic acid)** | **Scurvy** – **bleeding/ spongy gums**, delayed wound healing, perifollicular haemorrhage | **Amla (richest)**, guava, citrus, tomato; **destroyed by heat** |

- **Fat-soluble**: **A, D, E, K** (stored – toxicity possible). **Water-soluble**: B complex, C (excreted – daily needed).

### Minerals
| Mineral | Deficiency | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Iron** | **Microcytic hypochromic anaemia** (most common nutritional deficiency) | Haem iron (meat) better absorbed; **Vit C ↑ absorption**; tea/ coffee, calcium, phytates ↓ |
| **Calcium** | Rickets, osteoporosis, tetany | Milk (richest), ragi |
| **Iodine** | **Goitre**, cretinism | **Iodized salt (≥ 15 ppm at consumer)** |
| **Zinc** | Growth retardation, poor wound healing, diarrhoea (give zinc with ORS) | |
| **Fluoride** | Dental caries (deficiency); **fluorosis** (excess – > 1.5 ppm in water) | |
| **Sodium** | Hyponatraemia | Salt < 5 g/day (WHO) |
| **Potassium** | Hypokalaemia | Banana, orange, coconut water, potato |

### Malnutrition (PEM)
- **Kwashiorkor** – **protein deficiency** (calories adequate) – **oedema**, flag sign (hair), flaky-paint dermatosis, moon face, fatty liver, apathy; "**the disease of the displaced child**" (Cicely Williams).
- **Marasmus** – **protein + calorie deficiency** – **severe wasting (skin & bones), no oedema**, "**old man face**", alert & hungry.
- **SAM (Severe Acute Malnutrition)** – **WHZ < −3 SD** or **MUAC < 11.5 cm** or bilateral pitting oedema → **NRC (Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre)**; F-75 (stabilization) → F-100 (rehabilitation).

## 11.2 Therapeutic Diets

| Diet | Description | Indication |
|---|---|---|

| **Full liquid** | Clear + **milk, custard, ice cream, strained cream soups, yogurt** | Transition, chewing/ swallowing difficulty |
| **Soft diet** | Easily chewed & digested, low fibre – khichdi, mashed potato, eggs, dal | Post-op progression, GI disorders, elderly |

| **Bland diet** | Non-irritating – no spices, caffeine, alcohol, fried | Peptic ulcer (traditional), gastritis |

## 11.3 Feeding the Helpless Patient

- **Position – high Fowler's / sitting upright 90°**, head slightly flexed (**chin tuck**) – aspiration prevention; remain upright **30–60 min after** meal.
- Oral care before meals (appetite ↑), hand hygiene, clean pleasant environment, remove bedpan/ unpleasant sights.
- **Small bites**, food on **unaffected side** of mouth (stroke), allow time to chew & swallow, check for **pocketing**, alternate solids & liquids, **thickened liquids** for dysphagia; **never rush**; sit at eye level.
- **Visually impaired** – describe food position using **clock method** (e.g. rice at 12 o'clock, dal at 3).
- **Dysphagia screening** before first oral intake (stroke) – water swallow test; signs of aspiration – **coughing, choking, wet/ gurgling voice, drooling, ↓ SpO₂**.
- Record intake (% of meal), I/O.

## 11.4 Enteral Feeding (Tube feeding)

- **Indications**: GI tract working but can't eat – **unconscious, dysphagia (stroke), head & neck surgery/ cancer, oesophageal obstruction, critical illness, anorexia nervosa, burns**. "**If the gut works, use it**" – enteral preferred over parenteral (maintains gut mucosa, ↓ infection, cheaper).
- **Routes**: **Nasogastric (NG/ Ryle's)** – short-term (< 4–6 weeks); **Nasoduodenal/ nasojejunal** – aspiration risk/ gastroparesis; **Gastrostomy (PEG)/ Jejunostomy (PEJ)** – long-term (> 4–6 weeks); orogastric (neonates, basilar skull fracture).

:::key NG (Ryle's) tube insertion – key points
- **Tube size**: adult **14–18 Fr** (feeding 8–12 Fr fine-bore), child 8–10 Fr; Ryle's tube length ~**105 cm**; markings at **40, 50, 60, 70 cm**.
- **Measure – NEX method**: **N**ose tip → **E**arlobe → **X**iphoid process (+ ~**5–10 cm** for stomach); mark with tape. Adult usual **~50–55 cm** (45–55).
- Position – **high Fowler's (sitting upright)**, **head tilted back for insertion through nostril**, then **flex head forward (chin to chest)** when tube reaches nasopharynx (closes trachea, opens oesophagus).
- **Lubricate with water-soluble jelly (lignocaine/ KY)** – **never oil-based (lipid aspiration pneumonia)**.
- Ask patient to **swallow (sips of water if allowed)** as tube advances.
- **Coughing, choking, cyanosis, inability to speak** → tube in trachea → **withdraw immediately**.
- **Confirm placement**: **X-ray – gold standard (most accurate, before first feed)**; bedside – **aspirate gastric content: pH ≤ 5 (1–5.5)**, appearance (grassy green/ clear/ brown); auscultation of air (**whoosh test**) – **not reliable**, not recommended alone; **check placement before every feed/ medication & every 4–8 hrs**.
- Secure with tape to nose (no pressure on nares), mark exit point.
:::

### Feeding procedure
- **Check placement + gastric residual volume (GRV)** before intermittent feed (or 4-hrly in continuous). **GRV > 250–500 ml** (policy) → hold & reassess; **return aspirate** (electrolytes) unless excessive/ abnormal.
- Position – **semi-Fowler's/ HOB 30–45° during feed and for 30–60 min after** (aspiration prevention – **most important**).
- **Flush with 30 ml water (15–30 ml)** before & after each feed & medication, **between medicines**, every 4 hrs in continuous feed (tube patency).
- Feed at **room temperature**, by **gravity** – syringe barrel held **~15–30 cm (6–12 inch) above stomach/ insertion point**; **slowly over 15–30 min** (~200–400 ml per bolus); **never force with plunger**.
- **Medicines**: liquid form preferred; crush tablets individually, dissolve in water; **don't crush enteric-coated/ sustained-release/ sublingual**; don't mix with feed; flush between.
- **Continuous feeding** – pump; open system hang time **≤ 4–8 hrs**; change bag & tubing **every 24 hrs**.
- Oral & nasal care every 4 hrs; lip moisturizer; nostril care.
- **Complications**: **aspiration (most serious)**, **diarrhoea (most common** – hyperosmolar feed, rapid rate, contamination, antibiotics), tube displacement/ blockage (flush with **warm water**; **don't use cola/ juices routinely**), nausea/ vomiting, distension, **dumping**, refeeding syndrome (↓ PO₄, K, Mg), sinusitis, nasal erosion, dehydration/ hyperglycaemia.
- **Removal**: flush with air/ water, **ask patient to hold breath** (closes epiglottis), withdraw quickly & steadily.
- **Gastrostomy (PEG)** care: clean site daily with NS, dry, rotate external bumper, check for leakage/ infection; feed same as NG.

## 11.5 Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)

- **Total Parenteral Nutrition** – IV nutrition – **dextrose (high concentration 20–50 %), amino acids, lipids (fat emulsion), electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements** – when **GI tract non-functional** (bowel obstruction, short bowel, severe pancreatitis, fistula, prolonged ileus).
- **Central line** (subclavian/ PICC) for TPN – **hypertonic (> 900 mOsm/L)**; **PPN (peripheral)** – < 900 mOsm, short-term.
- **Nursing**: **strict asepsis** (sepsis risk), **dedicated lumen – no other drugs/ blood through TPN line**, **infusion pump**, **don't speed up to "catch up"** (hyperglycaemia), **change bag & tubing every 24 hrs** (lipids – 12 hrs), **filter (1.2 µm for lipids, 0.22 µm for non-lipid)**, refrigerate bags (take out **30–60 min before** use), **monitor blood sugar every 4–6 hrs** (hyperglycaemia – most common metabolic complication), daily weight, I/O, electrolytes.
- **Never stop TPN abruptly** → **rebound hypoglycaemia** → if bag finishes & new not available, hang **10 % dextrose (D10W)** at same rate; **taper off** gradually.
- Complications: **infection/ sepsis (catheter-related)**, **hyperglycaemia**, hypoglycaemia (abrupt stop), **air embolism** (left side, head down), pneumothorax (insertion), fluid overload, refeeding syndrome, liver dysfunction.
- **Lipid emulsion (Intralipid)** – **check egg/ soy allergy**; milky; don't use if oil separates/ cracked.

## 11.6 Intake & Output (I/O) Chart

- **Intake**: oral fluids, IV fluids, tube feeds, **ice chips (½ volume)**, foods liquid at room temp (**ice cream, jelly, soup**), irrigations not removed.
- **Output**: urine, vomitus, diarrhoea, drainage (wound, chest tube, NG suction), blood loss, **insensible loss** (~800–900 ml/day – estimated, not measured).
- Record every shift, 24-hr total; **normal urine ≥ 30 ml/hr (0.5 ml/kg/hr)**. 1 kg weight = 1 L fluid.
- Measure urine in **graduated container at eye level**; weigh diapers (**1 g = 1 ml**).

- Richest source of Vit C – **amla**; Vit A first sign – **night blindness**; pellagra – **3 D's (niacin)**.
