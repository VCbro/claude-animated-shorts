# Nursing Process, Critical Thinking & Priority Setting
<!-- desc: ADPIE in depth, data types, NANDA diagnosis & PES, Maslow & ABC priority, goals, care plan, critical thinking -->

## 4.1 Nursing Process – Overview

@fig nursing_process_cycle.svg | Nursing process ke 5 steps – cyclic, patient-centred

:::key Basic facts
- **Definition**: Systematic, rational, **problem-solving method** of planning & giving individualized nursing care.
- Term "nursing process" first used – **Lydia Hall (1955)**. **Yura & Walsh (1967)** – first 4 steps (APIE). Nursing diagnosis step added – **NANDA (1973; NANDA-I 2002)**.
- 5 steps: **A**ssessment → **D**iagnosis → **P**lanning (outcome identification + planning) → **I**mplementation → **E**valuation. (ANA – 6 steps: Assessment, Diagnosis, **Outcome identification**, Planning, Implementation, Evaluation.)
- Characteristics: **cyclic, dynamic, systematic, goal-directed, client-centred, interpersonal, universally applicable**, uses critical thinking.
:::

## 4.2 Step 1 – Assessment

- **Purpose**: complete **database** banana. **First & most important step**; continuous throughout care.
- **Types of assessment**:
 - **Initial/ comprehensive** – on admission (within 24 hrs as per policy)
 - **Focused/ problem-oriented** – specific problem (e.g. pain)
 - **Emergency** – life-threatening (ABC)
 - **Time-lapsed/ ongoing** – later comparison
- **Types of data**:

| Subjective (Symptoms) | Objective (Signs) |
|---|---|
| Patient **khud batata** hai – feelings, perceptions | Nurse **observe/ measure** karti hai |
| Pain, nausea, anxiety, itching, dizziness | BP, pulse, temp, wound redness, vomiting, lab values, weight |
| Quote in patient's words | Measurable, verifiable |
| "Covert" data | "Overt" data |

- **Sources**: **Primary – patient (best/ most reliable)**; **Secondary** – family, other health team, records, lab reports, literature.
- **Methods**: **Interview (nursing history)**, **Observation**, **Physical examination (IPPA)**.
- **Validation** – data verify karna (double-check), esp. if inconsistent.
- **Data organization frameworks**: Gordon's **11 functional health patterns**, Maslow, body systems, Henderson's 14 needs.
- **Cues** (data) → **Inferences** (interpretation).

## 4.3 Step 2 – Nursing Diagnosis

:::def NANDA-I definition
"A **clinical judgment** concerning a human **response** to health conditions/ life processes, or vulnerability to that response, by an individual, family, group or community." Nursing diagnosis **nurse independently treat** kar sakti hai.
:::

| Type | Format | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Actual (problem-focused)** | **3 parts – PES**: Problem + **related to** (Etiology) + **as evidenced by** (Signs/symptoms) | Ineffective airway clearance **r/t** thick secretions **AEB** crackles & ineffective cough |
| **Risk** | **2 parts – P + risk factors** ("**related to**/ as evidenced by risk factors") – **no S** | Risk for infection r/t surgical incision |
| **Health promotion** | 1 or 2 parts; "**Readiness for enhanced** …" | Readiness for enhanced nutrition |
| **Syndrome** | 1 part – cluster | Disuse syndrome, Rape-trauma syndrome |
| **Possible** (older) | Insufficient data | Possible social isolation |

- **PES** = **Problem (diagnostic label)** + **Etiology** + **Signs & Symptoms (defining characteristics)**.
- **Components**: **Label** (name), **Definition**, **Defining characteristics**, **Related/ risk factors**.
- **NANDA-I** – current taxonomy II: **13 domains**, 47 classes, **267 diagnoses (2021–23 edition)**, 277 (2024–26 edition); NIC (Nursing Interventions Classification), NOC (Outcomes Classification).

### Nursing vs Medical diagnosis vs Collaborative problem
| Nursing diagnosis | Medical diagnosis | Collaborative problem |
|---|---|---|
| **Patient's response** to illness | **Disease/ pathology** | Potential complication (PC) needing doctor + nurse |
| Changes as response changes | Remains same till cured | e.g. "PC: Hypoglycaemia" |
| Nurse treats independently | Doctor treats | Nurse monitors + both treat |
| e.g. **Acute pain**, Impaired mobility | e.g. **Appendicitis**, Fracture femur | |

:::warn Common errors in writing nursing diagnosis
- Medical diagnosis ko etiology bana dena ✘ ("Acute pain r/t appendicitis") → ✔ "r/t inflammation & surgical incision"
- Problem & etiology same likhna ✘; legally inadvisable statements ✘ ("r/t nurse's negligence")
- Nursing intervention/ equipment ko diagnosis likhna ✘ ("Needs suctioning")
- Value judgements ✘
:::

## 4.4 Step 3 – Planning

- **Priority setting** → **Goals/ outcomes** → **Interventions** → **Care plan documentation**.

### Priority setting
@fig maslow_pyramid.svg | Maslow ka pyramid – neeche wali need pehle

- **High priority** – life-threatening (airway, breathing, circulation, severe bleeding, shock). **Medium** – non-life threatening but health threat (acute pain, unsafe mobility). **Low** – developmental/ future needs (teaching about diet).
- Priority rules: **ABC → Maslow → Acute before chronic → Actual before risk (usually) → Safety → Patient's own priority** (if not conflicting). **Assessment before intervention** (unless emergency).
- **Kalish** – modified Maslow (adds **stimulation needs** – sex, activity, exploration, novelty – between physiological & safety).

### Goals & expected outcomes
- **Long-term** (weeks/ months) vs **Short-term** (hours/ days).
- **SMART**: **S**pecific, **M**easurable, **A**chievable/ attainable, **R**ealistic/ relevant, **T**ime-bound.
- Components of outcome statement: **Subject** (patient) + **Verb (measurable)** + **Condition** + **Criteria (performance)** + **Time**. e.g. "**Patient will walk** with walker **50 m** in corridor **by 3rd post-op day**."
- **Measurable verbs** ✔: walk, list, state, demonstrate, identify, drink, verbalize. **Non-measurable** ✘: know, understand, learn, appreciate, think.

### Nursing interventions & care plan
- **Independent** (nurse-initiated) – turning, teaching, positioning, hygiene. **Dependent** (physician-initiated) – medications, IV. **Collaborative/ interdependent** – with physiotherapist, dietician.
- **Rationale** – scientific reason for each intervention.
- **Nursing care plan columns**: Assessment – Nursing diagnosis – Goal/ Objectives – Interventions – Rationale – Evaluation.
- Types: **Standardized** (protocols), **Individualized**, **Concept map**, Critical pathway (clinical pathway – interdisciplinary, timeline).

## 4.5 Step 4 – Implementation

- **Action phase** – plan ko execute karna; **reassess patient before implementing** (status change ho sakta hai).
- Skills needed: **cognitive** (intellectual), **interpersonal**, **technical (psychomotor)**.
- **Delegation** (5 rights): **Right task, Right circumstance, Right person, Right direction/ communication, Right supervision/ evaluation**. **Assessment, teaching, evaluation & unstable patients – delegate nahi** kar sakte (to ANM/ assistant).
- **Documentation** after implementation.

## 4.6 Step 5 – Evaluation

- Compare patient's **response with expected outcomes** → **Goal met / partially met / not met**.
- Not met → **reassess, revise plan** (diagnosis/ goals/ interventions). Continue/ modify/ terminate plan.
- Types: **Structure** (setting/ resources), **Process** (nurse's performance), **Outcome** (patient's change – most important), **Concurrent (formative)** & **Retrospective (summative)**.
- **Quality assurance (QA)** – nursing audit (Phaneuf's audit – 7 functions).

## 4.7 Critical Thinking & Problem Solving

- **Critical thinking** – purposeful, reflective reasoning for clinical judgment. Skills: **interpretation, analysis, inference, evaluation, explanation, self-regulation** (Facione).
- **Attitudes**: confidence, independence, fairness, responsibility, risk-taking, discipline, perseverance, creativity, curiosity, integrity, humility.
- **Problem-solving method (scientific)**: Identify problem → collect data → hypothesis → plan → implement → evaluate. **Trial & error** – least effective; **Intuition** – expert nurses (Benner).
- **Decision making** – choosing best alternative.
- **Evidence-based practice (EBP)** – best research evidence + clinical expertise + patient preferences. **PICO** – Population, Intervention, Comparison, Outcome.

:::tip Priority questions – step-by-step approach
1. **Unstable vs stable** – unstable pehle (e.g. post-op with falling BP > diabetic asking for breakfast)
2. **ABC** – airway sabse pehle (stridor > chest pain > fracture)
3. **Acute > chronic**, **actual > potential**, **safety**
4. Agar option me "**assess**" hai aur situation emergency nahi – **assessment pehle**
5. Delegation question me – **stable, routine, predictable** task hi delegate
:::

:::exam PYQ style
- Nursing process ka first step – **Assessment**; last – **Evaluation**.
- "Patient states pain 8/10" – **subjective data**; "BP 150/90" – **objective**.
- Primary source of data – **patient**.
- NANDA ka full form – **North American Nursing Diagnosis Association** (2002 se NANDA-International).
- Nursing diagnosis me "**related to**" = **etiology**; "**as evidenced by**" = **signs/symptoms**.
- Risk diagnosis has – **2 parts** (no S).
- Goal statement me measurable verb – **demonstrate/ list/ walk** ("understand" nahi).
- Maslow ka highest need – **Self-actualization**; lowest – **Physiological**.
:::
