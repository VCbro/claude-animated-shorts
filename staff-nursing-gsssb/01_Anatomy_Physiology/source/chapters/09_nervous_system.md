## 9.1 Organisation & Cells

- **CNS** – brain + spinal cord; **PNS** – **12 pairs cranial + 31 pairs spinal nerves**; PNS → **somatic (voluntary)** & **autonomic (involuntary – sympathetic & parasympathetic)**; **afferent (sensory)** & **efferent (motor)**.
- **Neuron – structural & functional unit**; parts – **cell body (soma, Nissl bodies)**, **dendrites (carry impulse towards cell body)**, **axon (away from cell body; axon hillock – impulse origin)**, **myelin sheath**, **nodes of Ranvier**, axon terminals; **neurons do not divide (no centrioles)** – poor regeneration in CNS.
- **Types** – **multipolar (motor – most common)**, **bipolar (retina, olfactory, inner ear)**, **pseudo-unipolar (sensory – dorsal root ganglion)**.
- **Neuroglia** (supporting – more numerous than neurons): **astrocytes (most abundant – blood-brain barrier)**, **oligodendrocytes (myelin in CNS)**, **microglia (phagocytes – macrophages of CNS)**, **ependymal cells (line ventricles – CSF)**; PNS – **Schwann cells (myelin in PNS – aid regeneration)**, satellite cells.
- **Grey matter** – cell bodies (cortex outside in brain; **H-shaped inside in spinal cord**); **white matter** – myelinated axons.

### Nerve impulse
- **Resting membrane potential ≈ −70 mV** (Na⁺-K⁺ pump, K⁺ leak); **depolarisation – Na⁺ influx** (to ~+30 mV); **repolarisation – K⁺ efflux**; hyperpolarisation; **threshold ≈ −55 mV**; **all-or-none law**; **refractory period**.
- **Saltatory conduction** – impulse jumps node to node in myelinated fibres (**faster – up to ~120 m/s**); demyelination (multiple sclerosis, GBS) slows conduction.
- **Synapse** – presynaptic terminal releases **neurotransmitter (Ca²⁺-dependent exocytosis)** → synaptic cleft → postsynaptic receptors; **one-way conduction**; **synaptic delay ~0.5 ms**.

| Neurotransmitter | Key role / disorder |
|---|---|
| **Acetylcholine** | NMJ, parasympathetic, memory (**↓ in Alzheimer's**); myasthenia (receptor antibodies) |
| **Dopamine** | Movement (**↓ in Parkinson's – substantia nigra**), reward; **↑ in schizophrenia** |
| **Noradrenaline** | Sympathetic, mood (↓ depression) |
| **Serotonin (5-HT)** | Mood, sleep, appetite (**↓ depression**) |
| **GABA** | **Main inhibitory (brain)** – BZD act here; ↓ in seizures, anxiety |
| **Glycine** | Inhibitory (spinal cord) – **tetanus toxin & strychnine block** |
| **Glutamate** | **Main excitatory** |
| **Endorphins/ enkephalins** | Natural analgesics |

## 9.2 Brain

- **Weight ≈ 1.3–1.4 kg (~2 % body weight)**; uses **~20 % of body O₂ & ~15 % of cardiac output (~750 ml/min)**; **glucose – main fuel**; **irreversible damage after ~4–6 min of anoxia**.
- **Parts** – **forebrain (cerebrum + diencephalon)**, **midbrain**, **hindbrain (pons, medulla, cerebellum)**; **brainstem = midbrain + pons + medulla**.

### Cerebrum (largest part)
- **2 hemispheres** joined by **corpus callosum (largest commissure)**; **gyri & sulci**; **central sulcus (of Rolando)** separates frontal & parietal; **lateral sulcus (Sylvian fissure)** separates temporal; **longitudinal fissure** between hemispheres; **left hemisphere dominant in ~95 % (language)**.

| Lobe | Functions |
|---|---|
| **Frontal (largest)** | **Primary motor cortex (precentral gyrus – motor homunculus)**, **Broca's area (motor speech – left inferior frontal; lesion → expressive/ non-fluent aphasia)**, personality, judgment, intellect, planning, emotions |
| **Parietal** | **Primary somatosensory cortex (postcentral gyrus)** – touch, pain, temperature, proprioception; spatial orientation |
| **Temporal** | **Auditory cortex (hearing)**, **Wernicke's area (understanding speech – lesion → receptive/ fluent aphasia)**, **memory (hippocampus)**, smell |
| **Occipital (smallest)** | **Visual cortex** – vision |
| **Limbic system** | **Emotions, behaviour, memory** – hippocampus (memory), amygdala (fear, aggression) |
| **Insula** | Taste, visceral sensation |

- **Basal ganglia/ nuclei** – caudate, putamen, globus pallidus, substantia nigra – **control of movement** (Parkinson's, Huntington's chorea).
- **Motor pathways cross (decussate) – right brain controls left body** (pyramidal decussation in medulla).

### Diencephalon
- **Thalamus** – **relay centre for all sensations (except smell)**.
- **Hypothalamus** – **"master of autonomic & endocrine control"** – **temperature regulation (thermostat)**, **hunger & satiety**, **thirst & water balance (ADH, oxytocin synthesised in supraoptic & paraventricular nuclei)**, sleep–wake (circadian – suprachiasmatic nucleus), emotions, controls **pituitary (releasing hormones)**, sexual behaviour.
- **Epithalamus** – **pineal gland (melatonin – sleep; calcified – landmark on X-ray)**.

### Brainstem & cerebellum
- **Midbrain** – visual & auditory reflexes (superior & inferior colliculi), **substantia nigra (dopamine)**, cerebral aqueduct, CN III & IV nuclei.
- **Pons** – bridge; **pneumotaxic & apneustic respiratory centres**, CN V–VIII nuclei; pontine micturition centre.
- **Medulla oblongata** – **vital centres – cardiac, respiratory, vasomotor**; reflex centres – **vomiting, coughing, sneezing, swallowing**; **pyramidal decussation**; CN IX–XII; **coning (tonsillar herniation through foramen magnum) → death**.
- **Reticular activating system (RAS)** – brainstem – **consciousness, wakefulness, arousal**.
- **Cerebellum ("little brain", 2nd largest)** – **balance (equilibrium), posture, coordination of voluntary movements, muscle tone**; lesion → **ataxia, intention tremor, nystagmus, dysdiadochokinesia** (same side); alcohol affects.

## 9.3 Protection – Meninges, CSF & BBB

- **Meninges (outer → inner)** – **Dura mater (tough, double layer – dural venous sinuses)**, **arachnoid mater**, **pia mater (adherent to brain)** – "**DAP – Dura, Arachnoid, Pia**".
- **Spaces** – **epidural (extradural – middle meningeal artery bleed → extradural haematoma, lucid interval; epidural anaesthesia in spine)**, **subdural (bridging veins → subdural haematoma – elderly, alcoholics)**, **subarachnoid (CSF – subarachnoid haemorrhage – berry aneurysm, "worst headache of life"; spinal anaesthesia & LP)**.
- **Ventricles** – **2 lateral → (foramen of Monro) → 3rd → (cerebral aqueduct of Sylvius – commonest site of obstruction → hydrocephalus) → 4th → (foramina of Magendie & Luschka) → subarachnoid space**.
- **CSF** – **formed by choroid plexus (mainly lateral ventricles)**; **~500 ml/day; total volume ~120–150 ml**; **absorbed by arachnoid villi/ granulations into dural venous sinuses (superior sagittal)**; **clear, colourless; pressure 60–180 mmH₂O (lying)**; **protein 15–45 mg/dl, glucose 50–80 mg/dl (~⅔ of blood glucose)**, cells 0–5 lymphocytes/µl; functions – cushion/ shock absorber, buoyancy, nutrition, waste removal.
- **CSF in meningitis** – **bacterial: turbid, ↑ neutrophils, ↑ protein, ↓↓ glucose**; **viral: clear, lymphocytes, normal glucose**; **TB: cobweb clot, lymphocytes, ↓ glucose, ↑↑ protein**.
- **Blood-brain barrier** – capillary endothelium tight junctions + astrocytes – blocks most drugs, toxins; lipid-soluble substances cross; immature in neonates (**kernicterus**).
- **Intracranial pressure (ICP)** – **normal 5–15 mmHg** (0–10 in some texts); **Monro-Kellie doctrine** (brain + blood + CSF = constant); **Cushing's triad – hypertension (widened pulse pressure), bradycardia, irregular respiration** – late sign of ↑ ICP.

## 9.4 Spinal Cord & Reflexes

- **Spinal cord** – from **foramen magnum (medulla) to L1–L2 in adults (conus medullaris)**; **L3 in newborn**; **~45 cm (male), 42–43 cm (female)**; **cervical & lumbar enlargements** (limb plexuses); **cauda equina** – nerve roots below L1/2; **filum terminale**.
- **Lumbar puncture at L3–L4 or L4–L5** – below cord (safe); position – **lateral (fetal) or sitting, flexed**; post-LP – lie flat (per protocol), fluids.
- **Cross-section** – **H-shaped grey matter** – **anterior (ventral) horn – motor**, **posterior (dorsal) horn – sensory**, lateral horn (T1–L2 – sympathetic); **dorsal root ganglion – sensory cell bodies**; **dorsal root – sensory, ventral root – motor** ("**DAVE** – Dorsal Afferent, Ventral Efferent"); **spinal nerve – mixed**.

| Tract | Carries |
|---|---|
| **Dorsal column (fasciculus gracilis & cuneatus)** | **Fine touch, vibration, proprioception (position sense)** – cross in medulla |
| **Lateral spinothalamic** | **Pain & temperature** – cross in spinal cord (1–2 segments up) |
| **Anterior spinothalamic** | Crude touch, pressure |
| **Spinocerebellar** | Unconscious proprioception |
| **Corticospinal (pyramidal) – lateral** | **Voluntary motor** – crosses in medulla; **UMN lesion → spasticity, hyperreflexia, Babinski +ve**; **LMN lesion → flaccidity, hyporeflexia, atrophy, fasciculations** |

- **Reflex arc** – **receptor → sensory (afferent) neuron → integration centre (spinal cord) → motor (efferent) neuron → effector**; **knee jerk (patellar) – monosynaptic stretch reflex – L2–L4 (mainly L3, L4)**; **ankle jerk – S1–S2**; **biceps – C5–C6**; **triceps – C7**; **supinator – C5–C6**; withdrawal reflex – polysynaptic.
- **Babinski sign (extensor plantar)** – **UMN lesion**; **normal up to ~1–2 yrs** (immature pyramidal tract).
- **Grading reflexes** – 0 absent, 1+ diminished, **2+ normal**, 3+ brisk, 4+ clonus.

## 9.5 Cranial Nerves (12 pairs)

| No. | Name | Type | Function / test |
|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | **Olfactory** | S | **Smell** (test each nostril – coffee, soap) |
| **II** | **Optic** | S | **Vision** – acuity (Snellen), fields, fundus |
| **III** | **Oculomotor** | M | **Most eye movements, eyelid elevation, pupil constriction (parasympathetic), accommodation**; palsy → **ptosis, eye "down & out", dilated pupil** |
| **IV** | **Trochlear (smallest, thinnest, longest intracranial course)** | M | **Superior oblique – eye down & in** |
| **V** | **Trigeminal (largest)** | B | **Facial sensation (3 divisions – ophthalmic, maxillary, mandibular)**, **muscles of mastication**, **corneal reflex (afferent)**; **trigeminal neuralgia** |
| **VI** | **Abducens** | M | **Lateral rectus – abduction**; palsy → convergent squint (↑ ICP – long course over petrous apex → **false localising sign**) |
| **VII** | **Facial** | B | **Facial expression**, **taste anterior ⅔ tongue**, lacrimal & salivary (submandibular, sublingual) secretion, corneal reflex (efferent – eye closure); **Bell's palsy** |
| **VIII** | **Vestibulocochlear (auditory)** | S | **Hearing (Rinne, Weber) & balance** |
| **IX** | **Glossopharyngeal** | B | **Taste posterior ⅓**, **gag reflex (afferent)**, parotid secretion, swallowing; carotid sinus |
| **X** | **Vagus (longest, most widely distributed)** | B | **Parasympathetic to heart, lungs, GI tract (up to ⅔ transverse colon)**, swallowing, **voice (recurrent laryngeal)**, **gag reflex (efferent)**; "say ah" – uvula deviation |
| **XI** | **Accessory (spinal accessory)** | M | **Sternocleidomastoid & trapezius – shoulder shrug, head turning** |
| **XII** | **Hypoglossal** | M | **Tongue movements** – lesion → tongue deviates to **same side** |

- **Parasympathetic fibres** in **III, VII, IX, X** ("**1973**" → 10, 9, 7, 3).
- **Mnemonic names** – "**Oh Oh Oh To Touch And Feel Very Good Velvet, Ahh Heaven**"; type – "**Some Say Marry Money, But My Brother Says Big Brains Matter More**" (S = sensory, M = motor, B = both).

## 9.6 Spinal Nerves & Plexuses

- **31 pairs** – **cervical 8, thoracic 12, lumbar 5, sacral 5, coccygeal 1** (**C8 exists though only 7 cervical vertebrae**).
- **Dermatome** – skin area supplied by one spinal nerve (**C6 – thumb, T4 – nipple, T10 – umbilicus, L1 – inguinal, L4 – knee/ medial leg, S1 – lateral foot/ little toe**); herpes zoster follows dermatomes.

| Plexus | Roots | Key nerves |
|---|---|---|
| **Cervical** | C1–C4 | **Phrenic (C3–C5 – diaphragm)** |
| **Brachial** | **C5–T1** | **Musculocutaneous, axillary (deltoid – surgical neck humerus #), radial (wrist drop – crutch palsy, shaft of humerus #), median (carpal tunnel syndrome, "ape hand"; "labourer's nerve"), ulnar (claw hand; "funny bone" – medial epicondyle; "musician's nerve")**; **Erb's palsy (C5–C6 – "waiter's tip")**, **Klumpke's (C8–T1 – claw hand)** – birth injuries |
| **Lumbar** | L1–L4 | **Femoral (quadriceps – knee extension)**, obturator (adductors) |
| **Sacral** | L4–S4 | **Sciatic (L4–S3) – largest & longest nerve in body** → tibial & **common peroneal (foot drop – fibular neck)**; **pudendal (S2–S4 – external sphincters, perineum; pudendal block)** |

## 9.7 Autonomic Nervous System

| Feature | **Sympathetic ("fight or flight")** | **Parasympathetic ("rest & digest")** |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | **Thoracolumbar (T1–L2)** | **Craniosacral (CN III, VII, IX, X + S2–S4)** |
| Ganglia | Near spinal cord (sympathetic chain) – **short preganglionic, long postganglionic** | Near/ in organ – **long preganglionic, short postganglionic** |
| Neurotransmitter (post-ganglionic) | **Noradrenaline** (except sweat glands – ACh) | **Acetylcholine** |
| Pupil | **Dilation (mydriasis)** | **Constriction (miosis)** |
| Heart | **↑ rate & force** | **↓ rate** (vagus) |
| Bronchi | **Dilation** | Constriction, ↑ secretion |
| GI | **↓ motility & secretion**, sphincters contract | **↑ motility & secretion** |
| Bladder | Detrusor relaxes, sphincter contracts (**retention**) | **Detrusor contracts (micturition)** |
| Blood glucose | **↑ (glycogenolysis)** | – |
| Salivary | Thick, scanty | **Profuse watery** |
| Sex | **Ejaculation** | **Erection** ("**P**oint & **S**hoot") |
| Adrenal medulla | **Adrenaline release** (modified sympathetic ganglion) | – |

- **Preganglionic neurotransmitter – ACh in both (nicotinic receptors)**.

## 9.8 Consciousness, Sleep & GCS

- **Sleep stages** – **NREM (stages N1, N2, N3 – deep/ slow-wave sleep: GH release, night terrors, sleepwalking, enuresis)** and **REM (dreaming, rapid eye movements, muscle atonia, nightmares; ~20–25 % in adults; ~50 % in newborn)**; cycle **~90 min**; newborn sleeps **16–18 hrs/day**, adult 7–8 hrs.
- **EEG waves** – **alpha (awake relaxed, eyes closed)**, beta (alert), theta (children/ drowsy), **delta (deep sleep)**.
- **Glasgow Coma Scale (Teasdale & Jennett, 1974)** – **Eye opening (4) + Verbal (5) + Motor (6) = 3–15**; **≤ 8 – coma/ severe head injury (intubate – "8 intubate")**; **9–12 moderate, 13–15 mild**; motor response best predictor.
- **Pupils** – normal **2–5 mm (PERRLA)**; **unilateral fixed dilated pupil – ipsilateral CN III compression (uncal herniation)**; **pin-point – pontine haemorrhage, opioids**.
- **Decorticate (flexor) posture** – lesion above midbrain; **decerebrate (extensor) posture** – brainstem/ midbrain lesion (worse prognosis).
