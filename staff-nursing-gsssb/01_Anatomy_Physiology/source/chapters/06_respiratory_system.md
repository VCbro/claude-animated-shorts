## 6.1 Anatomy of Respiratory Tract

- **Upper tract** – nose, nasal cavity, paranasal sinuses, pharynx, larynx; **lower tract** – trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli (lungs).
- **Conducting zone** (nose → terminal bronchioles – **anatomical dead space ≈ 150 ml**); **respiratory zone** (respiratory bronchioles → alveolar ducts → **alveoli – gas exchange**).
- **Nose** – warms, humidifies, filters air (hairs, mucus, cilia); **nasal septum** (cartilage + vomer + ethmoid); **Little's area (Kiesselbach's plexus) – anterior septum – commonest site of epistaxis**; **olfactory epithelium** – roof.
- **Paranasal sinuses** – **maxillary (largest; commonest sinusitis; present at birth)**, frontal, ethmoid, sphenoid; lighten skull, voice resonance.
- **Pharynx** (~12–14 cm) – **nasopharynx (adenoids, opening of Eustachian tube)**, **oropharynx (palatine tonsils)**, **laryngopharynx** – **common passage for air & food**.
- **Larynx (voice box)** – **C3–C6**; **9 cartilages** – **thyroid (largest – Adam's apple)**, **cricoid (only complete ring – narrowest part of airway in children < 8 yrs)**, **epiglottis (elastic – closes larynx during swallowing)**, paired arytenoid, corniculate, cuneiform; **vocal cords**; **glottis – narrowest part in adults**; **nerve – recurrent laryngeal (injury in thyroidectomy → hoarseness)**.
- **Trachea (windpipe)** – **~10–12 cm, from C6 to T4/T5 (sternal angle)**; **16–20 C-shaped hyaline cartilage rings** (open posteriorly – oesophagus expands); **carina – bifurcation (most sensitive area – cough reflex)**; lined by **pseudostratified ciliated columnar epithelium with goblet cells – mucociliary escalator**.
- **Bronchi** – **right main bronchus – shorter, wider, more vertical → foreign bodies & aspiration commonly enter right side (right lower lobe)**; bronchi → bronchioles (no cartilage, smooth muscle – bronchospasm) → terminal → respiratory bronchioles.

## 6.2 Lungs & Pleura

| Feature | Right lung | Left lung |
|---|---|---|
| Lobes | **3 (upper, middle, lower)** | **2 (upper, lower)** |
| Fissures | **2 (oblique + horizontal)** | **1 (oblique)** |
| Size | **Larger, heavier, shorter, wider** | Smaller, longer, narrower; **cardiac notch, lingula** |
| Bronchopulmonary segments | 10 | 8–10 |

- **Apex** – rises **2–3 cm above clavicle** (into neck – risk in subclavian cannulation → pneumothorax); **base** – on diaphragm; **hilum** – root (bronchus, pulmonary vessels, nerves, lymphatics).
- **Pleura** – **visceral (on lung – no pain fibres)** & **parietal (lines chest wall – pain sensitive)**; **pleural cavity with ~10–20 ml serous fluid** – lubrication; **negative intrapleural pressure (≈ −4 to −5 cmH₂O)** keeps lungs expanded; **air → pneumothorax, fluid → pleural effusion, pus → empyema, blood → haemothorax**.
- **Blood supply** – **pulmonary circulation (gas exchange)** + **bronchial arteries (from aorta – nourish lung tissue)**.

### Alveoli
- **~300 million (300–500 million) alveoli**; **surface area ≈ 70 m²** (tennis court ~ 70–100 m²); **respiratory membrane ≈ 0.5 µm thick**.
- **Type I pneumocytes – ~95 % surface, gas exchange**; **type II – secrete surfactant**; **alveolar macrophages (dust cells)**.
- **Surfactant (dipalmitoyl phosphatidylcholine/ lecithin)** – **↓ surface tension → prevents alveolar collapse (atelectasis)**; production starts **~24–28 weeks, adequate by ~34–35 weeks**; deficiency → **RDS (hyaline membrane disease) in preterm** → **antenatal corticosteroids, exogenous surfactant**; **L/S ratio ≥ 2 – lung maturity**.

## 6.3 Mechanics of Breathing

- **Inspiration – active** – **diaphragm contracts & descends (main – ~75 %)**, **external intercostals** raise ribs → **↑ thoracic volume → ↓ intrapulmonary pressure (Boyle's law) → air enters**.
- **Expiration – passive (quiet)** – elastic recoil; **forced expiration – active** (internal intercostals, abdominal muscles).
- **Accessory muscles** – SCM, scalenes, pectoralis (dyspnoea, COPD).
- **Compliance** – ease of lung expansion (↓ in fibrosis, RDS; ↑ in emphysema); **airway resistance** (↑ in asthma).

### Lung volumes & capacities (adult male approx.)
| Volume | Value | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (TV)** | **≈ 500 ml** | Normal quiet breath |
| **Inspiratory reserve volume (IRV)** | ≈ 3000 ml | Extra inspiration after normal |
| **Expiratory reserve volume (ERV)** | ≈ 1100–1200 ml | Extra expiration |
| **Residual volume (RV)** | **≈ 1200 ml** | **Remains after maximal expiration – can't be measured by spirometry** |
| **Vital capacity (VC)** | **≈ 4500–4800 ml** | **TV + IRV + ERV** (max air expelled after max inspiration) |
| **Total lung capacity (TLC)** | **≈ 5800–6000 ml** | VC + RV |
| **Functional residual capacity (FRC)** | ≈ 2300–2400 ml | ERV + RV |
| **Inspiratory capacity (IC)** | ≈ 3500 ml | TV + IRV |

- **Minute ventilation = TV × RR ≈ 500 × 12 = 6 L/min**; **alveolar ventilation = (TV − dead space) × RR ≈ 4.2 L/min**.
- **FEV1/FVC** – normal **≥ 70–80 %**; **↓ in obstructive (asthma, COPD)**; normal/ ↑ in restrictive (FVC ↓).
- **Peak expiratory flow (PEF)** – peak flow meter – asthma monitoring.
- **Normal RR** – **adult 12–20/min**, **newborn 30–60/min**, infant 30–50, child 20–30; ratio **inspiration : expiration ≈ 1 : 2**.

## 6.4 Gas Exchange & Transport

- **Diffusion** – partial pressure gradient (**Dalton's law**); **CO₂ diffuses ~20 times faster than O₂** (more soluble).
- **Partial pressures** – **alveolar PO₂ ≈ 100 mmHg, PCO₂ ≈ 40**; **arterial PaO₂ 80–100, PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg**; **venous PO₂ ≈ 40, PCO₂ ≈ 46**; **SpO₂ normal 95–100 %**.
- **O₂ transport** – **~97–98.5 % bound to haemoglobin (oxyhaemoglobin)**, **~1.5–3 % dissolved in plasma**.
- **Oxygen-haemoglobin dissociation curve – sigmoid (S-shaped)**; **shift to RIGHT (↓ affinity – O₂ released to tissues)** – **↑ CO₂, ↑ H⁺ (↓ pH – Bohr effect), ↑ temperature, ↑ 2,3-DPG, exercise**; **shift to LEFT** – ↓ temperature, alkalosis, **HbF, carbon monoxide (CO affinity ~200–250 × O₂)**; **P50 ≈ 27 mmHg**.
- **CO₂ transport** – **~70 % as bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻ – carbonic anhydrase in RBC; chloride shift/ Hamburger phenomenon)**, **~20–23 % as carbaminohaemoglobin**, **~7–10 % dissolved**; **Haldane effect** – deoxygenated Hb carries more CO₂.

## 6.5 Control of Respiration

- **Respiratory centres** – **medulla oblongata (dorsal – inspiratory, ventral respiratory group; rhythm generator – pre-Bötzinger complex)**, **pons (pneumotaxic – limits inspiration; apneustic)**.
- **Central chemoreceptors (medulla)** – **most sensitive to ↑ CO₂ / H⁺ in CSF (main drive)**; **peripheral chemoreceptors (carotid & aortic bodies)** – respond to **↓ O₂ (PaO₂ < 60)**, ↑ CO₂, ↓ pH; **COPD with chronic CO₂ retention – hypoxic drive** (controlled O₂ – target SpO₂ 88–92 %).
- **Hering-Breuer reflex** – stretch receptors prevent over-inflation (vagus).
- **Cough reflex** (vagus), **sneeze**, **hiccup** (diaphragm spasm – phrenic).

### Terms & abnormal patterns
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Eupnoea** | Normal breathing |
| **Tachypnoea / bradypnoea** | **> 20 / < 12 per min** (adult) |
| **Apnoea** | Absence of breathing (**> 20 s in newborn**) |
| **Dyspnoea / orthopnoea** | Difficulty / **difficulty lying flat (heart failure – sleeps on pillows)**; **PND** – paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea |
| **Hyperpnoea / hyperventilation** | ↑ depth / ↑ rate & depth (↓ CO₂ – respiratory alkalosis) |
| **Kussmaul's breathing** | **Deep, rapid, laboured – metabolic acidosis (DKA)** – acetone breath |
| **Cheyne-Stokes** | **Cyclic waxing-waning with apnoea – heart failure, raised ICP, dying, brainstem lesion** |
| **Biot's (ataxic)** | Irregular with apnoea – **meningitis, medulla damage** |
| **Apneustic** | Prolonged inspiratory gasps – pons lesion |
| **Hypoxia / hypoxaemia** | ↓ O₂ in tissues / in blood; **types – hypoxic, anaemic, stagnant (circulatory), histotoxic (cyanide)** |
| **Cyanosis** | Bluish discolouration when **deoxygenated Hb > 5 g/dl**; central (tongue, lips) vs peripheral; may be absent in severe anaemia |
| **Asphyxia** | Hypoxia + hypercapnia |
