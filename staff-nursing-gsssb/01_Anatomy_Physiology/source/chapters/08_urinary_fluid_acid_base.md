## 8.1 Kidneys

- **Location** – **retroperitoneal, T12–L3**; **right kidney slightly lower (liver above)**; bean-shaped, **~11–12 cm × 6 × 3 cm, ~130–150 g**; **hilum** – renal artery, vein, ureter (**anterior to posterior – vein, artery, pelvis "VAP"**).
- **Coverings** – fibrous capsule, perirenal fat, renal fascia (Gerota's).
- **Internal** – **cortex (outer – glomeruli)**, **medulla (renal pyramids 8–18 – loops & collecting ducts)**, **renal papilla → minor calyx → major calyx → renal pelvis → ureter**; renal columns.
- **Blood supply** – **renal artery (from aorta, L1–L2)**; receives **~20–25 % of cardiac output (~1.2 L/min)**; afferent arteriole → **glomerulus** → **efferent arteriole** → peritubular capillaries/ **vasa recta**.

### Functions of kidney
- **Excretion of waste** – **urea, uric acid, creatinine**, drugs, toxins.
- **Water & electrolyte balance**, **acid–base balance (excretes H⁺, reabsorbs HCO₃⁻)**, **BP regulation (renin – RAAS)**.
- **Endocrine** – **erythropoietin (RBC production – CKD → anaemia)**, **calcitriol (active vitamin D – 1α-hydroxylation; CKD → renal osteodystrophy)**, **renin**, prostaglandins.
- **Gluconeogenesis** (prolonged fasting).

## 8.2 Nephron & Urine Formation

@fig nephron.svg | Nephron – parts & functions with key renal numbers

- **Nephron – structural & functional unit (~1 million per kidney)**; **cortical (85 %)** & **juxtamedullary (15 % – long loops, concentrate urine)**.
- **Parts** – **renal corpuscle (glomerulus + Bowman's capsule)** → **PCT** → **loop of Henle (descending – water permeable; thick ascending – impermeable to water, Na⁺-K⁺-2Cl⁻ pump)** → **DCT** → **collecting duct**.
- **Juxtaglomerular apparatus** – **JG cells (afferent arteriole – secrete renin)** + **macula densa (DCT – senses NaCl)** + extraglomerular mesangial cells.

| Step | Site | Details |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Glomerular filtration** | Glomerulus | **Pressure-driven ultrafiltration**; **GFR ≈ 125 ml/min (180 L/day)**; filtrate = plasma minus proteins & cells; **net filtration pressure ≈ 10 mmHg** (glomerular hydrostatic 55 − oncotic 30 − capsular 15) |
| **2. Tubular reabsorption** | **PCT (maximum ~65–70 %)** | **100 % glucose & amino acids**, Na⁺, water, HCO₃⁻, K⁺; **renal threshold for glucose ≈ 180 mg/dl** (glycosuria above) |
| | Loop of Henle | **Counter-current multiplier** → concentrated medulla |
| | DCT | **Aldosterone – Na⁺ reabsorption, K⁺ & H⁺ secretion**; **PTH – Ca²⁺ reabsorption** |
| | Collecting duct | **ADH (vasopressin) – aquaporins → water reabsorption (concentrated urine)**; ADH deficiency → diabetes insipidus |
| **3. Tubular secretion** | PCT, DCT | **H⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺, creatinine, drugs (penicillin, PAH)** |

- **~99 % of filtrate reabsorbed** → urine ~1–1.5 L/day.
- **Clearance** – **inulin clearance = true GFR**; **creatinine clearance – clinical GFR estimate (~100–125 ml/min)**; **PAH clearance – renal plasma flow (~625 ml/min)**.
- **Hormones acting** – **ADH (posterior pituitary – water)**, **aldosterone (adrenal cortex – Na⁺ in, K⁺ out)**, **ANP (atria – ↑ Na⁺ & water excretion)**, **PTH**.

## 8.3 Ureters, Bladder & Urethra

- **Ureters** – **~25–30 cm**, **peristalsis** propels urine; **3 constrictions** – **pelvi-ureteric junction**, **crossing iliac vessels (pelvic brim)**, **vesico-ureteric junction (narrowest – commonest site of stone impaction)**; transitional epithelium.
- **Urinary bladder** – **hollow muscular organ (detrusor muscle)** in pelvis behind pubic symphysis; **capacity ~500–600 ml (max ~800–1000)**; **urge to void at ~200–300 ml (first desire ~150–200)**; **trigone** – smooth triangular area between 2 ureteric openings & internal urethral orifice (no rugae; infection-prone); lined with **transitional epithelium**.
- **Urethra** – **female ~4 cm** (short → **UTI more common**; catheter insert **5–7.5 cm (2–3 inches)**); **male ~18–20 cm** (prostatic, membranous – narrowest/ least dilatable part except external meatus, spongy/ penile; catheter insert **17–22 cm (7–9 inches)**).
- **Sphincters** – **internal (smooth, involuntary)**, **external (skeletal, voluntary – pudendal nerve)**.
- **Micturition reflex** – bladder stretch → **sacral spinal cord S2–S4 (parasympathetic – pelvic splanchnic nerves) → detrusor contraction & internal sphincter relaxation**; **voluntary control – pons (pontine micturition centre) & cortex**; **sympathetic (T11–L2) – storage**.
- **Bladder control** develops ~2–3 yrs (day), night ~ by 5 yrs (enuresis beyond).

### Normal urine
| Parameter | Normal |
|---|---|
| **Volume** | **1–1.5 L/day (≥ 0.5 ml/kg/hr; ≥ 30 ml/hr adult)**; child **1–2 ml/kg/hr** |
| Colour | **Pale yellow/ amber (urochrome)** |
| **Specific gravity** | **1.010–1.025** (1.003–1.030 range) |
| **pH** | **~6 (4.5–8)** – slightly acidic |
| Odour | Aromatic; ammoniacal on standing; **fruity (ketones)**; mousy (PKU) |
| Abnormal constituents | **Protein, glucose, ketones, blood, bile, pus cells** (normally absent or trace) |

- **Oliguria < 400 ml/day (< 0.5 ml/kg/hr)**, **anuria < 100 ml/day**, **polyuria > 2.5–3 L/day**, **nocturia, dysuria, frequency, urgency, haematuria, pyuria, enuresis, retention, incontinence**.
- **Blood urea 15–40 mg/dl (BUN 7–20)**, **serum creatinine 0.6–1.2 mg/dl**, **uric acid 3–7 mg/dl**.

## 8.4 Body Fluids

- **Total body water (TBW)** – **~60 % of body weight in adult male (~42 L in 70 kg)**, **~50–55 % female** (more fat), **~75–80 % newborn** (higher risk of dehydration), ~50 % elderly.
- **Intracellular fluid (ICF) – ⅔ (~40 % body weight; 28 L)**; **extracellular fluid (ECF) – ⅓ (~20 %; 14 L)** = **interstitial (¾ of ECF ~10.5 L)** + **plasma/ intravascular (¼ ~3.5 L)** + transcellular (CSF, synovial, pleural…).
- **Daily intake ≈ 2.5 L** (drinking ~1.5 L, food ~0.8 L, **metabolic water ~0.3 L**); **output ≈ 2.5 L** (**urine ~1.5 L**, skin/ sweat ~0.5 L, lungs ~0.3–0.4 L, faeces ~0.1–0.2 L); **insensible loss ≈ 800–1000 ml/day** (↑ 10–12 % per °C fever).
- **Maintenance fluid (Holliday-Segar)** – **100 ml/kg for first 10 kg + 50 ml/kg next 10 kg + 20 ml/kg each kg above 20** (per 24 hrs).
- **Osmolality** – **plasma 275–295 mOsm/kg**; **thirst centre – hypothalamus**.
- **Starling forces** – capillary hydrostatic pressure (out) vs plasma oncotic pressure (in – albumin); **oedema** – ↑ hydrostatic (heart failure), ↓ oncotic (hypoalbuminaemia – nephrotic, cirrhosis, malnutrition), ↑ permeability (inflammation, burns), lymphatic obstruction (filariasis).

## 8.5 Electrolytes – Normal Values & Key Facts

| Electrolyte | Normal | Key facts |
|---|---|---|
| **Sodium (Na⁺)** | **135–145 mEq/L** | **Main ECF cation**; water balance, nerve impulse; hypo – confusion, seizures; hyper – thirst, CNS |
| **Potassium (K⁺)** | **3.5–5.0 mEq/L** | **Main ICF cation**; **cardiac & muscle function**; hypo – weakness, **U waves**, arrhythmia; hyper – **tall T, arrhythmia, cardiac arrest** |
| **Chloride (Cl⁻)** | **96–106 mEq/L** | Main ECF anion |
| **Bicarbonate (HCO₃⁻)** | **22–26 mEq/L** | Main buffer |
| **Calcium (total)** | **8.5–10.5 mg/dl** (ionised 4.5–5.3 mg/dl) | Bones, clotting, muscle contraction; hypo – **tetany, Chvostek's & Trousseau's signs**, carpopedal spasm |
| **Magnesium** | **1.5–2.5 mEq/L** (1.7–2.4 mg/dl) | Second ICF cation; hypo → arrhythmia, tetany |
| **Phosphate** | **2.5–4.5 mg/dl** | Main ICF anion; inverse with calcium |
| **Anion gap** | **8–12 (Na − (Cl + HCO₃))** | ↑ in DKA, lactic acidosis, renal failure, poisoning |

## 8.6 Acid–Base Balance

- **Normal arterial pH 7.35–7.45** (**acidosis < 7.35, alkalosis > 7.45**; **compatible with life ~6.8–7.8**); **PaCO₂ 35–45 mmHg** (respiratory component); **HCO₃⁻ 22–26 mEq/L** (metabolic component); **PaO₂ 80–100 mmHg**; **SaO₂ 95–100 %**; **base excess −2 to +2**.
- **Henderson-Hasselbalch** – pH ∝ HCO₃⁻ / PaCO₂ (ratio **20 : 1**).
- **Regulation** – **1. buffers (seconds – bicarbonate (most important ECF), phosphate (ICF & urine), proteins/ haemoglobin (most abundant intracellular))**, **2. lungs (minutes – CO₂ excretion)**, **3. kidneys (hours–days – most powerful; H⁺ excretion, HCO₃⁻ reabsorption, ammonia)**.

| Disorder | pH | Primary change | Compensation | Causes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory acidosis** | ↓ | **↑ PaCO₂** | ↑ HCO₃⁻ (kidney) | **Hypoventilation – COPD, respiratory depression (opioids), neuromuscular weakness, airway obstruction** |
| **Respiratory alkalosis** | ↑ | **↓ PaCO₂** | ↓ HCO₃⁻ | **Hyperventilation – anxiety/ panic, high altitude, early salicylate poisoning, fever, PE, ventilator** |
| **Metabolic acidosis** | ↓ | **↓ HCO₃⁻** | ↓ PaCO₂ (**Kussmaul**) | **DKA, lactic acidosis (shock), renal failure, diarrhoea (HCO₃⁻ loss), methanol/ salicylate** |
| **Metabolic alkalosis** | ↑ | **↑ HCO₃⁻** | ↑ PaCO₂ (hypoventilation) | **Vomiting/ NG suction (HCl loss), diuretics, hypokalaemia, antacid excess** |

- **ROME** – **R**espiratory **O**pposite (pH ↑ → PaCO₂ ↓), **M**etabolic **E**qual (pH & HCO₃⁻ same direction).
- **ABG steps** – 1. pH (acidosis/ alkalosis) → 2. PaCO₂ (respiratory?) → 3. HCO₃⁻ (metabolic?) → 4. compensation (partial if pH abnormal, full if pH normal) → 5. oxygenation.
- **Example** – pH 7.30, PaCO₂ 55, HCO₃⁻ 26 → **uncompensated respiratory acidosis**; pH 7.48, PaCO₂ 40, HCO₃⁻ 32 → **metabolic alkalosis**.
- **ABG sample** – **radial artery (Allen's test first)**, heparinised syringe, no air bubbles, **ice/ analyse within 15–30 min**, pressure 5 min (10 if anticoagulated).
