# Hospitalised Child, Paediatric Procedures & Drug Calculations

## 5.1 Child's Reaction to Hospitalisation

- **Stressors** – **separation (major stressor in infants & toddlers)**, **loss of control**, **bodily injury & pain**, unfamiliar environment, strangers.
- **Separation anxiety** – peaks **6 months – 30 months** (toddler most affected). **3 stages (Robertson/ Bowlby)**:
  1. **Protest** – cries loudly, clings to parent, refuses others, screams.
  2. **Despair** – hopeless, quiet, withdrawn, **regression**, disinterested in play/ food.
  3. **Detachment (denial)** – seems "adjusted", interacts superficially – **most serious (prolonged separation)**, may lose attachment.
- **Regression** – common in toddler (loses toilet training, bottle again) – **don't scold, accept**, it is temporary.
- **Age-wise**: infant – separation, stranger anxiety; toddler – separation, loss of autonomy/ routine; **preschool – fear of bodily injury/ mutilation, illness as punishment (magical thinking)**; school age – loss of control, fear of death, separation from peers; adolescent – **loss of independence, privacy, body image**, separation from peers.
- **Nursing**: **rooming-in / parents stay (family-centred care)**, maintain home routine, favourite toy (**transitional object**), primary nurse, **play/ therapeutic play**, honest age-appropriate explanation, **give choices** (sense of control), privacy for adolescents, school work/ peers contact.
- **Atraumatic care** – **EMLA cream** (60 min before venipuncture), sucrose 24 % for neonates, distraction, parents present during procedures, **treatment room for painful procedures (bed = safe place)**.

## 5.2 Paediatric Vital Signs

| Age | Heart rate (/min) | Respiratory rate (/min) | Systolic BP (mmHg) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Newborn** | **120–160** | **30–60** | 60–90 |
| Infant (1–12 m) | 100–160 | 30–50 | 70–100 |
| Toddler (1–3 y) | 90–150 | 24–40 | 80–110 |
| Preschool (3–6 y) | 80–140 | 22–34 | 80–110 |
| School (6–12 y) | 70–120 | 18–30 | 85–120 |
| Adolescent | 60–100 | 12–20 | 95–130 |

- **Minimum (lower limit) SBP (1–10 yrs) = 70 + (2 × age in years)**; < 1 yr – 60 (neonate)/ 70 (infant); **> 10 yrs – 90 mmHg**.
- **Pulse – apical** (stethoscope) in **< 2 yrs**, full 1 minute; radial after 2 yrs.
- **RR – count by observing abdomen** (infants are **abdominal/ diaphragmatic breathers**), **full 1 minute**, when calm (before disturbing procedures).
- **Temperature** – **axillary preferred in newborn/ infants** (safe); **rectal – avoided routinely** (perforation, NEC, diarrhoea; insert ≤ 1.5 cm infants); tympanic > 2 yrs.
- **Cuff width** – **40 % of arm circumference / 2/3 of upper arm length**; bladder encircles 80–100 % of arm. **Narrow cuff → falsely high BP**.
- **Order of assessment** – least invasive first: **RR → HR → temperature → BP**.

## 5.3 Pain Assessment in Children

| Scale | Age |
|---|---|
| **NIPS** (Neonatal Infant Pain Scale), **PIPP** (premature), CRIES | Neonates |
| **FLACC** (Face, Legs, Activity, Cry, Consolability) – each 0–2, total 10 | **2 months – 7 yrs / non-verbal** |
| **Wong-Baker FACES** | **≥ 3 yrs** |
| **Oucher scale** (photographs + numbers) | 3–12 yrs |
| **Poker chip tool** ("pieces of hurt") | 4–8 yrs |
| **Numeric (0–10)/ VAS** | **≥ 7–8 yrs** (who can count/ understand numbers) |

- **Non-pharmacological** – **non-nutritive sucking, 24 % sucrose (neonates, 2 min before)**, **breastfeeding during procedure**, **KMC/ skin-to-skin**, swaddling, distraction (bubbles, cartoons), guided imagery, cold/ heat.
- **Drugs** – **paracetamol (15 mg/kg/dose, 4–6 hourly; max 60 mg/kg/day)**, ibuprofen (10 mg/kg/dose, > 3 months), opioids (morphine 0.1 mg/kg); **aspirin avoided (< 16 yrs – Reye's syndrome)**.

## 5.4 Medication Administration

- **Dose based on body weight (mg/kg)** – most accurate; or **BSA** (chemotherapy). Always **double-check** high-alert drugs (insulin, digoxin, potassium, heparin, opioids).

| Rule | Formula |
|---|---|
| **Clark's rule** (weight) | **Child dose = (Weight in lb ÷ 150) × adult dose** (kg version: weight kg ÷ 70 × adult dose) |
| **Young's rule** (age, 1–12 yrs) | **Child dose = Age ÷ (Age + 12) × adult dose** |
| **Fried's rule** (infants < 1 yr) | **Child dose = Age in months ÷ 150 × adult dose** |
| **Cowling's rule** | (Age at next birthday ÷ 24) × adult dose |
| **BSA rule** (most accurate) | **Child dose = (BSA in m² ÷ 1.73) × adult dose** |
| **BSA (Mosteller)** | **√ (height cm × weight kg ÷ 3600)** |

- **Example**: 6-yr child, adult dose 500 mg → Young's = 6/(6+12) × 500 = **166.7 mg**.
- **Example**: paracetamol 15 mg/kg for 12-kg child = **180 mg**; syrup 250 mg/5 ml → 180 × 5 / 250 = **3.6 ml**.
- **Oral** – use **calibrated dropper/ oral syringe** (not household spoon), place in **side of mouth (cheek pocket)** slowly – prevents aspiration; **hold infant semi-upright**; **don't mix in milk/ essential foods** (food aversion, incomplete dose); crush tablets with small amount of jam/ honey (> 1 yr); never call medicine "candy".
- **IM injection** – **vastus lateralis (anterolateral thigh) – preferred site for infants & children < 3 yrs**; **ventrogluteal** safe for all ages; **deltoid** after 18 months–3 yrs (small volume ≤ 0.5–1 ml); **dorsogluteal avoided until child walks ≥ 1 yr (sciatic nerve)**. Max volume: infant 0.5–1 ml, child 1–2 ml.
- **Ear drops** – **< 3 yrs: pull pinna DOWN & BACK**; **> 3 yrs (and adults): UP & BACK**.
- **Eye drops** – lower conjunctival sac, press inner canthus 1 min.
- **Nasal drops** – head extended over edge of bed/ pillow.
- **Rectal** – suppository inserted with **little finger** in < 3 yrs.

## 5.5 IV Therapy & Fluids

- **Maintenance fluid (Holliday-Segar)**: **100 ml/kg/day (first 10 kg) + 50 ml/kg/day (11–20 kg) + 20 ml/kg/day (> 20 kg)**. Hourly ("4-2-1 rule"): **4 ml/kg/hr + 2 ml/kg/hr + 1 ml/kg/hr**.
- **Example**: 25-kg child → 1000 + 500 + 100 = **1600 ml/day** (≈ 65 ml/hr).
- **Fluid bolus (shock)** – **10–20 ml/kg NS/ RL** over 5–20 min (reassess after each); SAM/ cardiac – 10–15 ml/kg slowly.
- **Microdrip (paediatric) set: 60 drops = 1 ml** → **drops/min = ml/hr**. Macro set: 15–20 drops/ml.
- **Use infusion pump/ burette (Soluset, 100–150 ml)** – never hang large bottle for infants (fluid overload).
- **IV site** – **dorsum of hand/ foot**, scalp veins (infants), antecubital; **check site hourly** (infiltration, phlebitis); splint the limb.
- **Intraosseous (IO)** – emergency when IV fails in 90 sec – **proximal tibia (1–2 cm below tibial tuberosity, anteromedial)**.
- **Daily weight** – best indicator of fluid balance; **urine output normal: infant 2 ml/kg/hr, child 1 ml/kg/hr** (< 1 ml/kg/hr = oliguria in child).
- **Diaper weighing** – **1 g wet diaper weight = 1 ml urine**.

## 5.6 Restraints

| Restraint | Use |
|---|---|
| **Mummy (swaddle) restraint** | **Short procedures on head/ neck** – venipuncture (scalp/ jugular), gavage, eye exam |
| **Elbow restraint** | **Prevent touching face/ head** – **cleft lip repair**, eczema, scalp IV |
| **Jacket restraint** | Keep child in bed/ chair, high chair |
| **Clove-hitch** | **Single limb** (extremity) – IV site |
| **Papoose board** | Commercial, whole body |
| **Crib net / top** | Prevent climbing out |

- Rules – **order required**, explain to parents, **check circulation every 15–30 min**, **release every 2 hrs** (ROM, skin care), **tie with square/ quick-release knot to bed frame (not side rails)**, document; least restrictive.

## 5.7 Specimen Collection & Procedures

- **Urine** – **sterile urine bag** (perineum cleaned & dried; check every 15–30 min); **clean-catch**; **suprapubic aspiration – most reliable culture in infants**; catheter specimen.
- **Stool** – from diaper; **24-hr urine** – start with empty bladder, discard first void.
- **Blood** – **heel prick (lateral/ medial plantar surface of heel – avoid posterior curvature/ centre – calcaneus osteomyelitis)**; warm heel; **neonatal screening (TSH, PKU)**.
- **Lumbar puncture** – **sitting (infants) or lateral recumbent with knee-chest (flexed spine)**, **L3–L4 / L4–L5**; post-LP lie flat; LP contraindicated in **↑ ICP (papilloedema), local infection, bleeding disorder**.
- **Nasogastric/ orogastric (gavage) tube** – **neonates prefer orogastric (obligatory nose breathers)**; length – **NEX: nose tip → earlobe → xiphisternum** (+ midpoint to umbilicus in newborn — NEMU); confirm by **aspirate pH < 5.5** / X-ray; feeding by **gravity (not push)**, 15–20 cm above; hold baby/ right side/ prone after feed.
- **Gastrostomy** feeding; **tracheostomy care** (suction ≤ 5 sec, catheter half size of tube).
- **Suctioning** – **mouth before nose**; pressure – neonate 60–80, infant 80–100, child 100–120 mmHg; < 5 sec.
- **Oxygen therapy** – **nasal prongs** (≤ 2 L/min infant), **head box/ hood (5–7 L/min to wash CO₂)**, mask, incubator; **retinopathy of prematurity (ROP)** risk – target SpO₂ **90–95 % in preterms**.
- **Exchange transfusion, phototherapy** – see newborn chapter.
- **Bathing** – sponge bath till cord falls; **tub bath** water **37–38 °C (test with elbow)**; never leave child alone in water.
