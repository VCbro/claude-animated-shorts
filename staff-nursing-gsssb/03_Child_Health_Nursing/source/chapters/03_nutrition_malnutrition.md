## 3.1 Nutritional Requirements

| Age | Energy | Protein |
|---|---|---|
| **0–6 months** | **~100 kcal/kg/day** (≈ 90–110) | ~1.2–1.5 g/kg |
| 6–12 months | ~80 kcal/kg/day | ~1.5 g/kg |
| **Fluid (maintenance – Holliday-Segar)** | **100 ml/kg (first 10 kg) + 50 ml/kg (next 10 kg) + 20 ml/kg (each kg above 20)** | – |

- **Newborn fluid** – day 1: **60 ml/kg**, increase by 15–20 ml/kg/day to **150 ml/kg/day by day 7** (term).
- **Exclusive breastfeeding** till **6 months** – no water, honey, ghutti, glucose water (**prelacteal feeds harmful**).
- **Breast milk** – ~**67–70 kcal/100 ml**, protein 1.1 g/dl (lactalbumin – easily digested), **lactose** (main carbohydrate), **whey : casein = 60 : 40** (cow's milk 20 : 80), **low solute load**, iron poor but **50 % absorbed (lactoferrin)**, rich in **IgA**, lysozyme, bifidus factor, DHA; **colostrum (first 3 days)** – high protein, **IgA, vitamin A**, laxative (meconium passage).
- **Disadvantages of cow's milk < 1 yr** – high protein & solute load, **iron-deficiency anaemia (occult GI bleed)**, low iron/ vit C, allergy, infection risk with bottles.

## 3.2 Infant & Young Child Feeding (IYCF)

- **Early initiation within 1 hour** · **exclusive breastfeeding 0–6 months** · **complementary feeding from 6 months (180 days)** · **continue breastfeeding up to 2 years or beyond**.
- **Complementary feeding** – **semi-solid, mashed**, home-made, **energy-dense** (add a spoon of oil/ ghee), hygienically prepared; start with **2–3 spoons** and increase.

| Age | Frequency | Amount per meal | Consistency |
|---|---|---|---|
| **6–8 months** | **2–3 meals/day** + breastfeeding | **2–3 tablespoons → ½ katori (125 ml)** | Thick mashed/ pureed |
| **9–11 months** | **3–4 meals** + 1–2 snacks | **½ katori (250-ml katori)** | Finely chopped, mashed, finger foods |
| **12–24 months** | **3–4 meals** + 1–2 snacks | **¾ – 1 katori** | Family foods |

- **Responsive feeding**, add **variety (≥ 5 of 8 food groups – minimum dietary diversity)**, avoid **bottle feeding** (use **katori-spoon**/ paladai), avoid junk & sugary drinks.
- **Weaning foods** – khichdi, dalia, mashed banana, suji kheer, **"Sattu"**; **multimix** principle (cereal + pulse + vegetable + fat).
- **Breastfeeding contraindications (rare)** – **galactosaemia** (baby), mother on **anticancer drugs/ radioactive iodine**, untreated active TB (temporary separation; give INH to baby later breastfeed), HIV (India – **exclusive breastfeeding with ART** recommended).

## 3.3 Protein Energy Malnutrition (PEM)

@fig muac_sam.svg | MUAC tape colours, marasmus vs kwashiorkor & SAM criteria

:::def Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) – WHO / India (6–59 months)
Any one: **Weight-for-height < −3 SD** OR **MUAC < 11.5 cm** OR **bilateral pitting oedema** (nutritional). **MAM** – WFH −3 to −2 SD or MUAC **11.5–12.5 cm**. **SAM with complications** (medical complication, failed appetite test, < 6 months) → **NRC (Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre)**; uncomplicated → community management (CMAM).
:::

| Classification | Criteria |
|---|---|
| **IAP (weight-for-age % of expected/ 50th centile)** | Normal > 80 % · **Grade I 71–80 %** · **II 61–70 %** · **III 51–60 %** · **IV ≤ 50 %** (suffix K if oedema) |
| **Gomez** (weight-for-age) | 1st degree 75–90 % · 2nd 60–75 % · **3rd < 60 %** |
| **Waterlow** | Wasting (weight-for-height) & stunting (height-for-age) |
| **Wellcome Trust** | Weight 60–80 % + oedema = **kwashiorkor**; < 60 % no oedema = **marasmus**; < 60 % + oedema = **marasmic kwashiorkor**; 60–80 % no oedema = underweight |

| | **Marasmus** | **Kwashiorkor** |
|---|---|---|
| Deficiency | **Calories (energy) + protein** – "starvation" | Mainly **protein** (with adequate/ low calories) |
| Age | **< 1 yr** (early weaning, bottle feeding) | **1–4 yrs** ("disease of the displaced child" – after next sibling) |
| Oedema | **Absent** | **Present (pitting) – hallmark** |
| Weight | **< 60 %**, severe wasting – "**skin & bones**", "**old man/ monkey face**", loose skin folds | 60–80 % (masked by oedema), **moon face** |
| Appetite / mood | **Good appetite, alert** | **Anorexia, apathy, irritable, miserable** |
| Skin | Dry, loose | **Flaky-paint dermatosis**, depigmentation |
| Hair | Thin, dry | **Sparse, easily pluckable, pale – "flag sign"** (alternate bands) |
| Liver | Normal | **Fatty liver – hepatomegaly** |
| Serum albumin | Normal/ slightly low | **Very low** |
| Prognosis | Better | Worse |

### WHO 10 steps of SAM management (facility)
1. **Hypoglycaemia** – treat/ prevent (< 54 mg/dl → 50 ml 10 % glucose/ sucrose; frequent 2-hrly feeds day & night).
2. **Hypothermia** – keep warm (**KMC, cover, warm room**; rectal < 35.5 °C).
3. **Dehydration** – **ReSoMal** (low sodium, high potassium ORS) **5 ml/kg every 30 min × 2 hrs**, then 5–10 ml/kg/hr; **avoid IV** unless shock (IV only in shock – 15 ml/kg over 1 hr).
4. **Electrolytes** – extra **potassium & magnesium**; **sodium restricted**.
5. **Infection** – **give antibiotics to ALL** (signs often absent).
6. **Micronutrients** – vitamin A, zinc, copper, folic acid; **iron NOT in stabilisation phase** (start in rehabilitation, after 2 weeks).
7. **Cautious feeding** – **F-75** (75 kcal & 0.9 g protein/100 ml) – stabilisation phase (days 1–7).
8. **Catch-up growth** – **F-100** (100 kcal/ 100 ml) – rehabilitation phase.
9. Sensory stimulation & emotional support (**play therapy**).
10. Prepare for **follow-up** after recovery.
- **Phases**: **stabilisation (1–7 days, steps 1–7)** → **rehabilitation (2–6 weeks)** → follow-up.
- **Refeeding syndrome** – ↓ phosphate, K, Mg on aggressive feeding → go slow.
- **Discharge criteria (NRC)** – **15 % weight gain** / WFH ≥ −2 SD, oedema free, eating well.
- **Appetite test** with RUTF (Ready-to-Use Therapeutic Food) – failed → admit.

## 3.4 Vitamin Deficiencies

| Vitamin | Deficiency disease | Key signs | Treatment/ prevention |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** (retinol) | **Xerophthalmia** | **Night blindness (XN) – earliest symptom**; **conjunctival xerosis (X1A) – earliest sign**; **Bitot's spots (X1B)** – foamy triangular patches; corneal xerosis (X2), **keratomalacia (X3) – irreversible blindness** | Treatment: **day 0, 1, 14** (< 6 m 50,000 IU; 6–12 m 1 lakh; > 1 yr 2 lakh IU). Prophylaxis: **9 months – 1 lakh IU, then 2 lakh IU every 6 months till 5 yrs (9 doses)** |
| **D** | **Rickets** (children), osteomalacia (adults) | **Craniotabes (earliest, ping-pong skull)**, large AF, **frontal bossing**, **rachitic rosary** (costochondral junction), **Harrison's sulcus**, **pigeon chest**, **wrist widening**, **bow legs (genu varum)/ knock knees**, delayed dentition, hypotonia, ↑ **ALP (earliest biochemical)**; X-ray – **cupping & fraying of metaphysis** | **Vitamin D** – daily oral dose or large single **"stoss" dose** (as prescribed) + **calcium**; **sunlight**; prophylaxis **400 IU/day** for infants |
| **C** | **Scurvy** | **Bleeding (swollen spongy) gums**, subperiosteal haemorrhage – painful limbs, **"frog position"**, **pseudoparalysis**, scorbutic rosary, perifollicular haemorrhage | Vitamin C 100–200 mg/day; citrus fruits, amla |
| **B1** (thiamine) | **Beriberi** – wet (cardiac), dry (neuritis), **infantile beriberi** (breastfed babies of deficient mothers – aphonia, cardiac failure) | Polished rice eaters | Thiamine |
| **B2** (riboflavin) | Ariboflavinosis | **Angular stomatitis, cheilosis**, magenta tongue | Riboflavin |
| **B3** (niacin) | **Pellagra** | **3 D's – Dermatitis (Casal's necklace), Diarrhoea, Dementia** (+ Death); maize/ jowar eaters | Niacin |
| **B6** | – | **Convulsions** in infants, anaemia (INH therapy) | Pyridoxine |
| **B12 / Folate** | **Megaloblastic anaemia** | Pallor, glossitis; B12 – neuro signs, knuckle pigmentation | B12, folic acid |
| **K** | **Haemorrhagic disease of newborn (VKDB)** | Bleeding day 2–7 (classical) | **Vit K 1 mg IM at birth** |

## 3.5 Minerals & Anaemia

- **Iron-deficiency anaemia – most common nutritional deficiency & most common anaemia in children** (6 months–2 yrs peak – depleted stores, low iron diet, worms). S/S – pallor, irritability, **pica**, koilonychia, poor school performance, **breath-holding spells**. Rx – **elemental iron 3–6 mg/kg/day** (orally, between meals, with vit C; **stains teeth – use straw/ brush teeth**; black stools normal).
- **WHO anaemia cut-offs**: **6–59 months Hb < 11 g/dl**; 5–11 yrs < 11.5; 12–14 yrs < 12.

| **Anaemia Mukt Bharat – prophylaxis** | Dose |
|---|---|
| **6–59 months** | **IFA syrup 1 ml (20 mg iron + 100 µg folic acid) biweekly** (ASHA) |
| **5–9 years** | **IFA pink tablet (45 mg iron + 400 µg FA) weekly** (school) |
| **10–19 years** | **IFA blue tablet (60 mg iron + 500 µg FA) weekly** (WIFS) |
| Deworming | **Albendazole 400 mg** biannually (**200 mg for 1–2 yrs**) |

- **Iodine deficiency** – goitre, **cretinism** (endemic), **deaf-mutism**, mental retardation; **iodised salt – 15 ppm at consumer level (30 ppm at production)**; NIDDCP (1992; earlier NGCP 1962).
- **Zinc deficiency** – growth retardation, **acrodermatitis enteropathica**, diarrhoea; **zinc in diarrhoea** for 14 days.
- **Fluorosis** – dental mottling, skeletal fluorosis (fluoride > 1.5 ppm); **dental caries** (< 0.5 ppm).

## 3.6 Childhood Obesity

- **BMI-for-age > +2 SD (> 95th centile)** = obesity; > +1 SD (85th) = overweight.
- Causes – sedentary lifestyle, screen time, junk food, genetics; rarely hypothyroidism, Cushing's, Prader-Willi.
- Complications – T2DM, HTN, dyslipidaemia, fatty liver, sleep apnoea, low self-esteem, slipped capital femoral epiphysis.
- Management – **lifestyle modification (family-based)**, ≥ 60 min physical activity daily, **screen time < 2 hrs** (none < 2 yrs), healthy diet.

:::exam PYQ style
- Exclusive breastfeeding – **6 months**; complementary feeding starts – **6 months (180 days)**.
- Main carbohydrate in breast milk – **lactose**; protein – **lactalbumin (whey)**.

- SAM – **MUAC < 11.5 cm / WFH < −3 SD / bilateral oedema**.
- Hallmark of kwashiorkor – **oedema**; flag sign – **kwashiorkor**; old man face – **marasmus**.
- F-75 used in – **stabilisation phase**; ReSoMal – **dehydration in SAM**.
- Iron in SAM started – **rehabilitation phase** (not in first week).
- Earliest symptom of vitamin A deficiency – **night blindness**; earliest sign – **conjunctival xerosis**.
- Vitamin A prophylaxis first dose – **9 months, 1 lakh IU**; total – **9 doses**.
- Earliest sign of rickets – **craniotabes**; rachitic rosary – **costochondral junction**.
- Frog position, bleeding gums – **scurvy**; 3 D's – **pellagra**.
- IFA syrup 6–59 months – **1 ml biweekly (20 mg iron)**.
- Iodised salt at consumer level – **15 ppm**.
