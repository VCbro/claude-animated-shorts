## 11.1 Urine Output & Basics

- **First urine** – within **24 hrs** of birth (most within 12 hrs); **delayed > 48 hrs** – investigate.
- **Normal urine output**: **infant 2 ml/kg/hr**, **child 1 ml/kg/hr**, adolescent 0.5 ml/kg/hr; **oliguria < 1 ml/kg/hr (child)**, < 0.5 ml/kg/hr (adult).
- **Bladder capacity (ml) = (age in yrs + 2) × 30** (approx).

## 11.2 Nephrotic Syndrome

:::def Nephrotic syndrome – tetrad
**Heavy proteinuria** (**> 40 mg/m²/hr** or **spot urine protein : creatinine > 2** or **3+/ 4+ dipstick**) + **hypoalbuminaemia (< 2.5 g/dl)** + **generalised oedema** + **hyperlipidaemia** (cholesterol > 200 mg/dl).
:::

- **Minimal change disease (MCNS) – ~80–90 % in children, steroid-sensitive**; age **2–6 yrs**, boys.
- **Pathophysiology** – ↑ glomerular permeability → protein loss → ↓ oncotic pressure → oedema; liver ↑ lipoprotein synthesis → hyperlipidaemia; ↑ clotting factors → **hypercoagulable**.
- **S/S** – **periorbital (puffy eyes, morning) → generalised oedema (anasarca)**, ascites, scrotal/ labial oedema, **weight gain**, **frothy urine**, ↓ urine output, pale, irritable, anorexia, diarrhoea (gut oedema); **BP usually normal**, **no gross haematuria**, normal C3.
- **Complications** – **infections (most common – spontaneous bacterial peritonitis – pneumococcus, cellulitis)**, **thrombosis** (renal vein), hypovolaemia/ shock, AKI, malnutrition, steroid side effects.

### Treatment (ISPN/ IAP)
- **Initial episode** – **prednisolone 2 mg/kg/day (60 mg/m²) single morning dose × 6 weeks**, then **1.5 mg/kg (40 mg/m²) alternate days × 6 weeks** (total 12 weeks).
- **Remission** – urine protein **nil/ trace for 3 consecutive days**; **relapse** – 3+/4+ for 3 consecutive days.
- **Steroid-dependent/ frequent relapsers** – levamisole, cyclophosphamide, MMF, tacrolimus, rituximab; **steroid-resistant** → renal biopsy (FSGS).
- **Diuretics** (frusemide) with care; **albumin infusion (20 %)** + frusemide for severe oedema.

### Nursing care
- **Daily weight (same time, same scale, same clothes)**, **abdominal girth** (at umbilicus), strict I/O, **urine albumin daily (dipstick – first morning sample)**.
- **Diet** – **no added salt during oedema** (salt restriction), **normal protein for age (1.5–2 g/kg) – high protein NOT recommended**, adequate calories; fluid restriction only if severe oedema/ hyponatraemia.
- **Skin care** – oedematous skin breaks easily: **change position 2-hourly**, support scrotum, separate skin surfaces (cotton), loose clothes, **avoid IM injections in oedematous areas**.
- **Infection prevention** – hand hygiene, avoid crowds/ sick contacts, **pneumococcal & varicella vaccination (live vaccines deferred during high-dose steroids)**, recognise peritonitis (fever, abdominal pain).
- **Steroid side effects** – **cushingoid face, weight gain, ↑ appetite, hypertension, hyperglycaemia, behaviour change, peptic ulcer, infection, growth suppression, cataract, osteoporosis**; give **with food in the morning**, **never stop abruptly (adrenal crisis)**.
- **Parent teaching** – home urine testing with dipstick, relapse recognition, medication diary, body-image support.

## 11.3 Acute Post-Streptococcal Glomerulonephritis (APSGN/ AGN)

- **Immune-complex** disease after **Group A β-haemolytic Streptococcus** – **throat (1–3 weeks)** or **skin – impetigo/ pyoderma (3–6 weeks)**; age **5–12 yrs**.
- **Nephritic syndrome** – **haematuria (cola/ smoky/ tea-coloured urine) – hallmark**, **hypertension**, **oliguria**, **oedema (periorbital, mild)**, mild proteinuria, **azotaemia**.
- **Investigations** – urine: **RBCs & RBC casts**; **↑ ASO titre** (throat), anti-DNase B (skin), **↓ C3 complement (returns to normal in 6–8 weeks)**, ↑ urea/ creatinine.
- **Complications** – **hypertensive encephalopathy** (headache, vomiting, blurred vision, seizures), **pulmonary oedema/ CHF**, **AKI**, hyperkalaemia.
- **Treatment** – mostly **supportive**; **penicillin × 10 days** (eradicate strep – doesn't change course); **fluid restriction = insensible loss (400 ml/m²/day) + previous day's urine output**; **salt restriction**; protein restriction only if azotaemia; **frusemide**, antihypertensives (amlodipine, nifedipine); dialysis if needed.
- **Nursing** – **bed rest during acute phase** (oedema, HTN, gross haematuria), **BP monitoring 4-hourly**, **daily weight, strict I/O**, urine colour, neuro checks (encephalopathy), low-salt diet, diversion activities; **prognosis excellent (> 95 % recover)**.

| Feature | **Nephrotic** | **Nephritic (AGN)** |
|---|---|---|
| Age | **2–6 yrs** | **5–12 yrs** |
| Onset | Insidious | Acute, post-strep |
| Oedema | **Massive, generalised** | Mild, periorbital |
| Proteinuria | **Massive (3+/4+)** | Mild (1+/2+) |
| Haematuria | Absent/ microscopic | **Gross – cola coloured** |
| BP | Normal | **Raised** |
| Serum albumin | **Very low** | Normal/ slightly low |
| Cholesterol | **↑↑** | Normal |
| C3 | Normal | **Low** |
| Treatment | **Steroids** | **Supportive, fluid/ salt restriction, penicillin** |

## 11.4 Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

- **E. coli (most common, ~80 %)**, Klebsiella, Proteus.
- **More common in girls (short urethra)** except **first year (boys – uncircumcised, anomalies)**.
- **Risk factors** – **VUR (vesico-ureteric reflux)**, posterior urethral valves (boys), constipation, poor hygiene (**wiping back → front**), bubble baths, tight clothes, holding urine, catheter.
- **S/S** – **infants: fever, poor feeding, vomiting, irritability, FTT, jaundice (non-specific)**; older: **dysuria, frequency, urgency, foul urine, enuresis (new), suprapubic pain**; pyelonephritis – high fever, flank pain.
- **Diagnosis** – **urine culture (gold standard)** – **suprapubic aspiration (any growth significant)**, catheter (≥ 50,000 CFU/ml), **clean-catch midstream (≥ 1,00,000 CFU/ml)**; urine routine (pus cells > 5/hpf, nitrite +ve).
- **Treatment** – oral **amoxicillin-clavulanate/ cefixime/ cotrimoxazole/ nitrofurantoin** 7–14 days; IV for infants < 3 months/ toxic.
- Imaging after first febrile UTI – **USG KUB**, **MCU (micturating cystourethrogram) – for VUR**, **DMSA scan – renal scarring**.
- **Prevention/ teaching** – **wipe front to back**, **adequate fluids**, regular voiding (every 3–4 hrs), **avoid bubble baths & tight nylon underwear (use cotton)**, treat constipation, complete antibiotic course.
- **Posterior urethral valves (PUV)** – most common cause of **lower urinary tract obstruction in boys**; poor stream, dribbling, palpable bladder, antenatal hydronephrosis → **valve ablation**.

## 11.5 Wilms Tumour (Nephroblastoma)

- **Most common renal malignancy of childhood**; age **2–5 yrs**; **WT1 gene (chromosome 11)**; associations – **WAGR, Beckwith-Wiedemann**, aniridia, hemihypertrophy.
- **S/S** – **painless, firm, smooth abdominal mass, usually unilateral, does not cross midline** (often noticed by mother while bathing/ dressing), haematuria, hypertension (renin), fever.
- (vs **neuroblastoma** – crosses midline, irregular, adrenal, < 2 yrs, ↑ urinary VMA, "raccoon eyes")
- **Treatment** – **nephrectomy + chemotherapy (vincristine, actinomycin D) ± radiotherapy**; **excellent prognosis (~90 %)**.

:::warn Wilms tumour – DO NOT palpate the abdomen
Place sign **"Do not palpate abdomen"** on bed – palpation can **rupture the capsule & spread tumour cells (seeding)**; careful bathing/ handling; surgery within 24–48 hrs of diagnosis.
:::

## 11.6 Genital Anomalies

| Condition | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Hypospadias** | **Urethral opening on ventral (under) surface** of penis; ± **chordee** (ventral curvature); **NO circumcision** (foreskin used for repair); repair at **6–18 months** (before toilet training/ body awareness); post-op – **catheter/ stent care**, double diaper, prevent kinking, fluids, analgesia |
| **Epispadias** | Opening on **dorsal (upper)** surface; with **bladder exstrophy** |
| **Undescended testis (cryptorchidism)** | Common in **preterms**; most descend by **3–6 months**; **orchidopexy at 6–12 months (before 18 months)** – risk of **infertility, malignancy (seminoma), torsion**, inguinal hernia; **retractile testis** – normal (cremasteric reflex) |
| **Phimosis** | Non-retractable foreskin – **physiological till 3–5 yrs**; **don't forcibly retract**; pathological – steroid cream/ circumcision |
| **Paraphimosis** | Retracted foreskin can't return – **emergency** (cold compress, manual reduction) |
| **Testicular torsion** | **Sudden severe scrotal pain**, high-riding testis, **absent cremasteric reflex** – **surgical emergency (within 6 hrs to save testis)** |
| **Hydronephrosis** | PUJ obstruction (commonest antenatal finding) |
| **Ambiguous genitalia** | **Congenital adrenal hyperplasia (21-hydroxylase – most common)** – salt-wasting crisis; don't assign sex hastily |

## 11.7 Enuresis

- **Involuntary passage of urine in a child ≥ 5 yrs** (at least twice a week × 3 months). **Nocturnal (most common)**, diurnal; **primary** (never dry) – most common, family history; **secondary** (after ≥ 6 months dry) – stress, UTI, diabetes, new sibling.
- **Management** – **reassure, don't punish/ shame**, **star chart (positive reinforcement)**, void before bed, **limit fluids 1–2 hrs before bedtime**, avoid caffeine, wake to void; **enuresis alarm (most effective long-term)**, **desmopressin (DDAVP)** at bedtime (short-term – camps), imipramine (rarely); bladder training.
- **Encopresis** – faecal soiling **after 4 yrs**; usually **chronic constipation with overflow**.

## 11.8 Renal Failure (brief)

- **AKI** – prerenal (dehydration – most common in children), renal (**HUS – common cause in young children**, AGN), postrenal (PUV); **hyperkalaemia** – most dangerous (ECG – tall T); Rx – fluid balance, **calcium gluconate, salbutamol nebulisation, insulin-glucose, sodium bicarbonate, kayexalate**, dialysis (**peritoneal dialysis** preferred in small children).
- **CKD** – congenital anomalies (CAKUT – commonest cause in children); growth failure, renal osteodystrophy, anaemia (erythropoietin).
- **HUS** – triad: **microangiopathic haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, AKI**; after **E. coli O157:H7/ Shigella** dysentery.

:::exam PYQ style
- Nephrotic syndrome commonest type – **minimal change disease**; age – **2–6 yrs**.
- Hallmark of nephrotic – **massive proteinuria + hypoalbuminaemia + oedema + hyperlipidaemia**.
- Earliest oedema in nephrotic – **periorbital (morning)**.
- Prednisolone dose – **2 mg/kg/day × 6 wks → 1.5 mg/kg alternate day × 6 wks**.
- Best indicator of fluid status – **daily weight**.
- Commonest complication of nephrotic – **infection (peritonitis)**.
- AGN follows – **streptococcal throat/ skin infection**; urine – **cola coloured**; C3 – **low**.
- Fluid allowed in AGN – **insensible loss + previous day urine output**.
- Commonest organism in UTI – **E. coli**; gold standard – **urine culture**.
- Teach girls to wipe – **front to back**.
- Wilms tumour nursing – **do not palpate abdomen**.
- Hypospadias – **ventral opening; avoid circumcision**.
- Orchidopexy age – **6–12 months**.
- Enuresis diagnosed after – **5 years**; most effective therapy – **enuresis alarm**.
- HUS triad – **haemolytic anaemia, thrombocytopenia, AKI**.
:::
