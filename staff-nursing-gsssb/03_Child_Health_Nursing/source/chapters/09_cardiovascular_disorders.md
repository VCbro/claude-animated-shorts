## 9.1 Circulation Changes at Birth

- Fetal shunts – **ductus venosus** (umbilical vein → IVC), **foramen ovale** (RA → LA), **ductus arteriosus** (pulmonary artery → aorta).
- At birth – lungs expand → **pulmonary resistance ↓**, **LA pressure > RA → foramen ovale closes (functional within hours)**; ↑ PaO₂ & ↓ prostaglandins → **ductus arteriosus closes functionally in 10–15 hrs (anatomically 2–3 wks)**.
- Remnants – **fossa ovalis, ligamentum arteriosum, ligamentum venosum, ligamentum teres** (umbilical vein), **medial umbilical ligaments** (umbilical arteries).

## 9.2 Congenital Heart Disease (CHD)

- **Incidence ~8–10 per 1000 live births**. Causes – mostly multifactorial; **maternal rubella (PDA, PS)**, **diabetes (TGA, VSD)**, alcohol, drugs (lithium – **Ebstein's**; warfarin; phenytoin), **chromosomal – Down syndrome (AVSD/ endocardial cushion defect)**, **Turner (coarctation)**, **Marfan**, **DiGeorge (truncus, interrupted arch)**.

| | **Acyanotic (L → R shunt / obstruction)** | **Cyanotic (R → L shunt)** |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | **VSD (most common CHD)**, **ASD**, **PDA**, AVSD; obstructive – **coarctation, aortic stenosis, pulmonary stenosis** | **TOF (most common cyanotic after infancy)**, **TGA (most common cyanotic in newborn)**, tricuspid atresia, TAPVC, truncus arteriosus, Ebstein's, HLHS |
| Mnemonic | – | **"5 T's"** – TOF, TGA, Tricuspid atresia, TAPVC, Truncus arteriosus |
| Features | **CHF, recurrent chest infections, FTT**, murmur | **Cyanosis, clubbing, polycythaemia, squatting, hypoxic spells**, brain abscess, stroke |

| Defect | Key points |
|---|---|
| **VSD** | **Most common CHD (~25–30 %)**; **pansystolic murmur, left lower sternal border**; small (muscular) may **close spontaneously**; large → CHF, pulmonary HTN → **Eisenmenger syndrome** (reversal R → L, cyanosis) |
| **ASD (secundum – commonest)** | **Wide fixed split S2** (pathognomonic); often asymptomatic childhood; ejection systolic murmur (pulmonary area) |
| **PDA** | **Preterms, rubella**; **continuous "machinery" murmur** (left infraclavicular), **bounding pulses, wide pulse pressure**; closure – **indomethacin/ ibuprofen/ paracetamol** (preterm), device/ surgical ligation; **keep open in duct-dependent lesions – Prostaglandin E1 (alprostadil)** |
| **Coarctation of aorta** | Narrowing near ductus; **upper limb HTN, weak/ delayed femoral pulses (radio-femoral delay)**, **BP arms > legs**; X-ray – **rib notching (> 8 yrs)**, **"3" sign**; Turner syndrome |
| **TOF** | 4 defects – **VSD, pulmonary stenosis, overriding aorta, RVH**; **boot-shaped heart (coeur en sabot)**; cyanosis after 3–6 months; **squatting**; **tet spells** |
| **TGA** | Aorta from RV, PA from LV – **parallel circulation**; severe cyanosis at birth; **"egg on a string"** X-ray; survival needs mixing (PDA/ ASD/ VSD) – **PGE1, balloon atrial septostomy (Rashkind)**, **arterial switch (Jatene)** |
| **TAPVC** | **"Snowman / figure of 8"** heart (supracardiac) |
| **Ebstein's anomaly** | Downward displaced tricuspid; maternal **lithium**; **"box-shaped" heart** |
| **HLHS** | Most common cause of cardiac death in first week |

### Hypercyanotic (Tet) spell – nursing
- Triggers – crying, feeding, defecation, dehydration, waking up; peak **2–4 months to 2 yrs**, morning.
- S/S – ↑ cyanosis, hyperpnoea, irritability, **disappearance/ softening of murmur**, limpness, convulsions, LOC.
- **Management**: **knee-chest position** (↑ SVR → ↓ R→L shunt), **calm the child (parent's arms)**, **humidified O₂**, **morphine 0.1–0.2 mg/kg SC/ IV** (↓ infundibular spasm, hyperpnoea), **IV fluids, sodium bicarbonate** (acidosis), **propranolol** (prevention & acute), phenylephrine (↑ SVR); avoid dehydration (polycythaemia → stroke).
- Older children **squat** spontaneously after exertion.
- Surgery – **Blalock-Taussig (BT) shunt** (palliative – subclavian to pulmonary artery), **total correction at ~6–12 months**.

### Nursing care in CHD
- Assess – colour, **apical pulse 1 min**, RR, **SpO₂ (pre- & post-ductal)**, feeding difficulty (**sweating during feeds**, tires easily), weight gain, edema.
- **Feeding** – **small frequent feeds**, **soft nipple with larger hole**, rest during feeds, **semi-upright position**, high-calorie formula/ fortified EBM, NG if needed (feeding time ≤ 30 min).
- **Rest**, organize care, avoid crying, **prevent infection** (hand hygiene, immunization – including influenza, PCV), **dental hygiene** (infective endocarditis prophylaxis in selected cyanotic CHD before dental procedures).
- **Hydration** in cyanotic CHD (polycythaemia – thrombosis), **iron supplementation** (relative anaemia).
- **Cardiac catheterisation post-care** – **check distal pulses, colour, warmth below the site every 15 min** (first hour), **keep limb straight 4–6 hrs**, pressure dressing, bleeding, vitals, hydration.
- Parent education – **CPR**, signs of CHF, medications (digoxin).

## 9.3 Congestive Heart Failure (CHF) in Children

- Causes – **infants: CHD (large VSD, PDA, AVSD – most common)**; older: **rheumatic heart disease**, myocarditis, cardiomyopathy, severe anaemia, hypertension (AGN).
- **S/S in infants** – **tachypnoea, tachycardia, feeding difficulty, sweating (forehead) during feeds**, **poor weight gain/ FTT**, **hepatomegaly (most reliable sign of right failure in infants)**, gallop rhythm, wheeze, **periorbital oedema** (peripheral oedema & ↑ JVP uncommon in infants), cardiomegaly.
- **Management**: **propped-up (semi-Fowler's/ infant seat 45°)**, **O₂**, **restrict fluids & sodium**, small frequent high-calorie feeds, **diuretics (frusemide – monitor K⁺)**, **ACE inhibitors (captopril/ enalapril)**, **digoxin**, daily weight & I/O, treat cause.

:::warn Digoxin in children
- **Check apical pulse for 1 full minute before giving**: **withhold if HR < 90–110/min (infant)**, **< 70 (older child)**, **< 60 (adolescent/ adult)** – inform doctor.
- Measure dose exactly (**0.1 ml can be a big difference**), give **1 hr before or 2 hrs after feeds**, don't mix in food, **don't repeat dose if vomited**; regular intervals.
- **Toxicity – vomiting (earliest in children), nausea, anorexia, bradycardia, arrhythmias**, visual disturbances; **hypokalaemia ↑ toxicity** (diuretics) – antidote **digoxin-specific Fab (Digibind)**.
:::

## 9.4 Rheumatic Fever & Rheumatic Heart Disease

- **Autoimmune reaction** 2–4 weeks after **Group A β-haemolytic streptococcal pharyngitis** (**not skin infection**); age **5–15 yrs**; **molecular mimicry** (M protein).
- **Revised Jones criteria (AHA 2015)**: **2 major OR 1 major + 2 minor**, **plus evidence of preceding GAS infection** (↑/ rising **ASO titre**, throat culture, rapid antigen test).

| Major (J♥NES) | Minor |
|---|---|
| **J** – Joints: **migratory polyarthritis** (large joints; most common, ~75 %, **heals without deformity**) | **Fever** (≥ 38.5 °C) |
| **♥** – **Carditis** (pancarditis; **most serious – only one causing permanent damage**; mitral valve most affected) | **Arthralgia** |
| **N** – Subcutaneous **Nodules** (painless, over bony prominences) | **↑ ESR (≥ 60)/ CRP (≥ 3 mg/dl)** |
| **E** – **Erythema marginatum** (non-itchy, trunk, evanescent) | **Prolonged PR interval** (ECG) |
| **S** – **Sydenham's chorea** (St. Vitus dance – late, may occur alone; emotional lability) | |

- **Treatment** – **bed rest** (strict in carditis), **eradicate strep – benzathine penicillin G 1.2 MU IM single dose** (0.6 MU if < 27 kg) or oral penicillin/ amoxicillin × 10 days; **aspirin (anti-inflammatory) 75–100 mg/kg/day** for arthritis; **steroids (prednisolone) for severe carditis**; haloperidol for chorea.
- **Secondary prophylaxis (most important)** – **benzathine penicillin 1.2 MU IM every 21 days (3 weeks)** in India; duration – **no carditis: 5 yrs or till 21 yrs (whichever longer)**; carditis without residual: 10 yrs or till 21; **residual valve disease: 10 yrs or till 40 yrs (sometimes lifelong)**.
- **Primary prevention** – treat streptococcal sore throat with penicillin × 10 days.
- **RHD** – **mitral regurgitation (commonest in children)**, mitral stenosis (adults), aortic regurgitation.
- **Nursing** – rest, **monitor pulse (sleeping pulse ↑ = carditis)**, joint care (warm compress, support with pillows, cradle), safety in chorea (padded side rails), **compliance with penicillin prophylaxis** (test dose, anaphylaxis tray), nutrition, school continuity.

## 9.5 Other Disorders

- **Infective endocarditis** – Strep viridans (post-dental), Staph aureus; fever, new murmur, splinter haemorrhages, Osler's nodes, **Janeway lesions**, Roth spots; **blood cultures × 3** before antibiotics; IV antibiotics 4–6 weeks.
- **Kawasaki disease (mucocutaneous lymph node syndrome)** – < 5 yrs; **fever ≥ 5 days** + 4 of 5 (**CRASH**): **C**onjunctivitis (bilateral, non-purulent), **R**ash, **A**denopathy (cervical, unilateral), **S**trawberry tongue/ cracked red lips, **H**ands & feet – oedema, erythema, **periungual desquamation** (2nd–3rd week); complication – **coronary artery aneurysm** (most serious; echo); Rx – **IVIG 2 g/kg** single infusion + **aspirin** (high dose → low dose); **one of the few indications of aspirin in children**.
- **Hypertension in children** – BP ≥ 95th centile; **secondary HTN more common in young children (renal – most common)**.
- **Myocarditis** – viral (Coxsackie B).
