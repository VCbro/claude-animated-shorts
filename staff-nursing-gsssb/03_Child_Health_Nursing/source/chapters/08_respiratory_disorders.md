# Respiratory Disorders in Children
<!-- desc: ARI, common cold, tonsillitis, otitis media, croup, epiglottitis, bronchiolitis, pneumonia, asthma, foreign body, TB in children, cystic fibrosis, nursing care -->

## 8.1 Why Children Are More Prone

- **Narrow airways** (small oedema → big obstruction – resistance ∝ 1/r⁴), **short, horizontal Eustachian tube** (otitis media), **obligatory nose breathers (up to 3–4 months)**, large tongue, **large occiput**, funnel-shaped larynx – **narrowest at cricoid (< 8 yrs)**, immature immunity, ↑ metabolic rate & O₂ demand, abdominal breathing.
- **ARI (Acute Respiratory Infection)** – most common cause of OPD visits; **pneumonia – leading infectious killer of under-5 children**.

## 8.2 Upper Respiratory Tract

| Condition | Key points & nursing |
|---|---|
| **Common cold (nasopharyngitis)** | **Rhinovirus**; nasal saline drops, suction before feeds, fluids, no antibiotics |
| **Pharyngitis/ Tonsillitis** | Viral or **Group A β-haemolytic Streptococcus** (**risk of rheumatic fever, AGN**) → **penicillin/ amoxicillin × 10 days**; warm saline gargles |
| **Tonsillectomy** | Indications – **recurrent tonsillitis (≥ 7/yr, 5/yr × 2 yrs, 3/yr × 3 yrs)**, obstructive sleep apnoea, peritonsillar abscess (quinsy); **post-op: prone/ side-lying (tonsillar position)**, **ice collar**, **cold fluids, ice cream**; avoid **red/ brown fluids, straws, citrus, hot food**, avoid vigorous coughing/ nose blowing; **frequent swallowing = earliest sign of haemorrhage** (within 24 hrs/ 5–10 days); **avoid aspirin** |
| **Acute otitis media (AOM)** | **Short, wide, horizontal Eustachian tube**; **S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae**; ear pulling, fever, irritability; **bottle feeding in supine position** ↑ risk; Rx – amoxicillin; **myringotomy/ grommets** for recurrent; **CSOM** – wicking, keep ear dry |
| **Sinusitis, adenoid hypertrophy** | Adenoid facies, mouth breathing, snoring |

## 8.3 Croup & Epiglottitis

| | **Croup (Laryngotracheobronchitis)** | **Acute Epiglottitis** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Parainfluenza virus** (commonest) | **H. influenzae type b** (bacterial) |
| Age | **6 months – 3 yrs** | **2–6 yrs** |
| Onset | Gradual (after URI), worse at night | **Sudden, rapid progression** |
| Features | **Barking (seal-like) cough**, **inspiratory stridor**, hoarseness, low fever | **High fever, toxic, drooling, dysphagia, dysphonia, tripod position**, **no cough**, muffled voice |
| X-ray | **Steeple sign** (subglottic narrowing, AP view) | **Thumb sign** (swollen epiglottis, lateral view) |
| Treatment | **Humidified O₂/ cool mist, dexamethasone (0.15–0.6 mg/kg), nebulised adrenaline** | **Airway first – intubation in OT**, IV ceftriaxone; **prevented by Hib vaccine** |

:::warn Epiglottitis – NEVER examine the throat
**Do not use tongue depressor or throat swab**, don't lay child supine, keep child calm in parent's lap (sitting) – can trigger **complete laryngospasm/ obstruction**. Emergency tracheostomy/ intubation set at bedside.
:::

- **Spasmodic croup** – sudden night attacks, no fever. **Diphtheria** – grey pseudomembrane, bull neck (see communicable diseases).
- **Laryngomalacia** – most common cause of **congenital stridor** (floppy larynx, improves by 1–2 yrs).

## 8.4 Lower Respiratory Tract

### Bronchiolitis
- **RSV (Respiratory Syncytial Virus) – commonest cause**; **< 2 yrs (peak 2–6 months)**, winter.
- Coryza → **cough, wheeze, tachypnoea, chest retractions, hyperinflation, fine crepitations**, apnoea in young infants.
- **Management – supportive**: **humidified O₂ (SpO₂ < 90–92 %)**, nasal suction, hydration (NG/ IV), small frequent feeds; **bronchodilators/ steroids/ antibiotics NOT routinely**; hypertonic saline nebulisation (some); **contact isolation** (RSV spreads by hands/ fomites); **palivizumab** (prophylaxis in high risk).

### Pneumonia
- **Most common cause – S. pneumoniae** (bacterial); **viral – RSV**; **Staph aureus** (post-measles, pneumatocele, empyema); **H. influenzae**; neonates – GBS, E. coli; **atypical – Mycoplasma** (school age).
- **Types**: lobar, **bronchopneumonia (most common in children)**, interstitial.
- **S/S** – **fever, cough, fast breathing (most sensitive sign)**, **chest indrawing**, nasal flaring, **grunting**, cyanosis, crepitations, bronchial breathing.
- **Investigations** – CXR, CBC, CRP, blood culture, **pulse oximetry**.
- **Treatment** – **amoxicillin (oral) – non-severe**; **severe – injectable ampicillin + gentamicin / ceftriaxone**; O₂ if SpO₂ < 90 %.
- **Nursing** – **semi-Fowler's/ propped-up position**, O₂, **airway clearance (suction, chest physiotherapy, postural drainage)**, tepid sponging/ paracetamol for fever, **hydration** (avoid fluid overload), small frequent feeds (NG if RR > 60–70), rest, monitor RR/ SpO₂, isolation if needed.
- **Complications** – **empyema (Staph)**, pleural effusion, lung abscess, **pneumatocele**, sepsis, respiratory failure.
- **Prevention** – **PCV, Hib, measles, pertussis vaccines**, exclusive BF, nutrition, **reduce indoor air pollution**, **SAANS campaign (2019)**.

### Bronchial Asthma
- **Chronic inflammatory airway disease** with **reversible** airway obstruction, bronchial hyperresponsiveness; **most common chronic disease in children**.
- Triggers – **allergens (house dust mite – commonest)**, smoke, cold air, viral infections, exercise, pollen, pets, aspirin.
- **S/S** – **recurrent wheeze (expiratory)**, cough (**nocturnal**), chest tightness, breathlessness; **silent chest = ominous (severe)**.
- **Investigations** – **PEFR**, spirometry (**> 5 yrs**) – **↑ FEV₁ ≥ 12 % after bronchodilator**.
- **Acute attack** – **O₂, nebulised salbutamol (SABA) – first-line (every 20 min × 3)**, **ipratropium**, **systemic steroids (prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg)**, MgSO₄ IV (severe), **aminophylline** (rarely).
- **Long-term** – **inhaled corticosteroids (budesonide/ fluticasone) – most effective controller**, LABA (+ICS > 5 yrs), montelukast.
- **Inhaler devices**: **< 4 yrs – MDI + spacer with face mask**; **4–8 yrs – MDI + spacer (mouthpiece)**; > 8 yrs – DPI/ MDI; **rinse mouth after ICS (oral thrush)**.
- **Nursing** – **high Fowler's / tripod**, calm child, avoid triggers, **peak flow diary**, action plan (green/ yellow/ red zones), exercise allowed with pre-treatment.

### Foreign Body Aspiration
- **Common 1–3 yrs**; peanuts, beads, coins (coin → oesophagus).
- **Right main bronchus** – more common (**wider, shorter, more vertical**).
- Sudden **choking, coughing, wheeze (unilateral)**, stridor, decreased air entry; X-ray – **obstructive emphysema (check-valve)**/ collapse.
- **Rx – rigid bronchoscopy**. First aid – **infant: 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts**; **child > 1 yr: abdominal thrusts (Heimlich)**; **no blind finger sweep**.

### Cystic Fibrosis
- **Autosomal recessive**, **CFTR gene (chromosome 7)**; thick secretions – lungs, pancreas, intestines.
- **Meconium ileus (earliest manifestation)**, **salty skin ("salty kiss")**, steatorrhoea (bulky, greasy, foul stools), recurrent chest infections (**Pseudomonas, Staph**), FTT.
- **Diagnosis – sweat chloride test > 60 mEq/L (pilocarpine iontophoresis)**.
- **Rx** – **chest physiotherapy (postural drainage) 2–4 times/day**, pancreatic enzymes **with meals**, high-calorie high-protein diet with **extra salt & fat-soluble vitamins (A, D, E, K)**, antibiotics, dornase alfa.

## 8.5 Tuberculosis in Children

- Usually **primary complex (Ghon focus + hilar lymph node)**; **sputum often unavailable** – **gastric aspirate (early morning, 2 consecutive days)**, induced sputum; **CBNAAT/ GeneXpert**; **Mantoux (TST)** – **0.1 ml (5 TU) PPD intradermal, read at 48–72 hrs; ≥ 10 mm induration = positive** (≥ 5 mm in HIV/ SAM).
- Forms – pulmonary, **TB lymphadenitis (cervical) – most common extrapulmonary**, **TB meningitis** (most serious), miliary TB, spinal (Pott's).
- **Treatment (NTEP)** – **2HRZE + 4HRE** (intensive 2 months + continuation 4 months), **weight-band paediatric FDC (dispersible)**; **TBM/ bone TB – 2HRZE + 10HRE** (12 months) + steroids.
- **TB preventive therapy (TPT)** – **child < 5 yrs household contact** of pulmonary TB (after excluding active TB) – **INH 10 mg/kg × 6 months** (or 3HP for older children).
- **Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana** – **₹ 1000/month (increased from ₹ 500 in 2024)** nutrition support during treatment.
- **BCG** – protects against **severe forms (TBM, miliary)** in children.
