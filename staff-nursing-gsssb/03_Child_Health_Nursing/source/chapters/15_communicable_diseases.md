## 15.1 Viral Exanthems

| Disease | Agent | Incubation | Key features | Infectivity/ isolation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Measles (Rubeola)** | Paramyxovirus (morbillivirus) | **10–14 days** | **Prodrome – fever, 3 C's (cough, coryza, conjunctivitis)**, **Koplik's spots (pathognomonic – white spots on red base, buccal mucosa opposite lower molars, before rash)**; **maculopapular rash on day 4 – behind ears/ hairline → face → trunk → limbs (centrifugal, downward)**, fades with **brown staining & branny desquamation** | **4 days before to 4 days after rash** – **airborne isolation** |
| **Rubella (German/ 3-day measles)** | Togavirus | 14–21 days | Mild fever, **post-auricular & sub-occipital lymphadenopathy**, pink rash (face → body, 3 days), **Forchheimer spots** (soft palate); arthralgia | 7 days before to 7 days after rash; **keep away from pregnant women** |
| **Chickenpox (Varicella)** | **Varicella-zoster virus** | **14–16 days (10–21)** | **Centripetal rash (trunk > limbs)**, **pleomorphic – all stages at same time (macule → papule → vesicle → pustule → crust) in crops**, **"dew drop on rose petal"**, **intensely itchy**, involves scalp & mucosa | **1–2 days before rash till all lesions crusted** (~5–7 days) – airborne + contact |
| **Mumps** | Paramyxovirus | **16–18 days** | **Parotitis (bilateral ~70 %)** – earache, pain on chewing/ sour food; ear lobe pushed up & out | 7 days before to 9 days after swelling (droplet) |
| **Roseola infantum (exanthem subitum)** | **HHV-6** | 5–15 days | **6 months–2 yrs, high fever 3–4 days → rash appears when fever subsides**; febrile seizures | – |
| **Erythema infectiosum (fifth disease)** | **Parvovirus B19** | – | **"Slapped cheek"** appearance, lacy rash; aplastic crisis in sickle cell; hydrops fetalis | – |
| **Hand, foot & mouth disease** | **Coxsackie A16, Enterovirus 71** | 3–5 days | Vesicles on **palms, soles, mouth**, buttocks; < 5 yrs | – |
| **Scarlet fever** (bacterial) | **Group A Strep** | 2–4 days | **Sandpaper rash**, **strawberry tongue**, **Pastia's lines**, circumoral pallor; penicillin | – |

### Measles – details
- **Complications** – **pneumonia (most common cause of death)**, **diarrhoea**, otitis media, **malnutrition/ vitamin A deficiency → corneal ulcer/ blindness**, encephalitis, **SSPE** (subacute sclerosing panencephalitis – 7–10 yrs later), **flare-up of TB** (immunosuppression – Mantoux may become negative).
- **Treatment** – **supportive + vitamin A (2 doses on 2 consecutive days: < 6 m 50,000 IU; 6–11 m 1 lakh IU; ≥ 12 m 2 lakh IU; third dose after 2–4 wks if eye signs)**, eye care (clean with saline), fluids, nutrition, isolation.
- **Prevention** – **MR vaccine at 9–12 & 16–24 months**; **measles vaccine within 72 hrs of exposure** (post-exposure); **immunoglobulin within 6 days**; **Measles-Rubella elimination target** (India).

### Chickenpox – nursing
- **Calamine lotion, antihistamines**, **cut nails short / mittens** (prevent scratching & secondary infection), cool baths with baking soda/ oatmeal, loose cotton clothes, **paracetamol for fever – NEVER aspirin (Reye's syndrome)**, oral hygiene, **isolate till all lesions crusted**.
- **Acyclovir** – adolescents, immunocompromised, severe; **VZIG** – high-risk exposed (newborn of mother with rash 5 days before–2 days after delivery, immunocompromised).
- **Complications** – **secondary bacterial skin infection (most common)**, pneumonia, encephalitis (cerebellar ataxia), Reye's; **congenital varicella syndrome** (1st/ 2nd trimester – limb hypoplasia, scarring); **herpes zoster (shingles)** later – reactivation.

### Mumps – complications & care
- **Orchitis (post-pubertal males – sterility rare)**, **aseptic meningitis (most common extra-salivary)**, pancreatitis, oophoritis, **sensorineural deafness (unilateral)**, encephalitis.
- **Soft/ liquid diet, avoid sour/ acidic foods**, warm/ cold compress, oral hygiene, scrotal support & ice packs in orchitis, analgesics.

## 15.2 Vaccine-Preventable Bacterial Diseases

| Disease | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Diphtheria** | ***Corynebacterium diphtheriae*** (exotoxin); **2–5 days** IP; **greyish-white adherent pseudomembrane** (bleeds on removal) on tonsils/ pharynx, **"bull neck"** (cervical adenitis + oedema), low fever, toxic; **laryngeal → stridor, airway obstruction**; **complications – myocarditis (most common cause of death, 2nd week), palatal/ neuritic palsy**; Dx – throat swab (**Albert's stain – Chinese letter pattern, Loeffler's serum slope**); **Rx – Anti-Diphtheritic Serum (ADS) immediately (after test dose)** + **penicillin/ erythromycin**; **strict isolation till 2 negative cultures**; **bed rest 2–3 weeks** (myocarditis); contacts – erythromycin + booster; **Schick test** (susceptibility) |
| **Pertussis (whooping cough)** | ***Bordetella pertussis***; **7–14 days** IP; **3 stages – catarrhal (1–2 wks, most infectious), paroxysmal (2–6 wks – bouts of cough ending in inspiratory "whoop", vomiting, face red/ cyanosed, tongue protruded), convalescent ("100-day cough")**; infants – apnoea instead of whoop; **marked lymphocytosis**; complications – **pneumonia (death), subconjunctival haemorrhage, hernia, rectal prolapse, convulsions, encephalopathy**; **Rx – azithromycin/ erythromycin** (reduces infectivity); **droplet isolation 5 days after antibiotics**; small frequent feeds (**re-feed after vomiting**), suction, O₂, quiet environment |
| **Tetanus neonatorum** | ***Clostridium tetani*** (spores – **unclean cord cutting/ dressing – cow dung, ash**); **onset day 3–10 ("8th-day disease")**; **first sign – inability to suck (trismus)**, excessive crying, **risus sardonicus**, **spasms/ rigidity, opisthotonus**, triggered by touch/ sound/ light; **CFR very high (~80 %)**; Rx – **TIG (human tetanus immunoglobulin 500 IU IM)**, **diazepam** for spasms, metronidazole/ penicillin, NG feeding, airway; **quiet, dark room, minimal handling**; **prevention – Td to mother (2 doses/ booster) + clean delivery (6 cleans)**; **India eliminated maternal & neonatal tetanus (MNTE) – May 2015** (< 1 case per 1000 live births in every district) |
| **Tetanus (child)** | Wound contamination; trismus (lockjaw); TIG + vaccine + wound care |
| **Poliomyelitis** | **Poliovirus (types 1, 2, 3; feco-oral)**; **anterior horn cells** → **acute flaccid paralysis – asymmetric, proximal > distal, legs**, **no sensory loss**, fever at onset; most infections asymptomatic (~90 %+); **India polio-free since 27 March 2014** (WHO SEAR); **AFP surveillance – any child < 15 yrs with acute flaccid paralysis (or any age with suspected polio) → 2 stool samples 24 hrs apart within 14 days of onset (reverse cold chain)**; nursing – **bed rest in acute phase (no IM injections – provocation paralysis)**, **positioning – footboard (foot drop), hot packs**, physiotherapy after acute phase, respiratory support (bulbar) |
| **Typhoid (enteric fever)** | ***Salmonella Typhi*** (feco-oral); **step-ladder fever**, relative bradycardia, **coated tongue**, **rose spots (trunk)**, splenomegaly, abdominal pain, constipation/ diarrhoea; complications – **intestinal perforation & haemorrhage (3rd week)**; **blood culture – 1st week (best)**, **Widal (after 1st week, ≥ 1:160 O)**, stool culture (3rd week); Rx – **ceftriaxone/ cefixime/ azithromycin**; **typhoid conjugate vaccine (TCV) ≥ 6 months**; carriers – gallbladder |

## 15.3 Vector-Borne Diseases

### Dengue
- **Dengue virus (4 serotypes) – Aedes aegypti (day-biting, breeds in clean stored water)**; IP 4–7 days.
- **Phases** – **febrile (2–7 days – high fever, retro-orbital pain, myalgia "breakbone fever", rash, positive tourniquet test)** → **critical (around defervescence, 24–48 hrs – plasma leakage)** → recovery (convalescent rash – "white islands in sea of red", bradycardia, fluid reabsorption).
- **Warning signs** – **abdominal pain/ tenderness, persistent vomiting, clinical fluid accumulation (ascites, effusion), mucosal bleeding, lethargy/ restlessness, liver enlargement > 2 cm, ↑ Hct with rapid ↓ platelets**.
- **Severe dengue** – shock (**dengue shock syndrome – narrow pulse pressure ≤ 20 mmHg**), severe bleeding, organ impairment.
- **Diagnosis** – **NS1 antigen (day 1–5)**, **IgM ELISA (after day 5)**; CBC – **thrombocytopenia, ↑ haematocrit (haemoconcentration)**, leucopenia.
- **Management** – **paracetamol for fever (NO aspirin/ NSAIDs/ steroids)**, **oral fluids (ORS, juices)**, tepid sponging, **monitor Hct & platelets**, IV crystalloids for warning signs/ shock (5–10–20 ml/kg as per protocol), **platelet transfusion only if bleeding** (or < 10,000 with risk), watch fluid overload in recovery phase.
- **Prevention** – **source reduction (empty water containers weekly – "dry day")**, mosquito nets/ repellents, full-sleeve clothes; **National Dengue Day – 16 May**.

### Malaria
- ***Plasmodium vivax* (most common in India)**, ***P. falciparum*** (**severe/ cerebral malaria – most dangerous**); **female Anopheles (night-biting)**.
- **Paroxysm** – cold stage (chills) → hot stage → sweating stage; anaemia, splenomegaly; **children – severe anaemia, hypoglycaemia, convulsions, cerebral malaria**.
- **Diagnosis** – **RDT (rapid diagnostic test – bivalent)**, **peripheral blood smear (gold standard – thick for detection, thin for species)**.
- **Treatment (NVBDCP/ NCVBDC)** – **vivax: chloroquine 3 days (25 mg/kg total) + primaquine 0.25 mg/kg × 14 days** (radical cure; **not < 1 yr / pregnancy / G6PD deficiency**); **falciparum: ACT (artesunate + sulfadoxine-pyrimethamine; AL in north-east) + primaquine single dose (0.75 mg/kg) on day 2**; **severe malaria – IV/ IM artesunate**.
- **Prevention** – **LLINs (long-lasting insecticidal nets)**, IRS, source reduction; **World Malaria Day – 25 April**.

### Others
- **Japanese encephalitis** – Culex (rice fields), pigs amplify; vaccine in endemic districts.
- **Chikungunya** – Aedes, severe arthralgia.
- **Kala-azar** – sandfly, Leishmania donovani; Bihar/ UP; hepatosplenomegaly, darkening skin.
- **Scrub typhus** – mite (chigger) – **eschar**; doxycycline/ azithromycin; AES cause.
- **Leptospirosis** – rat urine, flood water; jaundice, renal failure.
- **Rabies** – dog bite; **wash wound with soap & running water 15 min (most important first aid)**; **ARV (intradermal 0.1 ml at 2 sites on days 0, 3, 7, 28 – Updated Thai Red Cross)** / IM; **RIG (rabies immunoglobulin) for category III** – infiltrated into wound; **hydrophobia, aerophobia**; 100 % fatal once symptoms appear.

## 15.4 HIV/ AIDS in Children

- **Mother-to-child transmission (MTCT) – most common route (~90 %)** – pregnancy, **labour & delivery (most)**, breastfeeding.
- **PPTCT (Prevention of Parent-To-Child Transmission)** – **universal HIV screening of pregnant women**, **lifelong ART for mother (TLD – tenofovir + lamivudine + dolutegravir)** irrespective of CD4, **infant prophylaxis – nevirapine syrup × 6 weeks (low risk); high risk – nevirapine + zidovudine × 12 weeks**, **exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months (mother on ART)**, **cotrimoxazole prophylaxis from 6 weeks** till HIV excluded.
- **Infant diagnosis** – **HIV DNA PCR (EID – Early Infant Diagnosis via DBS) at 6 weeks, 6 months, 12 months**; **antibody test valid only after 18 months** (maternal antibodies persist).
- **S/S** – FTT, persistent diarrhoea, **oral thrush (recurrent)**, recurrent pneumonia (**PCP – Pneumocystis jirovecii** in infants), lymphadenopathy, hepatosplenomegaly, parotid enlargement, developmental delay, TB.
- **Immunization** – all UIP vaccines; **BCG avoided in symptomatic HIV**; live vaccines with caution if severe immunosuppression.
- **Nursing** – **standard precautions**, ART adherence (> 95 %), nutrition, infection prevention, **disclosure (age-appropriate)**, stigma reduction, confidentiality.

## 15.5 Skin Infections & Infestations (brief)

| Condition | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Scabies** | *Sarcoptes scabiei*; **intense nocturnal itching**, burrows in **web spaces, wrists, axillae, genitals** (infants – palms, soles, face/ scalp); **permethrin 5 % cream** – apply **neck down (whole body incl. scalp in infants)**, leave **8–12 hrs**, repeat after 1 week; **treat all family members simultaneously**; wash clothes/ bedding in hot water/ sun-dry |
| **Pediculosis capitis (head lice)** | Nits on hair shafts; **permethrin 1 %**, fine-toothed comb |
| **Impetigo** | **Staph aureus/ Strep**; **honey-coloured crusts** around nose/ mouth; contagious; **mupirocin**, oral antibiotics; risk of **AGN** (strep) |
| **Oral thrush** | Candida – white patches not easily wiped off (unlike milk curds); **nystatin/ clotrimazole** |
| **Diaper (napkin) dermatitis** | Ammonia/ irritant; **keep dry, frequent changes, zinc oxide barrier**, air exposure; candidal – satellite lesions → clotrimazole |
| **Atopic eczema** | Itchy, flexural (older child), cheeks (infant); emollients, avoid soap, topical steroids; **elbow restraints/ mittens** |

## 15.6 Isolation & Incubation – Quick Table

| Disease | Incubation | Isolation |
|---|---|---|
| Measles | 10–14 days | Airborne – till 4 days after rash |
| Chickenpox | 14–16 days | Airborne + contact – till all crusted |
| Mumps | 16–18 days | Droplet – till 9 days after swelling (5 days CDC) |
| Rubella | 14–21 days | Droplet – 7 days after rash |
| Diphtheria | 2–5 days | Droplet – till 2 negative cultures |
| Pertussis | 7–14 days | Droplet – 5 days after antibiotic start |
| Meningococcal | 3–4 days | Droplet – 24 hrs after antibiotics |
| Hepatitis A | 15–45 days (~30) | Contact/ enteric |
| Typhoid | 10–14 days | Enteric precautions |
| Tetanus | 3–21 days (neonatal 3–10) | No isolation (not communicable) |
