## 12.1 Hydrocephalus

:::def Hydrocephalus
Excess accumulation of **CSF in the ventricles** due to **obstruction, impaired absorption or (rarely) over-production** → ventricular dilatation & ↑ ICP. Normal CSF ~ **500 ml/day produced** (choroid plexus), volume ~ 50 ml infant / 150 ml adult.
:::

- **Types** – **Non-communicating (obstructive) – most common in children** (**aqueductal stenosis – commonest cause**, Arnold-Chiari II, Dandy-Walker, tumour); **communicating** – impaired absorption at arachnoid villi (post-meningitis, haemorrhage).
- **S/S in infants (sutures open)** – **rapid increase in head circumference (earliest & most important)**, **bulging, tense anterior fontanelle**, **separated sutures**, **Macewen's sign ("cracked-pot" sound on percussion)**, **"setting-sun" eyes** (sclera visible above iris), **dilated scalp veins, shiny thin scalp**, **high-pitched (shrill) cry**, irritability, poor feeding, vomiting, frontal bossing, **head lag**, spasticity.
- **Older children (sutures closed)** – classic ↑ ICP – **headache (morning), projectile vomiting, papilloedema**, diplopia, ataxia, behaviour change; **Cushing's triad** (late) – ↑ BP (wide pulse pressure), ↓ pulse, irregular respiration.
- **Diagnosis** – **serial OFC**, USG (through AF), **CT/ MRI**, transillumination (hydranencephaly).
- **Treatment** – **VP (ventriculoperitoneal) shunt – most common** (one-way valve; peritoneal end allows growth), **ETV (endoscopic third ventriculostomy)** for obstructive; acetazolamide (temporary).

### Nursing care
- **Pre-op** – **measure OFC daily** (same site, same time), neuro checks, monitor ↑ ICP, **support large head** while lifting/ feeding (**prevent neck strain**), **change position 2-hrly; skin care on head (sheepskin/ water pillow – pressure sores)**, small frequent feeds (vomiting).
- **Post-op (VP shunt)** – **position flat (supine) initially** – avoids rapid CSF drainage (subdural haematoma) – raise head gradually per order; **lie on unoperated side** (avoid pressure on valve/ shunt site); check **fontanelle (sunken = overdrainage, bulging = blockage)**, OFC, neuro & vitals; watch abdominal distension (peritonitis, ileus); **pump the shunt only if ordered**.
- **Complications** – **shunt infection (most common early, within 2 months – fever, redness along tract)**, **shunt malfunction/ blockage (most common overall – signs of ↑ ICP)**, over-drainage, subdural haematoma, **need for revision with growth**.
- **Parent teaching** – signs of ↑ ICP & infection, development stimulation, regular follow-up.

## 12.2 Neural Tube Defects (NTDs)

- Failure of **neural tube closure by day 28** (anterior neuropore day 25, posterior day 27–28).
- **Anencephaly** (absent brain/ skull – incompatible with life), **encephalocele** (occipital commonest), **spina bifida**.

| Type | Features |
|---|---|
| **Spina bifida occulta** | Vertebral arch defect only, **no sac**; **tuft of hair, dimple, lipoma, haemangioma** over lumbosacral area; usually asymptomatic |
| **Meningocele** | Sac contains **meninges + CSF** only; **no neurological deficit** (usually); transilluminates |
| **Meningomyelocele (myelomeningocele) – most common & severe** | Sac contains **meninges, CSF, spinal cord/ nerve roots**; **lumbosacral** commonest; **flaccid paralysis of lower limbs, loss of sensation, neurogenic bladder (dribbling) & bowel**, club feet, hip dislocation; **associated hydrocephalus (~80–90 %, Arnold-Chiari II)** |

- **Prevention** – **folic acid 400 µg (0.4 mg)/day from ≥ 1 month before conception to 12 wks**; **4–5 mg/day if previous NTD/ on antiepileptics**; fortified foods.
- **Antenatal diagnosis** – **↑ maternal serum AFP & amniotic fluid AFP**, acetylcholinesterase; **USG – lemon sign, banana sign**.
- **Pre-op nursing (meningomyelocele)**:
  - **Prone position** (or side-lying), **hips slightly flexed & abducted** (pad between legs), head turned to side – **no pressure on sac**.
  - **Sterile, moist (warm normal saline) non-adherent dressing** over sac, changed every 2–4 hrs; **plastic drape/ barrier** below sac to **prevent faecal contamination**.
  - **Inspect sac for leakage, tears, infection**; **measure OFC daily** (hydrocephalus); **no rectal temperature**; no diaper over sac.
  - Feed in prone/ side; touch & cuddle without pressure; **latex precautions (high latex allergy risk)**.
- **Surgery** – closure **within 24–72 hrs** (prevents infection – meningitis).
- **Long-term** – **clean intermittent catheterisation (CIC)**, bowel training, mobility aids/ orthoses, skin care (insensate), UTI prevention.

## 12.3 Meningitis

- **Inflammation of meninges** (pia & arachnoid).
- **Organisms**: **neonates – E. coli, Group B Streptococcus, Listeria, Klebsiella**; **2 months–5 yrs – S. pneumoniae, Hib, N. meningitidis**; **> 5 yrs – S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis**; TB meningitis (gradual).
- **S/S** – **fever, headache, vomiting, irritability, photophobia, seizures, altered sensorium**; **neck stiffness (nuchal rigidity)**, **Kernig's sign** (pain on knee extension with hip flexed), **Brudzinski's sign** (neck flexion → hip & knee flexion); **infants – bulging fontanelle (most important), high-pitched cry, poor feeding, lethargy** (neck stiffness may be absent); **meningococcal – petechial/ purpuric rash, Waterhouse-Friderichsen syndrome** (adrenal haemorrhage – shock).
- **Diagnosis – Lumbar puncture (CSF)**:

| CSF | Normal | Bacterial | Viral | Tubercular |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Clear | **Turbid/ purulent** | Clear | Clear/ **cobweb clot** |
| Cells | < 5/mm³ | **↑↑ neutrophils** | ↑ lymphocytes | ↑ lymphocytes |
| Protein | 15–45 mg/dl | **↑↑** | Normal/ ↑ | **↑↑↑** |
| Glucose | ~ 2/3 of blood | **↓↓** | Normal | **↓** |

- **Treatment** – **ceftriaxone/ cefotaxime ± vancomycin**; neonates – ampicillin + cefotaxime/ gentamicin; **dexamethasone** (Hib – reduces deafness, give before/ with first antibiotic dose); anticonvulsants; fluids (SIADH watch).
- **Nursing** – **droplet isolation – for 24 hrs after start of antibiotics** (meningococcal, Hib); **quiet, dark room** (photophobia), minimal stimulation; **head end elevated 30°**, **side-lying (no neck flexion)**; **seizure precautions**; neuro observations (GCS), **OFC daily in infants**; fever control; I/O, daily weight (**SIADH**); hearing test after recovery.
- **Complications** – **sensorineural deafness (most common sequela)**, hydrocephalus, subdural effusion (Hib/ pneumococcus in infants), seizures, cerebral palsy, mental retardation, SIADH.
- **Chemoprophylaxis for contacts** – **rifampicin** (meningococcal, Hib), ciprofloxacin/ ceftriaxone.
- **Prevention** – **Hib, PCV, BCG (TBM)**, meningococcal vaccines.

## 12.4 Encephalitis
- Inflammation of brain parenchyma; **JE (Japanese encephalitis – Culex mosquito, pigs reservoir, ardeid birds)** – endemic districts → JE vaccine; HSV, measles, **AES (Acute Encephalitis Syndrome)** – fever + altered sensorium/ seizures.
- Supportive – airway, ICP control, seizures, fever; **acyclovir for HSV**.

## 12.5 Seizures

### Febrile seizures
- **Seizure with fever (≥ 38 °C) without CNS infection**, **age 6 months – 5 yrs (peak 18 months)**; **most common seizure in children**; family history common.
- **Simple (~80 %)** – **generalised, < 15 min, once in 24 hrs**, no post-ictal deficit; **complex** – focal, > 15 min, recurrent within 24 hrs.
- **Recurrence ~30 %**; risk of epilepsy only slightly ↑ (complex).
- **Management** – **protect from injury, lateral (recovery) position, nothing in mouth**, time the seizure, O₂; if **> 5 min** – **midazolam (buccal/ intranasal 0.2–0.3 mg/kg) or diazepam rectal (0.5 mg/kg)/ IV (0.3 mg/kg)/ lorazepam IV 0.1 mg/kg**; **antipyretic (paracetamol) & tepid sponging for comfort** (antipyretics don't prevent recurrence); find & treat fever source; **LP if meningitis suspected (esp. < 12 months)**.
- **No long-term anticonvulsant prophylaxis** for simple febrile seizures; intermittent **clobazam** during fever (selected cases).
- **Parent teaching** – first aid, **don't put anything in mouth, don't restrain, don't give water during seizure**, when to go to hospital.

### Epilepsy
- **≥ 2 unprovoked seizures > 24 hrs apart**.
- **Generalised** – **tonic-clonic (grand mal)**, **absence (petit mal)** – brief staring spells **(5–10 sec), many per day, 3 Hz spike-wave EEG**, provoked by **hyperventilation**, school performance ↓ → **ethosuximide/ valproate**; myoclonic, atonic (drop attacks).
- **Focal** – simple/ complex partial.
- **Infantile spasms (West syndrome)** – **3–12 months**, **"jack-knife/ salaam" spasms in clusters**, developmental regression, **hypsarrhythmia (EEG)**; Rx – **ACTH, vigabatrin (tuberous sclerosis)**, prednisolone.
- **Drugs** – **valproate** (broad spectrum; hepatotoxicity, weight gain, tremor), **carbamazepine** (focal; rash – SJS, hyponatraemia), **phenytoin** (**gum hypertrophy – oral hygiene**, hirsutism, ataxia, nystagmus, **IV in NS only – precipitates with dextrose**), **phenobarbitone** (neonates; sedation, hyperactivity), levetiracetam, lamotrigine.
- **Nursing/ teaching** – **regular medication, never stop abruptly** (status), drug levels, seizure diary, **safety – no swimming/ cycling alone, showers not tub baths, helmet**, school informed, **medical ID card**, adequate sleep, avoid triggers (flashing lights, sleep deprivation).

### Status epilepticus
- Seizure **≥ 5 minutes** or recurrent seizures without regaining consciousness (operational); **medical emergency**.
- **ABC, O₂, glucose check, IV access** → **0–5 min: benzodiazepine (lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg IV / midazolam / diazepam)**, repeat once after 5–10 min → **phenytoin/ fosphenytoin 20 mg/kg** or **valproate 20–40 mg/kg** or **levetiracetam 40–60 mg/kg** → **phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg** → refractory: midazolam infusion/ thiopentone (ICU).

## 12.6 Cerebral Palsy (CP)

:::def Cerebral palsy
**Non-progressive** disorder of **movement & posture** due to **injury to the developing brain** (prenatal, perinatal, postnatal up to ~2 yrs). Lesion is static but clinical picture may change with growth.
:::

- **Causes** – **prematurity (most important risk factor – periventricular leukomalacia)**, **birth asphyxia/ HIE**, kernicterus (**dyskinetic/ athetoid CP**), infections (TORCH, meningitis), LBW, multiple births, head trauma.
- **Types**: **Spastic (most common ~70–80 %) – UMN – ↑ tone, brisk reflexes, scissoring gait, clasp-knife** (quadriplegia, **diplegia – preterm**, hemiplegia); **dyskinetic/ athetoid** (involuntary writhing – **basal ganglia – kernicterus**); **ataxic** (cerebellum – wide-based gait, intention tremor); **mixed**.
- **Early signs** – **persistence of primitive reflexes (Moro, ATNR beyond 6 months)**, delayed milestones, **abnormal tone**, **hand preference before 12–18 months** (hemiplegia), poor head control, feeding difficulty, **scissoring of legs**.
- **Associated problems** – **intellectual disability, seizures (epilepsy)**, visual (strabismus), hearing & speech defects, feeding/ swallowing (aspiration), drooling, constipation, contractures, dental problems.
- **Management (multidisciplinary – no cure)** – **physiotherapy (most important)**, occupational & speech therapy, **orthoses**, **baclofen, diazepam, botulinum toxin (spasticity)**, surgery (tendon release), anticonvulsants, special education.
- **Nursing** – **safety (fall/ injury)**, **feeding – upright, small bites, chin tuck, high-calorie**, aspiration prevention, **ROM exercises & positioning (prevent contractures)**, skin care, communication aids, promote independence (ADLs), **family support & respite**, school inclusion.

## 12.7 Other Neurological Conditions

| Condition | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Head injury** | Falls (commonest); GCS (modified paediatric for < 2 yrs); **"shaken baby syndrome"** – retinal haemorrhages, subdural – abuse; monitor neuro obs, vomiting, ↑ ICP |
| **Reye's syndrome** | **Aspirin during viral illness (influenza, varicella)** → **acute encephalopathy + fatty liver (hepatic dysfunction)**; vomiting, lethargy → coma; ↑ ammonia; **avoid aspirin < 16 yrs** |
| **Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS)** | Post-infectious **ascending, symmetrical flaccid paralysis**, areflexia, **albumino-cytological dissociation (CSF)**; watch **respiratory failure**; IVIG/ plasmapheresis; **AFP surveillance < 15 yrs** (GBS most common non-polio cause) |
| **Duchenne muscular dystrophy** | **X-linked recessive (boys)**, dystrophin absent; onset 3–5 yrs; **Gowers' sign** (climbs up own legs), **calf pseudohypertrophy**, waddling gait, ↑ **CPK**; wheelchair by ~12 yrs; death – cardio-respiratory |
| **Breath-holding spells** | 6 months–5 yrs; cyanotic (crying → apnoea → cyanosis → LOC) or pallid; **benign; iron deficiency association**; reassure, iron |
| **Autism, ADHD** | See behavioural disorders chapter |
| **Brain tumours** | **Most common solid tumours of childhood**; **infratentorial (posterior fossa) more common** – medulloblastoma, cerebellar astrocytoma; morning headache, vomiting, ataxia |

:::exam PYQ style
- Earliest sign of hydrocephalus in infant – **rapidly increasing head circumference**.
- Setting-sun eyes & cracked-pot sign – **hydrocephalus**.
- Commonest cause of obstructive hydrocephalus – **aqueductal stenosis**.
- VP shunt – most common complication – **malfunction/ blockage**; early – **infection**.
- Position after VP shunt – **flat, on unoperated side**.
- Meningomyelocele pre-op position – **prone**; dressing – **sterile moist saline**.
- NTD prevention – **folic acid 400 µg preconception**; marker – **↑ AFP**.
- Meningitis signs – **Kernig's, Brudzinski's, neck rigidity**; infant – **bulging fontanelle**.
- CSF in bacterial meningitis – **↑ protein, ↓ glucose, ↑ neutrophils**.
- Isolation in meningococcal meningitis – **droplet, 24 hrs after antibiotics**.
- Commonest sequela of meningitis – **deafness**.
- Febrile seizure age – **6 months – 5 years**; simple – **< 15 min, generalised, once in 24 hrs**.
- Status epilepticus – **≥ 5 min**; first drug – **benzodiazepine (lorazepam/ midazolam)**.
- Phenytoin side effect – **gum hypertrophy**; dilute only in **normal saline**.
- Absence seizure drug – **ethosuximide/ valproate**; infantile spasms – **ACTH/ vigabatrin**.
- Commonest type of CP – **spastic**; kernicterus causes – **athetoid CP**.
- Reye's syndrome – **aspirin + viral infection**.
- Gowers' sign – **Duchenne muscular dystrophy**.
:::
