## 16.1 Congenital Talipes Equinovarus (CTEV – Clubfoot)

- **Most common congenital foot deformity**; **boys 2 : 1**, bilateral ~50 %; associated with oligohydramnios, spina bifida, arthrogryposis.
- **Components (CAVE)** – **C**avus (high arch), forefoot **A**dduction, hindfoot **V**arus (inversion), **E**quinus (plantar flexion) – foot points **down & in**.
- **Positional** (correctable passively) vs **structural/ true** (rigid).
- **Treatment – Ponseti method (gold standard)** – start **within 1–2 weeks of birth**; **serial manipulation & long-leg plaster casts changed weekly (5–7 casts)** → **percutaneous Achilles tenotomy (~80–90 %)** → **foot abduction brace (Denis Browne splint/ Steenbeek) – 23 hrs/day × 3 months, then nights & naps till 4–5 yrs** (prevents relapse – **non-compliance = most common cause of relapse**).
- Surgery (PMSTR) for resistant/ late cases.
- **Cast care** – elevate, check **5 P's/ neurovascular status (toes – colour, warmth, capillary refill, swelling, movement)**, keep cast dry & clean (cover with plastic during diaper change), **petal edges**, don't insert anything inside, report foul smell/ crying (pressure sore).

## 16.2 Developmental Dysplasia of Hip (DDH)

- Abnormal development of hip – **subluxation → dislocation**; **risk – females (6:1), breech, first-born, family history, oligohydramnios**, **tight swaddling with legs extended**.
- **Tests (newborn – up to 3 months)**: **Barlow test – dislocates** (adduct & push posteriorly – "clunk out"); **Ortolani test – relocates** (abduct & lift – "clunk in").
- **Later signs** – **limited hip abduction (most reliable after 3 months)**, **asymmetric thigh & gluteal folds**, **Galeazzi/ Allis sign (unequal knee heights)**, **Trendelenburg sign & waddling gait (walking child)**, telescoping.
- **Diagnosis – USG (< 4–6 months)**; X-ray after 4–6 months (ossific nucleus).
- **Treatment** – **0–6 months: Pavlik harness (hips flexed & abducted – "frog position")** worn **23 hrs/day for 6–12 weeks**; 6–18 months: closed reduction + **spica cast**; older: open reduction/ osteotomy.
- **Pavlik harness care** – don't remove unless allowed, check skin (sponge bath), **diaper under the straps**, light knee socks/ vest under straps, don't adjust straps.
- **Hip spica cast care** – **turn every 2 hrs**, **petal & protect perineal area with plastic** (soiling), check circulation/ skin, **never use abduction bar to lift**, pillows for support; drying with exposed cast (24–48 hrs plaster).

## 16.3 Fractures in Children

- **Bones are more porous & flexible**, thick **periosteum**, **growth plates (epiphyseal)** → **heal faster**, **remodel**; **non-union rare**.
- **Greenstick fracture** – **most common in children** (one cortex breaks, other bends); **buckle/ torus** (compression – distal radius); **plastic deformation**; **Salter-Harris** (growth plate – type II most common; type V crush – growth arrest).
- **Supracondylar fracture of humerus** – **most common elbow fracture** (fall on outstretched hand, 5–7 yrs); **brachial artery injury → Volkmann's ischaemic contracture** (compartment syndrome – **pain on passive finger extension – earliest sign**), median/ radial nerve injury, **cubitus varus (gunstock deformity)**.
- **Clavicle fracture** – commonest birth fracture.
- **Fracture femur < 2 yrs (< 12–15 kg) – Bryant's (gallows) traction**: both legs suspended vertically, **hips flexed 90°, knees extended**, **buttocks slightly off the bed (just clearing – hand-breadth)** – body weight = counter-traction; check circulation (**risk of vascular compromise**).
- **Older children – Russell's traction**, skeletal traction, **hip spica**, elastic nails.
- **Traction care** – **weights hang freely** (never rest on floor/ bed), **don't remove weights**, ropes in pulleys, proper alignment, counter-traction, **neurovascular checks hourly (first 24 hrs)**, pin-site care (skeletal), skin care, diversion/ play, prevent complications of immobility (constipation, pressure sores).
- **Non-accidental injury (child abuse)** – **spiral fractures in non-ambulant infant**, **posterior rib fractures**, multiple fractures of different ages, **metaphyseal "corner/ bucket-handle" fractures**, story inconsistent with injury.
- **Osteogenesis imperfecta** – **brittle bones, blue sclera**, deafness – handle gently (differential of abuse).

## 16.4 Infections & Other Conditions

| Condition | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Acute osteomyelitis** | **Staph aureus (most common)**; **metaphysis of long bones (femur, tibia)** – haematogenous; high fever, **severe localised pain, refuses to move limb (pseudoparalysis)**, tenderness; **X-ray changes after 10–14 days**; **MRI** earliest; **IV antibiotics 4–6 weeks** (cloxacillin/ cefazolin), drainage; limb immobilisation; **sequestrum/ involucrum** in chronic |
| **Septic arthritis** | **Hip (infants) – emergency**; limb held flexed, abducted, externally rotated; urgent aspiration/ drainage |
| **Perthes disease (Legg-Calvé-Perthes)** | **Avascular necrosis of femoral head**, boys 4–8 yrs, painless limp/ knee pain |
| **SCFE (slipped capital femoral epiphysis)** | Obese adolescent boys; knee/ hip pain, limp |
| **Scoliosis** | **Lateral curvature of spine**; idiopathic adolescent – **girls**; **screening – Adam's forward bend test (rib hump)**; Cobb angle; **Milwaukee/ Boston brace (23 hrs/day)** for 25–40°; **surgery (spinal fusion – Harrington rods) > 40–50°**; post-op log rolling |
| **Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA)** | Arthritis **> 6 weeks, onset < 16 yrs**; **systemic (Still's)** – quotidian fever, salmon rash; **iridocyclitis (uveitis) – regular eye exams (oligoarticular, ANA +)**; NSAIDs, methotrexate; **warm baths/ ROM exercises in the morning**, splints |
| **Duchenne MD** | See neuro chapter (Gowers' sign) |
| **Torticollis** | Sternomastoid tumour – head tilted to same side, chin to opposite; stretching exercises |
| **Rickets deformities** | Bow legs, knock knees – vit D |

## 16.5 Burns in Children

- **Scalds (hot liquids) – most common burns in children < 5 yrs**; flame (older), electrical, chemical; **child abuse** – immersion burns ("glove/ stocking" pattern), cigarette burns.
- **Children more vulnerable** – **thin skin (deeper burns at lower temperature)**, **larger body surface area : weight ratio (more fluid & heat loss)**, immature immunity, **head relatively larger**.

| Depth | Features |
|---|---|
| **Superficial (1st degree)** | Epidermis; red, painful, no blisters (sunburn); heals 3–7 days |
| **Partial thickness – superficial (2nd degree)** | **Blisters, very painful, moist, blanches**; heals 10–21 days |
| **Partial – deep** | Dermis deeper; mottled, less painful; heals > 3 wks/ graft |
| **Full thickness (3rd degree)** | **Leathery, white/ charred, painless (nerve endings destroyed)**, no blanching; **needs grafting** |
| **4th degree** | Muscle/ bone |

### Estimating TBSA in children
- **Lund & Browder chart – most accurate for children** (adjusts for age).
- **Modified "Rule of Nines" for children (infant)**: **head & neck 18 %**, **each upper limb 9 %**, **anterior trunk 18 %**, **posterior trunk 18 %**, **each lower limb 14 %**, perineum 1 % (vs adult: head 9 %, leg 18 %). **For each year after 1, subtract 1 % from head and add 0.5 % to each leg** (till adult values at ~10 yrs).
- **Palm method** – **child's palm (with fingers) ≈ 1 % TBSA** (scattered burns).
- **Do NOT include superficial (1st degree) burns** in TBSA calculation.

### Fluid resuscitation
- **IV fluids if burns > 10 % TBSA in children** (> 15 % adults).
- **Parkland formula – 4 ml × weight (kg) × % TBSA of Ringer lactate** in first 24 hrs – **half in the first 8 hrs (from time of burn)**, remaining half in next 16 hrs. **Children – add maintenance fluid with dextrose (5 % dextrose in ½ NS/ RL)** – **hypoglycaemia risk (low glycogen)**.
- **Example**: 20-kg child, 30 % burns → 4 × 20 × 30 = **2400 ml** RL → **1200 ml in first 8 hrs**, 1200 in next 16 hrs + maintenance (1500 ml/day).
- **Best guide – urine output 1 ml/kg/hr** (child), 0.5 ml/kg/hr adult; **Foley catheter**.

### Nursing care
- **First aid** – stop burning (**stop, drop & roll**), remove clothing/ jewellery (not stuck), **cool with running tap water (15–25 °C) for 20 minutes within 3 hrs** – **NO ice, toothpaste, oil, butter, ghee, turmeric**; cover with **clean cling film/ sheet**; **keep child warm (hypothermia)**.
- **Airway** – inhalational injury (singed nasal hair, soot, hoarseness, facial burns) → early intubation.
- **Pain** – IV morphine (before dressing), distraction.
- **Wound care** – **silver sulfadiazine 1 % (not on face; not < 2 months)**, **closed (occlusive) vs exposed method**, **aseptic technique, reverse barrier isolation**, debridement, **skin grafting**; **tetanus prophylaxis**.
- **Nutrition** – **high-calorie, high-protein diet** (hypermetabolism – **Curreri formula**), NG feeding early, vitamins & zinc.
- **Complications** – **hypovolaemic shock (first 48 hrs)**, **infection/ sepsis (most common cause of death after 48–72 hrs – Pseudomonas, Staph)**, **Curling's ulcer** (stress ulcer – H2 blockers/ PPI), hypothermia, AKI, **contractures** (**position of anti-deformity – neck extended, shoulders abducted, elbows & knees extended**, splints), **hypertrophic scars/ keloid (pressure garments 23 hrs/day for 12–18 months)**, body-image problems.
- **Escharotomy** – circumferential full-thickness burns (limb/ chest).
- **Electrical burns** – entry/ exit wounds, **cardiac arrhythmias (ECG monitoring)**, myoglobinuria (↑ fluids).
- **Chemical burns** – **irrigate with copious water (20–30 min)**.
- **Prevention** – **keep hot liquids/ kettles out of reach, handles turned inward**, test bath water (**≤ 38 °C; geyser at ≤ 49 °C**), matches/ lighters away, kerosene stove safety, **socket covers**, fire-retardant clothing.
