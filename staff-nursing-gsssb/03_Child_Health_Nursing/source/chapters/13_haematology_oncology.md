## 13.1 Normal Values

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Hb – newborn | **14–20 g/dl** (cord ~16.5) |
| Hb – physiological nadir | **~9–11 g/dl at 2–3 months (term)** – "physiological anaemia of infancy" (preterm earlier & lower) |
| Hb – 6 months–5 yrs | ≥ 11 g/dl |
| **HbF at birth** | **~70–80 %** → < 2 % by 1 yr |
| Platelets | 1.5–4.5 lakh/mm³ |
| Blood volume | **Newborn ~80–85 ml/kg**; child ~75 ml/kg |

## 13.2 Anaemias

- **Iron-deficiency anaemia – most common** (see nutrition chapter) – **microcytic hypochromic**, ↓ ferritin; Rx – oral iron 3–6 mg/kg/day × 3 months after Hb normalises.
- **Megaloblastic** – B12/ folate (goat milk – folate deficiency).
- **Aplastic anaemia** – pancytopenia; BMT.

### Thalassaemia
- **Autosomal recessive**; ↓/ absent **β-globin chain** synthesis → ineffective erythropoiesis, haemolysis. **Common in Gujarat (Kutchi, Sindhi, Lohana, Bhanushali communities)**, Punjabis, Bengalis; **carrier rate in India ~3–4 %**.
- **β-thalassaemia major (Cooley's anaemia)** – symptoms at **6 months** (HbF → HbA switch); **severe pallor, jaundice, hepatosplenomegaly (massive spleen)**, FTT, **"chipmunk/ thalassaemic facies"** (frontal bossing, maxillary prominence – marrow expansion), **"hair-on-end" skull X-ray**.
- **Investigations** – **microcytic hypochromic, target cells**, **Hb electrophoresis/ HPLC – ↑ HbF, ↓/ absent HbA**; trait – **↑ HbA2 (> 3.5 %)**; **NESTROFT** (screening test for carriers).
- **Treatment** – **regular packed RBC transfusion every 2–4 weeks – keep pre-transfusion Hb 9–10.5 g/dl** (hypertransfusion); **iron chelation – desferrioxamine (SC infusion over 8–12 hrs, 5–7 nights/wk)**, **deferasirox/ deferiprone (oral)** – start when **serum ferritin > 1000 ng/ml** (or after 10–20 transfusions); **folic acid**; **avoid iron supplements & iron-rich foods (tea with meals ↓ absorption)**; **splenectomy** (hypersplenism, > 5 yrs; pneumococcal, Hib, meningococcal vaccines + penicillin prophylaxis); **bone marrow (HSC) transplant – only cure**.
- **Complications** – **iron overload (haemosiderosis)** – heart failure (**most common cause of death**), liver cirrhosis, diabetes, hypothyroidism, delayed puberty, bronze skin; transfusion infections (HBV, HCV, HIV).
- **Prevention** – **carrier screening, premarital/ antenatal screening & counselling**, **prenatal diagnosis (CVS 10–12 wks)**.

### Sickle Cell Disease
- **Autosomal recessive**; **HbS** (valine replaces glutamic acid at 6th position of β-chain); **tribal belts of Gujarat** (south Gujarat – Dangs, Valsad, Surat, Tapi), MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Chhattisgarh. **National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission (2023, by 2047)**.
- Sickling triggered by **hypoxia, dehydration, acidosis, cold, infection, fever, exertion, high altitude**.
- **Crises**: **vaso-occlusive (painful) – most common**; **dactylitis/ hand-foot syndrome (first presentation in infants 6 m–2 yrs)**; **acute chest syndrome (leading cause of death)**; **splenic sequestration** (young children – sudden splenomegaly + shock); **aplastic crisis (parvovirus B19)**; haemolytic crisis; stroke; priapism.
- **Autosplenectomy** (by 5 yrs) → **infection risk (pneumococcus, Salmonella osteomyelitis)**.
- **Diagnosis** – **sickling test, solubility test, Hb electrophoresis/ HPLC (HbS)**.
- **Management of crisis** – **HYDRATION (IV/ oral 1.5 × maintenance) – most important**, **analgesia (paracetamol/ NSAIDs → opioids – morphine)**, **O₂ if hypoxic**, **warmth (warm compress – NOT cold)**, rest, treat infection, transfusion (severe).
- **Long-term** – **hydroxyurea (↑ HbF)** – mainstay; **penicillin prophylaxis till 5 yrs**, pneumococcal & all vaccines, **folic acid**, avoid triggers (dehydration, cold, high altitude, strenuous exercise), regular follow-up.

### G6PD Deficiency
- **X-linked recessive (boys)**; **haemolysis on oxidant stress** – drugs (**primaquine, dapsone, sulphonamides, nitrofurantoin, high-dose aspirin**), **fava beans (favism)**, naphthalene (moth balls), infections; neonatal jaundice.

## 13.3 Bleeding Disorders

| | **Haemophilia A** | **Haemophilia B (Christmas disease)** |
|---|---|---|
| Factor | **VIII** (most common – ~80–85 %) | **IX** |
| Inheritance | **X-linked recessive – males affected, females carriers** | Same |
| Lab | **↑ aPTT**, **normal PT, platelets & bleeding time** | Same |

- **S/S** – **haemarthrosis (most common – knees, elbows, ankles)** → joint deformity, prolonged bleeding after circumcision/ dental extraction/ injury, **intramuscular haematomas**, **intracranial haemorrhage (most serious)**, easy bruising as toddler starts walking.
- **Treatment** – **factor replacement (factor VIII/ IX concentrate) – prophylaxis or on-demand**; **cryoprecipitate/ FFP** (if concentrate unavailable); **desmopressin (DDAVP)** – mild haemophilia A; **tranexamic acid** (mucosal bleeding); emicizumab.
- **Nursing** – **RICE for joint bleed (Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation)** + immobilise, factor replacement **ASAP**; **avoid IM injections** (SC/ oral/ IV only), **avoid rectal temperature**, **no aspirin/ NSAIDs** (paracetamol for pain); **pressure on venipuncture sites ≥ 5 min**; soft toothbrush; **safe environment – padded cot, helmet, knee/ elbow pads**; **swimming, cycling (with helmet) allowed – avoid contact sports**; **medical alert ID**; genetic counselling; physiotherapy after bleed.
- **Von Willebrand disease** – **most common inherited bleeding disorder** overall; **autosomal dominant**; mucosal bleeding (epistaxis, menorrhagia); ↑ BT.

### Immune Thrombocytopenic Purpura (ITP)
- **Autoimmune platelet destruction**, often **1–4 weeks after viral infection**; age 2–5 yrs.
- **Sudden petechiae, purpura, bruises, epistaxis** in otherwise **well child**, **no hepatosplenomegaly/ lymphadenopathy**; **platelets < 1 lakh (often < 20,000)**, normal Hb & WBC.
- **Mostly self-limiting (~80 % resolve within 6 months)**; observation if no bleeding; **IVIG, steroids (prednisolone), anti-D** if significant bleeding; **platelet transfusion only in life-threatening bleed**.
- Nursing – **avoid trauma/ contact sports, IM injections, aspirin/ NSAIDs**; soft toothbrush; watch for ICH (headache).
- **Henoch-Schönlein purpura (IgA vasculitis)** – **palpable purpura on buttocks & legs (normal platelets)**, arthritis, abdominal pain, nephritis.

## 13.4 Childhood Cancers

- **Most common childhood cancer – leukaemia (ALL)**; then **brain tumours (most common solid tumour)**, lymphomas, neuroblastoma, Wilms, retinoblastoma, bone tumours.

### Acute Lymphoblastic Leukaemia (ALL)
- **~75–80 % of childhood leukaemia**; **peak 2–5 yrs**; **Down syndrome ↑ risk** (also AML), radiation, chemicals.
- **Bone marrow failure** – **anaemia** (pallor, fatigue), **thrombocytopenia** (petechiae, bruising, bleeding), **neutropenia** (fever, infections); **infiltration** – **bone pain/ limp** (refuses to walk), **hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy**, CNS (headache, vomiting), testicular swelling, mediastinal mass (T-cell).
- **Diagnosis – bone marrow aspiration/ biopsy (> 20–25 % blasts) – confirmatory**; CBC – blasts, CSF for CNS involvement.
- **Treatment phases** – **induction** (vincristine, prednisolone/ dexamethasone, L-asparaginase, ± daunorubicin) → **remission** → **consolidation/ CNS prophylaxis (intrathecal methotrexate)** → **delayed intensification** → **maintenance (6-mercaptopurine daily + methotrexate weekly) – 2–3 yrs total**.
- **Prognosis** – **~85–90 % cure** (good: age 1–10 yrs, WBC < 50,000, girls, rapid response); **CNS & testes – sanctuary sites (relapse)**.
- **Tumour lysis syndrome** – at start of chemo – **hyperuricaemia, hyperkalaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia** → AKI; prevention – **hyperhydration, allopurinol/ rasburicase**, monitor electrolytes & urine output.

### Retinoblastoma
- **Most common intraocular malignancy of childhood**; **< 5 yrs (mostly < 2 yrs)**; **RB1 gene – chromosome 13q14**; **hereditary (bilateral) ~40 %**.
- **Leukocoria (white pupillary reflex / "cat's eye reflex") – most common sign** (noticed in photos), **strabismus (second)**, red painful eye, glaucoma, proptosis.
- Rx – **enucleation** (large), chemotherapy, laser, cryotherapy, radiotherapy; **genetic counselling**; screening of siblings; **second malignancy – osteosarcoma**.
- Post-enucleation care – pressure dressing, prosthesis (~6 weeks), eye socket cleaning.

### Other tumours
| Tumour | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Neuroblastoma** | Adrenal medulla/ sympathetic chain; **< 2 yrs**; **irregular abdominal mass crossing midline**, **"raccoon eyes"** (periorbital ecchymosis), **↑ urinary VMA/ HVA**, opsoclonus-myoclonus; may regress spontaneously in infants (stage 4S) |
| **Wilms tumour** | Kidney, 2–5 yrs, smooth, **doesn't cross midline – don't palpate** |
| **Hodgkin lymphoma** | Adolescents; **painless cervical lymphadenopathy**, **Reed-Sternberg cells**, B symptoms (fever, night sweats, weight loss) |
| **Osteosarcoma** | Adolescents, **metaphysis of long bones (around knee)**, **sunburst appearance, Codman's triangle** |
| **Ewing sarcoma** | Diaphysis, **"onion-peel"** periosteal reaction, fever mimics osteomyelitis |
| **Medulloblastoma** | Most common malignant brain tumour, posterior fossa (cerebellum), ataxia |

### Nursing care of child on chemotherapy
- **Infection prevention (neutropenia – ANC < 500/mm³)** – **protective (reverse) isolation**, **strict hand hygiene**, no fresh flowers/ plants, **no raw/ uncooked food**, avoid crowds & sick people, **no rectal temperature/ suppositories**, report **fever ≥ 38 °C immediately (febrile neutropenia – emergency: IV broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hr)**; **no live vaccines** (siblings – inactivated polio, avoid OPV contact).
- **Bleeding (thrombocytopenia)** – soft toothbrush, avoid IM, NSAIDs, contact sports, nose picking.
- **Nausea/ vomiting** – **antiemetics (ondansetron) before chemo**, small frequent bland meals.
- **Mucositis** – soft bland diet, **saline/ soda-bicarbonate mouthwash**, oral hygiene, topical anaesthetic, nystatin/ clotrimazole for thrush.
- **Alopecia** – reassure (temporary; regrows in 3–6 months), cap/ scarf, body-image support.
- **Specific drug toxicities** – **vincristine – peripheral neuropathy, constipation, jaw pain** (fatal if intrathecal); **doxorubicin/ daunorubicin – cardiotoxicity, red urine**; **cyclophosphamide – haemorrhagic cystitis (hydrate, MESNA)**; **L-asparaginase – anaphylaxis, pancreatitis**; **methotrexate – mucositis (leucovorin rescue)**; **steroids – moon face, ↑ appetite, mood changes, hyperglycaemia**; **cisplatin – nephro/ ototoxicity**.
- **Extravasation** of vesicants – stop infusion, aspirate, cold (most)/ warm (vinca alkaloids) compress, antidote.
- **Safe handling** – **chemo gloves, gown, biosafety cabinet, cytotoxic waste (yellow bag/ purple label)**; pregnant staff avoid.
- **Psychosocial** – honest age-appropriate information, play/ school continuity, sibling support, **palliative care & pain management (WHO analgesic ladder)**, bereavement support.
- **Child's concept of death** – **< 3 yrs: no concept (separation)**; **3–5 yrs: temporary, reversible (sleep)**; **5–9 yrs: irreversible but may personify (bogeyman), not universal**; **> 9–10 yrs: irreversible, universal, inevitable (adult concept)**.
