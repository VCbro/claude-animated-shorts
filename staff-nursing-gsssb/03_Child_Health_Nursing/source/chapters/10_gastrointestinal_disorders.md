## 10.1 Cleft Lip & Cleft Palate

- **Failure of fusion** – lip (maxillary & medial nasal processes, **5–6 wks**), palate (palatal shelves, **8–12 wks**). **Cleft lip ± palate** – more in **males**; isolated cleft palate – females.
- Causes – genetic, **maternal smoking, anticonvulsants (phenytoin, valproate)**, **folic acid deficiency**, alcohol, steroids.
- **Problems** – **feeding difficulty (most immediate)**, aspiration, **recurrent otitis media/ hearing loss** (palate), speech defects, dental problems, cosmetic/ psychological.
- **Feeding** – **upright position (45–60°)**, **ESSR method (Enlarge nipple, Stimulate suck, Swallow, Rest)**, special nipples (cleft palate/ squeeze bottle, **Haberman feeder**), **spoon/ paladai/ dropper**, burp frequently (more air swallowed), small frequent feeds; **breastfeeding possible with cleft lip**; palatal **obturator**.
- **Surgery timing** – **cleft lip (cheiloplasty) ~3 months** – **"Rule of 10s": ≥ 10 weeks age, ≥ 10 lb (4.5 kg) weight, Hb ≥ 10 g/dl, WBC < 10,000**; **cleft palate (palatoplasty) 9–18 months** (before speech develops; many centres 6–12 months).

### Post-operative care
| Cleft lip repair | Cleft palate repair |
|---|---|
| **Supine / side-lying – NEVER prone** (protects suture line) | **Prone / side-lying** (drainage of secretions) |
| **Logan bow (metal bar)/ tape** – protects suture line from tension | No straws, spoons, pacifiers, forks in mouth |
| **Elbow restraints** – prevent touching | **Elbow restraints** |
| Clean suture line with **saline/ sterile water after feeds** (remove crusts), apply antibiotic ointment | **Rinse mouth with water after feeding** |
| **Avoid crying & sucking** (stress on sutures) – feed with dropper/ syringe on side | **Cup feeding**; soft/ liquid diet; no hard food ~ 3 weeks |

- Team care – plastic surgeon, ENT, **speech therapist**, orthodontist, audiologist.

## 10.2 Oesophageal Atresia & Tracheo-oesophageal Fistula (TEF)

- **Type C (proximal oesophageal atresia + distal TEF) – most common (~85 %)**; Type A – pure atresia (no gas in abdomen); **H-type (E)** – fistula without atresia (recurrent pneumonia, late diagnosis).
- **Antenatal** – **maternal polyhydramnios**.
- **S/S** – **excessive frothy salivation/ drooling (first sign)**, **3 C's – Choking, Coughing, Cyanosis during feeding**, regurgitation, abdominal distension (type C), aspiration pneumonia.
- **Diagnosis** – **inability to pass a stiff red rubber catheter (No. 8–10 Fr) into stomach – coils in pouch** (X-ray shows tube coiled at T2–T4); associated **VACTERL** anomalies (Vertebral, Anorectal, Cardiac, TE, Renal, Limb).
- **Pre-op nursing** – **NPO**, **head end elevated 30–45° (reverse Trendelenburg) / prone** (prevents reflux of gastric acid via fistula), **continuous/ frequent suction of upper pouch (Replogle tube)**, IV fluids, O₂, antibiotics, warmth.
- **Surgery** – primary anastomosis with fistula ligation; gastrostomy if long gap. Post-op – suction (marked length, not beyond anastomosis), chest drain care, **watch anastomotic leak, stricture (dilatation), GERD**.

## 10.3 Hypertrophic Pyloric Stenosis (HPS)

- **Hypertrophy & hyperplasia of pyloric circular muscle**; **first-born males (4 : 1)**; **age 2–6 weeks (peak 3 wks)**; erythromycin exposure.
- **S/S** – **projectile, non-bilious vomiting** shortly after feeds, **hungry after vomiting ("hungry vomiter")**, **visible gastric peristalsis (left → right)**, **palpable olive-shaped mass (RUQ/ epigastrium) – after vomiting/ during test feed**, dehydration, weight loss, constipation, jaundice.
- **Metabolic** – **hypochloraemic, hypokalaemic metabolic alkalosis** (loss of HCl), paradoxical aciduria.
- **Diagnosis** – **USG (pyloric muscle thickness ≥ 3–4 mm, length ≥ 15–17 mm)** – investigation of choice; barium – **string sign, shoulder sign**.
- **Management** – correct dehydration & electrolytes FIRST (NS + KCl), NPO, NG decompression → **Ramstedt's pyloromyotomy** (it is **not a surgical emergency** – fluid & electrolyte stabilisation comes first).
- **Post-op** – start feeds **4–6 hrs** after surgery (small, clear fluids → breast milk), **upright position, burp**, mild vomiting may occur initially.

## 10.4 Intussusception

- **Telescoping of one bowel segment (intussusceptum) into adjacent distal (intussuscipiens)**; **ileocolic (most common)**.
- **Age 6 months – 2 yrs (peak 5–9 months)**, boys; **most common cause of intestinal obstruction in this age group**; idiopathic (Peyer's patch hypertrophy after viral infection), lead points (Meckel's, polyp) in older; rotavirus vaccine (rare).
- **Classic triad** – **sudden severe colicky abdominal pain** (child draws legs up, screams, pale, then quiet between attacks), **vomiting**, **red currant jelly stools** (blood + mucus; late), **sausage-shaped mass (RUQ)**, **Dance's sign (empty RIF)**, lethargy.
- **Diagnosis** – **USG – target/ doughnut sign (transverse), pseudokidney sign (longitudinal)**; contrast enema – **claw sign, coiled spring**.
- **Treatment** – **non-operative reduction – air (pneumatic) or hydrostatic (saline/ barium) enema under USG/ fluoroscopy** (if no peritonitis/ perforation/ shock); **passage of normal brown stool = reduction successful – report to doctor**; surgery (manual reduction/ resection) if failed. **Recurrence** ~10 % (mostly in first 72 hrs).

## 10.5 Hirschsprung Disease (Congenital Aganglionic Megacolon)

- **Absence of ganglion cells (Meissner & Auerbach plexus) in distal bowel** – **rectosigmoid (most common)** → no peristalsis → functional obstruction, proximal dilatation (megacolon). Males; **Down syndrome**.
- **Newborn** – **failure to pass meconium within 24–48 hrs** (**most important early sign**), bilious vomiting, abdominal distension; **"blast/ squirt sign"** on PR exam (explosive gas & stool).
- **Older child** – **chronic constipation since birth, ribbon-like/ pellet foul-smelling stools**, abdominal distension, FTT, palpable faecal mass, **empty rectum** on PR.
- **Complication** – **Hirschsprung-associated enterocolitis (HAEC)** – fever, explosive diarrhoea – life-threatening.
- **Diagnosis** – **rectal suction biopsy (gold standard – absent ganglion cells, ↑ AChE)**; barium enema – **transition zone**; anorectal manometry (absent recto-anal inhibitory reflex).
- **Treatment** – **rectal washouts (NS – never tap water: water intoxication)**; **colostomy** → definitive **pull-through (Swenson, Duhamel, Soave, transanal)**.
- **Nursing** – **NS enemas/ washouts**, stoma care, measure abdominal girth (**at umbilicus, same place**), low-residue diet pre-op, post-op: no rectal temps/ suppositories.

## 10.6 Anorectal Malformation (Imperforate Anus)

- Absence/ abnormal location of anal opening; **detected at birth – first newborn exam/ first rectal temp (unable to insert)**, **no meconium in 24 hrs**, meconium in urine (rectourethral fistula)/ vagina; abdominal distension.
- Classification – **high (above levator ani – colostomy first)** vs **low (perineal anoplasty)**; **invertogram/ cross-table prone lateral X-ray at 18–24 hrs**.
- Surgery – **PSARP (posterior sagittal anorectoplasty – Pena)**; post-op **anal dilatation** (Hegar dilators) for weeks, stoma care.

## 10.7 Other GI Conditions

| Condition | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Omphalocele (exomphalos)** | Abdominal contents **through umbilicus, covered by sac (membrane)**; **associated anomalies common** (Beckwith-Wiedemann, trisomies, cardiac) |
| **Gastroschisis** | **Defect lateral (right) to umbilicus, NO covering sac** – bowel exposed; few associated anomalies |
| Nursing (both) | **Cover with sterile warm moist saline dressing + plastic wrap (bowel bag)**, position on side (prevent kinking), NG decompression, IV fluids, temperature (↑ heat/ fluid loss), **prevent infection** |
| **Umbilical hernia** | Common in **preterms, African babies, hypothyroidism**; **most close spontaneously by 3–5 yrs** – **no strapping/ coin application**; surgery if > 4–5 yrs |
| **Inguinal hernia** | Patent processus vaginalis; males, preterm; **risk of incarceration – early herniotomy** |
| **Hydrocele** | Transilluminates; most resolve by 1–2 yrs |
| **GERD** | Regurgitation, poor weight gain, irritability; **thickened feeds, upright position after feeds, small frequent feeds, left-lateral/ prone only when awake & observed** (**sleep supine – SIDS**) |
| **Coeliac disease** | **Gluten intolerance (wheat, barley, rye – "BROW": barley, rye, oats, wheat)**; after wheat introduction; **chronic diarrhoea, steatorrhoea, abdominal distension, wasting of buttocks, FTT, anaemia**; **anti-tTG IgA**, **duodenal biopsy (villous atrophy)**; **lifelong gluten-free diet – rice, maize, jowar, bajra allowed** |
| **Biliary atresia** | **Obliteration of bile ducts** – **persistent jaundice > 2 weeks, clay/ pale stools, dark urine**, hepatomegaly; **Kasai portoenterostomy before 60 days (8 weeks)** – best outcome; liver transplant |
| **Hepatitis A** | Feco-oral; most common viral hepatitis in children; self-limiting; vaccine available |
| **Hepatitis B** | Vertical (perinatal) – **90 % chronic if infected at birth** → **HBIG + Hep B vaccine within 12–24 hrs** |
| **Acute appendicitis** | > 5 yrs; periumbilical pain → **RIF (McBurney's point)**, fever, vomiting; **no enema/ laxative/ heat pad** (perforation); perforation common in < 5 yrs |
| **Meckel's diverticulum** | **"Rule of 2s"** – 2 % population, 2 ft from ileocaecal valve, 2 inches long, 2 types of ectopic tissue (gastric/ pancreatic), symptomatic by 2 yrs; **painless rectal bleeding**; **technetium-99m (Meckel's) scan** |
| **Lactose intolerance** | After gastroenteritis – watery, frothy, acidic stools, perianal excoriation; **lactose-free diet temporarily** |
| **Colic (infantile)** | **Rule of 3s** – crying > 3 hrs/day, > 3 days/week, > 3 weeks; 2 wks–4 months; burping, holding, rocking |
| **Constipation** | Most functional; fibre, fluids, toilet routine |
