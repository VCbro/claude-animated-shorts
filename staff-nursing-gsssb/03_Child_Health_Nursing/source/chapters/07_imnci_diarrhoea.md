## 7.1 IMNCI – Integrated Management of Neonatal & Childhood Illness

:::def IMNCI
WHO-UNICEF **IMCI (1992/1995)** adapted in India as **IMNCI (2003)** – added **newborn (0–7 days) care** and **home visits**. Two age groups: **young infant 0–2 months** and **child 2 months – 5 years**. Focus on **5 major killers** – **pneumonia, diarrhoea, malaria, measles, malnutrition** (+ neonatal problems).
:::

- **Case management process**: **Assess → Classify → Identify treatment → Treat → Counsel the mother → Follow-up**.
- **Components**: improve health worker case-management skills, **health system** (drugs, referral), **family & community practices**.

@fig imnci_triage.svg | IMNCI colour coding, general danger signs & fast breathing cut-offs

| Colour | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **PINK** | Severe classification | **Urgent pre-referral treatment & referral** |
| **YELLOW** | Moderate | **Specific treatment at OPD/ home** (antibiotic, ORS, antimalarial) |
| **GREEN** | Mild | **Home management & advice** |

- **General danger signs (2 m–5 y)**: (1) **not able to drink or breastfeed**, (2) **vomits everything**, (3) **convulsions**, (4) **lethargic or unconscious** (+ convulsing now). Any one → **pink → urgent referral**.
- **Main symptoms assessed**: **cough or difficult breathing, diarrhoea, fever, ear problem** → then **malnutrition & anaemia**, **immunization status**, feeding, other problems.

### Cough/ difficult breathing (2 months – 5 yrs)

| Signs | Classification | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| Any danger sign **or stridor in calm child** (or SpO₂ < 90 %) | **Severe pneumonia or very severe disease** (pink) | First dose **injectable antibiotic**, refer urgently |
| **Fast breathing** and/ or **chest indrawing** | **Pneumonia** (yellow) | **Oral amoxicillin × 5 days** (chest indrawing – as per updated guideline; older Indian chart – refer) |
| No signs | **Cough or cold – no pneumonia** (green) | Soothe throat (home remedies), follow-up 5 days if not better |

- **Fast breathing**: **< 2 months ≥ 60/min**; **2–12 months ≥ 50/min**; **12 months – 5 yrs ≥ 40/min** (count for **full 1 min**, calm child).
- **Chest indrawing** – **lower chest wall goes IN when child breathes IN** (subcostal).
- **Young infant (0–2 m)** – **possible serious bacterial infection (PSBI)** signs: not feeding well, convulsions, **fast breathing ≥ 60**, **severe chest indrawing**, **temp ≥ 37.5 or < 35.5 °C**, movement only when stimulated, umbilicus redness extending to skin, skin pustules many; **jaundice – palms/ soles yellow = severe**.

### Fever & ear problem
- **Fever** – classify by malaria risk: high-risk area – **RDT/ blood smear**; **very severe febrile disease (any danger sign or stiff neck)** – pink. **Measles** – rash + cough/ runny nose/ red eyes – vitamin A; complications (clouding cornea, deep mouth ulcers) – pink.
- **Ear** – **tender swelling behind ear = mastoiditis (pink)**; pus < 14 days = **acute ear infection** (amoxicillin 5 days, **dry ear by wicking**); pus ≥ 14 days = **chronic ear infection** (wicking, topical quinolone drops).

## 7.2 Diarrhoea

:::def Diarrhoea (WHO)
Passage of **3 or more loose/ watery stools in 24 hours** (or more frequent than normal for that individual). **Frequent passing of formed stools is not diarrhoea**; **breastfed babies' pasty stools are normal**.
:::

| Type | Definition | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute watery diarrhoea** | < 14 days | Most common – **rotavirus (commonest cause < 2 yrs)**, E. coli (ETEC), cholera – **dehydration** is the danger |
| **Dysentery** | **Blood in stool** | **Shigella (commonest)** – **ciprofloxacin 15 mg/kg BD × 3 days** (IMNCI); amoebic – metronidazole |
| **Persistent diarrhoea** | **≥ 14 days** (started acutely) | Malnutrition, needs feeding advice; severe persistent (with dehydration) – refer |
| **Chronic diarrhoea** | > 30 days / insidious | Malabsorption – coeliac, cystic fibrosis |

- **Cholera** – **rice-water stools**, fishy smell, no fever, rapid severe dehydration.
- **Mechanisms** – secretory (toxins – cholera, ETEC), osmotic (lactose intolerance), invasive (Shigella), motility.
- **Complications** – **dehydration (most common cause of death)**, **hypokalaemia**, hypo/ hypernatraemia, **metabolic acidosis (Kussmaul breathing)**, AKI, malnutrition, HUS (E. coli O157, Shigella).

| Sign | **No dehydration** | **Some dehydration** | **Severe dehydration** |
|---|---|---|---|
| General condition | Well, alert | **Restless, irritable** | **Lethargic or unconscious** |
| Eyes | Normal | **Sunken** | **Sunken** |
| Thirst | Drinks normally | **Drinks eagerly, thirsty** | **Not able to drink/ drinks poorly** |
| Skin pinch | Goes back quickly | **Goes back slowly** | **Goes back very slowly (> 2 sec)** |
| Fluid deficit | < 5 % | **5–10 %** | **> 10 %** |
| Plan | **A (home)** | **B (ORS at facility)** | **C (IV)** |

- **Classify** with **two or more signs** in a column.
- **Skin pinch** – abdomen, midway between umbilicus & side, pinch longitudinally 1 sec, release; in SAM/ obese – unreliable.
- **Other signs**: depressed AF, dry mouth, no tears, oliguria, weak rapid pulse, cold extremities, delayed **CRT > 3 sec** (shock).

### Treatment plans

- **Plan A (no dehydration) – 4 rules of home treatment**:
  1. **Give extra fluids** – ORS after each loose stool: **< 2 yrs: 50–100 ml**; **2–10 yrs: 100–200 ml**; > 10 yrs – as much as wanted; plus home fluids (rice water, lassi, soup, nimbu pani, coconut water) – **avoid sugary drinks/ soft drinks/ glucose**.
  2. **Give zinc** – **2–6 months: 10 mg/day**; **> 6 months: 20 mg/day × 14 days** (dissolved in breast milk/ water) – ↓ duration, severity & recurrence (2–3 months).
  3. **Continue feeding** (breastfeeding + usual food) – **never starve**.
  4. **When to return** – not able to drink, becomes sicker, blood in stool, fever, drinks poorly.
- **Plan B (some dehydration)** – **ORS 75 ml/kg over 4 hours** at health facility (or by age/weight table), **small frequent sips/ spoon**; **if vomits – wait 10 min, then give more slowly**; continue breastfeeding; **reassess after 4 hrs** → reclassify; give zinc.
- **Plan C (severe dehydration)** – **IV Ringer lactate (or NS) 100 ml/kg**:

| Age | First 30 ml/kg in | Then 70 ml/kg in |
|---|---|---|
| **Infants (< 12 months)** | **1 hour** | **5 hours** (total 6 hrs) |
| **Children (12 months – 5 yrs)** | **30 minutes** | **2½ hours** (total 3 hrs) |

  - Repeat first 30 ml/kg once if radial pulse still weak; **reassess every 15–30 min**; start **ORS 5 ml/kg/hr** as soon as child can drink (3–4 hrs infants, 1–2 hrs children); if IV not possible – **NG ORS 20 ml/kg/hr × 6 hrs**; refer if no IV facility within 30 min.
- **Antibiotics – NOT routinely** (only dysentery, cholera with severe dehydration, associated infections). **Antidiarrhoeals/ antimotility drugs (loperamide), antiemetics – not recommended in children**.

### ORS
- **Low-osmolarity ORS (WHO 2002) – 245 mOsm/L**: **NaCl 2.6 g, glucose (anhydrous) 13.5 g, KCl 1.5 g, trisodium citrate dihydrate 2.9 g per litre** → Na 75, Cl 65, K 20, citrate 10, glucose 75 mmol/L. (Old ORS 311 mOsm/L – Na 90, glucose 111.)
- **Advantages of low-osmolarity** – ↓ stool output by ~20 %, ↓ vomiting ~30 %, ↓ need for IV by ~33 %.
- Principle – **glucose-coupled sodium transport (SGLT-1)** remains intact in diarrhoea.
- **Preparation** – wash hands, **1 packet in 1 litre clean drinking water** (boiled & cooled), stir; **use within 24 hrs**; don't boil ORS solution, don't add sugar/ salt.
- **Home-made salt-sugar solution (SSS)** – **1 level teaspoon salt (5 g) + 8 level teaspoons sugar (40 g) in 1 litre** (when ORS not available).
- **ReSoMal** – for **SAM** (less sodium, more potassium).
- **ORS + zinc = "ORS-Zinc" co-pack**; **ORS Day 29 July**.

### Prevention
- Exclusive breastfeeding, safe water, **hand washing with soap (critical times)**, sanitation (**ODF**), **rotavirus & measles vaccine**, vitamin A, food hygiene.

## 7.3 Intestinal Worms

| Worm | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Roundworm (Ascaris)** | Most common; **Loeffler's pneumonitis**, intestinal obstruction, malnutrition |
| **Hookworm (Ancylostoma/ Necator)** | **Barefoot walking** (skin penetration); **iron-deficiency anaemia**, ground itch |
| **Pinworm/ threadworm (Enterobius)** | **Perianal itching at night**, autoinfection; **cellophane (Scotch) tape test** in morning; treat whole family, cut nails |
| **Tapeworm (Taenia)** | Undercooked pork/ beef; **neurocysticercosis (seizures)** |
| **Giardia** | Malabsorption, foul greasy stools – metronidazole |

- **Albendazole 400 mg single dose** (200 mg for 1–2 yrs); **National Deworming Day 10 Feb & 10 Aug** (1–19 yrs); mebendazole 100 mg BD × 3 days.
