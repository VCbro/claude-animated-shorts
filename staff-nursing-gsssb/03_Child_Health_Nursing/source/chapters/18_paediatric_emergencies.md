## 18.1 ETAT – Emergency Triage Assessment & Treatment (WHO)

- **Triage** = sorting patients by urgency on arrival; done in **< 1 minute**.

| Category | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **E – Emergency** (red) | **Obstructed/ absent breathing, severe respiratory distress, central cyanosis, shock (cold hands + CRT > 3 sec + weak fast pulse), coma (AVPU – P/U), convulsing now, severe dehydration (lethargy + sunken eyes + very slow skin pinch)** | **Immediate treatment** |
| **P – Priority** (yellow) | **"3 TPR + MOB"** – **T**iny baby (< 2 months), **T**emperature very high, **T**rauma; **P**allor (severe), **P**oisoning, **P**ain (severe); **R**espiratory distress, **R**estless/ irritable/ lethargic, **R**eferral (urgent); **M**alnutrition (visible severe wasting), **O**edema of both feet, **B**urns (major) | Assess & treat **next/ quickly** |
| **Q – Queue / Non-urgent** (green) | None of the above | Wait in queue |

- **ABCD** – **A**irway, **B**reathing, **C**irculation, **C**oma, **C**onvulsion, **D**ehydration (severe).
- **AVPU scale** – **A**lert, responds to **V**oice, responds to **P**ain, **U**nresponsive (**P or U = emergency**, ≈ GCS ≤ 8).

## 18.2 Paediatric Basic Life Support (CPR)

@fig paediatric_cpr.svg | Infant vs child CPR – hand position, depth, ratios & key numbers

- **Sequence – C-A-B** (Compressions, Airway, Breathing) (AHA); for drowning/ asphyxial arrest, early breaths are especially important. **Lone rescuer, unwitnessed collapse – 2 minutes (5 cycles) of CPR before leaving to call for help**; witnessed sudden collapse – call first (shockable).
- **Check responsiveness & breathing (≤ 10 sec)**, pulse check – **infant: brachial**, **child: carotid/ femoral**.

| | **Newborn** | **Infant (< 1 yr)** | **Child (1 yr – puberty)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compression method | **2-thumb encircling** | **2 fingers (1 rescuer)** / **2-thumb encircling (2 rescuers)** | **1 or 2 hands (heel)** |
| Site | Lower third of sternum | **Just below inter-nipple line** (lower half of sternum) | **Lower half of sternum** |
| Depth | 1/3 AP diameter | **~4 cm (1.5 inch) – 1/3 AP** | **~5 cm (2 inch) – 1/3 AP** |
| Rate | **90 compressions + 30 breaths/min** | **100–120/min** | **100–120/min** |
| Ratio | **3 : 1** | **30 : 2 (single), 15 : 2 (two rescuers)** | **30 : 2 (single), 15 : 2 (two rescuers)** |
| Start compressions if | **HR < 60 after 30 sec effective PPV** | No pulse / **HR < 60 with poor perfusion** | No pulse / HR < 60 with poor perfusion |

- **Airway** – **infant – neutral (sniffing) position** (don't hyperextend – large occiput, soft trachea; **towel under shoulders**); child – **head tilt–chin lift**; trauma – **jaw thrust**.
- **Breaths** – **infant: mouth-to-mouth-and-nose**; each breath over 1 sec, **just enough to make chest rise**; with advanced airway – **1 breath every 2–3 sec (20–30/min)** (AHA 2020).
- **Rescue breathing only (pulse present, inadequate breathing)** – **1 breath every 2–3 sec**.
- **AED/ defibrillation** – **paediatric pads/ attenuator for < 8 yrs**; **2 J/kg first shock → 4 J/kg** (subsequent ≥ 4 J/kg, max 10 J/kg); shockable rhythms – VF/ pulseless VT (less common in children – **most paediatric arrests are asphyxial → bradycardia → asystole**).
- **Most common cause of cardiac arrest in children – respiratory failure/ hypoxia** (not primary cardiac).

### Choking (FBAO)
| Infant (< 1 yr) | Child (> 1 yr) |
|---|---|
| **5 back blows** (head down, prone on forearm, between shoulder blades) **+ 5 chest thrusts** (2 fingers, CPR site) – repeat | **Abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre)** – repeat until object out/ unresponsive |
| **No abdominal thrusts** (liver injury) | Coughing effectively – **encourage coughing, don't interfere** |
| If unresponsive → **CPR; look in mouth before breaths, remove only if visible** – **no blind finger sweep** | Same |

## 18.3 Shock in Children

- **Hypovolaemic (most common – diarrhoea/ dehydration, haemorrhage, burns)**, **septic (distributive)**, cardiogenic, obstructive, anaphylactic.
- **Compensated shock** – **tachycardia (earliest sign)**, **CRT > 3 sec (> 2 sec)**, cold peripheries, weak peripheral pulses, ↓ urine output, irritability; **BP maintained**.
- **Hypotension is a LATE sign in children** (decompensated – pre-arrest).
- **Fluid bolus** – **20 ml/kg NS/ RL over 5–20 min**, reassess (liver, crackles, HR, CRT); repeat up to **40–60 ml/kg** in first hour (septic, no overload signs); **cautious 10 ml/kg in SAM (15 ml/kg over 1 hr – WHO), heart disease, severe anaemia (blood)**, DKA.
- **Septic shock** – fluids + **antibiotics within 1 hr**, **adrenaline/ noradrenaline** (vasoactive) if fluid-refractory, correct hypoglycaemia & hypocalcaemia.
- **Vascular access** – peripheral IV; **intraosseous (proximal tibia) if IV fails in 90 sec/ 2 attempts**.

## 18.4 Emergency Drug Doses (Paediatric)

| Drug | Dose |
|---|---|
| **Adrenaline (cardiac arrest)** | **0.01 mg/kg IV/ IO = 0.1 ml/kg of 1 : 10,000** (max 1 mg), every 3–5 min; ET 0.1 mg/kg (1:1000) |
| **Adrenaline (anaphylaxis)** | **0.01 mg/kg IM of 1 : 1000 (0.01 ml/kg) – max 0.5 mg**, anterolateral thigh; repeat 5–15 min |
| **Atropine** | 0.02 mg/kg IV (bradycardia from vagal/ AV block) |
| **Amiodarone** | 5 mg/kg IV (VF/ pVT) |
| **10 % dextrose (hypoglycaemia)** | **2–5 ml/kg IV** (neonate 2 ml/kg) |
| **10 % calcium gluconate** | **0.5–1 ml/kg slow IV** (max 20 ml) – hypocalcaemia/ hyperkalaemia |
| **Sodium bicarbonate** | 1 mEq/kg (prolonged arrest/ hyperkalaemia – not routine) |
| **Midazolam (seizure)** | 0.2–0.3 mg/kg buccal/ intranasal; **0.1–0.2 mg/kg IV** |
| **Lorazepam** | **0.1 mg/kg IV** (max 4 mg) |
| **Diazepam** | 0.3 mg/kg IV / **0.5 mg/kg rectal** |
| **Phenytoin/ fosphenytoin** | **20 mg/kg (PE)** IV (in NS, ≤ 1 mg/kg/min) |
| **Phenobarbitone** | 20 mg/kg IV |
| **Mannitol 20 %** | **0.5–1 g/kg (2.5–5 ml/kg)** IV over 20–30 min – ↑ ICP |
| **Naloxone** | 0.1 mg/kg (opioid) |
| **Salbutamol nebulisation** | **2.5 mg (< 20 kg) / 5 mg (> 20 kg)** |
| **Hydrocortisone** | 4–5 mg/kg IV (asthma, anaphylaxis, adrenal crisis) |
| **Paracetamol** | **15 mg/kg/dose** 4–6 hourly |
| **Ibuprofen** | 10 mg/kg/dose 6–8 hourly (> 3 months) |
| **ET tube size (uncuffed)** | **(Age/4) + 4** mm; cuffed (age/4) + 3.5; depth (cm) = **3 × ET size** |

- **Broselow tape** – length-based weight & dose estimation in emergencies.
- **Weight estimation (APLS)** – 1–12 months: (0.5 × age in months) + 4; 1–5 yrs: (2 × age) + 8; 6–12 yrs: (3 × age) + 7.

## 18.5 Respiratory Distress & Oxygen Therapy

- **Signs** – **tachypnoea (earliest)**, **nasal flaring**, **chest retractions (subcostal, intercostal, suprasternal)**, **grunting (expiratory – keeps alveoli open)**, head bobbing, stridor/ wheeze, cyanosis, ↓ SpO₂ (< 90–92 %), altered sensorium, **silent chest/ bradycardia/ apnoea = impending respiratory failure**.
- **Position** – **position of comfort** (sitting/ in parent's lap), **don't force supine**.
- **O₂ delivery** –

| Device | Flow | FiO₂ |
|---|---|---|
| **Nasal prongs/ cannula** | **0.5–1 L/min infant, 1–2 L/min (≤ 4) child** | ~30–40 % |
| **Nasal catheter** | 0.5–1 L/min | – |
| **Head box (oxygen hood)** | **5–7 L/min** (washes CO₂) | 40–80 % |
| **Simple face mask** | 5–10 L/min | 40–60 % |
| **Non-rebreathing mask** | **10–15 L/min** | 60–90 % |
| **CPAP (bubble CPAP)** | 5–7 cm H₂O | Neonatal RDS |

- **Humidify O₂** (dries mucosa), monitor SpO₂ (target **≥ 94 %** in emergency; **90–95 % preterm**), O₂ is a drug – prescribed, avoid hyperoxia in preterm (ROP).

## 18.6 Raised ICP & Unconscious Child

- **Signs** – infants: **bulging fontanelle, ↑ OFC, high-pitched cry, setting-sun eyes, irritability, poor feeding**; older: **headache, projectile vomiting, papilloedema, diplopia**, ↓ consciousness; **late – Cushing's triad (↑ BP, ↓ HR, irregular respiration), pupillary changes, decerebrate/ decorticate posturing**.
- **Nursing** – **head end elevated 15–30°**, **head in midline** (venous drainage), avoid neck flexion/ hip flexion, **minimal handling & stimulation**, prevent coughing/ straining (stool softeners), suction ≤ 10–15 sec (pre-oxygenate), **avoid hypotonic fluids** (restrict to 2/3 maintenance), maintain normothermia & normoglycaemia, **mannitol/ 3 % saline**, seizure control, neuro observations (GCS, pupils) hourly.
- **Paediatric GCS (< 2 yrs / pre-verbal)** – Eye (4), **Verbal: 5 coos/ babbles, 4 irritable cry, 3 cries to pain, 2 moans to pain, 1 none**, Motor (6 – spontaneous movement); **GCS ≤ 8 = severe – protect airway (intubation)**.
- **Care of unconscious child** – airway (lateral/ recovery position, suction), **eye care (saline/ lubricant, tape lids)**, **oral care**, **2-hrly position change**, skin care, **NG feeding**, bladder (catheter/ diaper) & bowel care, ROM exercises, talk to child (hearing last to go), family support.

## 18.7 Other Emergencies

- **Anaphylaxis** – sudden urticaria, angioedema, stridor, wheeze, hypotension after food/ drug/ sting → **IM adrenaline (first & most important)**, call help, supine with legs raised (or sitting if breathless), O₂, IV fluids 20 ml/kg, then antihistamine & hydrocortisone (adjuncts), observe ≥ 6–24 hrs (biphasic).
- **Acute severe asthma** – O₂, **salbutamol nebs (back-to-back)**, ipratropium, steroids, **IV MgSO₄ 50 mg/kg**.
- **Hypoglycaemia** – < 54 mg/dl (or < 45 neonate) – **10 % dextrose 2–5 ml/kg IV** (avoid 25 %/ 50 % in small children), then infusion & feeds.
- **Hyperthermia/ high fever** – > 40 °C – remove excess clothes, **tepid sponging (lukewarm water, not cold/ alcohol)** – after antipyretic, fluids; heat stroke – active cooling.
- **Hypothermia** – rewarm (KMC, warm blankets, radiant warmer), check glucose.
- **Status epilepticus** – see neuro chapter (benzodiazepine first).
- **Head injury, poisoning, burns, drowning** – see respective chapters.
