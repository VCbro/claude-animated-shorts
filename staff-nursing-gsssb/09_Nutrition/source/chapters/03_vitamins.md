## 3.1 General

- **Vitamins** – organic compounds required in **small amounts**, **not synthesised (adequately) in body**, essential for metabolism – act as **coenzymes**; term "**vitamine**" – **Casimir Funk (1912)**; **first vitamin discovered – vitamin A (McCollum)**; **first B vitamin – thiamine (Eijkman – beriberi in chickens)**.
- **Fat-soluble (A, D, E, K)** – absorbed with fat (need bile), **stored (liver, adipose)**, deficiency develops slowly, **toxicity possible (A & D)**, not excreted in urine.
- **Water-soluble (B-complex, C)** – not stored (except **B12 – liver stores for 3–5 yrs**), excess excreted in urine, **daily intake needed**, toxicity rare, easily lost in cooking/ washing.

## 3.2 Vitamin A (Retinol)

- **Forms** – **retinol (preformed – animal)**, retinal, retinoic acid; **β-carotene (provitamin A – plant; 1 RAE = 1 µg retinol = 12 µg β-carotene)**.
- **Functions** – **vision in dim light (rhodopsin/ visual purple in rods – Wald's cycle)**, **maintenance of epithelial tissues (skin, cornea, conjunctiva, mucosa)**, **immunity ("anti-infective vitamin")**, growth, reproduction, bone.
- **Sources** – **richest – fish liver oils (cod, shark, halibut)**, liver, egg yolk, butter, ghee, milk; **carotene – green leafy vegetables (drumstick/ moringa leaves, spinach, amaranth), yellow-orange fruits & vegetables (carrot, mango, papaya, pumpkin, sweet potato)**, **red palm oil**.
- **RDA (ICMR 2020)** – adult man **~900–1000 µg**, woman **~840 µg** RAE/day; **children ~400–600 µg**.
- **Deficiency – xerophthalmia (WHO classification)**: **XN – night blindness (earliest symptom)**, **X1A – conjunctival xerosis**, **X1B – Bitot's spots (foamy, triangular, greyish-white spots on temporal bulbar conjunctiva – most characteristic sign)**, **X2 – corneal xerosis**, **X3A/ X3B – corneal ulceration/ keratomalacia (< ⅓ or ≥ ⅓ cornea – blindness; emergency)**, **XS – corneal scar**, **XF – xerophthalmic fundus**; also **follicular hyperkeratosis (toad skin)**, ↑ infections (measles severity, diarrhoea, respiratory), growth retardation.
- **Treatment (WHO) – on diagnosis: day 1, day 2 & after 2 weeks (14 days) – age-specific dose** (< 6 months 50,000 IU; 6–12 months 1,00,000 IU; > 1 yr 2,00,000 IU).
- **Prophylaxis (India – National Vitamin A Prophylaxis Programme, 1970)** – **9 doses: 1st – 1 lakh IU at 9 months (with MR-1)**, **2nd – 2 lakh IU at 16–18 months (with DPT booster/ MR-2)**, then **2 lakh IU every 6 months till 5 yrs**; **oral, 1 ml = 1 lakh IU** (spoon provided).
- **Hypervitaminosis A** – acute: **raised ICP (pseudotumour cerebri – bulging fontanelle in infants), headache, vomiting, desquamation**; chronic: liver damage, bone pain, alopecia; **teratogenic (isotretinoin – avoid high doses in pregnancy)**; **carotenaemia** – yellow skin (harmless, sclera spared).

## 3.3 Vitamin D (Calciferol – "sunshine vitamin")

- **Forms** – **D3 (cholecalciferol – skin & animal)**, **D2 (ergocalciferol – plants/ fungi)**; **activation – liver (25-OH D – storage/ measured form) → kidney (1,25-(OH)₂D – calcitriol – active hormone; 1α-hydroxylase stimulated by PTH)**.
- **Synthesis** – **7-dehydrocholesterol in skin + UVB (sunlight)** → D3; **sun exposure ~15–30 min, arms & face, 10 AM–3 PM** (ICMR suggests midday), 2–3 times/week (skin type).
- **Functions** – **↑ intestinal absorption of calcium & phosphorus**, **bone mineralisation**, renal reabsorption of Ca, immunity, muscle function.
- **Sources** – **sunlight (best/ main)**, **fish liver oil (richest dietary)**, fatty fish, egg yolk, liver, butter, **fortified milk & oil (FSSAI +F)**; **breast milk – poor in vitamin D** → **infant supplement 400 IU/day**.
- **RDA** – **600 IU (15 µg) adults & children; 400 IU infants**; 800 IU elderly; **1 µg = 40 IU**.
- **Deficiency** – **rickets (children)**: **craniotabes (earliest – ping-pong ball skull, < 6 months)**, **frontal bossing, delayed fontanelle closure, delayed dentition**, **rickety rosary (costochondral junctions)**, **Harrison's sulcus**, **pigeon chest**, **widening of wrists (earliest/ best seen – lower end of radius & ulna on X-ray – cupping, fraying, splaying)**, **bow legs (genu varum)/ knock knees (genu valgum)**, double malleoli, pot belly, hypotonia, tetany; **lab – ↑ alkaline phosphatase (earliest biochemical), ↓ phosphate, normal/ ↓ calcium, ↑ PTH, ↓ 25(OH)D (< 12 ng/ml deficiency; < 20 insufficiency)**; **osteomalacia (adults – bone pain, proximal myopathy, waddling gait, Looser's zones)**; osteoporosis contribution.
- **Treatment** – **stoss therapy (single large dose 3–6 lakh IU oral/ IM)** or daily 2000 IU × 3 months + calcium.
- **Hypervitaminosis D** – **hypercalcaemia** (anorexia, vomiting, polyuria, constipation, **renal stones, metastatic calcification**).

## 3.4 Vitamin E (Tocopherol) & Vitamin K

- **Vitamin E** – **α-tocopherol** – **most important lipid-soluble antioxidant** (protects cell membranes, PUFA, RBCs from oxidation); "**anti-sterility vitamin**" (in rats); **sources – vegetable oils (wheat germ oil – richest, sunflower, safflower), nuts, seeds, GLV**; **RDA ~7.5–10 mg**; **deficiency – haemolytic anaemia in premature infants**, peripheral neuropathy, ataxia, retinopathy (preterm – ROP prevention), myopathy; fat malabsorption.
- **Vitamin K (phylloquinone – K1 plants; menaquinone – K2 bacteria; menadione – K3 synthetic)** – "**anti-haemorrhagic/ coagulation vitamin**" – **needed for synthesis of clotting factors II, VII, IX, X (γ-carboxylation in liver)** + proteins C & S, osteocalcin; **sources – green leafy vegetables (richest), cabbage, cauliflower, soybean, liver; gut bacteria (large intestine)**; **RDA ~55 µg**.
- **Vitamin K deficiency** – **haemorrhagic disease/ vitamin K deficiency bleeding (VKDB) of newborn** (sterile gut, low placental transfer, **breast milk low in vitamin K**) – classic onset **day 2–7** (bleeding from cord, GI, circumcision, intracranial); **prophylaxis – vitamin K1 1 mg IM (0.5 mg if birth weight < 1000 g) at birth – anterolateral thigh**; also in **obstructive jaundice, malabsorption, prolonged broad-spectrum antibiotics, warfarin**; **↑ PT/ INR**.
- **Warfarin** – vitamin K antagonist → **keep vitamin K (GLV) intake consistent**; **antidote – vitamin K**.

## 3.6 Vitamin C (Ascorbic Acid – "anti-scorbutic")

- **Functions** – **collagen synthesis (hydroxylation of proline & lysine – wound healing, capillaries, bones, teeth, gums)**, **powerful water-soluble antioxidant**, **↑ non-haem iron absorption (reduces Fe³⁺ → Fe²⁺)**, immunity, folate → THF, adrenal steroid & neurotransmitter synthesis, carnitine.
- **Sources** – **amla (Indian gooseberry – richest natural source ~600 mg/100 g; heat-stable relatively)**, **guava (~200 mg)**, citrus (orange, lemon, sweet lime), **drumstick leaves**, GLV, cabbage, tomato, capsicum, **sprouted pulses (germination ↑ vitamin C)**; **milk & cereals are poor sources**; **most easily destroyed vitamin** (heat, air/ oxidation, alkali, copper vessels, cutting & storage).
- **RDA** – **~80 mg (men), ~65 mg (women)** (ICMR 2020); **pregnancy +20, lactation +40 mg**; **infants – 25 mg**; **smokers need more**.
- **Deficiency – scurvy** – **swollen, spongy, bleeding gums (if teeth present)**, **perifollicular haemorrhages & corkscrew hair**, petechiae, ecchymoses, **poor wound healing, anaemia**, joint pain/ haemarthrosis, weakness, **"Sailor's disease" (James Lind 1747 – citrus cured scurvy)**; **infantile scurvy (Barlow's disease, 6–12 months – formula/ boiled milk fed) – subperiosteal haemorrhage, "frog-leg" position, pseudoparalysis, painful limbs, scorbutic rosary (sharp, angular – vs rounded rickety rosary)**; X-ray – **Wimberger's ring, white line of Frankel, ground-glass bone**.
- **Treatment** – 100–300 mg/day + dietary sources; **excess** – oxalate kidney stones, diarrhoea.

## 3.7 Antioxidants

- **Vitamin E (lipid), vitamin C (aqueous), β-carotene, selenium, zinc, copper, manganese, polyphenols (flavonoids – tea, berries), lycopene (tomato)** – neutralise **free radicals** → ↓ ageing, CHD, cancer, cataract.
