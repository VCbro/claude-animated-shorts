## 6.1 Forms of Malnutrition

- **Undernutrition** – **wasting** (acute – low weight-for-height), **stunting** (chronic – low height-for-age), **underweight** (low weight-for-age – composite), **micronutrient deficiencies ("hidden hunger" – iron, iodine, vitamin A, zinc)**.
- **Overnutrition** – **overweight & obesity**, diet-related NCDs.
- **"Double/ triple burden of malnutrition"** – coexistence of undernutrition, micronutrient deficiency & overweight in same community/ household.
- **Causes** – **immediate: inadequate dietary intake & disease (infection – vicious cycle "malnutrition–infection")**; **underlying: household food insecurity, inadequate care practices (feeding, hygiene), poor health services & WASH**; **basic: poverty, illiteracy, gender inequality, large family, social & political factors** (**UNICEF conceptual framework**).
- **Ecology** – **"malnutrition–infection cycle"** – infection ↓ appetite, ↑ losses, ↑ requirement → malnutrition → ↓ immunity → infection (measles, diarrhoea, TB, worms).

## 6.2 Protein-Energy Malnutrition (PEM)

@fig malnutrition.svg | Marasmus vs kwashiorkor, MUAC tape & SAM criteria, WHO 10 steps

| Feature | **Marasmus** | **Kwashiorkor** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Severe deficiency of energy (& protein) – "starvation"** | **Mainly protein deficiency with relatively adequate calories** ("**the disease of the deposed child**" – **Cicely Williams**, Ga language, Ghana – when next child born, weaning on starchy foods) |
| Age | **< 1 yr (early weaning, bottle feeding, diarrhoea)** | **1–4 yrs (after weaning)** |
| **Oedema** | **Absent** | **Present – pitting, feet & legs → generalised (hallmark)** |
| Weight | **< 60 % of expected (severe wasting)** | 60–80 % (masked by oedema) |
| Muscle wasting | **Severe – "skin & bones", loose skin folds (baggy pants buttocks)** | Present but masked |
| Subcutaneous fat | **Absent** | Present (some) |
| Face | **Old man/ monkey face (shrunken), sunken eyes, prominent cheek bones** | **Moon face (puffy)** |
| Mental state | **Alert, irritable, hungry (good appetite)** | **Apathetic, miserable, irritable, anorexic** |
| Hair | Thin, dry | **Sparse, thin, easily pluckable, depigmented (reddish-brown/ pale) – "flag sign" (alternate bands of light & dark – intermittent nutrition)** |
| Skin | Dry, wrinkled | **"Flaky-paint"/ crazy-pavement dermatosis (hyper/ hypopigmented peeling patches)** |
| Liver | Not enlarged | **Enlarged – fatty liver** |
| Serum albumin | Normal/ slightly ↓ | **Markedly ↓ (< 2.5 g/dl)** |
| Prognosis | Better | **Worse (higher mortality)** |

- **Marasmic kwashiorkor** – features of both (severe wasting + oedema).
- **Common complications** – **hypoglycaemia, hypothermia (main killers)**, dehydration & electrolyte imbalance (↓ K⁺, ↓ Mg²⁺), **infections (often without fever)**, **anaemia**, **vitamin A deficiency (xerophthalmia)**, heart failure (overhydration, transfusion), **refeeding syndrome**.

### Classifications of malnutrition
| Classification | Basis | Grades |
|---|---|---|
| **Gomez (1956)** | **Weight-for-age (% of reference median)** | Normal > 90 %; **Grade I 75–90 %; II 60–75 %; III < 60 %** |
| **IAP (Indian Academy of Paediatrics, 1972)** | **Weight-for-age (% of standard)** | Normal > 80 %; **Grade I 71–80 %; II 61–70 %; III 51–60 %; IV ≤ 50 %**; add "K" if oedema |
| **Waterlow** | **Weight-for-height (wasting) & height-for-age (stunting)** | Wasting: normal > 90 %, mild 80–90, moderate 70–80, severe < 70 %; Stunting: normal > 95 %, mild 90–95, moderate 85–90, severe < 85 % |
| **Wellcome** | Weight-for-age + oedema | Kwashiorkor (60–80 % + oedema), marasmus (< 60 %, no oedema), marasmic kwashiorkor (< 60 % + oedema), underweight (60–80 %, no oedema) |
| **WHO (Z-scores/ SD)** | **WHO Child Growth Standards (2006 – MGRS)** | **Moderate: −2 to −3 SD; Severe: < −3 SD** for weight-for-height (wasting), height-for-age (stunting), weight-for-age (underweight); **> +2 SD weight-for-height – overweight, > +3 SD – obese** |

### SAM & MAM (6–59 months)
- **SAM (severe acute malnutrition) – any one**: **weight-for-height/ length < −3 SD (WHO)**, **MUAC < 11.5 cm**, **bilateral pitting oedema** (nutritional origin).
- **MAM (moderate acute malnutrition)** – **WFH between −2 and −3 SD**, or **MUAC 11.5–12.5 cm**.
- **Complicated SAM** (→ **facility-based care – Nutrition Rehabilitation Centre, NRC**) – **failed appetite test** (RUTF), **medical complications** (severe oedema +++, hypothermia, hypoglycaemia, dehydration, high fever, lower respiratory infection, severe anaemia, lethargy/ unconsciousness, convulsions, persistent vomiting), **age < 6 months**; **uncomplicated SAM** (good appetite, no complications) → **community-based management (CMAM)** with **RUTF**/ augmented home food.
- **NRCs** – (since 2008, NRHM/ NHM) – **10–20 beds at district/ CHC**; **average stay ~14 days**; mother stays with child (wage loss compensation, training in feeding & care); follow-up visits (4 visits after discharge – every 15 days); **discharge criteria – 15 % weight gain/ oedema-free for 2 weeks, WFH ≥ −2 SD**.

### WHO 10 steps of SAM management
1. **Treat/ prevent hypoglycaemia** (blood glucose < 54 mg/dl – **50 ml of 10 % glucose/ sucrose orally/ NG**; feed 2-hourly day & night).
2. **Treat/ prevent hypothermia** (axillary < 35 °C/ rectal < 35.5 °C – **KMC/ skin-to-skin, cover head, warm room 25–30 °C**; avoid hot water bottles).
3. **Treat/ prevent dehydration** – **ReSoMal (rehydration solution for malnutrition – low sodium 45 mmol/L, high potassium 40 mmol/L)** orally/ NG – **5 ml/kg every 30 min × 2 hrs, then 5–10 ml/kg/hr**; **avoid IV fluids except in shock** (risk of heart failure); signs of dehydration unreliable.
4. **Correct electrolyte imbalance** – **extra potassium & magnesium** (in feeds/ CMV – combined mineral vitamin mix); **↓ sodium** (body sodium is high – don't treat oedema with diuretics).
5. **Treat/ prevent infection** – **broad-spectrum antibiotics routinely** (amoxicillin; parenteral ampicillin + gentamicin if complicated); measles vaccine if unvaccinated (> 6 months).
6. **Correct micronutrient deficiencies** – **vitamin A (day 1 – unless given in last month)**, **folic acid 5 mg day 1 then 1 mg/day**, zinc, copper, multivitamins; **iron NOT in stabilisation phase – start after ~2 weeks (rehabilitation phase, once gaining weight)**.
7. **Start cautious feeding** – **stabilisation phase (day 1–7) – F-75 (75 kcal/100 ml, 0.9 g protein/100 ml) – 130 ml/kg/day (100 ml/kg if severe oedema)**, small frequent feeds (**2-hourly → 3-hourly → 4-hourly**), by cup/ NG.
8. **Achieve catch-up growth** – **rehabilitation/ transition phase – F-100 (100 kcal/100 ml, 2.9 g protein) or RUTF**, gradually ↑ to 150–220 kcal/kg/day; **target weight gain > 10 g/kg/day** (good), 5–10 moderate, < 5 poor.
9. **Provide sensory stimulation & emotional support** – tender loving care, play, structured activities, mother's involvement.
10. **Prepare for follow-up after recovery** – teach mother feeding, hygiene, immunisation, vitamin A, growth monitoring, family planning.
- **Phases** – **stabilisation (steps 1–7, days 1–7)** → **rehabilitation (weeks 2–6)** → follow-up.
- **RUTF (ready-to-use therapeutic food)** – **peanut paste + milk powder + oil + sugar + micronutrients (~545 kcal/100 g)**; no water needed, low risk of contamination.
- **Refeeding syndrome** – too rapid feeding after starvation → **hypophosphataemia (hallmark), hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia**, fluid overload, **heart failure, arrhythmias, death** → start slowly (F-75), monitor electrolytes, thiamine.
- **Appetite test** – offer RUTF (~30–45 min, by mother, quiet place) – **passes if eats ≥ ⅔ of offered/ minimum amount**.

### Growth failure indicators
- **Stunting** – chronic undernutrition (long-term, intergenerational, poor sanitation) – **height-for-age < −2 SD**; **NFHS-5: ~35.5 %** under-5s.
- **Wasting** – acute undernutrition (recent weight loss, illness) – **weight-for-height < −2 SD**; **NFHS-5: ~19.3 %**; **severe wasting ~7.7 %**.
- **Underweight** – **weight-for-age < −2 SD**; **NFHS-5: ~32.1 %**.
- **Overweight (children under 5)** – **NFHS-5: ~3.4 %**; adult women overweight/ obese ~24 %.
- **Growth faltering** – earliest sign of malnutrition (flattening of growth curve) – **growth monitoring & promotion (GMP)**.
- **Low birth weight** – reflects maternal nutrition.

## 6.3 Overweight & Obesity

- **BMI (Quetelet's index) = weight (kg) / height (m²)**.

| BMI | WHO (international) | **Asia-Pacific/ Indian (2009 consensus)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Underweight** | < 18.5 | < 18.5 |
| **Normal** | 18.5–24.9 | **18.5–22.9** |
| **Overweight** | 25–29.9 | **23–24.9** |
| **Obese** | ≥ 30 (I 30–34.9, II 35–39.9, III ≥ 40 – morbid) | **≥ 25** |

- **Abdominal (central) obesity** – **waist circumference: Indian men ≥ 90 cm, women ≥ 80 cm** (WHO Europeans – ≥ 102/ 88 cm); **waist-hip ratio > 0.9 (men), > 0.85 (women)**; **"apple shape" (android) – higher CVD/ DM risk** vs "pear shape" (gynoid); **Asian Indian phenotype** – more body fat & central obesity at lower BMI ("thin-fat Indian").
- **Other measures** – **Broca's index (ideal weight = height in cm − 100)**, **Ponderal index**, **Corpulence index (actual/ desirable weight > 1.2 = obese)**, **skinfold thickness (triceps – Harpenden calipers)**, bioelectrical impedance, DEXA.
- **Causes** – positive energy balance (overeating, HFSS foods, sugary drinks, sedentary lifestyle/ screen time), genetics, endocrine (hypothyroidism, Cushing's, PCOS), drugs (steroids, antipsychotics), psychological, ageing.
- **Complications** – **type 2 DM, hypertension, CHD, stroke, dyslipidaemia, metabolic syndrome**, osteoarthritis (knees), **sleep apnoea**, gallstones, fatty liver (NAFLD), GERD, cancers (breast, endometrial, colon), PCOS/ infertility, varicose veins, psychosocial problems, ↑ surgical & obstetric risk.
- **Management** – **diet: ↓ ~500 kcal/day (weight loss ~0.5 kg/week)**, low-fat, high-fibre, low-GI, adequate protein, ↓ sugar & refined foods, small frequent meals; **physical activity (≥ 150–300 min/week)**; **behaviour modification**; drugs (orlistat, GLP-1 agonists – semaglutide); **bariatric surgery (BMI ≥ 40 or ≥ 35 with comorbidities; Asians lower thresholds)**; **crash diets avoided**.
- **Metabolic syndrome (IDF)** – central obesity + any 2: TG ≥ 150, HDL < 40/50, BP ≥ 130/85, FBS ≥ 100.
- **Eat Right India (FSSAI, 2018)**, **Fit India Movement (2019)**, **"Aaj Se Thoda Kam" (reduce salt, sugar, fat by 10 %)**.

## 6.4 Nutritional Assessment – "ABCD"

### A – Anthropometry
| Measurement | Points |
|---|---|
| **Weight** | **Most sensitive indicator of current nutritional status/ growth (sensitive to short-term changes)**; **Salter spring balance** (hanging) for children; infant weighing scale; **birth weight doubles at ~5 months, triples at 1 yr, quadruples at 2 yrs** |
| **Height/ length** | **Infantometer (recumbent length < 2 yrs)**, **stadiometer (standing > 2 yrs)**; **best indicator of chronic malnutrition (stunting)**; birth length ~50 cm → 75 cm at 1 yr → 100 cm at 4 yrs |
| **MUAC (mid-upper arm circumference)** | **Left arm, midpoint between acromion & olecranon**, arm hanging; **"age-independent" between 1–5 yrs (6–59 months – remains ~constant 15–16 cm in well-nourished)**; **Shakir's tape**; **SAM < 11.5 cm, MAM 11.5–12.5 cm, normal > 12.5 cm (13.5 in older)**; **adults (pregnancy) – MUAC < 23 cm = undernutrition** |
| **Head circumference** | **At birth ~33–35 cm**, **~45–46 cm at 1 yr**; **> chest circumference till ~6–9 months, equal at 1 yr (~45 cm), then chest > head** (**head/ chest ratio < 1 after 1 yr**; ratio > 1 beyond 1 yr suggests malnutrition) |
| **Chest circumference** | **Birth ~31–33 cm** |
| **Skinfold thickness** | **Triceps (most common), subscapular, biceps, suprailiac** – **Harpenden/ Lange calipers** – body fat estimate |
| **Waist circumference, WHR, BMI** | Adults |
| **Weight-for-height** | Wasting (acute); independent of age |

- **Growth charts** – **WHO Child Growth Standards (2006)** – used in **Mother & Child Protection (MCP) card**; **growth monitoring: monthly till 1 yr (or 3 yrs ICDS), quarterly till 5 yrs**; **colour bands (green – normal, yellow – moderately underweight (−2 to −3 SD), orange/ red – severely underweight (< −3 SD))**; **"Road to Health" chart (David Morley)**; **direction of growth curve more important than position**; **flat/ falling curve – danger sign**.
- **Velocity** – most children gain **~500–700 g/month (0–6 m), ~250–400 g/month (6–12 m)**.

### B – Biochemical tests
- **Hb (anaemia)**, **serum albumin (protein status – long half-life ~20 days; < 3.5 g/dl low)**, **prealbumin/ transthyretin (short half-life 2 days – acute changes)**, transferrin, total lymphocyte count (immunity), **serum ferritin (iron stores)**, serum retinol (vitamin A), **urinary iodine**, 25(OH)D, B12, folate, **urinary creatinine-height index (muscle mass)**, nitrogen balance, lipid profile, blood glucose/ HbA1c.

### C – Clinical examination
- **General** – weight loss, wasting, oedema, apathy; **hair** (flag sign, dull, sparse – PEM), **eyes** (Bitot's, xerosis – vitamin A; pallor – anaemia), **lips** (angular stomatitis, cheilosis – B2), **tongue** (glossitis – B-complex, magenta – B2, beefy red – B12, pale – iron), **gums** (spongy, bleeding – vitamin C), **teeth** (mottling – fluorosis; caries), **skin** (flaky paint – kwashiorkor; phrynoderma – EFA/ vitamin A; Casal's necklace – pellagra; petechiae – vitamin C/ K), **nails** (koilonychia – iron), **thyroid** (goitre – iodine), **bones** (rickety rosary, bow legs – vitamin D), **nervous system** (neuropathy – B1, B12).
- **Jelliffe (1966) – "assessment of nutritional status of the community"** – WHO monograph.

### D – Dietary assessment
- **24-hour dietary recall (most common, quick)**, **diet history**, **food frequency questionnaire (FFQ)**, **food diary/ record (3–7 days)**, **weighment method (most accurate – family/ community surveys)**, **inventory method**, **oral questionnaire**; compare with **RDA** – adequacy.
- **Also** – **vital statistics (IMR, under-5 mortality, LBW rate, MMR), ecological factors (food production, socio-economic), health service data**.

### Screening tools (hospital)
- **SGA (subjective global assessment)**, **MUST (malnutrition universal screening tool)**, **NRS-2002**, **MNA (elderly)**, **STRONGkids/ STAMP** (children).
