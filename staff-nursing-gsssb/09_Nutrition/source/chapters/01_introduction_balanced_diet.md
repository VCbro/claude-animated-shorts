## 1.1 Basic Terms

:::def Definitions
- **Nutrition** – science of food, the nutrients in it, their **action, interaction & balance in relation to health and disease**, and the processes of ingestion, digestion, absorption, transport, utilisation & excretion.
- **Food** – anything eaten/ drunk that nourishes the body; **nutrients** – chemical substances in food needed for growth, maintenance & energy.
- **Dietetics** – application of nutrition science to feeding individuals/ groups in health & disease; **dietitian** plans therapeutic diets.
- **Diet** – food & drink regularly consumed; **balanced diet** – diet containing **all nutrients in correct amounts & proportions** to meet requirements, with a **small margin of safety** for lean periods.
- **Malnutrition** – pathological state from **relative or absolute deficiency or excess** of one or more nutrients – **undernutrition, overnutrition, specific deficiency, imbalance** ("double/ triple burden").
- **Optimal nutrition** – best possible nutritional status for health.
- **Father of nutrition – Antoine Lavoisier** (respiration & calorimetry); **Father of Indian nutrition research – Robert McCarrison** (Nutrition Research Laboratories, Coonoor 1918 → **NIN Hyderabad**, 1958).
:::

## 1.2 Classification of Nutrients & Foods

- **Macronutrients** (required in **grams**) – **carbohydrates, proteins, fats** (+ water); **micronutrients** (mg/ µg) – **vitamins & minerals**.
- **By function** – **energy-yielding** (carbohydrates, fats, proteins), **body-building** (proteins – milk, meat, pulses, eggs), **protective/ regulatory** (vitamins, minerals – fruits, vegetables, milk, eggs).
- **Water** – most essential nutrient (death in days without it); ~**60 %** of body weight.
- **Dietary fibre** – non-digestible plant polysaccharides – essential for bowel health.

### ICMR-NIN food groups
| Group | Main nutrients |
|---|---|
| **Cereals & millets** (rice, wheat, jowar, bajra, ragi) | **Energy (CHO)**, protein (lysine-poor), B-vitamins, fibre; **ragi – richest in calcium**; bajra – iron |
| **Pulses & legumes** (dals, soybean, chana) | **Protein (methionine-poor, lysine-rich)**, B-vitamins, iron, fibre; **soybean – richest plant protein (~40 %)** |
| **Milk & milk products, meat, fish, eggs** | **High-quality protein**, calcium (milk), B12 (animal only), iron (meat), vitamin A |
| **Vegetables** (green leafy, roots & tubers, others) & **fruits** | **Vitamins (A – carotene, C, folate), minerals (iron, calcium), fibre, antioxidants** |
| **Nuts & oilseeds, fats & oils, sugars** | **Energy (concentrated)**, essential fatty acids, vitamin E |

- **Basic 5 food groups** (old) – cereals; pulses; milk & meat products; fruits & vegetables; fats & sugars.
- **Cereal + pulse combination (ideal 3:1 or 4:1, e.g. khichdi, idli, dal-rice)** – **mutual supplementation** of amino acids (cereal lacks **lysine**, pulse lacks **methionine**) → improves protein quality.

## 1.3 Energy

- **Unit – kilocalorie (kcal)** – heat required to raise **1 kg of water by 1 °C** (from 14.5 to 15.5 °C); **1 kcal = 4.184 kJ** (≈ 4.2 kJ); SI unit – **joule** (megajoule).
- **Physiological fuel values (Atwater factors)** – **carbohydrate 4 kcal/g, protein 4 kcal/g, fat 9 kcal/g, alcohol 7 kcal/g**; fibre ~2 kcal/g.
- **Bomb calorimeter** – measures gross energy of food.
- **Total energy expenditure = BMR (~60–70 %) + physical activity (~20–30 %) + thermic effect of food/ SDA (~10 %)** (+ growth, pregnancy, lactation).

### Basal metabolic rate (BMR)
- Energy needed to maintain **vital functions at complete physical & mental rest**, **12–14 hrs after last meal (post-absorptive)**, awake, lying, at **comfortable temperature (thermoneutral)**.
- **~1 kcal/kg/hr (adult male)**; ~**1500–1700 kcal/day** adult man.
- **↑ BMR** – **infancy & childhood (highest per kg)**, growth, pregnancy (later), **fever (~13 % per °C rise / 7 % per °F)**, **hyperthyroidism**, cold climate, stress/ adrenaline, **male (more lean mass)**, athletes, burns.
- **↓ BMR** – **old age, starvation/ malnutrition, hypothyroidism, sleep (~10 % lower), female**, obesity (per kg), sedentary.
- **SDA (specific dynamic action)/ thermic effect of food** – extra heat produced by digestion & metabolism – **protein ~30 % (highest)**, carbohydrate ~5–6 %, fat ~4 %; mixed diet ~10 %.
- **Physical activity level (PAL)** – sedentary (~1.53), moderate (~1.8), heavy (~2.3) × BMR.

## 1.4 Nutrient Requirements (ICMR-NIN 2020)

- **EAR (Estimated Average Requirement)** – meets needs of **50 %** of healthy individuals; **RDA (Recommended Dietary Allowance) = EAR + 2 SD – meets needs of ~97.5 %** of population; **TUL (Tolerable Upper Limit)**; **AI**.
- **Reference man (ICMR 2020)** – **age 19–39 yrs, weight 65 kg, height ~177 cm, BMI ~21**, moderately active; **reference woman – 19–39 yrs, 55 kg, ~162 cm** (earlier 2010 – 60 kg & 55 kg).

| Group (ICMR 2020) | Energy (kcal/day) | Protein (g/day) |
|---|---|---|
| **Adult man – sedentary / moderate / heavy** | **~2110 / 2710 / 3470** | **~54** (0.83 g/kg) |
| **Adult woman – sedentary / moderate / heavy** | **~1660 / 2130 / 2720** | **~46** |
| **Pregnancy (2nd & 3rd trimester)** | **+350 kcal** | **+9.5 g (2nd), +22 g (3rd)** |
| **Lactation (0–6 months / 6–12 months)** | **+600 / +520 kcal** | **+17 / +13 g** |
| **Infant 0–6 / 6–12 months** | ~**90 / 80 kcal/kg** | ~1.4 / 1.2 g/kg |
| **Children 1–3 / 4–6 / 7–9 yrs** | ~**1110 / 1360 / 1700** | ~13 / 16 / 23 |

- **Protein RDA (adult) ≈ 0.83 g/kg/day** (safe level); (older ICMR – 1 g/kg).
- **Fat** – visible fat **~20–30 g/day** (25–30 g) adult; **total fat ~20–30 % of energy**; **saturated < 10 %**, trans fat < 1 %.
- **Water** – ~**2–3 L/day (8 glasses)**; **1 ml/kcal (adults), 1.5 ml/kcal (infants)**.
- **Salt** – **< 5 g/day (WHO)**; iodised salt.
- **Sugar** – < 5–10 % of energy (**< 25 g/day added sugar**).

## 1.5 Balanced Diet & Dietary Guidelines

@fig my_plate.svg | ICMR-NIN "My Plate for the Day" (2024) – proportions & daily quantities

- **ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians (2024) – 17 guidelines**, e.g.: eat a variety of foods (balanced diet), extra food & care in pregnancy/ lactation, **exclusive breastfeeding for 6 months & continue till ≥ 2 yrs**, start **complementary foods at 6 months**, adequate & appropriate diets for children & adolescents, **ensure use of vegetables & fruits (half the plate)**, use oils/ fats in moderation, **adequate protein**, **restrict salt**, **limit sugar & ultra-processed (HFSS) foods**, safe & clean foods, **adequate water**, read food labels, **physical activity**, healthy diet for elderly, avoid protein supplements for muscle building.
- **"My Plate for the Day"** – **~45 % energy from cereals & millets (half as whole grains/ millets)**, **~14–15 % from pulses/ egg/ meat**, **~15 % from nuts, fats & milk**; **vegetables (incl. GLV) & fruits ≈ half the plate by volume**.
- **Food pyramid** – base: cereals (eat most); then fruits & vegetables; milk & pulses (moderate); top: fats, oils, sugar (eat least – sparingly).
- **Millets (shree anna)** – jowar, bajra, ragi, kodo, kutki etc. – **International Year of Millets – 2023** (India's proposal); rich in fibre, calcium (ragi), iron, low GI.
- **Principles of meal planning** – meet nutritional requirements, individual needs (age, sex, activity, physiological state), **economics/ budget**, **food habits, culture & religion**, availability/ season, variety, satiety, **appropriate cooking methods**, family size.

## 1.6 Cooking & Nutrient Conservation

- **Benefits of cooking** – improves digestibility & palatability, destroys microbes & anti-nutritional factors (trypsin inhibitors in soybean, avidin in raw egg white, goitrogens), adds variety.
- **Methods** – **moist heat** (boiling, steaming, **pressure cooking – best for nutrient retention, fast**), **dry heat** (roasting, baking, grilling), **frying** (fat medium – ↑ calories, loss of heat-labile vitamins, reheated oil – trans fats & toxic polar compounds), **microwave**, sautéing.
- **Nutrient losses** – **vitamin C (most labile – heat, air, alkali, cutting, light), thiamine (heat, alkali, washing rice – polishing)**, folate, riboflavin (light); fat-soluble vitamins more stable.
- **Tips** – **wash vegetables before cutting**, **cut into large pieces just before cooking**, **cook with lid**, minimal water & **use cooking water (soups/ dal)**, **don't add soda (sodium bicarbonate) – destroys thiamine & vitamin C**, **don't wash rice repeatedly/ discard rice water**, **parboiled rice** retains B-vitamins (thiamine migrates into endosperm), avoid reheating; **germination/ sprouting ↑ vitamin C & B, ↓ phytates (better iron absorption)**; **fermentation (idli, dosa, curd) ↑ B-vitamins & digestibility**.
- **Anti-nutritional factors** – **phytates (cereals – ↓ iron, zinc, calcium absorption)**, **oxalates (spinach – ↓ calcium)**, **tannins (tea – ↓ iron)**, **goitrogens (cabbage, cauliflower – ↓ iodine uptake)**, **trypsin inhibitors (soybean – destroyed by heat)**, **avidin (raw egg white – binds biotin)**, **BOAA – neurotoxin in Lathyrus sativus (khesari dal) → lathyrism (spastic paralysis of legs)**, **sanguinarine – argemone oil → epidemic dropsy**, **aflatoxin** (fungal).
