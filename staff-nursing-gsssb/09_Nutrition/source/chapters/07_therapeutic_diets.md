## 7.1 Principles of Diet Therapy

- **Therapeutic diet** – modification of normal diet to meet requirements of a disease – in **consistency/ texture, energy, nutrients (protein, fat, CHO, Na, K, fluid), fibre, frequency & timing**.
- **Aims** – maintain/ restore nutritional status, correct deficiencies, rest the affected organ, eliminate harmful foods, adjust to body's ability to use nutrients.
- **Principles** – base on **normal balanced diet**, minimum changes needed, consider **patient's food habits, culture, religion, economic status**, palatability, **small frequent meals**, patient education & follow-up; **dietitian** plans, **nurse** implements & monitors (intake-output, weight, acceptance).

### Hospital diets (progressive)
| Diet | Foods | Indications |
|---|---|---|
| **NPO / nil by mouth** | Nothing orally | Pre-op (**solids 6–8 hrs, clear liquids 2 hrs**), acute abdomen, GI bleeding, pancreatitis, unconscious |
| **Clear liquid** | **Transparent fluids – water, clear soups/ broth, strained fruit juices (no pulp), coconut water, glucose water, tea (no milk), ORS, jelly** – minimal residue, low nutrition (**not > 24–48 hrs**) | **First post-op feed, acute GI upset, before colonoscopy/ bowel prep, severe vomiting/ diarrhoea** |
| **Full liquid** | Clear liquids + **milk, milkshakes, custard, curd/ lassi, strained cereal porridge, cream soups, ice cream** | Transition, dysphagia, after mouth/ jaw surgery, inability to chew |
| **Soft diet** | **Easily digestible, low fibre, non-spicy, soft texture – khichdi, dalia, idli, mashed potato, boiled egg, cooked vegetables, banana** | **Post-operative transition, fever, GI disorders, chewing difficulty, elderly** |
| **Mechanical soft/ pureed** | Minced/ blended foods | Dysphagia (stroke), dental problems |
| **Regular/ normal** | Normal balanced diet | No restriction |

## 7.2 Diabetes Mellitus

- **Goals** – **blood glucose control (FBS 80–130, PPBS < 180; HbA1c < 7 %)**, achieve healthy weight, prevent hypoglycaemia & complications, optimal lipids & BP.
- **Energy** – as per ideal body weight & activity (**~20–25 kcal/kg for obese, 30 normal, 35–40 underweight/ active**).
- **Carbohydrate ~45–60 % (complex, low-GI – whole grains, millets, pulses, oats, vegetables)**; **avoid simple sugars (sugar, jaggery, honey, sweets, sweetened drinks, fruit juices)**; **consistent CHO distribution across meals**; **fruits allowed in moderation (whole fruit, not juice; low GI – guava, apple, orange, papaya; limit mango, banana, chikoo, grapes)**.
- **Protein ~15–20 %** (0.8–1 g/kg; restrict in nephropathy); **fat < 30 %** (SFA < 7 %, avoid trans fat); **high fibre 25–40 g/day** (methi seeds, isabgol, oats, salad); **salt < 5 g**.
- **Meal pattern** – **small frequent meals (3 main + 2–3 snacks), regular timing, never skip meals (esp. on insulin/ sulfonylureas)**; **bedtime snack** (insulin – prevent nocturnal hypo).
- **Exchange list system** – foods grouped with similar CHO/ protein/ fat/ energy – allows variety.
- **Hypoglycaemia** – **15–20 g fast-acting CHO (3–4 tsp sugar/ glucose, ½ cup juice, glucose tablets) → recheck in 15 min ("rule of 15")** → then complex CHO snack.
- **Sick-day rules** – continue insulin, take fluids & easily digestible CHO (every 3–4 hrs), monitor glucose & ketones.
- **Gestational DM** – medical nutrition therapy first; avoid sugar, small frequent meals with bedtime snack, adequate calories (don't starve – ketosis).
- **Alcohol** – avoid/ limit (hypoglycaemia); artificial sweeteners in moderation.

## 7.3 Hypertension & Cardiovascular Disease

- **DASH diet (Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension)** – **↑ fruits, vegetables, whole grains, low-fat dairy, nuts, legumes; ↓ saturated fat, red meat, sweets** – rich in **potassium, calcium, magnesium, fibre**; ↓ SBP ~8–14 mmHg.
- **Sodium restriction** – **< 5 g salt/day (~2 g sodium)**; **avoid pickles, papad, chutneys with salt, namkeen/ chips, processed & canned foods, cheese, sauces, baking soda/ powder (bakery items), ajinomoto (MSG), salted butter, dry fish**; **no extra salt at table**; use **lemon, herbs, spices** for flavour; **"low-sodium salt" (KCl) – caution in renal failure**.
  - **Mild restriction (no added salt at table) ~2–3 g Na**; **moderate (~1000 mg Na)**; **strict (~500 mg Na – salt-free cooking)** – CHF, ascites.
- **Heart disease (CHD/ dyslipidaemia)** – **low saturated fat (< 7 %), no trans fat, ↓ cholesterol (< 200 mg/day), ↑ fibre (oats, isabgol, methi), ω-3 (fish, flaxseed), plant sterols, nuts**; **mixed oils**; avoid ghee/ butter/ vanaspati/ fried foods, organ meats, egg yolk (limit); **weight reduction**; **limit alcohol, stop smoking**.
- **Myocardial infarction (acute)** – **first 24–48 hrs: liquid/ soft, small frequent feeds, low-sodium, low-fat, easily digestible**, avoid very hot/ cold & caffeine, avoid heavy meals & straining (**stool softeners**).
- **Congestive heart failure** – **sodium restriction (< 2 g Na), fluid restriction (~1–1.5 L/day in severe)**, small frequent meals (prevent abdominal distension), adequate protein, **daily weight (gain > 1 kg/day or 2 kg/week – report)**.

## 7.4 Renal Disease

| Condition | Diet |
|---|---|
| **Acute glomerulonephritis** | **Restrict sodium & fluids (oedema, HTN, oliguria)**; **protein restriction if azotaemia**; adequate CHO; **fluid = previous day's urine output + 400–500 ml (insensible loss)** |
| **Nephrotic syndrome** | **Normal protein (~1 g/kg; high protein not recommended – worsens proteinuria)**, **sodium restriction (oedema)**, low fat/ cholesterol, adequate energy; no fluid restriction unless severe oedema |
| **Acute kidney injury** | Energy 25–35 kcal/kg; protein 0.8–1 g/kg (↑ if dialysis/ catabolic); **restrict K⁺, Na⁺, phosphate, fluids (output + 500 ml)** |
| **CKD (pre-dialysis)** | **Low protein (0.6–0.8 g/kg) – mostly high biological value (egg white, milk, fish)**; **adequate energy (30–35 kcal/kg – prevent catabolism)**; **restrict sodium, potassium (avoid banana, coconut water, citrus, tomato, potato, dry fruits; leach vegetables – peel, cut, soak in warm water 2 hrs, boil & discard water), phosphorus (milk, cheese, nuts, colas, whole grains; phosphate binders with meals)**, fluid as per urine output |
| **Haemodialysis** | **↑ protein (1.2 g/kg)**, energy 30–35 kcal/kg; restrict K, Na, P, **fluid (interdialytic weight gain < 2–2.5 kg; output + 500 ml)**; water-soluble vitamins (lost in dialysis); **peritoneal dialysis – protein 1.2–1.3 g/kg (protein loss in dialysate), less K restriction** |
| **Renal stones** | **↑ fluids (≥ 2.5–3 L/day; urine output ≥ 2 L)** for all; **calcium oxalate – ↓ oxalate (spinach, beetroot, tomato seeds, chocolate, tea, nuts, amaranth), normal dietary calcium (don't restrict), ↓ sodium & animal protein**; **uric acid stones – ↓ purines, alkalinise urine**; **cystine – alkaline urine, fluids** |

## 7.5 Liver & Biliary Disease

- **Acute viral hepatitis** – **high-calorie, high-carbohydrate, moderate protein (1–1.2 g/kg), moderate fat (as tolerated)**; small frequent palatable meals, adequate fluids; **no need for strict fat avoidance/ "no turmeric/ oil" myths**; **avoid alcohol** strictly; sugarcane juice/ glucose for energy.
- **Cirrhosis (compensated)** – **high energy (35–40 kcal/kg), adequate protein (1.2–1.5 g/kg) – protein restriction is NOT routinely recommended**; **late evening snack** (prevent overnight catabolism); **ascites/ oedema – sodium restriction (≤ 2 g Na/ ~5 g salt), fluid restriction only if hyponatraemia (< 125)**; vitamin supplements (B1, folate, fat-soluble); **oesophageal varices – soft foods, avoid rough/ hard/ spicy foods**; zero alcohol.
- **Hepatic encephalopathy** – **avoid prolonged protein restriction**; temporary restriction only in acute severe encephalopathy, then reintroduce; **vegetable & dairy protein preferred (better tolerated)**, **BCAA (branched-chain amino acids)**; **lactulose**; avoid constipation.
- **Gall bladder disease (cholecystitis/ gallstones)** – **low-fat diet (avoid fried, fatty, rich foods, ghee, butter, egg yolk)**; small frequent meals; acute – NPO/ clear liquids; avoid gas-forming foods if bloating.
- **Pancreatitis** – acute: **NPO initially → early enteral feeding (within 24–72 hrs as tolerated) – low-fat**; chronic: **low-fat, small frequent meals, pancreatic enzyme supplements with meals, MCT oil, no alcohol**, fat-soluble vitamins, manage diabetes.

## 7.6 Gastrointestinal Disorders

| Condition | Diet |
|---|---|
| **Peptic ulcer/ gastritis** | **Small frequent meals, regular timings**, **avoid foods that cause discomfort – spicy, fried, alcohol, caffeine (tea, coffee, colas), smoking, NSAIDs**; **no late-night meals**; **bland diet not essential (individualised)**; **milk is not a treatment (rebound acid – avoid excessive milk)** |
| **GERD** | **Small meals, avoid lying down 2–3 hrs after meals, head-end elevation**, weight loss, avoid fatty foods, chocolate, peppermint, citrus, tomato, coffee, alcohol, carbonated drinks, smoking; **last meal ≥ 3 hrs before bed** |
| **Acute diarrhoea** | **ORS (low osmolarity) + zinc**; **continue feeding (age-appropriate diet & breastfeeding)** – don't starve; **easily digestible – khichdi, curd rice, banana, boiled potato, dal water, lassi, coconut water**; avoid high-sugar drinks (soft drinks, packaged juices), fatty & spicy foods; **extra meal for 2 weeks after recovery** |
| **Constipation** | **High fibre (whole grains, bran, vegetables, fruits with skin, isabgol)**, **fluids 2–3 L**, prunes, figs, exercise, **regular bowel habit (gastrocolic reflex – after breakfast)** |
| **Lactose intolerance** | **Avoid/ limit milk; curd, buttermilk, cheese usually tolerated**; lactose-free milk; ensure calcium |
| **Coeliac disease (gluten-sensitive enteropathy)** | **Lifelong strict gluten-free diet – avoid wheat, barley, rye (+ oats contaminated) – "BROW"**; **allowed – rice, maize, millets (jowar, bajra, ragi), potato, pulses, sabudana, amaranth (rajgira), buckwheat (kuttu)** |
| **IBD (ulcerative colitis/ Crohn's)** | Flare – **low-residue/ low-fibre**, high protein & energy, small meals; remission – normal diet; supplements (iron, B12 – ileal disease, folate, calcium, vitamin D) |
| **Dumping syndrome (post-gastrectomy)** | **Small frequent dry meals, avoid simple sugars & fluids with meals (take fluids 30–60 min after/ between meals)**, ↑ protein & fat, **lie down 20–30 min after meals** |
| **Malabsorption/ steatorrhoea** | Low fat, **MCT oil**, fat-soluble vitamins, high protein |
| **Irritable bowel syndrome** | Low-FODMAP, regular meals, fibre (soluble) |
| **Haemorrhoids/ fissure** | High fibre, fluids, sitz bath |

## 7.7 Fever, Infections & Catabolic States

- **Fever** – **↑ energy (BMR ↑ ~13 % per °C)**, **high protein (↑ breakdown – negative N balance)**, **↑ fluids (2.5–3 L; insensible losses ↑)**, electrolytes (Na, K – sweating), vitamins (A, C, B-complex); **soft, easily digestible, small frequent meals** (anorexia); **typhoid – high calorie, high protein, low residue/ fibre (prevent intestinal perforation – avoid roughage), soft diet, plenty of fluids**.
- **Tuberculosis** – **high energy (35–40 kcal/kg), high protein (1.2–1.5 g/kg; ~1.5 g/kg)**, vitamins A, C, D, B6 (INH), calcium, iron, zinc; **Ni-kshay Poshan Yojana (₹1000/month – nutrition support during treatment, since 2024)**; **food baskets – Pradhan Mantri TB Mukt Bharat Abhiyaan (Ni-kshay Mitra)**.
- **HIV/AIDS** – ↑ energy (+10 % asymptomatic, +20–30 % symptomatic), adequate protein, food safety (avoid raw/ undercooked foods), manage diarrhoea/ oral thrush.
- **Burns** – **highest energy & protein needs (hypermetabolism)** – **Curreri formula: 25 kcal/kg + 40 kcal × % TBSA burn**; **protein 1.5–2 (–2.5) g/kg**; **early enteral feeding (within 24 hrs)**; vitamin C, A, zinc (wound healing); fluids (Parkland formula – 4 ml × kg × %TBSA in 24 hrs).
- **Surgery** – **pre-op – correct malnutrition, carbohydrate loading**; **post-op – early oral/ enteral feeding (ERAS – within 24 hrs if possible; bowel sounds not mandatory)**, progress clear → full liquid → soft → normal; **high protein, vitamin C (collagen), zinc (wound healing), vitamin K, iron**.
- **Cancer** – high energy & protein, small frequent meals, manage nausea (dry foods, cold foods, ginger), mucositis (soft, bland, cool, non-acidic), taste changes (plastic utensils for metallic taste), **neutropenic diet (well-cooked foods, avoid raw/ unwashed salads, unpasteurised products)**; cachexia.
- **Pressure ulcers/ wounds** – ↑ protein, energy, vitamin C, zinc, arginine, fluids.

## 7.8 Other Conditions

| Condition | Diet |
|---|---|
| **Gout/ hyperuricaemia** | **Low purine – avoid organ meats (liver, kidney, brain), sardines, anchovies, shellfish, red meat, meat extracts/ gravies, yeast, alcohol (beer)**; moderate pulses/ spinach/ mushrooms; **↑ fluids (≥ 3 L)**; low-fat dairy, cherries; avoid fasting & fructose drinks; weight loss gradually |
| **Iron deficiency anaemia** | Iron-rich foods (GLV, jaggery, meat, liver, dates, garden cress seeds, ragi, bajra) **+ vitamin C**; avoid tea/ coffee with meals; iron vessels |
| **Osteoporosis** | Calcium (1000–1200 mg), vitamin D, protein, weight-bearing exercise, ↓ caffeine/ salt/ alcohol |
| **Phenylketonuria (PKU)** | **Low-phenylalanine diet (lifelong, esp. till brain maturity)** – avoid high-protein foods & **aspartame**; special formula (Lofenalac); **breastfeeding allowed partially with monitoring**; tyrosine supplement |
| **Galactosaemia** | **Lactose-/ galactose-free diet – stop breastfeeding & milk**; soy-based formula |
| **Maple syrup urine disease** | Restrict BCAAs (leucine, isoleucine, valine) |
| **Epilepsy (refractory)** | **Ketogenic diet** – high fat (4:1 fat : protein + CHO) |
| **Wilson's disease** | Low copper – avoid liver, shellfish, nuts, chocolate, mushrooms |
| **Obesity** | Low calorie (−500 kcal/day), high fibre, low fat & sugar |
| **Hypothyroidism / goitre** | Iodised salt, avoid excess goitrogens (raw cabbage/ cauliflower) |
| **Pre-eclampsia** | Balanced diet, adequate protein & calcium; **normal salt (no strict restriction)** |

## 7.9 Enteral & Parenteral Nutrition

### Enteral (tube) feeding – "if the gut works, use it"
- **Preferred over parenteral** – maintains gut integrity (prevents mucosal atrophy & bacterial translocation), fewer infections, cheaper, physiological.
- **Indications** – functional GI tract but unable to eat adequately – **unconsciousness, stroke/ dysphagia, head & neck cancer/ surgery, oesophageal obstruction, burns, critical illness, preterm infants, anorexia**.
- **Routes** – **nasogastric (NG – short-term < 4–6 weeks)**, **nasoduodenal/ nasojejunal (aspiration risk, gastroparesis)**, **gastrostomy (PEG – long-term > 4–6 weeks)**, jejunostomy; **orogastric** in neonates.
- **NG tube length – NEX (nose → earlobe → xiphisternum) (or nose-ear-mid-xiphoid-umbilicus in neonates)**; **confirm position before every feed – X-ray (gold standard, initial), pH of aspirate (≤ 5.5 gastric)**; **auscultation (whoosh test) – unreliable, not recommended alone**.
- **Methods** – **bolus (200–400 ml over 10–15 min, 4–6 times/day by gravity/ syringe)**, **intermittent**, **continuous (pump – critically ill, jejunal feeds)**.
- **Feeds** – **blenderised kitchen feeds (hospital – milk, egg, dal water, cereals, sugar, oil – strained)**, **commercial polymeric formulas (1 kcal/ml)**, elemental/ peptide-based (malabsorption), disease-specific (renal, diabetic).
- **Procedure** – **hand hygiene; patient in Fowler's/ semi-Fowler's position (≥ 30–45°) during & 30–60 min after feed (aspiration prevention)**; check placement & **gastric residual volume** (hold if large – e.g. > 250–500 ml as per policy); feed at **room temperature**, by gravity slowly; **flush with 30 ml water before & after feeds and medications**; never mix drugs with feed; **crush only crushable tablets** (not enteric-coated/ sustained-release); **change feed bag/ set every 24 hrs**; prepared feeds – use within 4 hrs (open system) / refrigerate ≤ 24 hrs; **oral care**; record intake-output, weight.
- **Complications** – **aspiration pneumonia (most serious)**, **diarrhoea (commonest – fast rate, hyperosmolar feed, contamination, antibiotics, cold feed)**, constipation, nausea/ vomiting, tube displacement/ blockage (flush; warm water), nasal erosion, sinusitis, **refeeding syndrome**, hyperglycaemia, electrolyte imbalance, dehydration.

### Parenteral nutrition (PN/ TPN)
- **Indications** – **non-functional/ inaccessible GI tract** – **short bowel syndrome, intestinal obstruction/ ileus, high-output fistula, severe pancreatitis (if enteral not tolerated), severe malabsorption, prolonged NPO > 5–7 days**, chemo-induced severe mucositis, preterm (VLBW) infants.
- **Routes** – **central (TPN – hypertonic, > 900 mOsm/L – via CVC/ PICC – subclavian)**; **peripheral (PPN – < 900 mOsm/L, short-term)**.
- **Components** – **dextrose (CHO), amino acids (protein), lipid emulsion (fat – 10/ 20 % – Intralipid; white)**, electrolytes, vitamins, trace elements, water; **3-in-1 (all-in-one) bags**.
- **Nursing care** – **strict asepsis (dedicated lumen – no other drugs/ blood through TPN line)**, **infusion pump, constant rate – never speed up to "catch up"**, **change bag/ tubing every 24 hrs (lipid tubing every 12–24 hrs; lipids alone hang ≤ 12 hrs)**, check bag before hanging (cracking/ oil droplets in 3-in-1 → discard), **protect lipids/ vitamins from light**, store in fridge & warm to room temp before use (remove 1 hr prior), **monitor blood glucose (6-hourly initially), electrolytes, LFT, triglycerides, weight daily, intake-output**, signs of infection; **don't stop abruptly (rebound hypoglycaemia) – taper or hang 10 % dextrose**.
- **Complications** – **catheter-related bloodstream infection (sepsis – commonest serious)**, **hyperglycaemia (commonest metabolic)**, hypoglycaemia (abrupt stop), **refeeding syndrome**, electrolyte imbalance, fluid overload, **air embolism (Valsalva/ Trendelenburg left-side during line change)**, pneumothorax (insertion), thrombosis, **liver dysfunction (cholestasis, fatty liver – PNALD)**, gallstones, gut atrophy.

## 7.10 Drug–Food Interactions (Selected)

| Drug | Interaction / advice |
|---|---|
| **Warfarin** | Keep **vitamin K (GLV)** intake consistent |
| **MAO inhibitors** | **Avoid tyramine (aged cheese, wine, pickled/ smoked foods, soy sauce)** – hypertensive crisis |
| **Tetracyclines, fluoroquinolones** | Avoid with **milk, antacids, iron, calcium** (chelation) |
| **Iron** | Avoid tea/ coffee/ milk; take with vitamin C |
| **Levothyroxine** | Empty stomach; avoid soy, calcium, iron within 4 hrs |
| **Grapefruit juice** | ↑ levels of statins, CCBs, cyclosporine |
| **Isoniazid** | Pyridoxine supplement; avoid tyramine/ histamine-rich fish |
| **Levodopa** | Avoid high-protein meals with dose; avoid pyridoxine supplements |
| **Metformin** | Take with meals; long-term **B12 deficiency** |
| **Diuretics (loop/ thiazide)** | **K⁺-rich foods**; K-sparing – avoid excess K |
| **Alcohol + metronidazole** | Disulfiram-like reaction |
| **Steroids** | ↑ protein, calcium, vitamin D; ↓ sodium & sugar |
| **Lithium** | **Maintain consistent salt & fluid intake** (low salt → toxicity) |

## 7.11 Nurse's Role in Nutrition

- **Nutritional screening & assessment** on admission (weight, height, BMI, MUAC, diet history, appetite).
- **Plan with dietitian**, explain diet to patient/ family, respect preferences.
- **Assist feeding** – position (upright), mouth care, prepare environment, assist dependent patients, adequate time; **feeding dysphagic patients – thickened fluids, chin tuck, small bites**.
- **Monitor intake-output, food charts, weight, lab values**; identify & report poor intake, nausea, vomiting.
- **Tube feeding & TPN care**; prevent complications.
- **Health education** – balanced diet, special diets, food hygiene, breastfeeding & complementary feeding, discharge diet counselling.
- **Protected mealtimes**; "red tray" system for patients needing help.
