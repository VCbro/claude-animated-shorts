## 8.1 Food Hygiene

- **Food hygiene** – all conditions & measures necessary to ensure **safety & wholesomeness of food** at all stages – production, processing, storage, distribution, preparation & consumption.
- **WHO "Five Keys to Safer Food"** – **1. Keep clean** (hands, surfaces, utensils), **2. Separate raw & cooked** (avoid cross-contamination), **3. Cook thoroughly** (core temperature **≥ 70–75 °C**), **4. Keep food at safe temperatures** (**danger zone 5–60 °C** – bacteria multiply fastest; don't keep cooked food at room temp > 2 hrs; refrigerate < 5 °C; reheat to > 70 °C/ steaming hot), **5. Use safe water & raw materials**.
- **Food handlers** – **medical examination & stool test (carriers – typhoid, amoebiasis, Shigella)**; **exclude if diarrhoea, vomiting, fever, jaundice, infected skin lesions/ boils (Staph), sore throat, discharging ears**; **hand washing** (after toilet, before handling food), short clean nails, clean apron & head cover, no smoking/ spitting; health education; **typhoid vaccination**.
- **Kitchen hygiene** – clean premises, screened windows (flies), fly-proof & rodent-proof storage, safe water, proper drainage & waste disposal (covered bins), separate chopping boards (raw meat vs vegetables), clean utensils (wash with detergent & hot water, air-dry).
- **Meat hygiene** – **ante-mortem & post-mortem inspection** at licensed slaughterhouses; diseases transmitted – **taeniasis (T. solium – pork, T. saginata – beef), trichinosis, toxoplasmosis, anthrax, brucellosis, TB (bovine), salmonellosis**; **thorough cooking**; **"measly pork/ beef"** – cysticerci.
- **Fish & egg** – fresh fish – bright eyes, red gills, firm flesh; **egg freshness – sinks in water (fresh), floats (stale)**; candling.

## 8.2 Milk Hygiene

- **Milk-borne diseases** – **from animals (zoonoses): bovine TB (M. bovis), brucellosis, Q fever, Salmonella, anthrax, FMD**; **from humans/ handlers: typhoid, paratyphoid, cholera, dysentery, hepatitis A, diphtheria, streptococcal infections, Staph food poisoning**.
- **Pasteurisation** – kills pathogens (not all bacteria/ spores):
  - **Holder (vat) method – 63–66 °C for 30 min**, then rapid cooling to < 5 °C.
  - **HTST (flash) – 72 °C for 15–20 seconds** (most common commercially), cooled to < 4 °C.
  - **UHT (ultra-high temperature) – 125–140 °C for 2–4 seconds** – Tetra Pak, long shelf life without refrigeration.
- **Tests** – **phosphatase test (efficiency of pasteurisation – alkaline phosphatase should be destroyed)**, **methylene blue reduction test (bacterial load/ keeping quality)**, standard plate count, coliform count, turbidity test (sterilised milk).
- **Boiling** – simple home method (kills most pathogens).
- **Toned milk (3 % fat)**, **double-toned (1.5 %)**, **standardised (4.5 %)**, **full cream (6 %)**, **skimmed (≤ 0.5 %)** – fat content; **Operation Flood (1970) – Dr Verghese Kurien ("Father of the White Revolution", Amul – Anand, Gujarat)**; **National Milk Day – 26 November**.

## 8.3 Food-borne Diseases & Food Poisoning

- **Food poisoning** – acute gastroenteritis from food contaminated with **bacteria/ toxins/ chemicals**; **characteristics – sudden outbreak, many people who ate common food, short incubation, similar symptoms**.

| Type | Organism / incubation | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Staphylococcal** | **S. aureus – preformed enterotoxin (heat-stable)**; **1–6 hrs (shortest)** | **Sudden nausea, severe vomiting, cramps**; no fever; **milk products, sweets, custard, cream, cold meats** handled by **carriers with boils/ nasal**; recovery in 24 hrs |
| **Bacillus cereus** | Emetic (preformed – 1–6 hrs) / diarrhoeal (8–16 hrs) | **Fried/ reheated rice ("Chinese restaurant syndrome")** |
| **Salmonella** | **Infection – 12–24 hrs (6–72)** | **Fever, diarrhoea, vomiting, abdominal pain**; **poultry, eggs, meat, milk** |
| **Clostridium perfringens** | 8–24 hrs | Diarrhoea, cramps; **meat stews/ gravies cooled slowly (reheated)** |
| **Clostridium botulinum** | **12–36 hrs (2 hrs–8 days)** | **Botulism – neurotoxin – descending flaccid paralysis, diplopia, ptosis, dysphagia, dysphonia, respiratory paralysis; NO fever/ diarrhoea usually**; **home-canned/ preserved low-acid foods, sausages, smoked fish; honey (infant)**; **highest mortality**; **antitoxin**; toxin destroyed by boiling 10 min |
| **Vibrio parahaemolyticus** | 12–24 hrs | Seafood |
| **E. coli (ETEC, EHEC)** | 1–3 days | Traveller's diarrhoea; HUS (EHEC – undercooked beef) |
| **Shigella, Campylobacter, Listeria, Norovirus, hepatitis A & E, Cholera** | Variable | Water & food |

- **Investigation of outbreak** – list of persons who ate & fell ill, **food histories (attack rate – food-specific)**, collect **samples of suspected food, vomitus & stool** for lab, inspect kitchen & handlers, **notify** authorities; **prevention – food hygiene, refrigeration, thorough cooking, health education**.
- **Other food toxins** – **lathyrism (Lathyrus sativus/ khesari dal – BOAA neurotoxin – spastic paralysis of lower limbs; prevention – steeping (soaking) in hot water, parboiling, vitamin C; ban on sale)**; **epidemic dropsy (argemone oil adulteration of mustard oil – sanguinarine → oedema of legs, glaucoma, cardiac failure; nitric acid test – orange-red; paper chromatography)**; **aflatoxicosis (Aspergillus flavus on groundnuts/ maize – liver damage, HCC)**; **ergotism (Claviceps purpurea – rye/ bajra – gangrene, convulsions)**; **endemic ascites (pyrrolizidine alkaloids – Crotalaria seeds in millet)**; **fluorosis (water)**; **mushroom poisoning**, **cassava (cyanide)**, **solanine (green potato)**; **heavy metals – lead, mercury (Minamata), cadmium (itai-itai)**; pesticides.

## 8.4 Food Preservation

- **Aim** – prevent spoilage by **microbes, enzymes, oxidation, insects**; extend shelf life while retaining nutrients.

| Method | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Low temperature** | **Refrigeration (0–5 °C; slows growth)**, **freezing (−18 °C or below; deep freezing/ quick freezing)**, cold storage |
| **High temperature** | **Pasteurisation**, **sterilisation/ canning (heat + airtight)**, boiling, blanching (enzyme inactivation of vegetables before freezing) |
| **Removal of water (drying/ dehydration)** | **Sun drying (papad, grains, fish, chillies), spray drying (milk powder), freeze-drying (lyophilisation – instant coffee)** |
| **Increasing osmotic pressure** | **Salting (pickles, salted fish)**, **sugar (jams, jellies, murabba, squash – 65–70 % sugar)** |
| **Chemicals (class II preservatives)** | **Sodium benzoate (fruit juices, squashes – acidic foods)**, **potassium metabisulphite (KMS – light-coloured fruits, juices)**, sorbic acid, vinegar (acetic acid), nitrites (meat curing) |
| **Class I preservatives (natural)** | **Salt, sugar, vinegar, spices, oil, honey, alcohol, wood smoke** |
| **Fermentation** | **Curd, idli, dosa, dhokla, vinegar, sauerkraut** – lactic acid |
| **Smoking** | Fish, meat |
| **Irradiation (gamma rays – cold sterilisation)** | Onions, potatoes (sprout inhibition), spices, grains; **Radura symbol** |
| **Modern** | Vacuum packing, modified-atmosphere packing, aseptic packaging (Tetra Pak) |

## 8.5 Food Additives & Fortification

- **Food additives** – substances added intentionally (in small quantities) to improve **appearance, flavour, texture, shelf life** – **preservatives, colours (permitted synthetic – tartrazine, sunset yellow; not in sweets beyond limit; natural – turmeric, saffron, caramel)**, **flavours, sweeteners (aspartame – PKU warning)**, **antioxidants (BHA, BHT – in oils)**, emulsifiers, stabilisers, thickeners, **MSG (ajinomoto – "Chinese restaurant syndrome", avoid in infants < 12 months)**, anti-caking agents; **must be within FSSAI limits; banned colours – metanil yellow, lead chromate, auramine, rhodamine, Sudan dyes**.
- **Food fortification** – **addition of micronutrients to commonly consumed foods** to improve nutritional quality with minimal risk – **cost-effective public-health strategy**.
- **FSSAI "+F" logo** (Food Safety and Standards (Fortification of Foods) Regulations, 2018) – **5 staples**:
  - **Salt – iodine (iodised); double-fortified salt (DFS – iodine + iron)**.
  - **Edible oil – vitamins A & D**.
  - **Milk – vitamins A & D**.
  - **Wheat flour (atta/ maida) – iron, folic acid, vitamin B12** (± zinc, vitamin A, B-complex).
  - **Rice – iron, folic acid, vitamin B12** (**fortified rice kernels – FRK, 1 : 100 blending**) – **distributed through PDS, ICDS & PM POSHAN – all government schemes by 2024 (PMGKAY)**.
- **Biofortification** – nutrient-rich crop varieties through breeding (**iron-rich pearl millet (Dhanashakti), zinc wheat/ rice, vitamin A–rich orange maize, golden rice (genetic)**).
- **Supplementation** vs fortification – supplements given as pills/ syrups to target groups (IFA, vitamin A).

## 8.6 Food Adulteration

- **Adulteration** – **intentional addition/ substitution/ removal** of substances that **lowers quality** or makes food injurious, or **incidental** contamination; **misbranding**.
- **Prevention of Food Adulteration Act (PFA) 1954** → replaced by **Food Safety and Standards Act (FSS Act) 2006** → **FSSAI (Food Safety and Standards Authority of India) – established 2008 (operational 2011), under MoHFW, HQ New Delhi**; **FSSAI licence/ registration number (14 digits)** on packed foods; **penalties – imprisonment & fines (up to life imprisonment if death)**; **Food Safety Officers**, **food testing labs**; **"Eat Right India", "Food Safety on Wheels" (mobile testing vans), DART book (Detect Adulteration with Rapid Test)**; **World Food Safety Day – 7 June**; **World Food Day – 16 October (FAO)**.

| Food | Common adulterant | Simple test |
|---|---|---|
| **Milk** | **Water** (most common), starch, urea, detergent, synthetic milk | **Lactometer (specific gravity ~1.028–1.032 – ↓ with water)**; **iodine → blue (starch)**; froth/ lather (detergent); flowing drop test on slanted surface (pure milk leaves white trail) |
| **Ghee/ butter** | **Vanaspati, mashed potato/ starch, animal fat** | **Baudouin test (HCl + sugar → crimson red – vanaspati/ sesame oil)**; iodine → blue (starch) |
| **Mustard oil** | **Argemone oil** | **Nitric acid test → orange-red/ brown (epidemic dropsy)** |
| **Edible oils** | Mineral oil, castor oil | Saponification test (turbidity – mineral oil) |
| **Turmeric powder** | **Metanil yellow (dye), lead chromate**, chalk | **Conc. HCl → pink/ violet (metanil yellow)** |
| **Chilli powder** | **Brick powder, sawdust, artificial colour (Sudan red)** | **Water – brick powder sinks, sawdust floats, colour streaks** |
| **Black pepper** | **Papaya seeds** (dried) | **Alcohol/ water – papaya seeds float**, shrivelled, oval |
| **Coriander/ spices powder** | Horse dung, sawdust | Water – floats, smell |
| **Tea leaves** | **Used/ exhausted tea leaves (coloured), iron filings** | **Wet filter paper – colour spots**; magnet (iron filings) |
| **Coffee** | **Chicory, tamarind seeds, date seeds** | Water – chicory sinks quickly, colours water |
| **Sugar/ jaggery** | Chalk powder, washing soda, metanil yellow | Dissolve in water – chalk settles; HCl → effervescence (washing soda) |
| **Honey** | Sugar syrup, jaggery | Cotton wick burns (pure); dissolves quickly in water (adulterated) |
| **Pulses (arhar/ toor dal)** | **Khesari dal (Lathyrus)**, metanil yellow | Visual (wedge-shaped khesari); HCl → pink |
| **Wheat flour/ rice** | Chalk, sand, iron filings, plastic rice | Water; magnet; burning test |
| **Asafoetida (hing)** | Soapstone, resin | Burns brightly (pure) |
| **Green vegetables (peas, chillies)** | **Malachite green** (colour) | Cotton dipped in liquid paraffin → green |
| **Fruits** | **Calcium carbide (artificial ripening – banned)**, wax coating | – |
| **Silver leaf (vark)** | Aluminium | Burns to grey ash (aluminium) vs silver ball |
| **Ice cream** | Washing powder | Lemon juice → bubbles |
| **Common salt** | White powdered stone/ chalk | Water – insoluble chalk |

## 8.7 Food Labelling

- **Mandatory (FSSAI Labelling & Display Regulations, 2020)** – **name of food, list of ingredients (descending order of weight), nutritional information (energy, protein, CHO, sugar, fat, saturated/ trans fat, sodium per 100 g/ serving), declaration of veg (green dot)/ non-veg (brown/ red dot) logo**, food additives, **name & address of manufacturer, FSSAI logo & licence number**, **net quantity**, **lot/ batch number**, **date of manufacture/ packing**, **"best before"/ "use by"/ expiry date**, country of origin (imports), instructions for use, allergen declaration.
- **"Best before"** – quality; **"use by/ expiry"** – safety (don't consume after).
- **Front-of-pack labelling (proposed)**, **"Indian Nutrition Rating" (star rating)**.
- **Organic – "Jaivik Bharat" logo**; **ISI (BIS – industrial products), AGMARK (agricultural products – honey, spices, ghee)**, **Hallmark (gold)**; **FPO mark (fruit products – earlier)**.
