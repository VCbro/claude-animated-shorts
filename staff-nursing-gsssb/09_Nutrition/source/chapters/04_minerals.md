## 4.1 Classification

- **Macrominerals (> 100 mg/day)** – **calcium (most abundant mineral in body ~1–1.2 kg, 99 % in bones & teeth)**, phosphorus (2nd), magnesium, sodium, potassium, chloride, sulphur.
- **Trace elements/ microminerals (< 100 mg/day)** – **iron, iodine, zinc, copper, fluoride, selenium, manganese, chromium, cobalt (in B12), molybdenum**.
- **Functions** – structural (bones, teeth), **regulation of body fluids & acid–base**, nerve & muscle function, **enzyme cofactors**, **constituents of hormones (iodine – thyroxine) & haemoglobin (iron)**.

## 4.2 Calcium & Phosphorus

- **Functions** – **bones & teeth**, **blood clotting (factor IV)**, **muscle contraction (incl. heart)**, **nerve transmission**, cell membrane permeability, enzyme activation, hormone secretion.
- **Sources** – **milk & milk products (best – well absorbed; ~120 mg/100 ml cow's milk)**, **ragi (richest cereal ~344 mg/100 g)**, **sesame (til) seeds (~1450 mg)**, **GLV (amaranth, drumstick leaves, colocasia)**, small fish with bones, curry leaves, jaggery, soybean, tofu, almonds; **lime in paan/ chuna**.
- **RDA (ICMR 2020)** – **adults 1000 mg/day**; **pregnancy & lactation – 1000 mg (+ supplement: 500 mg × 2 calcium + 250 IU vit D – from 14 weeks/ 2nd trimester till 6 months postpartum per MoHFW)**; children 1–3 yrs ~500 mg; adolescents ~1050 mg; elderly ~1200 mg.
- **Absorption ↑ by** – **vitamin D (calcitriol)**, **lactose**, acidic pH, protein, **body need (pregnancy, growth)**, PTH; **↓ by** – **phytates (cereals), oxalates (spinach, amaranth)**, **excess fibre**, excess phosphate/ fat, **caffeine**, steroids, alkaline pH, old age.
- **Regulation** – **PTH ↑ serum Ca, calcitonin ↓, calcitriol ↑**; **normal serum calcium 8.5–10.5 mg/dl**.
- **Deficiency** – **rickets, osteomalacia, osteoporosis (post-menopausal women, elderly – DEXA)**, **tetany (hypocalcaemia – carpopedal spasm, Chvostek & Trousseau signs, laryngospasm)**, dental caries, growth retardation; **excess** – hypercalcaemia, kidney stones, constipation, milk-alkali syndrome.
- **Phosphorus** – **2nd most abundant**; bones (Ca:P ~2:1), **ATP, DNA/ RNA, phospholipids**, buffers; widespread (milk, meat, cereals, pulses); **normal 2.5–4.5 mg/dl**; **restrict in CKD (hyperphosphataemia) – phosphate binders**; **refeeding syndrome → hypophosphataemia**.

## 4.3 Iron

- **Body iron ~3–4 g (men) / ~2–2.5 g (women)**; **~65–70 % in haemoglobin**, myoglobin (~5–10 %), **storage – ferritin & haemosiderin (liver, spleen, marrow ~25–30 %)**, transport – **transferrin**, enzymes (cytochromes, catalase).
- **Functions** – **O₂ transport (Hb), O₂ storage in muscle (myoglobin)**, cellular respiration (cytochromes), immunity, cognitive development.
- **Forms** – **haem iron (animal – meat, liver, fish, poultry – absorbed ~15–35 %)**; **non-haem iron (plant – ~2–10 % absorption)** – **GLV (amaranth, drumstick, fenugreek), jaggery, bajra, ragi, pulses, dates, dried fruits, garden cress (halim) seeds (very rich), rice flakes (poha)**, **iron cooking vessels**; **milk – poor source of iron**.
- **RDA (ICMR 2020)** – **adult man 19 mg**, **woman 29 mg**, **pregnancy 27 mg (+ supplementation)**, lactation 23 mg, adolescents 32 mg (boys)/ 27 mg (girls), children 1–3 yrs ~8 mg; **body loss ~1 mg/day (men), ~1.5–2 mg/day (menstruating women)**.
- **Absorption** – mainly in **duodenum & upper jejunum**, as **ferrous (Fe²⁺)**; regulated by **hepcidin** (liver); **only ~10 % of dietary iron absorbed**; **↑ by – vitamin C (most important enhancer), haem iron/ meat ("meat factor"), gastric HCl, ferrous form, iron deficiency/ pregnancy (need), fermentation, germination**; **↓ by – tea & coffee (tannins/ polyphenols), phytates (cereals, bran), oxalates, calcium/ milk & calcium supplements, antacids/ PPIs/ achlorhydria, eggs (phosvitin), fibre, zinc excess**.
- **Advice** – **take IFA with/ after meals (fewer GI effects) or between meals with lemon/ amla/ vitamin C**; **avoid tea/ coffee 1 hr before & after**; don't take with calcium tablets (gap 2 hrs).

### Iron deficiency anaemia (IDA)
- **Commonest nutritional deficiency & commonest anaemia worldwide/ India**; **NFHS-5 (2019–21) – anaemia in ~57 % women (15–49 yrs), ~52 % pregnant women, ~67 % children (6–59 months), ~25 % men**.
- **Causes** – inadequate intake, **↑ demand (pregnancy, growth, lactation)**, **blood loss (hookworm, menorrhagia, GI bleed, piles, frequent pregnancies)**, malabsorption, malaria.
- **Features** – fatigue, weakness, **pallor (conjunctiva, palms, nail beds, tongue)**, breathlessness, palpitation, **koilonychia (spoon nails)**, angular stomatitis, glossitis, **pica (eating mud/ ice – pagophagia)**, **Plummer-Vinson syndrome (dysphagia – oesophageal web)**, poor concentration/ school performance, ↓ immunity, **in pregnancy – preterm, LBW, PPH tolerance ↓, maternal death**.
- **Lab** – **↓ Hb, microcytic hypochromic RBCs (↓ MCV < 80, ↓ MCH), ↓ serum ferritin (< 15 µg/L – earliest & most specific indicator of depleted stores)**, ↓ serum iron, **↑ TIBC**, ↓ transferrin saturation (< 16 %), ↑ RDW.
- **WHO anaemia cut-offs (Hb g/dl)** – **children 6–59 months < 11**, children 5–11 yrs < 11.5, 12–14 yrs & **non-pregnant women < 12**, **pregnant women < 11 (2nd trimester < 10.5)**, **men < 13**; **severity (pregnancy, India)** – mild 10–10.9, moderate 7–9.9, **severe < 7**; very severe < 4.
- **Treatment** – oral iron (**ferrous sulphate – 200 mg tab = 60 mg elemental iron**) **2 tablets/day (therapeutic)** for 3 months + continue 3 months after Hb normal (replenish stores); **expected rise ~1 g/dl/ 2–3 weeks (~0.3 g/dl per week)**; **reticulocyte response in 5–10 days (earliest sign of response)**; **parenteral iron (IV ferric carboxymaltose – FCM, iron sucrose)** for moderate–severe anaemia in pregnancy/ intolerance; **blood transfusion** – severe (< 7 g/dl near term) or decompensation.
- **Side effects of oral iron** – **black stools (harmless)**, constipation, nausea, epigastric pain, metallic taste; **liquid iron → stains teeth (use straw/ dropper on back of tongue)**.
- **Iron overdose/ poisoning (children)** – **desferrioxamine**.
- **Haemochromatosis** – iron overload.

### Anaemia Mukt Bharat (AMB, 2018) – "6×6×6" strategy
| Beneficiary | IFA prophylaxis |
|---|---|
| **Children 6–59 months** | **IFA syrup 1 ml (20 mg iron + 100 µg folic acid) biweekly (twice a week)** – auto-disable dispenser |
| **Children 5–9 yrs** | **IFA pink tablet (45 mg iron + 400 µg FA) weekly** |
| **Adolescents 10–19 yrs (in & out of school)** | **IFA blue tablet (60 mg iron + 500 µg FA) weekly (WIFS – Weekly Iron & Folic acid Supplementation)** |
| **Women of reproductive age (non-pregnant, non-lactating 20–49 yrs)** | **IFA red tablet (60 mg + 500 µg) weekly** |
| **Pregnant women** | **IFA red tablet (60 mg iron + 500 µg FA) daily from 4th month (14–16 weeks) for 180 days**, & **lactating mothers – 180 days postpartum** |

- **6 interventions** – prophylactic IFA, **deworming (albendazole – National Deworming Day: 10 February & 10 August; 200 mg for 1–2 yrs, 400 mg > 2 yrs)**, **intensified year-round BCC**, **testing & treatment (digital Hb meter)**, **mandatory IFA-fortified foods in public programmes**, **addressing non-nutritional causes (malaria, haemoglobinopathies, fluorosis)**; **6 target groups**; **6 institutional mechanisms**.
- **Target (AMB)** – reduce anaemia by **3 percentage points per year**.
- **Delayed cord clamping (≥ 1–3 min)** at birth ↑ infant iron stores; **iron-folic acid in pregnancy started after first trimester**.

## 4.4 Iodine

- **Body ~15–20 mg (~70–80 % in thyroid gland)**; **essential for thyroid hormones (T3, T4)** → BMR, growth, **brain development (fetus & infancy)**.
- **Sources** – **iodised salt (main source in India)**, **sea food (sea fish, seaweed – richest)**, food grown in iodine-rich soil; **goitrogens (cabbage, cauliflower, radish, mustard, cassava/ tapioca – thiocyanates)** ↓ iodine uptake.
- **RDA** – **adults 150 µg/day**; **pregnancy & lactation 250 µg**; children 90–120 µg.
- **IDD (iodine deficiency disorders) spectrum** – **fetus: abortion, stillbirth, congenital anomalies, cretinism (neurological – deaf-mutism, squint, spastic diplegia; myxoedematous – hypothyroid, dwarfism)**, ↑ perinatal mortality; **neonate – neonatal hypothyroidism, goitre**; **child/ adolescent – goitre, impaired mental & physical development, ↓ IQ (~13.5 points lower)**; **adult – goitre (simple/ endemic), hypothyroidism, impaired mental function, iodine-induced hyperthyroidism**; **"most common preventable cause of mental retardation/ brain damage in the world"**.
- **Endemic goitre area** – total goitre rate **≥ 5 % in school children (6–12 yrs)**; **sub-Himalayan "goitre belt"**; India – **all states/ UTs have IDD-endemic districts**.
- **Indicators** – **total goitre rate (TGR)**, **median urinary iodine concentration (UIC – 100–199 µg/L adequate; best indicator of recent intake)**, **neonatal TSH (> 5 mIU/L in > 3 %)**, household iodised salt.
- **NIDDCP (1992; earlier NGCP 1962)** – **iodised salt – ≥ 30 ppm at production, ≥ 15 ppm at consumer level**; **ban on non-iodised salt for human consumption (2006)**; **double-fortified salt (DFS – iodine + iron)**; **spot testing kit** (blue colour); store salt in **closed container, away from moisture & sunlight; add after cooking**; **Global IDD Prevention Day – 21 October**; **iodised oil (lipiodol)** in severe endemic areas (older).

## 4.5 Zinc, Fluoride & Others

| Mineral | Functions | Sources | Deficiency / excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Zinc** | **Cofactor of >300 enzymes**, **growth, immunity, wound healing, taste & smell, sexual maturation**, DNA synthesis, insulin storage | **Meat, sea food (oysters – richest), milk, whole grains, pulses, nuts** | **Growth retardation/ stunting, hypogonadism, delayed wound healing, loss of taste (hypogeusia), anorexia, diarrhoea, alopecia, dermatitis**; **acrodermatitis enteropathica (genetic – periorificial & acral dermatitis, alopecia, diarrhoea)**; **zinc in diarrhoea – 20 mg/day × 14 days (10 mg < 6 months)** – ↓ duration & severity, prevents recurrence |
| **Fluoride** | **Prevents dental caries (fluorapatite – acid-resistant enamel)**, bone strength | **Drinking water (main source)**, tea, sea fish | **Optimum in water – 0.5–0.8 ppm (mg/L); permissible ≤ 1 ppm (BIS – 1–1.5 ppm max)**; **< 0.5 ppm → dental caries**; **> 1.5 ppm → dental fluorosis (mottled, chalky-white/ brown pitted enamel – children)**; **> 3–10 ppm → skeletal fluorosis (stiff spine, crippling, genu valgum, osteosclerosis)**; **Nalgonda technique (alum + lime + bleaching powder) – defluoridation**; **NPPCF (National Programme for Prevention & Control of Fluorosis, 2008–09)**; **endemic in Rajasthan, Gujarat (north – Mehsana, Banaskantha, Kutch), Andhra, Telangana** |
| **Magnesium** | Enzyme cofactor (ATP), neuromuscular function, bones | GLV (chlorophyll), whole grains, nuts | **Tetany (with hypocalcaemia), arrhythmias, seizures** (alcoholics, diarrhoea, diuretics); **MgSO₄ – eclampsia** |
| **Selenium** | **Antioxidant (glutathione peroxidase)**, thyroid hormone metabolism | Sea food, meat, cereals (soil-dependent) | **Keshan disease (cardiomyopathy – China)**, Kashin-Beck disease; **excess – selenosis (hair & nail loss, garlic breath)** |
| **Copper** | Iron metabolism (**ceruloplasmin – ferroxidase**), collagen (lysyl oxidase), melanin, myelin | Liver, shellfish, nuts, legumes | **Anaemia (microcytic), neutropenia, bone changes**; **Menkes disease (kinky hair)**; **excess – Wilson's disease (hepatolenticular degeneration – Kayser-Fleischer ring; penicillamine)**; **Indian childhood cirrhosis (copper vessels/ milk)** |
| **Chromium** | ↑ **insulin action (glucose tolerance factor)** | Whole grains, meat, brewer's yeast | Impaired glucose tolerance |
| **Manganese, molybdenum, cobalt** | Enzymes; **cobalt – part of vitamin B12** | – | Rare |
| **Sodium & potassium** | See chapter 2 | – | – |
