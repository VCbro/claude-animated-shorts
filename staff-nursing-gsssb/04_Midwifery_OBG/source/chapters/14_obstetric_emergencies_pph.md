# Obstetric Emergencies – PPH, Cord Prolapse, Shoulder Dystocia & More

## 14.1 Postpartum Haemorrhage (PPH)

:::def PPH
**Blood loss ≥ 500 ml after vaginal delivery** or **≥ 1000 ml after caesarean**, **or any amount causing haemodynamic instability** (WHO/ RCOG). **Primary PPH** – within **24 hrs** of delivery (most common, mostly within first hour). **Secondary (late) PPH** – **24 hrs to 6 weeks** (commonest cause – **retained bits/ infection – subinvolution**). **Leading cause of maternal death worldwide & India.**
:::

:::mnemo 4 T's – causes of PPH
**T**one – **uterine atony (~70–80 % – most common)** · **T**rauma – tears (cervix, vagina, perineum), rupture, inversion, haematoma (~20 %) · **T**issue – retained placenta/ bits, accreta (~10 %) · **T**hrombin – coagulopathy (DIC, abruption, HELLP, IUD, anticoagulants) (~1 %)
:::

- **Risk factors for atony**: **overdistended uterus (twins, polyhydramnios, macrosomia)**, **grand multiparity**, **prolonged/ precipitate labour**, **oxytocin augmentation**, **APH (abruption, praevia)**, **anaemia**, general anaesthesia (halothane), chorioamnionitis, **full bladder**, fibroids, previous PPH, MgSO₄/ tocolytics.
- **Signs**: excessive vaginal bleeding, **boggy/ soft uterus above umbilicus**, **tachycardia (earliest sign)**, hypotension (late), pallor, sweating, restlessness, ↓ urine, shock. **Shock index (HR/SBP) > 0.9–1** → significant.
- **Bleeding with well-contracted uterus → trauma (tears)**.

:::key PPH management – step by step
1. **Call for help** (team – "**HAEMOSTASIS**" mnemonic), note time
2. **Uterine massage (fundal rub)** – expel clots; **bimanual compression** if continues
3. **ABC, O₂, 2 large-bore IV (16–18 G)**, **blood sample – grouping, cross-match (4 units)**, Hb, coagulation; **IV crystalloids (NS/ RL)** rapidly; **legs raised**, keep warm
4. **Empty the bladder (Foley catheter)** – full bladder prevents contraction; hourly urine
5. **Uterotonics**:
 - **Oxytocin 10 IU IM/ slow IV** → **infusion 20–40 IU in 500–1000 ml NS/RL** (first-line)
 - **Methylergometrine (Methergine) 0.2 mg IM/ slow IV** – repeat every 15 min (max 5 doses / 1 mg); **contraindicated – hypertension, pre-eclampsia, heart disease**
 - **Carboprost (15-methyl PGF2α) 250 µg IM (or intramyometrial)** every 15–20 min, **max 8 doses (2 mg)**; **contraindicated – asthma**
 - **Misoprostol 800 µg sublingual/ rectal** (600 µg oral)
 - **Tranexamic acid 1 g IV** (slow over 10 min) **within 3 hrs of birth** – repeat after 30 min if bleeding (WOMAN trial)
6. **Examine placenta & membranes**; explore for **tears** (repair) and **retained tissue** (manual removal/ evacuation)
7. **Non-pneumatic anti-shock garment (NASG)**, **uterine balloon tamponade (UBT – condom catheter / Bakri balloon)**, **aortic compression**
8. **Surgical**: **B-Lynch compression suture**, **uterine artery ligation**, **internal iliac (hypogastric) artery ligation**, **hysterectomy (last resort – life-saving)**
9. **Blood & products** – PRBC, FFP, platelets, cryo (massive transfusion); monitor vitals every 15 min, urine output, Hb, DIC
:::

- **Prevention – AMTSL (Oxytocin 10 IU IM within 1 min)**, correct anaemia, **identify high-risk & deliver at FRU**, avoid prolonged labour, **empty bladder**, examine placenta, observe **4th stage closely (1–2 hrs)**, **Misoprostol 600 µg oral by ASHA/ ANM for home deliveries**.
- **Sheehan's syndrome** – **postpartum pituitary necrosis** after severe PPH/ shock → **failure of lactation (first sign)**, amenorrhoea, loss of axillary/ pubic hair, hypothyroidism, adrenal insufficiency.
- **Blood loss estimation**: soaked pad ~50–100 ml; **visual estimation underestimates** → weigh pads/ drapes (1 g = 1 ml), calibrated drapes (**BRASSS-V**).

## 14.2 Retained Placenta

- **Placenta not delivered within 30 min** (with AMTSL; 60 min physiological). Causes – **atony**, **trapped (cervix closed – constriction ring)**, **adherent (accreta)**.
- Management: **empty bladder**, oxytocin, **CCT** again; if fails/ bleeding → **manual removal of placenta (MRP)** under GA/ sedation with **IV antibiotics**, uterotonics after; **accreta** → hysterectomy/ conservative.

## 14.3 Acute Uterine Inversion

- **Uterus turns inside out** (fundus through cervix) – mostly **3rd stage** due to **mismanagement – excess cord traction on fundal placenta without counter-traction**, **fundal pressure (Crede's)** with relaxed uterus, short cord, adherent placenta.
- Signs: **severe shock (neurogenic – out of proportion to bleeding)**, haemorrhage, **fundus not palpable / cup-shaped depression**, mass in vagina/ outside.
- Management: **immediate manual replacement (Johnson's manoeuvre – push fundus up through cervix, last-out part first in)** **without removing placenta (if attached)**, treat shock, **tocolytics (terbutaline/ MgSO₄/ GA)** to relax; **O'Sullivan's hydrostatic method** (warm saline into vagina); after replacement – **oxytocin** to maintain, remove placenta; surgical (Huntington's, Haultain's).

## 14.4 Rupture of Uterus

- **Most common cause (today) – previous caesarean scar** (especially **classical CS – upper segment**); others – **obstructed labour**, **injudicious oxytocin/ prostaglandins (in multipara/ scarred uterus)**, **grand multiparity**, trauma, instrumental (internal podalic version, destructive ops), placenta percreta.
- **Signs**: **scar tenderness**, **sudden severe abdominal pain** (may "tearing" then **cessation of contractions**), **fetal distress/ loss of FHS (earliest & most consistent sign – abnormal CTG)**, **vaginal bleeding**, **shock**, **fetal parts palpable superficially**, **recession of presenting part**, haematuria, Bandl's ring before rupture.
- Management: **resuscitation + immediate laparotomy** – **repair** (young, small tear) or **hysterectomy**; baby usually dead if complete rupture.
- **VBAC (Vaginal Birth After Caesarean)/ TOLAC** – allowed after **1 previous LSCS (lower segment, transverse)** with non-recurring indication, spontaneous labour, institutional with emergency CS facility; success ~60–80 %; **rupture risk ~0.5–1 %**; **contraindicated – previous classical/ T-shaped incision, previous rupture, ≥ 2–3 LSCS, praevia**; avoid prostaglandins.

## 14.5 Cord Prolapse & Presentation

- **Cord presentation** – cord below presenting part, **membranes intact**; **cord prolapse** – cord **below presenting part after ROM** (**occult** – beside presenting part).
- **Risk**: **malpresentation (footling breech, transverse lie)**, **prematurity/ LBW**, **polyhydramnios**, **ARM with high/ unengaged head**, twins (2nd), long cord, multiparity, CPD.
- **Diagnosis**: cord felt/ seen in vagina; **sudden fetal bradycardia/ variable decelerations after ROM**.

:::warn Cord prolapse – EMERGENCY ("Push up, lift off, call")
1. **Call for help**; note time; **don't handle cord** (vasospasm) – if outside, **wrap in warm saline gauze/ replace into vagina gently**
2. **Relieve pressure on cord**: **gloved hand in vagina – push presenting part up** (keep hand there till CS)
3. **Position**: **knee-chest** or **exaggerated Sims' (left lateral with hips elevated)** or **steep Trendelenburg**
4. **Bladder filling – 500–750 ml NS via Foley** (lifts presenting part) + clamp
5. **Tocolysis** (terbutaline 0.25 mg SC) to ↓ contractions
6. **O₂ to mother**, IV line, continuous FHS
7. **Immediate delivery – LSCS** (fastest) unless **fully dilated & delivery imminent → vacuum/ forceps** (vertex) / breech extraction
8. Cord **pulsatile = baby alive**; neonatal team ready
:::

## 14.6 Shoulder Dystocia

- **Failure of shoulders to deliver after head by gentle downward traction** – **anterior shoulder impacted behind symphysis pubis**. **"Turtle sign"** – head delivered, then **retracts against perineum**.
- **Risk**: **macrosomia (> 4–4.5 kg) – most important**, **maternal diabetes**, obesity, post-term, prolonged 2nd stage, instrumental delivery, previous shoulder dystocia.
- **Complications**: fetal – **brachial plexus injury (Erb's palsy – C5–C6, "waiter's tip")**, **clavicle/ humerus fracture**, hypoxia, death; maternal – **PPH, 3rd/ 4th degree tears**.

:::key Management – "HELPERR"
**H** – **Call for Help** (note time; head-to-body interval) ·
**E** – **Evaluate for Episiotomy** (to allow hand entry) ·
**L** – **Legs – McRoberts manoeuvre** (hyperflex mother's thighs onto abdomen – **first & most effective; ~40–50 % success**) ·
**P** – **Suprapubic Pressure (Rubin I)** – on posterior aspect of anterior shoulder, **NOT fundal pressure** ·
**E** – **Enter** – internal rotational manoeuvres (**Rubin II, Woods' corkscrew, reverse Woods**) ·
**R** – **Remove posterior arm** (deliver posterior arm) ·
**R** – **Roll** – **all-fours (Gaskin manoeuvre)**.
Last resort – **cleidotomy** (dead fetus), **Zavanelli** (push head back + CS), symphysiotomy.
**NEVER: fundal pressure, excessive lateral/ downward traction on head, pivoting** → worsens impaction, brachial plexus injury.
:::

## 14.7 Amniotic Fluid Embolism & Others

- **Amniotic fluid embolism (AFE)** – amniotic fluid/ fetal cells enter maternal circulation → anaphylactoid reaction; during **labour/ delivery/ immediately postpartum**; **sudden dyspnoea, cyanosis, hypoxia, hypotension/ cardiovascular collapse, seizures, DIC (bleeding)** → **very high mortality**; risk – **precipitate labour, oxytocin hyperstimulation, abruption, CS, multiparity, advanced age**. Management – **ABC, CPR (perimortem CS within 4–5 min if > 20 wks)**, O₂/ ventilation, inotropes, treat DIC (blood products).
- **Obstetric shock** – haemorrhagic (PPH, APH, ectopic, rupture), **neurogenic (inversion – disproportionate)**, septic (abortion, chorioamnionitis), cardiogenic, anaphylactic, embolic (AFE, PE). Management – **legs raised (left lateral tilt if pregnant)**, O₂, 2 IV lines, fluids/ blood, treat cause.
- **DIC in obstetrics** – **abruptio placentae (most common cause)**, **IUD (retained > 4 wks)**, **AFE**, **septic abortion**, severe pre-eclampsia/ HELLP, massive PPH. Lab – **↓ platelets, ↓ fibrinogen (< 100), ↑ PT/ aPTT, ↑ D-dimer/ FDP**; **bedside clot observation test** (clot not formed in 7–10 min / clot lyses); management – **treat cause (deliver)**, FFP, cryoprecipitate, platelets, blood.
- **Pulmonary embolism / DVT** – leading direct cause of maternal death in developed countries; prophylaxis LMWH (high risk), early ambulation.
- **Maternal collapse – "4 Hs & 4 Ts" + pregnancy causes**; CPR in pregnancy – **manual left uterine displacement**, hands slightly higher on sternum.

:::exam PYQ style
- PPH definition – **≥ 500 ml vaginal / ≥ 1000 ml CS**; primary within **24 hrs**.
- Commonest cause of PPH – **uterine atony**; commonest cause of maternal death – **PPH**.
- Secondary PPH commonest cause – **retained bits/ infection**.
- First step in PPH – **call for help & uterine massage**; empty **bladder**.
- First-line uterotonic – **oxytocin**; **methergine contraindicated in HTN**; **carboprost contraindicated in asthma**.
- Tranexamic acid – **1 g IV within 3 hrs**.
- Bleeding with contracted uterus – **genital tract tears**.
- Retained placenta – **> 30 min** → **manual removal**.
- Uterine inversion – shock **out of proportion**; Rx – **immediate manual replacement (Johnson)**.
- Earliest sign of uterine rupture – **fetal distress (abnormal FHR)**; commonest cause – **previous CS scar**.
- Cord prolapse – **push presenting part up, knee-chest, LSCS**.
- Shoulder dystocia first manoeuvre – **McRoberts**; never **fundal pressure**.
- Turtle sign – **shoulder dystocia**; injury – **Erb's palsy**.
- Commonest obstetric cause of DIC – **abruptio placentae**.
- Lactation failure after PPH – **Sheehan's syndrome**.
:::
