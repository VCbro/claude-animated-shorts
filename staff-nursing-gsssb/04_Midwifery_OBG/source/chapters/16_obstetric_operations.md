## 16.1 Caesarean Section (CS)

:::def Caesarean section
Delivery of fetus **through incisions on abdominal wall (laparotomy) and uterus (hysterotomy)** after viability. **Lower segment CS (LSCS)** – transverse incision on **lower uterine segment** – **most common (~99 %)**; **Classical CS** – vertical incision on **upper segment**.
:::

| | **LSCS** | **Classical** |
|---|---|---|
| Incision | **Transverse, lower segment** (Kerr's) | **Vertical, upper segment** |
| Blood loss | Less | More |
| Healing | Better (passive segment) | Poor |
| **Scar rupture risk later** | **Low (0.5–1 %)** – VBAC possible | **High (4–9 %, may rupture before labour)** – **VBAC contraindicated** |
| Adhesions/ peritonitis | Less | More |
| Indications | Most cases | **Lower segment inaccessible** – dense adhesions, **fibroid in LUS**, **transverse lie with back down**, very preterm (no LUS), **perimortem CS**, Ca cervix, placenta praevia with large vessels |

- **Indications**: **Absolute** – **CPD/ contracted pelvis**, **central placenta praevia**, **obstructed labour (live baby)**, transverse lie (term), brow, **mento-posterior face**, Ca cervix, previous classical CS, previous VVF repair. **Relative** – **previous LSCS (commonest indication currently)**, **fetal distress**, **failed induction/ non-progress of labour**, breech (primi), malpresentation, severe pre-eclampsia/ eclampsia (unfavourable cervix), APH, cord prolapse, twins (1st non-vertex), **elderly primi**, bad obstetric history, macrosomia, HIV (high viral load), active herpes.
- **Categories (urgency – RCOG)**: **1** – immediate threat to life (**decision-to-delivery ≤ 30 min**), 2 – compromise, 3 – needs early delivery, 4 – elective.
- **Robson's 10-group classification** – CS audit (WHO).
- **Anaesthesia** – **spinal (most common)**, epidural, GA (emergency – aspiration risk: **Mendelson's syndrome** – acid aspiration → **antacid/ ranitidine/ PPI, metoclopramide pre-op, cricoid pressure**).

### Pre-operative care
- **Informed consent**, **NPO 6–8 hrs** (elective), **pre-op investigations** (Hb, grouping & **cross-match 2 units**, BT/CT, HIV, HBsAg, urine), **antacid prophylaxis**, **IV line**, **Foley catheter** (empty bladder – injury prevention), **skin prep (clipping)**, **prophylactic antibiotic (cefazolin 2 g IV within 60 min before incision)**, remove jewellery/ dentures, **left lateral tilt (15°) on table** (aortocaval compression), FHS check before shifting, neonatal team & resuscitation ready.

### Post-operative care
- **Vitals every 15 min (1st hr)**, then 30 min, hourly; **uterine tone & vaginal bleeding (PPH)**; dressing (soakage); **IV fluids**; **oxytocin infusion**; **urine output (catheter removed after 12–24 hrs)**; **pain relief** (opioids, NSAIDs, epidural); **position – lateral/ supine (spinal – flat few hrs not mandatory)**, then semi-Fowler's.
- **Early breastfeeding (within 1 hr – skin-to-skin in OT/ recovery)** – side-lying or **football hold**.
- **Oral fluids** after **6–8 hrs** (if bowel sounds; early feeding encouraged – ERAS), soft diet next day; **early ambulation – within 12–24 hrs** (DVT prevention), deep breathing & coughing (**splint wound**), leg exercises.
- **Wound** – dressing removed on day 2–3; **sutures/ staples removed day 7–8** (subcuticular absorbable – no removal).
- **Complications**: **PPH**, **infection (wound, endometritis, UTI)**, **paralytic ileus**, **DVT/ PE**, bladder/ bowel injury, anaesthesia complications, **spinal headache**, wound dehiscence; long-term – adhesions, **scar rupture**, **placenta accreta/ praevia in next pregnancy**, scar ectopic.
- **Discharge advice**: avoid heavy lifting **6 wks**, **contraception – space next pregnancy ≥ 18–24 months**, **institutional delivery next time (previous CS – high risk)**, wound care, warning signs.

## 16.2 Instrumental (Operative Vaginal) Delivery

### Prerequisites ("FORCEPS" / "ABCDEFGHIJ")
- **Cervix fully dilated**, **membranes ruptured**, **head engaged (≤ 1/5 palpable, station ≥ 0/ +2)**, **position known exactly**, **vertex (or face mento-anterior for forceps; no face for vacuum)**, **no CPD**, **empty bladder**, adequate analgesia (pudendal block/ epidural), **episiotomy**, informed consent, adequate contractions (vacuum), experienced operator, **back-up for CS ("trial in OT")**, neonatologist present.

| | **Obstetric Forceps** | **Vacuum (Ventouse)** |
|---|---|---|
| Inventor | **Chamberlen** (17th century) | **Malmström** (1954 – metal cup); silastic/ Kiwi |
| Principle | **Traction + rotation** (blades on fetal head – biparietal-bimalar) | **Suction cup on scalp – traction**; **flexion point** (3 cm anterior to posterior fontanelle on sagittal suture) |
| Maternal injury | **More (tears)** | **Less** |
| Fetal injury | Facial palsy, facial bruises, skull fracture | **Cephalhaematoma, subgaleal haemorrhage**, retinal haemorrhage, **chignon (artificial caput – disappears in hours)**, jaundice |
| **Can't be used** | – | **Preterm < 34 wks**, **face/ brow/ breech**, fetal bleeding disorder, recent scalp blood sampling |
| Anaesthesia | Needs good analgesia | Less needed |
| Special uses | **After-coming head of breech (Piper's)**, face (mento-anterior), **rotation (Kielland's)**, CS head delivery (Wrigley's) | Doesn't occupy space – rotation automatic |

- Types of forceps – **Wrigley's (outlet – short)**, **Simpson's/ Das's/ Neville-Barnes (mid/ low)**, **Kielland's (rotational – no pelvic curve, sliding lock)**, **Piper's (after-coming head of breech)**.
- Classification (ACOG): **outlet** (scalp visible without separating labia, rotation ≤ 45°), **low (station ≥ +2)**, **mid (station 0 to +2)**; **high forceps – obsolete**.
- **Vacuum** – pressure **0.6–0.8 kg/cm² (≈ 500–600 mmHg)**; **max 3 pulls with descent** / **cup detachment ≤ 2–3 times** / total ≤ 15–20 min → abandon → CS.
- **Indications**: **prolonged 2nd stage**, **maternal exhaustion**, **fetal distress in 2nd stage**, **maternal conditions where bearing down is harmful – heart disease, severe pre-eclampsia/ HTN, severe anaemia, previous CS (cut short 2nd stage)**, ↑ICP, retinal detachment.

## 16.3 Other Procedures

| Procedure | Key points |
|---|---|
| **ARM (Artificial Rupture of Membranes/ Amniotomy)** | Induction/ augmentation; **head engaged/ well applied (cord prolapse risk if high)**; **check FHS before & after**, note liquor colour & amount; contraindicated – praevia, HIV (relative), malpresentation |
| **Membrane sweeping (stripping)** | Finger separates membranes from lower segment → PG release → labour within 48 hrs |
| **External cephalic version (ECV)** | **Breech at 36–37 wks** – convert to cephalic; tocolysis; CTG; **anti-D if Rh –ve** |
| **Internal podalic version** | **Only for 2nd twin (transverse)** – hand in uterus, grasp foot, convert to breech → extract |
| **Manual removal of placenta (MRP)** | Retained placenta > 30 min/ bleeding – GA/ sedation, aseptic, **antibiotics**, uterotonics |
| **MVA (Manual Vacuum Aspiration)** | Incomplete abortion/ MTP **up to 12 wks** – **Karman cannula + 60 ml syringe (−60 to −70 cmHg)** – no electricity, **paracervical block**; **safer than D&C** |
| **D&C (Dilatation & Curettage)** | Hegar's dilators + curette – diagnostic (endometrial biopsy)/ evacuation; risk – **perforation**, **Asherman's syndrome (intrauterine adhesions)** |
| **Cervical cerclage** | **McDonald (most common)**/ Shirodkar – at **12–14 wks**; **removed at 37 wks** or in labour/ PROM |
| **Destructive operations** | **Dead fetus** in obstructed labour (rare now): **craniotomy** (perforate & extract head – hydrocephalus/ obstructed), **decapitation** (neglected shoulder), **cleidotomy** (cut clavicle – shoulder dystocia dead fetus), evisceration |
| **Symphysiotomy** | Divide symphysis pubis (rare) |
| **Amnioinfusion** | NS into uterine cavity – **variable decelerations from oligohydramnios/ cord compression**, thick meconium |
| **Amniocentesis, CVS, cordocentesis** | See fetal wellbeing chapter |

## 16.4 Analgesia & Anaesthesia in Labour

- **Non-pharmacological**: **continuous labour support (birth companion – most effective non-drug)**, breathing techniques, **ambulation/ positioning**, **back massage & counter-pressure**, warm compress, **hydrotherapy**, TENS, music, hypnosis, aromatherapy; **Lamaze (psychoprophylaxis)**, **Grantly Dick-Read (fear-tension-pain cycle – natural childbirth)**, **Bradley (husband-coached)**.
- **Inhalational** – **Entonox (50 % N₂O + 50 % O₂)** – self-administered, start inhaling **30 sec before contraction**.
- **Systemic opioids** – **Pethidine (meperidine) 50–100 mg IM** – avoid **within 2–4 hrs of expected delivery** (**neonatal respiratory depression** – antidote **naloxone 0.1 mg/kg**); fentanyl, tramadol.
- **Regional – Epidural analgesia (gold standard/ most effective)** – **L3–L4 / L2–L3**, catheter, bupivacaine + fentanyl; side effects – **hypotension (most common – preload IV fluids, left tilt)**, prolonged 2nd stage, ↑ instrumental, urinary retention, fever, **post-dural puncture headache**, high block; contraindications – coagulopathy, infection at site, hypovolaemia, patient refusal, ↑ICP.
- **Spinal** (single shot – CS); **CSE** (combined spinal-epidural).
- **Pudendal nerve block** – **S2–S4**, transvaginal near **ischial spine** – **outlet forceps/ vacuum, episiotomy repair**.
- **Paracervical block** – MVA/ D&C (fetal bradycardia if in labour).
- **Local perineal infiltration** – **1 % lignocaine** (max 3 mg/kg; with adrenaline 7 mg/kg) – episiotomy.
- **General anaesthesia** – emergency CS; risks – **aspiration (Mendelson's)**, failed intubation, awareness, neonatal depression, **atony (halothane)**.

:::exam PYQ style
- Commonest type of CS – **LSCS (transverse lower segment)**.
- Commonest indication of CS today – **previous CS**.
- VBAC contraindicated after – **classical CS**.
- Mendelson's syndrome – **acid aspiration under GA**; prevention – **antacids, H2 blockers, cricoid pressure**.
- Prerequisites for forceps – **full dilatation, ROM, engaged head, empty bladder**.
- Vacuum contraindicated – **preterm < 34 wks, face, breech**.
- Chignon – **vacuum** (artificial caput).
- Forceps for after-coming head – **Piper's**; rotational – **Kielland's**.
- MVA used up to – **12 wks**; complication of curettage – **Asherman's syndrome**.
- Cerclage removed at – **37 wks**.
- Pethidine avoided near delivery due to – **neonatal respiratory depression**.
- Most effective labour analgesia – **epidural**; common side effect – **hypotension**.
- Pudendal block nerve roots – **S2–S4**.
:::
