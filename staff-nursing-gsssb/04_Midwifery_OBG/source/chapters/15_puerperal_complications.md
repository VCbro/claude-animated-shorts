## 15.1 Puerperal Pyrexia & Sepsis

- **Puerperal pyrexia** – temperature **≥ 38 °C (100.4 °F)** on **2 separate occasions 24 hrs apart** (excluding first 24 hrs) **within first 10 days** postpartum.
- Causes: **genital tract infection (puerperal sepsis) – most common**, **UTI**, **breast (engorgement/ mastitis/ abscess)**, **wound infection (episiotomy/ CS)**, **thrombophlebitis**, respiratory (atelectasis), malaria, typhoid.
- **Puerperal sepsis** – infection of genital tract after childbirth (**within 42 days**); **major cause of maternal death**; organisms – **anaerobic Streptococci**, **E. coli**, **Group A β-haemolytic Streptococcus** (most virulent), Staph, Clostridium (gas gangrene).
- **Risk factors**: **prolonged labour**, **PROM > 18 hrs**, **frequent PV examinations**, **unclean delivery (home/ untrained)**, **retained products**, **CS**, PPH, anaemia, malnutrition, manual removal, instrumental, diabetes.
- **Types**: localized – **endometritis (most common)**, perineal/ vaginal/ cervical infection; spreading – **parametritis (pelvic cellulitis)**, salpingitis, **pelvic peritonitis**, thrombophlebitis; generalized – **septicaemia, peritonitis, septic shock**.
- **Signs**: fever, chills, tachycardia, **foul-smelling lochia (or scanty lochia – lochiometra)**, **lower abdominal pain, uterine tenderness, subinvolution**, malaise, headache.
- **Management**: **isolation/ barrier nursing**, **high vaginal swab/ blood/ urine culture before antibiotics**, **broad-spectrum IV antibiotics (ampicillin + gentamicin + metronidazole)**, IV fluids, **Fowler's position (drainage of lochia, localize pus in pelvis)**, perineal hygiene, evacuate retained products, drain abscess, nutrition, analgesics/ antipyretics, monitor vitals 4-hrly, I/O; septic shock – resuscitation.
- **Prevention**: **"6 cleans" (WHO/ clean delivery)** – **clean hands, clean surface, clean blade, clean cord tie, clean perineum, clean cord stump** (+ clean towel); **asepsis**, **limit PV examinations (4-hrly)**, prophylactic antibiotics (CS), early ambulation, nutrition, treat anaemia.

## 15.2 Other Puerperal Complications

| Complication | Key features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **UTI/ pyelonephritis** | Frequency, dysuria, fever, loin pain (catheterization, trauma) | Fluids, antibiotics (culture) |
| **Urinary retention** | Can't void within 6–8 hrs (perineal pain, epidural, oedema, trauma) | Privacy, warm water, ambulation, analgesics → **catheterization**; overdistension → **atony & PPH** |
| **Obstetric fistula (VVF/ RVF)** | **Prolonged obstructed labour – pressure necrosis** → **continuous dribbling of urine** (VVF) from 3rd–10th day; faecal (RVF) | **Continuous bladder drainage 10–14 days** (small fresh fistula may heal), **surgical repair after 3 months**; prevention – **avoid prolonged labour, catheter in obstructed labour** |
| **Breast engorgement** | Day 3–5, painful full breasts, low fever | Frequent feeding, warm compress & expression |
| **Mastitis/ breast abscess** | Wedge-shaped red tender area, fever (Staph aureus) | **Continue feeding**, antibiotics; abscess → drainage |
| **Subinvolution** | Uterus larger, boggy, persistent red lochia | Treat cause (retained bits – evacuation, infection – antibiotics), methergine |
| **Secondary PPH** | Day 1 – 6 weeks; retained bits, infection | Uterotonics, antibiotics, evacuation |
| **Superficial thrombophlebitis** | Varicose veins – tender cord, redness | Elevation, warm compress, analgesics, support stockings |
| **DVT (phlegmasia alba dolens – "white leg")** | Calf pain, swelling, **Homans' sign** (dorsiflexion → calf pain – not reliable), fever (2nd week) | **Doppler**; **heparin/ LMWH → warfarin** (breastfeeding safe); **bed rest with leg elevated** initially, **no massage**; stockings |
| **Pulmonary embolism** | Sudden dyspnoea, chest pain, tachycardia, collapse | O₂, propped up, heparin, thrombolysis |
| **Haematoma (vulval/ vaginal/ broad ligament)** | **Severe perineal pain, tense tender swelling, urinary retention**, shock | Small – ice packs; large/ expanding – **incision & evacuation** |
| **Wound infection/ dehiscence (episiotomy, CS)** | Redness, discharge, gaping | Sitz bath, antibiotics, resuturing (secondary) |
| **Postpartum eclampsia** | Fits within 48 hrs (up to 6 wks) | MgSO₄, BP control |
| **Coccydynia, symphysis separation, foot drop** | Birth trauma | Analgesics, support |

## 15.3 Postpartum Psychiatric Disorders

| Disorder | Onset | Incidence | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Postpartum blues (baby blues/ maternity blues)** | **Day 3–5 (peaks day 5)**, lasts **≤ 2 weeks** | **50–80 %** | Tearfulness, mood swings, anxiety, irritability, insomnia; **self-limiting** | **Reassurance, support, rest**; no drugs |
| **Postpartum depression** | Within **4 weeks–1 year** (common 2–3 months) | **10–15 %** | Persistent low mood > 2 wks, loss of interest, guilt, poor bonding, suicidal/ **infanticidal thoughts**; **Edinburgh Postnatal Depression Scale (EPDS)** screening | Counselling (CBT), **SSRIs (sertraline – compatible with breastfeeding)**, family support |
| **Postpartum (puerperal) psychosis** | **Within first 2 weeks** (abrupt, day 3–14) | **1–2 per 1000** | **Hallucinations, delusions** (often about baby), confusion, severe mood swings, **risk of suicide & infanticide** – **psychiatric emergency**; risk – **bipolar disorder**, previous episode | **Hospitalization (mother-baby unit)**, **antipsychotics, mood stabilizers, ECT**; **don't leave mother alone with baby**; supervised breastfeeding |

## 15.4 Common Neonatal Problems

### Birth asphyxia & HIE
- Failure to initiate/ sustain breathing at birth; **APGAR ≤ 3 at 5 min**, metabolic acidosis; **Hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (Sarnat staging I–III)** – lethargy, seizures, hypotonia. Rx – resuscitation, supportive, **therapeutic hypothermia (cooling 33.5 °C × 72 hrs)** in selected, anticonvulsants (**phenobarbitone – drug of choice for neonatal seizures**).

### Respiratory distress
- **RDS (Hyaline membrane disease)** – **surfactant deficiency** – **preterm (< 34 wks)**, IDM; onset **within 6 hrs**, **grunting, tachypnoea > 60, chest retractions, nasal flaring, cyanosis**; X-ray – **ground-glass, air bronchogram**; Rx – **CPAP**, **surfactant (intratracheal)**, O₂, ventilation; prevention – **antenatal steroids**. **Silverman-Anderson score** (0–10).
- **Transient tachypnoea of newborn (TTN)** – **delayed clearance of lung fluid** – **term, elective CS**; resolves in 24–72 hrs.
- **Meconium aspiration syndrome (MAS)** – **post-term, fetal distress**; chemical pneumonitis, PPHN.

### Neonatal hypoglycaemia
- **Blood glucose < 45 mg/dL (< 40)**; risk – **IDM (infant of diabetic mother – most common)**, preterm, SGA/ IUGR, LBW, hypothermia, sepsis, asphyxia.
- Signs – **jitteriness/ tremors**, lethargy, poor feeding, apnoea, hypothermia, high-pitched cry, **seizures**; may be asymptomatic → screen at-risk babies.
- Rx – **early & frequent breastfeeding**; symptomatic/ < 25 → **IV 10 % dextrose 2 ml/kg bolus** → infusion **6–8 mg/kg/min**.

### Neonatal sepsis
- **Early onset (< 72 hrs)** – maternal (PROM, chorioamnionitis, fever) – **E. coli, Group B Strep, Klebsiella**; **late onset (> 72 hrs)** – nosocomial/ community – **Klebsiella, Staph aureus**.
- Signs – **refusal to feed (earliest)**, lethargy, temperature instability (**hypothermia more common**), apnoea, jaundice, vomiting, distension, poor cry, **sclerema**, seizures, **bulging fontanelle (meningitis)**.
- Rx – **blood culture** → **ampicillin + gentamicin** (first-line), supportive; **hand hygiene** = best prevention; **omphalitis** – umbilical redness/ pus.
- **Ophthalmia neonatorum** – conjunctivitis **within 28 days**; **gonococcal (day 2–5, purulent – most serious, corneal ulcer)**, chlamydial (day 5–14); Rx – ceftriaxone (gono), erythromycin (chlamydia); prophylaxis – clean eyes.

### Birth injuries
| Injury | Features |
|---|---|
| **Caput succedaneum** | **Scalp oedema, crosses sutures**, at birth, resolves 2–3 days |
| **Cephalhaematoma** | **Subperiosteal blood**, **doesn't cross sutures**, appears after hours, resolves weeks–months; **jaundice risk**; **don't aspirate** |
| **Subgaleal haemorrhage** | Below aponeurosis – **vacuum** – can be massive (shock) |
| **Erb's palsy (Erb-Duchenne)** | **C5–C6** – **shoulder dystocia/ breech** – arm adducted, internally rotated, forearm pronated – **"waiter's tip"**; **asymmetric Moro**, grasp present; Rx – **positioning (abduct & externally rotate), physiotherapy** – most recover |
| **Klumpke's palsy** | **C8–T1** – claw hand, absent grasp, Horner's |
| **Clavicle fracture** | **Most common birth fracture** – crepitus, asymmetric Moro → heals spontaneously |
| **Facial palsy** | **Forceps** – CN VII; eye care |
| **Intracranial haemorrhage** | Preterm (IVH), breech, precipitate, excessive moulding |

### Common congenital anomalies (screen at birth)
- **Neural tube defects** – **anencephaly** (incompatible with life), **spina bifida/ meningomyelocele** (protect sac – **prone position, sterile moist saline dressing, no pressure**), encephalocele; prevention – **periconceptional folic acid**.
- **Hydrocephalus** – ↑ head circumference, **bulging fontanelle, sunset eyes**; VP shunt.
- **Cleft lip/ palate** – feeding (special nipple, upright, burp often); **cleft lip repair ~3 months (rule of 10s)**, palate 9–18 months; **elbow restraints** post-op.
- **Oesophageal atresia with TEF** – **polyhydramnios**, **excessive frothy drooling, choking/ cyanosis with feeds**; **can't pass NG tube (coils)** → **NPO, prop up, continuous suction of pouch**, surgery.
- **Imperforate anus**, **Hirschsprung's** (no meconium in 48 hrs), **congenital hypertrophic pyloric stenosis** (projectile non-bilious vomiting **2–6 wks**, olive mass), **CTEV (club foot – Ponseti casting)**, **DDH (Ortolani, Barlow; Pavlik harness)**, **Down syndrome (trisomy 21 – maternal age, simian crease, flat face, hypotonia)**, **congenital hypothyroidism (TSH screening)**, **congenital heart disease (PDA, VSD – commonest)**.
