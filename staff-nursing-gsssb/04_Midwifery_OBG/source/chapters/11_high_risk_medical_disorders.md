## 11.1 High-Risk Pregnancy

- Pregnancy in which **mother/ fetus/ newborn** is at **↑ risk of morbidity or mortality**. ~20–30 % of pregnancies; contribute ~70–80 % of perinatal deaths.
- **Risk factors**: **age < 18 or > 35**, **height < 140/ 145 cm**, weight < 40/ > 90 kg, **grand multipara (≥ 5)**, **short birth interval (< 18–24 months)**, **bad obstetric history (BOH)** – previous stillbirth, abortions, PPH, LSCS, preterm; **medical diseases** (HTN, DM, heart, renal, anaemia Hb < 7, TB, epilepsy, thyroid), **Rh –ve**, multiple pregnancy, malpresentation, APH, pre-eclampsia, **elderly primi**, infertility treatment, previous congenital anomaly, poor socioeconomic, smoking/ alcohol.
- **PMSMA red sticker = high risk**; refer to **FRU (First Referral Unit – CEmONC)**. **BEmONC 7 signal functions**: parenteral antibiotics, parenteral oxytocics, **parenteral anticonvulsants (MgSO₄)**, manual removal of placenta, removal of retained products (MVA), **assisted vaginal delivery (vacuum)**, **newborn resuscitation** (+ CEmONC: **C-section & blood transfusion**).

## 11.2 Hyperemesis Gravidarum

- **Severe vomiting** in pregnancy → **dehydration, ketosis, electrolyte imbalance (hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia, metabolic alkalosis/ ketoacidosis), weight loss > 5 %**; typically **first trimester**.
- More in **primigravida, multiple pregnancy, H. mole** (↑ hCG), young, psychological.
- Complications – **Wernicke's encephalopathy (thiamine B1 deficiency)**, Mallory-Weiss tear, IUGR.
- **Management**: **admission**, **NPO 24–48 hrs**, **IV fluids (NS/ RL; dextrose after thiamine)**, **thiamine 100 mg** before dextrose, **antiemetics (doxylamine-pyridoxine, promethazine, metoclopramide, ondansetron)**, correct electrolytes, then **small frequent dry carbohydrate meals**; I/O, urine ketones, weight, psychological support.

## 11.3 Anaemia in Pregnancy

- **Definition (WHO)**: **Hb < 11 g/dL** (1st & 3rd trimester), **< 10.5 g/dL (2nd)**. **Mild 10–10.9**, **moderate 7–9.9**, **severe < 7**, **very severe < 4**. India – **~52 % pregnant women anaemic (NFHS-5)**.
- **Most common – iron deficiency** (then folate/ B12 – megaloblastic, haemoglobinopathies – **sickle cell, thalassaemia** in Gujarat tribal).
- **Effects** – mother: fatigue, **PPH intolerance**, infections, **heart failure (Hb < 5 – especially in labour & immediately postpartum)**, preterm labour, maternal death; fetus: **LBW, IUGR, preterm**, stillbirth.
- **Management**: **prophylaxis – IFA 1 tab daily × 180 days** (from 2nd trimester) + 180 PNC; **treatment** – Hb 9–10.9: **2 IFA tabs daily (120 mg iron)**; moderate/ intolerant/ late (> 30 wks) → **IV iron sucrose / ferric carboxymaltose (FCM)**; **severe after 34 wks or Hb < 5–7 → packed RBC transfusion**; deworming, diet (green leafy vegetables, jaggery, dates, meat; **Vit C ↑ absorption**, tea/ coffee/ calcium ↓).
- Labour in severe anaemia: **left lateral/ propped up**, O₂, **cut short 2nd stage (forceps/ vacuum)**, AMTSL, **avoid fluid overload**, diuretics, antibiotics.

## 11.4 Hypertensive Disorders of Pregnancy

| Disorder | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Gestational hypertension (PIH)** | **BP ≥ 140/90 (2 readings 4 hrs apart) after 20 wks**, **no proteinuria**, resolves by 12 wks postpartum |
| **Pre-eclampsia** | **HTN after 20 wks + proteinuria (≥ 300 mg/24 hr, dipstick ≥ 1+)** or HTN + end-organ dysfunction (even without proteinuria) |
| **Severe pre-eclampsia** | **BP ≥ 160/110**, **proteinuria ≥ 2+/ 5 g**, **headache, visual disturbance, epigastric/ RUQ pain**, oliguria (< 400 ml/24 h), **platelets < 1 lakh**, ↑ liver enzymes, pulmonary oedema, creatinine > 1.1, IUGR |
| **Eclampsia** | **Pre-eclampsia + convulsions (generalised tonic-clonic)** not due to other cause – antepartum (~50 %), intrapartum, postpartum (within 48 hrs mostly) |
| **HELLP syndrome** | **H**aemolysis, **E**levated **L**iver enzymes, **L**ow **P**latelets – severe pre-eclampsia variant |
| **Chronic HTN** | Before pregnancy/ **before 20 wks**; superimposed pre-eclampsia possible |

- **Risk factors**: **primigravida (most common)**, extremes of age, **multiple pregnancy, H. mole (pre-eclampsia < 20 wks)**, diabetes, chronic HTN, renal disease, obesity, family/ previous history, polyhydramnios, APLA.
- **Pathology**: **failure of 2nd wave of trophoblastic invasion of spiral arteries** → placental ischaemia → **endothelial dysfunction, vasospasm**.
- **Triad (classic)**: **Hypertension + Proteinuria + Oedema** (oedema no longer required). **Earliest sign – rapid weight gain/ oedema**; **most important – hypertension**.
- **Imminent eclampsia signs**: **severe headache (frontal/ occipital), blurring of vision/ flashes (scotomas), epigastric pain**, vomiting, oliguria, **brisk reflexes/ clonus**.
- **Prevention** (high risk): **low-dose aspirin 75–150 mg from 12–16 wks till 36 wks**, **calcium 1–1.5 g/day**.
- **Antihypertensives**: **Labetalol** (oral/ IV – first-line), **Nifedipine** (oral, not sublingual), **Methyldopa** (oral – safe), **Hydralazine** (IV). **Avoid ACE inhibitors, ARBs** (fetotoxic), diuretics (↓ plasma volume). **Aim BP < 150/100 (target ~135/85)**.

:::key Eclampsia – management (ABC + MgSO₄ + delivery)
- **During fit**: **don't leave patient, call for help**, **protect from injury** (padded side rails, **no restraint**), **left lateral position**, **airway – mouth gag/ airway (between fits, not forcibly)**, **suction**, **O₂ 4–6 L**; note duration.
- **Anticonvulsant of choice – Magnesium sulphate (MgSO₄)** – **Pritchard regimen**:
 - **Loading dose**: **4 g IV (20 % solution – 20 ml, slowly over 5–10 min) + 10 g deep IM (5 g in each buttock – 50 % solution, 10 ml each, with 1 ml 2 % lignocaine)**
 - **Maintenance**: **5 g IM (50 %) every 4 hrs in alternate buttock**, continued **24 hrs after delivery or last fit** (whichever later)
 - **Zuspan regimen (IV)**: 4–6 g IV loading → **1–2 g/hr infusion**
- **Before each maintenance dose check**: **patellar (knee) reflex present**, **RR ≥ 16/min (≥ 12)**, **urine output ≥ 30 ml/hr (100 ml in 4 hrs)**.
- **Therapeutic serum Mg 4–7 mEq/L**; loss of patellar reflex – 8–10; respiratory depression 12–15; cardiac arrest > 25.
- **Toxicity antidote**: **10 % calcium gluconate 10 ml IV slowly (over 10 min)** – keep at bedside.
- **Recurrent fit** – extra **2 g IV MgSO₄**.
- **BP control** (labetalol/ nifedipine/ hydralazine), **Foley catheter** (hourly urine), **I/O chart** (restrict fluids ~**80 ml/hr** – pulmonary oedema risk), **quiet dark room**, lab (LFT, platelets, RFT, coagulation).
- **Definitive treatment – DELIVERY** (within 6–12 hrs of stabilization, irrespective of gestation) – vaginal (ARM + oxytocin) preferred if favourable, LSCS for obstetric indications; **avoid ergometrine/ methergine** (HTN).
- **Complications**: cerebral haemorrhage (most common cause of death), pulmonary oedema, aspiration pneumonia, HELLP, DIC, acute renal failure, **abruptio placentae**, PRES, retinal detachment; fetus – IUGR, prematurity, IUD.
:::

## 11.5 Diabetes in Pregnancy

- **GDM** – glucose intolerance first detected in pregnancy. **India – DIPSI single-step test**: **75 g oral glucose irrespective of last meal → 2-hr plasma glucose ≥ 140 mg/dL = GDM** (at first ANC & **24–28 wks**). **IADPSG/ WHO**: fasting ≥ 92, 1-hr ≥ 180, 2-hr ≥ 153.
- **Risk factors** – obesity, family history, **previous macrosomic baby (> 4 kg)**, previous GDM, unexplained stillbirth, PCOS, age > 25–30, Asian ethnicity, polyhydramnios.
- **Effects** – mother: **polyhydramnios**, pre-eclampsia, infections (UTI, candida), **shoulder dystocia**, LSCS, PPH, future **Type 2 DM (~50 %)**; fetus: **macrosomia (> 4 kg)**, **congenital anomalies (pre-gestational DM – caudal regression syndrome, cardiac, NTD)**, **unexplained IUD (after 36 wks)**, RDS (delayed lung maturity), birth injury; neonate: **hypoglycaemia (most common – fetal hyperinsulinaemia)**, hypocalcaemia, **polycythaemia, hyperbilirubinaemia**.
- **Management**: **MNT (diet) + exercise** first (2 weeks); targets **fasting < 95, 1-hr PP < 140, 2-hr PP < 120 mg/dL**; **insulin (drug of choice)** / **metformin** (now accepted); **oral sulfonylureas avoided**; SMBG; fetal surveillance; **deliver at 38–39 wks** (well controlled 39–40); during labour **insulin + dextrose infusion**, glucose 1–2 hrly; **neonatal blood sugar** within 1 hr; **insulin requirement falls sharply after delivery** (stop for GDM); **OGTT at 6–12 wks postpartum**.

## 11.6 Heart Disease in Pregnancy

- **Most common – rheumatic heart disease (India) – mitral stenosis**; congenital in west.
- **NYHA classes I–IV**; **critical periods (maximum risk of failure)**: **28–32 wks** (peak blood volume/ CO), **during labour (2nd stage)**, and **immediately after delivery (most dangerous – autotransfusion ~500 ml)**.
- **Management**: rest, iron, treat anaemia/ infection, **penicillin prophylaxis (RHD)**, **avoid fluid overload**, digoxin/ diuretics/ beta-blockers as needed, **anticoagulants** (mechanical valve – heparin/ LMWH 1st trimester & near term, warfarin 2nd), endocarditis prophylaxis.
- **Labour**: **propped-up (semi-Fowler's)/ left lateral**, O₂, **epidural analgesia** (↓ stress), **cut short 2nd stage (forceps/ vacuum – avoid bearing down)**; **3rd stage – oxytocin (slow); AVOID ERGOMETRINE (methergine)** – sudden ↑ venous return → pulmonary oedema; **furosemide after delivery**; monitor closely **first 24–48 hrs**. **LSCS only for obstetric indications**.
- **Breastfeeding allowed**; contraception – barrier/ progestin; **tubectomy** after family complete (preferably after puerperium); **Eisenmenger syndrome/ severe pulmonary HTN → pregnancy contraindicated (MTP)**.

## 11.7 Rh Isoimmunisation

- **Rh –ve mother + Rh +ve fetus** → fetal RBCs enter maternal circulation (**delivery, abortion, ectopic, amniocentesis, APH, ECV, trauma**) → mother forms **anti-D (IgG) antibodies** → in **next pregnancy** cross placenta → **fetal haemolysis** → **haemolytic disease of fetus & newborn (HDFN)** – anaemia, jaundice, **hydrops fetalis**, kernicterus. **First pregnancy usually not affected**.
- **ABO incompatibility** (mother O, baby A/B) – can affect **first pregnancy**, milder.
- **Tests**: **Indirect Coombs test (ICT) – mother** (at first visit & **28 wks**) – detects anti-D in maternal serum; **Direct Coombs test (DCT) – baby's cord blood** – antibodies on fetal RBCs. Severity – **MCA Doppler PSV** (fetal anaemia), amniotic fluid bilirubin (ΔOD 450 – Liley chart), cordocentesis.
- **Prevention – Anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM)**:
 - **Antenatal prophylaxis – 300 µg (1500 IU) IM at 28 wks** (non-sensitized, ICT –ve)
 - **Postnatal – 300 µg IM within 72 hrs of delivery** (if baby Rh +ve)
 - **After abortion/ ectopic/ MTP**: **< 12 wks – 50 µg**, **> 12 wks – 300 µg** within 72 hrs; also after amniocentesis, CVS, APH, ECV
 - **300 µg neutralizes ~30 ml fetal whole blood (15 ml RBCs)**; **Kleihauer-Betke test** – quantify fetomaternal haemorrhage
 - **Not given if mother already sensitized (ICT +ve)**
- **Treatment of affected fetus** – **intrauterine transfusion (O –ve)**, early delivery; newborn – **phototherapy, exchange transfusion (O –ve, cross-matched with mother)**.

## 11.8 Infections in Pregnancy

- **UTI & asymptomatic bacteriuria** (> 10⁵ CFU/ml) – screen & treat (nitrofurantoin, cephalexin, amoxicillin) → prevents **pyelonephritis (right side common)**, preterm labour.
- **TORCH** – see Chapter 3. **Rubella** in 1st trimester – **congenital rubella syndrome** (cataract, PDA, deafness, microcephaly) → MTP option; **MR vaccine not in pregnancy** (avoid pregnancy 1 month after).
- **Syphilis** – **VDRL/ RPR at first visit**; crosses placenta (any stage – mostly after 16 wks) → **congenital syphilis** (snuffles, rash, hepatosplenomegaly; late – Hutchinson's teeth, saddle nose); Rx – **Benzathine penicillin 2.4 MU IM** (mother & partner; single dose for early syphilis); Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction.
- **HIV (PPTCT)** – **lifelong ART (TLD) for all pregnant positive women irrespective of CD4** (Test & Treat); viral load monitoring; **safe institutional delivery – avoid ARM, episiotomy, instrumental, prolonged labour** (vaginal delivery fine if suppressed); baby – **Nevirapine syrup 6 weeks** (high risk – AZT + NVP for 12 wks), **cotrimoxazole from 6 wks**, **DNA PCR at 6 wks**; **exclusive breastfeeding + maternal ART** (no mixed feeding).
- **Hepatitis B** – HBsAg screening; baby **HBIG (0.5 ml) + Hep B vaccine within 12 hrs** (different thighs); breastfeeding allowed; **tenofovir** to mother in 3rd trimester if high viral load.
- **Malaria** – more severe in pregnancy (anaemia, abortion, IUGR, hypoglycaemia); Rx – **chloroquine (vivax)**, **ACT in 2nd/ 3rd trimester**, **quinine in 1st trimester (falciparum)**; **primaquine contraindicated**.
- **TB** – ATT safe (except **streptomycin – ototoxic**); breastfeeding ok; baby INH prophylaxis, BCG.
- **Group B Streptococcus** – intrapartum penicillin (west).

## 11.9 Multiple Pregnancy, IUGR & IUD

- **Twins**: incidence ~**1 in 80–90** (Hellin's rule – twins 1 : 80); **dizygotic (binovular, ~2/3 – heredity, ↑ maternal age, ovulation induction)** – 2 placentas, different sex possible; **monozygotic (identical)** – same sex. **Chorionicity by USG at 11–14 wks** – **lambda (twin peak) sign – dichorionic**, **T-sign – monochorionic** (**TTTS** risk).
 - Diagnosis – **fundal height > dates**, **multiple fetal parts**, **2 FHS (difference ≥ 10 bpm, heard simultaneously by 2 persons)**, USG.
 - Complications – **anaemia, pre-eclampsia, hyperemesis, polyhydramnios, preterm labour (most common)**, APH, malpresentation, **PPH (atonic – most common postpartum)**, cord prolapse, **locked twins** (first breech, second vertex).
 - Delivery – most common presentation **both vertex (~50 %)**; after first baby: **clamp cord (both ends – in case monozygotic)**, check lie of 2nd (USG/ palpation), FHS, **ARM after 2nd presenting part fixed**, deliver 2nd within ~30 min; **uterotonic only after last baby** (oxytocin infusion after delivery – PPH prophylaxis).
- **IUGR** – causes: maternal (**hypertension – most common**, anaemia, malnutrition, smoking, chronic disease), placental insufficiency, fetal (chromosomal, infections, multiple). Monitoring – **AC, Doppler (umbilical artery)**, BPP; deliver timely; neonate – hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, polycythaemia.
- **Intrauterine death (IUD)** – fetal death after viability before labour; signs – **absent fetal movements, absent FHS**, uterus smaller/ regressing, **Spalding's sign** (overlapping skull bones – X-ray ~7 days), **Robert's sign** (gas in fetal great vessels), USG – no cardiac activity (confirmatory). Complication – **DIC (if retained > 4 wks – hypofibrinogenaemia)**. Management – induction (**misoprostol**/ mifepristone + misoprostol), psychological support, lactation suppression, investigate cause, **Rh anti-D**.

:::exam PYQ style
- Anaemia in pregnancy – **Hb < 11 g/dL**; severe **< 7**.
- Pre-eclampsia – **HTN ≥ 140/90 after 20 wks + proteinuria**.
- Eclampsia drug of choice – **MgSO₄** (Pritchard: 4 g IV + 10 g IM loading; 5 g IM 4-hrly).
- MgSO₄ check – **knee jerk, RR ≥ 16, urine ≥ 30 ml/hr**; antidote – **10 % calcium gluconate**.
- Methergine contraindicated in – **pre-eclampsia, heart disease**.
- Antihypertensive first-line – **labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa**; avoid **ACE inhibitors**.
- GDM (DIPSI) – **75 g OGTT 2-hr ≥ 140 mg/dL**; drug of choice – **insulin**.
- Commonest neonatal problem in GDM – **hypoglycaemia**.
- Heart disease – most critical period – **immediately after delivery**.
- Anti-D dose after delivery – **300 µg IM within 72 hrs**; antenatal **28 wks**.
- Test for maternal antibodies – **indirect Coombs**; baby – **direct Coombs**.
- Twins – most common complication – **preterm labour**; dichorionic sign – **lambda/ twin peak**.
- Spalding's sign – **IUD (overlapping skull bones)**.
:::
