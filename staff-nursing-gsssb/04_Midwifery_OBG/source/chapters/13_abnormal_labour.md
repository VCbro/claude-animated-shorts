## 13.1 Malposition – Occipito-Posterior (OP)

- **Vertex with occiput in posterior quadrant** (ROP more common than LOP); ~**10–20 %** at onset of labour; **deflexed head → occipito-frontal 11.5 cm** presents → **prolonged labour**.
- Causes – **android/ anthropoid pelvis**, flat sacrum, deflexion, anterior placenta.
- **Signs**: **flattening of lower abdomen below umbilicus ("saucer-shaped")**, **fetal limbs anteriorly**, **FHS heard in flank**, **early ROM**, **backache (severe)**, early urge to push before full dilatation, slow descent, **anterior fontanelle felt easily** on PV.
- **Outcome**: **~90 % – long anterior rotation (3/8 circle → OA)** – normal delivery; **short posterior rotation → persistent OP ("face-to-pubis" delivery)**; **deep transverse arrest** (android pelvis); arrest.
- **Management**: wait (good contractions, **hands & knees/ lateral position**, back massage, hydration, empty bladder, analgesia/ epidural), oxytocin augmentation; persistent OP → **vacuum/ forceps (manual rotation/ Kielland's)**, **LSCS** for arrest/ CPD; large episiotomy (perineal tears more).

## 13.2 Breech Presentation

- **Buttocks/ lower limbs present**; **~3–4 % at term** (25 % at 28 wks); **most common malpresentation**.
- **Types**: **Frank (extended) – hips flexed, knees extended – most common (~65–70 %, primi)**; **Complete (flexed)** – hips & knees flexed (multipara); **Incomplete – footling/ knee** – feet/ knees present (**cord prolapse risk highest**).
- **Causes**: **prematurity (most common)**, multiparity (lax uterus), **placenta praevia**, uterine anomalies (septate/ bicornuate), polyhydramnios/ oligohydramnios, **hydrocephalus/ anencephaly**, multiple pregnancy, short cord, contracted pelvis.
- **Diagnosis**: **hard round ballotable head in fundus**, **FHS above umbilicus**, PV – soft irregular (anus, ischial tuberosities in line; genitalia; **meconium on glove – normal in breech**), USG.
- **Risks**: fetal – **cord prolapse**, **head entrapment (after-coming head)**, **birth asphyxia**, **intracranial haemorrhage (tentorial tear)**, **brachial plexus injury (Erb's)**, fractures (humerus, clavicle, femur), **visceral injury**; mother – perineal tears, PPH, LSCS.
- **Management**:
 - **External cephalic version (ECV)** – at **36–37 wks (primi 36, multi 37)** if no contraindication (**praevia, previous LSCS, PROM, twins, fetal compromise, APH, pre-eclampsia, Rh iso – give anti-D**); success ~50 %; tocolysis; CTG before & after.
 - **Mode of delivery** – **planned LSCS preferred** (Term Breech Trial 2000) especially primi, big baby > 3.5 kg, footling, hyperextended head, contracted pelvis, previous LSCS.
 - **Assisted vaginal breech delivery** (selected – frank/ complete, adequate pelvis, average size, experienced attendant): **"hands-off the breech"** till umbilicus; **episiotomy**; loop of cord pulled; **arms – Lovset's manoeuvre** (extended arms); **after-coming head** delivered by **Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit (MSV) manoeuvre (jaw flexion, shoulder traction)**, **Burns-Marshall method**, **Piper's forceps**; avoid hyperextension; **don't pull on breech**.
 - **Breech extraction** – only for 2nd twin.

## 13.3 Face, Brow, Shoulder & Compound Presentations

| Presentation | Key facts |
|---|---|
| **Face** | **Complete extension of head**; ~1 in 500; **denominator – mentum**; diameter **submento-bregmatic 9.5 cm**; causes – **anencephaly** (most common fetal cause), neck tumour, cord round neck, contracted pelvis, multiparity; **mento-anterior → vaginal delivery possible (by flexion)**; **mento-posterior persistent → LSCS (impossible vaginally)**; face oedema & bruising – reassure; **no vacuum** |
| **Brow** | **Partial extension (midway)**; **mento-vertical 13.5–14 cm (largest)** → **no vaginal delivery** in average-size baby → **LSCS** (may convert to face/ vertex); PV – **anterior fontanelle, orbital ridges, root of nose** |
| **Shoulder (transverse lie)** | ~1 in 300; causes – **multiparity (lax abdomen – most common)**, **placenta praevia**, polyhydramnios, contracted pelvis, uterine anomaly, prematurity, twins (2nd baby); **abdomen broad, fundus low**, no presenting part; **vaginal delivery impossible (term live baby) → LSCS**; danger – **cord prolapse, hand prolapse, obstructed labour, rupture uterus (neglected shoulder)**; ECV/ internal podalic version (2nd twin only) |
| **Compound** | Hand/ arm along with head – usually resolves; don't pull the hand |
| **Unstable lie** | Changing lie after 37 wks – admit, ECV, stabilizing induction |

## 13.4 Cephalopelvic Disproportion (CPD), Prolonged & Obstructed Labour

- **CPD** – **disparity between fetal head & maternal pelvis** → vaginal delivery not possible. Causes – **contracted pelvis** (rickets, osteomalacia, short stature < 145 cm), **big baby** (diabetes, post-term), malposition/ deflexion, hydrocephalus.
- **Assessment**: **head-fitting test (Müller-Munro Kerr)**, clinical pelvimetry (diagonal conjugate, sacral curve, ischial spines, sub-pubic angle, intertuberous diameter), **trial of labour** (borderline CPD – adequate contractions, partograph, 4–6 hrs).
- **Prolonged labour**: **combined 1st & 2nd stage > 18 hrs (some: 24 hrs)**; **active phase crossing action line**; prolonged latent phase (> 20 hrs primi, > 14 multi); **prolonged 2nd stage** – primi > 2 hrs (3 with epidural), multi > 1 hr (2). **Causes – 3 Ps**: Power (inefficient contractions – most common), Passage (CPD), Passenger (malposition, big baby).
- **Obstructed labour** – **no progress (descent) despite good contractions** due to **mechanical obstruction**.

:::warn Signs of obstructed labour
- **Mother**: exhaustion, **dehydration, ketosis**, **tachycardia, fever**, **hot dry vagina**, **oedematous vulva/ cervix**, **Bandl's (pathological retraction) ring** – rising ring between thick upper & thin lower segment (impending **rupture uterus**), **tonic uterine contraction**, distended bladder/ **haematuria** (bladder compression), **foul liquor**.
- **Fetus**: **excessive moulding (+3)**, **large caput**, head not descending, **fetal distress/ death**.
- **Complications**: **rupture uterus**, **vesicovaginal fistula (VVF – pressure necrosis)**, rectovaginal fistula, sepsis, PPH, foot drop (nerve injury), **maternal death**; fetal asphyxia/ death.
- **Management**: **resuscitate (IV fluids, correct ketosis/ electrolytes), antibiotics, catheterize (continuous drainage 10–14 days – prevent VVF)** → **immediate delivery – LSCS** (live baby); dead baby → **destructive operation (craniotomy)** in selected; **NEVER give oxytocin** in obstructed labour.
:::

### Abnormal uterine action
- **Hypotonic/ inefficient uterine contractions (inertia)** – weak, infrequent → **augmentation: ARM + oxytocin**.
- **Hypertonic/ incoordinate** – painful but ineffective; **colicky uterus**, **constriction ring** → analgesia, epidural, tocolysis, LSCS.
- **Precipitate labour** – **total labour < 3 hrs** (multipara) → **lacerations, PPH, inversion, amniotic fluid embolism**; baby – **intracranial haemorrhage, hypoxia, injury, infection (unsterile)**; history → admit near term.
- **Cervical dystocia** – rigid cervix (scarring).

## 13.5 Preterm Labour & PROM

- **Preterm labour** – **regular contractions with cervical changes between 20/ 28 and < 37 wks**. **Most common cause of neonatal death**.
- Causes – **idiopathic (most)**, **infection (UTI, BV, chorioamnionitis)**, **PPROM**, **multiple pregnancy, polyhydramnios**, APH, pre-eclampsia, **cervical incompetence**, uterine anomalies, previous preterm, smoking, low socioeconomic, short cervix (< 25 mm on TVS).
- **Prediction**: **fetal fibronectin (fFN)** (negative – reassuring), **cervical length < 25 mm**.
- **Management**:
 - **Tocolysis (delay 48 hrs for steroids)**: **Nifedipine (first-line)**, **atosiban**, indomethacin (< 32 wks, short course – PDA closure risk), ritodrine/ terbutaline (β-agonists – tachycardia, pulmonary oedema), MgSO₄; contraindications – chorioamnionitis, IUD, fetal distress, APH heavy, severe PE, PPROM with infection.
 - **Antenatal corticosteroids (24–34 wks)**: **Dexamethasone 6 mg IM 12-hrly × 4 doses** or **Betamethasone 12 mg IM 24-hrly × 2 doses** – ↓ **RDS, IVH, NEC**, neonatal death.
 - **MgSO₄ for neuroprotection (< 32 wks)** – ↓ cerebral palsy.
 - **Antibiotics** (GBS/ PPROM – erythromycin/ ampicillin), deliver at **higher centre with NICU** ("in-utero transfer" – best transport incubator is uterus).
 - **Progesterone (vaginal)** – prevention in short cervix/ previous preterm; **cerclage**.
- **PROM (premature/ prelabour rupture of membranes)** – **ROM before onset of labour** at any gestation; **PPROM** – **before 37 wks**; **prolonged ROM > 18–24 hrs** before delivery → infection risk.
 - Diagnosis – history of **gush/ leaking**, **sterile speculum – pooling in posterior fornix**, **nitrazine (blue)**, **fern test**, USG (oligohydramnios); **avoid digital PV** (infection).
 - Complications – **chorioamnionitis (fever, maternal & fetal tachycardia, foul liquor, uterine tenderness)**, **cord prolapse**, preterm, abruption, oligohydramnios → pulmonary hypoplasia.
 - **Management**: **term PROM – induce with oxytocin** (or expectant ≤ 24 hrs); **PPROM < 34 wks – expectant**: admit, **sterile pad**, temperature/ pulse 4-hrly, **TLC/ CRP**, **antibiotics (erythromycin ± ampicillin – latency)**, **steroids**, **avoid PV**, deliver at **34 wks** or earlier if infection/ distress; chorioamnionitis → **deliver (any gestation)** + antibiotics.

## 13.6 Post-term Pregnancy & Induction of Labour

- **Post-term – ≥ 42 wks (294 days)**; risks – **placental insufficiency**, **oligohydramnios**, **meconium aspiration**, macrosomia (shoulder dystocia), **post-maturity syndrome** (dry, peeling, wrinkled skin – "**washer-woman hands**", long nails, no vernix/ lanugo, alert look), IUD. **Induce at 41 wks** (WHO – 41 completed wks).
- **Induction of labour (IOL)** – artificial initiation of labour before spontaneous onset. **Indications**: **post-term**, **pre-eclampsia**, **PROM**, **IUGR**, **diabetes**, **IUD**, Rh isoimmunisation, chorioamnionitis, abruption (mild). **Contraindications**: **placenta praevia**, transverse lie, **CPD**, **previous classical CS/ > 1 LSCS** (relative), active genital herpes, cord presentation, fetal distress.

:::key Bishop score (cervical ripeness) – 0 to 13
| Factor | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Dilatation (cm)** | 0 | 1–2 | 3–4 | ≥ 5 |
| **Effacement (%)** | 0–30 | 40–50 | 60–70 | ≥ 80 |
| **Station** | −3 | −2 | −1/ 0 | +1/ +2 |
| **Consistency** | Firm | Medium | **Soft** | – |
| **Position** | Posterior | Mid | **Anterior** | – |
- **Score ≥ 8 (≥ 6) = favourable** → ARM + oxytocin likely successful; **< 6 unfavourable → cervical ripening first**.
:::

- **Methods**: **Cervical ripening** – **PGE2 gel (dinoprostone 0.5 mg intracervical, 6-hrly, max 3)**, **misoprostol (PGE1) 25 µg vaginal 4-hrly / 25–50 µg oral 2–4 hrly**, **Foley catheter balloon (mechanical – 30 ml)**, membrane sweeping (stripping); **ARM (amniotomy)** – check cord prolapse, liquor colour, FHS after; **Oxytocin infusion** – **low dose start 1–2 mU/min (2.5 units in 500 ml NS/RL)**, ↑ every 30 min till **3–4 contractions in 10 min, each 40–60 sec**; max 32 mU/min; **infusion pump**.
- **Oxytocin nursing care**: **never IV bolus**, **dedicated line/ pump**, **monitor contractions & FHS every 15–30 min**, **stop if hyperstimulation (> 5 contractions/10 min, contraction > 90 sec, FHR abnormality)** → left lateral, O₂, IV fluids, tocolytic (terbutaline); watch **water intoxication** (antidiuretic effect – high dose with large dextrose), **rupture uterus**, I/O.

:::exam PYQ style
- Commonest malpresentation – **breech**; commonest type – **frank breech**.
- Commonest cause of breech – **prematurity**.
- ECV done at – **36–37 wks**.
- After-coming head delivery – **Mauriceau-Smellie-Veit manoeuvre**; extended arms – **Lovset's**.
- Face presentation denominator – **mentum**; mento-posterior – **LSCS**.
- Brow – **mento-vertical 13.5–14 cm**, LSCS.
- Transverse lie commonest cause – **multiparity**; delivery – **LSCS**.
- OP position – **flattened lower abdomen, FHS in flank, backache**.
- Bandl's ring – **obstructed labour (impending rupture)**.
- Obstructed labour – **oxytocin contraindicated**; VVF prevention – **continuous catheter drainage**.
- Precipitate labour – **< 3 hrs**.
- Tocolytic first-line – **nifedipine**; antenatal steroid – **dexamethasone 6 mg × 4 doses 12-hrly**.
- PROM – **avoid digital PV**; nitrazine **blue**.
- Bishop score favourable – **≥ 8**.
- Post-term – **≥ 42 wks**; induce at **41 wks**.
- Oxytocin hyperstimulation – **stop infusion, left lateral, O₂**.
