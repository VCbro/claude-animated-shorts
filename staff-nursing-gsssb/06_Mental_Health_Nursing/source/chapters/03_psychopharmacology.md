## 3.1 Antipsychotics (Neuroleptics)

- **Mechanism** – **dopamine D2 receptor blockade** (mesolimbic → ↓ positive symptoms); atypicals also block **5-HT2A**.
- **Uses** – schizophrenia, mania, psychotic depression, delirium, agitation, Tourette's, hiccups (chlorpromazine), vomiting.

| Class | Drugs | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| **Typical (1st generation)** – low potency | **Chlorpromazine (Largactil)** – first (1952), **thioridazine** | Sedation, **anticholinergic**, **hypotension**, photosensitivity, **less EPS**; thioridazine – retinitis pigmentosa, QT |
| **Typical – high potency** | **Haloperidol (Serenace)**, **fluphenazine**, trifluoperazine | **More EPS**, less sedation; haloperidol – IM for acute agitation |
| **Atypical (2nd generation)** | **Risperidone**, **olanzapine**, **quetiapine**, **aripiprazole**, ziprasidone, **clozapine**, amisulpride | Fewer EPS, effective on **negative symptoms**; **metabolic syndrome (weight gain, diabetes, dyslipidaemia – olanzapine, clozapine)**; **risperidone – hyperprolactinaemia (galactorrhoea, amenorrhoea)**; **aripiprazole – partial agonist (least metabolic)** |
| **Clozapine** | – | **Treatment-resistant schizophrenia**, ↓ suicide; **agranulocytosis (1 %) – regular WBC/ ANC monitoring (weekly first 18 weeks)**; **seizures**, **sialorrhoea (hypersalivation)**, myocarditis, weight gain, constipation |
| **Depot (long-acting injectable)** | **Fluphenazine decanoate**, **haloperidol decanoate**, flupentixol, **paliperidone palmitate**, risperidone LAI | **Non-compliant patients** – deep IM (Z-track) every **2–4 weeks** |

### Side effects
- **Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS)** – **acute dystonia** (hours–days; **IM promethazine/ diphenhydramine/ benztropine**), **akathisia** (days–weeks; **propranolol**), **parkinsonism** (weeks; **trihexyphenidyl/ benztropine**), **tardive dyskinesia** (months–years; **irreversible; lip smacking, tongue movements; AIMS; stop/ switch to clozapine, tetrabenazine/ valbenazine**; anticholinergics worsen TD).
- **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)** – **rare, life-threatening**; **hyperthermia, lead-pipe rigidity, autonomic instability (labile BP, tachycardia, sweating), altered consciousness, ↑ CPK, leukocytosis, myoglobinuria**; Rx – **stop antipsychotic immediately**, **cooling, IV fluids, dantrolene (muscle relaxant), bromocriptine (dopamine agonist)**, ICU.
- **Anticholinergic** – dry mouth, blurred vision, constipation, **urinary retention**, tachycardia (**"can't see, can't pee, can't spit, can't…"**).
- **Anti-adrenergic** – **orthostatic hypotension** (rise slowly).
- **Endocrine** – hyperprolactinaemia, weight gain; **metabolic** – diabetes, lipids.
- **Others** – sedation, **photosensitivity (chlorpromazine – use sunscreen)**, **lowered seizure threshold**, **QT prolongation**, agranulocytosis, cholestatic jaundice (chlorpromazine), sexual dysfunction.

## 3.2 Antidepressants

| Class | Drugs | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **SSRIs** (Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors) – **first-line** | **Fluoxetine** (first, **longest half-life**), **sertraline** (safe in cardiac/ breastfeeding), **escitalopram**, paroxetine, fluvoxamine (OCD) | GI upset, **sexual dysfunction**, insomnia/ agitation, headache; **safe in overdose**; **SIADH (elderly)**; discontinuation syndrome (paroxetine) |
| **SNRIs** | **Venlafaxine, duloxetine** | Also for pain (duloxetine – diabetic neuropathy); ↑ BP (venlafaxine) |
| **TCAs** (Tricyclic) | **Imipramine** (first – 1957), **amitriptyline**, clomipramine (OCD), nortriptyline | **Anticholinergic**, sedation, **orthostatic hypotension**, weight gain, **cardiotoxic in overdose (QRS widening, arrhythmia) – most dangerous in overdose**; amitriptyline – neuropathic pain, migraine; imipramine – enuresis |
| **MAOIs** | **Phenelzine, tranylcypromine**, moclobemide (reversible) | **Hypertensive crisis ("cheese reaction") with tyramine-rich foods – aged cheese, wine/ beer, pickled/ smoked fish, soy sauce, fava beans, chocolate**; **serotonin syndrome with SSRIs** – **2-week washout** (5 weeks after fluoxetine) |
| **Atypical** | **Mirtazapine** (↑ appetite, sedation – at night), **bupropion** (smoking cessation, **no sexual dysfunction**, ↓ seizure threshold), trazodone (priapism), agomelatine | – |

- **Onset of action – 2–4 weeks (full effect 4–6 weeks)**; continue **6–12 months after remission** (first episode).
- **Suicide risk ↑ in early treatment** – energy returns before mood lifts ("**paradoxical**") – **close observation**, dispense limited quantity.
- **Serotonin syndrome** – **agitation, confusion, hyperthermia, sweating, tachycardia, myoclonus, hyperreflexia, tremor, diarrhoea** (vs NMS – rigidity, slower onset); Rx – stop drug, **cyproheptadine**, supportive.
- **Switching to mania** in bipolar patients.

## 3.3 Mood Stabilisers

### Lithium carbonate
- **Drug of choice for bipolar disorder (prophylaxis & acute mania)**; **reduces suicide**.
- **Therapeutic range – 0.6–1.2 mEq/L** (maintenance **0.6–1.0**; acute 0.8–1.2); **toxic > 1.5 mEq/L**; **severe > 2.5 (dialysis)**.
- **Serum level 12 hrs after last dose** (trough); weekly initially, then 3-monthly.
- **Narrow therapeutic index**; onset **7–14 days** (antipsychotic/ BZD added in acute mania).
- **Side effects** – **fine tremor (hands), polyuria & polydipsia (nephrogenic DI)**, nausea, metallic taste, **weight gain**, **hypothyroidism (goitre)**, acne, **leucocytosis**, **Ebstein's anomaly (1st trimester – teratogenic)**, renal impairment, T-wave changes.
- **Toxicity** – **coarse tremor, vomiting, diarrhoea, ataxia, slurred speech, drowsiness, confusion → seizures, coma, arrhythmia**; Rx – **stop lithium**, IV NS, **haemodialysis (most effective)**.
- **Nursing/ teaching** – **adequate fluids (2–3 L/day) & normal salt intake** (↓ sodium → ↑ lithium reabsorption → toxicity); **avoid dehydration (vomiting, diarrhoea, sweating, fever)**; **avoid diuretics (thiazides), NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors** (↑ lithium level); take with meals; regular serum levels, **thyroid & renal function tests**; recognise toxicity; contraception.

### Anticonvulsant mood stabilisers
| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Sodium valproate (divalproex)** | Rapid cycling, mixed mania; **hepatotoxicity, pancreatitis, weight gain, hair loss, tremor, thrombocytopenia, NTD (teratogenic), PCOS**; LFT monitoring |
| **Carbamazepine** | **Agranulocytosis/ aplastic anaemia**, **SJS (HLA-B*1502)**, hyponatraemia, enzyme inducer (↓ OCP effect) |
| **Lamotrigine** | Bipolar depression; **SJS rash (slow titration)** |

## 3.4 Anxiolytics & Hypnotics

| Class | Drugs | Key points |
|---|---|---|
| **Benzodiazepines (BZD)** | **Diazepam, lorazepam** (preferred in liver disease – no active metabolite; IM/ IV), **alprazolam**, clonazepam, **chlordiazepoxide** (alcohol withdrawal), midazolam, nitrazepam | **Enhance GABA**; anxiety, insomnia, **alcohol withdrawal**, seizures, **catatonia (lorazepam)**, premedication; **sedation, ataxia, dependence & withdrawal (seizures), tolerance, paradoxical agitation (elderly), respiratory depression with alcohol**; **antidote – flumazenil**; short-term use (2–4 weeks) |
| **Buspirone** | – | **Non-sedating, non-addictive** anxiolytic; onset 2–3 weeks |
| **Z-drugs** | **Zolpidem**, zopiclone | Insomnia |
| **Others** | Propranolol (performance anxiety – tremor, palpitations), hydroxyzine, melatonin | – |

## 3.5 Other Psychotropics

| Group | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **Anticholinergics (antiparkinsonian)** | **Trihexyphenidyl (benzhexol/ Pacitane)**, **benztropine**, **promethazine** – for EPS (**abuse potential – euphoria**) |
| **Anti-dementia** | **Cholinesterase inhibitors – donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine**; **NMDA antagonist – memantine** |
| **ADHD** | **Methylphenidate** (stimulant – anorexia, insomnia, growth delay; give in morning), **atomoxetine** |
| **Alcohol dependence** | **Disulfiram** (aldehyde dehydrogenase inhibitor – **disulfiram-ethanol reaction**: flushing, vomiting, palpitation, hypotension – **never give without patient's knowledge**; avoid alcohol in cough syrups, aftershave), **naltrexone** (↓ craving – opioid antagonist), **acamprosate** (↓ craving), baclofen, topiramate |
| **Opioid dependence** | **Methadone, buprenorphine** (OST), **naltrexone**, clonidine (withdrawal); overdose – **naloxone** |
| **Nicotine dependence** | **NRT (gum, patch)**, **varenicline**, bupropion |

## 3.6 Nurse's Responsibilities in Psychopharmacology

- **Ensure medication is swallowed** (check mouth – **"cheeking"/ hoarding** for suicide); **liquid forms** if suspected.
- **Observe for therapeutic & side effects**; vital signs (**BP lying & standing**), **weight, blood sugar, lipids**, **WBC (clozapine)**, **lithium levels**.
- **Patient & family education** – purpose, **delay in onset**, **don't stop abruptly**, side effects, **drug interactions**, **avoid alcohol**, **driving caution**, diet restrictions (MAOI), fluid/ salt (lithium), **sun protection (chlorpromazine)**.
- **Manage side effects** – dry mouth (sips of water, sugar-free gum), **constipation (fibre, fluids)**, **orthostatic hypotension (rise slowly, dangle legs)**, weight gain (diet, exercise).
- **Compliance** – depot injections, simplify regimen, involve family, psychoeducation.
- **Elderly** – start low, go slow.
