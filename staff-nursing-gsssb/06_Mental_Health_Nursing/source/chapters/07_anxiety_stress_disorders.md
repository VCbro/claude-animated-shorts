## 7.1 Anxiety – Basics

- **Anxiety** – vague, diffuse feeling of apprehension/ uneasiness in response to an **unknown/ internal threat** (vs **fear – response to a known external danger**).
- **Physical symptoms (sympathetic)** – palpitation, tachycardia, sweating, tremor, dry mouth, **hyperventilation**, butterflies, frequent micturition, diarrhoea, dizziness.
- **Levels (Peplau)** – **mild** (alert, learning ↑), **moderate** (narrowed perceptual field, selective inattention), **severe** (very narrow, focus on details, can't learn), **panic** (disorganised, terror, loss of control, may harm self).
- **Neurotransmitters** – **↓ GABA**, ↑ norepinephrine, serotonin dysregulation.

## 7.2 Types of Anxiety Disorders

| Disorder | Key features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Generalised Anxiety Disorder (GAD)** | **Excessive, uncontrollable worry about everyday matters**, **most days for ≥ 6 months**; restlessness, fatigue, muscle tension, poor concentration, irritability, insomnia | **SSRIs/ SNRIs (first-line)**, **buspirone**, short-term BZD, **CBT, relaxation** |
| **Panic disorder** | **Recurrent unexpected panic attacks** – sudden intense fear **peaking within 10 min** – palpitations, chest pain, **choking, sweating, fear of dying/ going crazy**, derealisation; **anticipatory anxiety**; ± agoraphobia | **SSRIs**, BZD (alprazolam/ clonazepam), **CBT**; during attack – **stay with patient, calm, slow breathing (paper bag for hyperventilation – rarely)** |
| **Phobic disorders** | **Persistent irrational fear** of specific object/ situation → **avoidance**; insight present | **Systematic desensitisation (Wolpe)**, **exposure therapy, flooding**, SSRIs; propranolol (performance anxiety) |
| – **Agoraphobia** | Fear of **open/ public places, crowds, situations where escape is difficult** (most disabling) | – |
| – **Social phobia (social anxiety)** | Fear of **scrutiny/ embarrassment** in social situations (public speaking) | SSRIs, CBT |
| – **Specific phobia** | Single object/ situation – **most common** | Exposure |

## 7.3 Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD)

- **Obsessions** – **recurrent, persistent, intrusive, unwanted thoughts, images or impulses**, recognised as **one's own** (product of own mind) & **irrational** (ego-dystonic), patient **tries to resist** → anxiety.
- **Compulsions** – **repetitive behaviours or mental acts** performed to **reduce anxiety** caused by obsessions (not pleasurable).
- **Common** – **contamination → washing (most common)**, doubt → **checking**, symmetry → ordering/ arranging, counting, **intrusive aggressive/ sexual/ religious thoughts**, hoarding.
- **Onset** – adolescence/ early adulthood; **chronic**; **insight usually present**; **serotonin dysfunction**; **basal ganglia/ orbitofrontal circuit**; associated with **depression**, Tourette's.
- **Defence mechanisms** – **undoing, reaction formation, isolation**.
- **Treatment** – **SSRIs in high doses (fluoxetine, fluvoxamine, sertraline)** / **clomipramine** (TCA – most serotonergic); **Exposure & Response Prevention (ERP) – psychotherapy of choice**; deep brain stimulation (refractory).
- **Y-BOCS** – severity scale.
- **Nursing** – **don't interrupt or stop the ritual abruptly** (↑ anxiety) – **allow time for rituals initially**, then **gradually limit/ set limits** with patient's agreement; structured schedule; **meet physical needs (skin care – dermatitis from washing)**; support during ERP; positive reinforcement; reduce anxiety; involve family (avoid accommodating rituals).
- **OCD spectrum** – **body dysmorphic disorder** (imagined defect in appearance), **trichotillomania (hair pulling)**, **hoarding disorder**, excoriation (skin picking).

## 7.4 Stress-Related Disorders

| Disorder | Features |
|---|---|
| **Acute stress reaction / disorder** | After **exceptional stressor**; symptoms within hours–days, **resolve within days–1 month**; daze, disorientation, anxiety, dissociation |
| **Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD)** | After **life-threatening trauma** (war, rape, disaster, accident); **symptoms > 1 month** (onset may be delayed up to 6 months); **re-experiencing (flashbacks, nightmares, intrusive memories)**, **avoidance** of reminders, **hyperarousal (hypervigilance, exaggerated startle, insomnia, irritability)**, negative mood, numbness, **survivor guilt**; Rx – **trauma-focused CBT, EMDR**, **SSRIs (sertraline, paroxetine)**, prazosin (nightmares) |
| **Adjustment disorder** | Emotional/ behavioural symptoms **within 1 month (DSM – 3 months) of identifiable stressor** (divorce, job loss, migration); resolves within **6 months**; supportive therapy |
| **Grief / bereavement** | Normal response to loss; **Kübler-Ross stages – Denial → Anger → Bargaining → Depression → Acceptance (DABDA)**; **prolonged grief disorder** (ICD-11 – > 6 months) |

- **Stress (Hans Selye)** – **General Adaptation Syndrome (GAS)** – **alarm reaction → resistance → exhaustion**; **eustress** (positive) vs **distress**; **Holmes & Rahe Social Readjustment Rating Scale** (life events – **death of spouse = 100**, highest).
- **Coping** – problem-focused & emotion-focused (Lazarus).

## 7.5 Dissociative (Conversion) Disorders

- **Dissociation** – **disruption of normal integration of consciousness, memory, identity, perception or motor control**, usually after **psychological stress/ conflict** (defence mechanism).

| Disorder | Features |
|---|---|
| **Dissociative (conversion) motor/ sensory disorder** (functional neurological symptom disorder) | **Loss/ alteration of motor or sensory function** (paralysis, **pseudoseizures (dissociative convulsions)**, aphonia, blindness, anaesthesia) **without organic cause**, following stress; **"la belle indifférence" (inappropriate lack of concern)**; **primary gain** (relief of anxiety) & **secondary gain** (attention, escape from responsibility); common in young females, low SES; Rx – **reassurance, suggestion, psychotherapy, avoid reinforcing secondary gain**, focus on stressors |
| **Dissociative amnesia** | **Inability to recall important personal (usually traumatic) information**; **localised** – most common |
| **Dissociative fugue** | **Sudden, unexpected travel away from home** with **amnesia for past & identity confusion/ new identity** |
| **Dissociative identity disorder (multiple personality)** | **≥ 2 distinct personality states** taking control; history of **childhood abuse** |
| **Depersonalisation/ derealisation disorder** | Persistent feelings of unreality of self/ surroundings; reality testing intact |
| **Possession/ trance disorder** | Culture-related (common in India) – temporary loss of identity, feels "possessed by spirit/ deity" |

### Pseudoseizure vs true seizure
| | **Dissociative (pseudo) seizure** | **Epileptic seizure** |
|---|---|---|
| Situation | In presence of others, emotional trigger | Any time, even sleep |
| Injury, tongue bite, incontinence | **Rare** | Common |
| Movements | Bizarre, asynchronous, **pelvic thrusting**, eyes closed tightly (resists opening) | Stereotyped, eyes open |
| Post-ictal confusion | Absent | Present |
| **Serum prolactin** | **Normal** | **↑ (within 20 min)** |
| EEG | Normal | Abnormal |

## 7.6 Somatic Symptom (Somatoform) Disorders

| Disorder | Features |
|---|---|
| **Somatisation disorder / somatic symptom disorder** | **Multiple, recurrent physical complaints** (pain, GI, sexual, pseudoneurological) **for years, no organic basis**; doctor shopping; females |
| **Hypochondriasis (illness anxiety disorder)** | **Preoccupation with fear of having a serious disease** (e.g. cancer) despite **negative investigations & reassurance** |
| **Persistent somatoform pain disorder** | Chronic pain without adequate physical cause |
| **Body dysmorphic disorder** | Imagined/ slight defect in appearance (OCD spectrum) |
| **Factitious disorder (Munchausen syndrome)** | **Intentional production of symptoms to assume sick role** (no external reward); **by proxy** – caregiver induces illness in child |
| **Malingering** | **Intentional** faking for **external gain** (money, avoid duty, legal) – **not a mental disorder** |

- **Nursing (somatoform)** – **acknowledge symptoms are real to patient**, avoid repeated unnecessary investigations, **don't reinforce sick role**, **focus on feelings & stressors** (not symptoms), teach coping/ relaxation, gradual return to responsibilities, consistent single caregiver.

## 7.7 General Nursing Care in Anxiety

- **Stay with patient** during severe anxiety/ panic; **calm, quiet environment**; **short, simple sentences**; **reduce stimuli**; **help identify & verbalise feelings**; **deep breathing, relaxation, distraction**; **physical activity**; recognise triggers; **avoid caffeine**; teach **coping skills, problem solving**; medication (BZD short-term – dependence).
