## 6.1 Depression (Major Depressive Disorder)

- **Most common psychiatric disorder**; lifetime prevalence ~15 %; **women 2 : 1**; leading cause of disability worldwide (WHO).
- **Aetiology** – **monoamine hypothesis (↓ serotonin, norepinephrine, dopamine)**, **HPA axis hyperactivity (↑ cortisol)**, genetic, **loss/ stress (life events)**, **Beck's cognitive triad**, **learned helplessness (Seligman)**, personality, medical illness (hypothyroidism, stroke, cancer), drugs (**reserpine, steroids, beta-blockers, OCPs**).
- **Diagnosis – ≥ 2 weeks**, most of the day, nearly every day; **core symptoms – depressed mood, anhedonia (loss of interest/ pleasure), fatigue/ ↓ energy**.
- **Other symptoms (mnemonic "SIG E CAPS")** – **S**leep disturbance (**early morning awakening – typical**), **I**nterest loss, **G**uilt/ worthlessness, **E**nergy ↓, **C**oncentration ↓, **A**ppetite/ weight change, **P**sychomotor retardation/ agitation, **S**uicidal thoughts.
- **Biological (somatic/ melancholic) features** – **early morning awakening (2 hrs early)**, **diurnal variation (worse in the morning)**, loss of appetite & weight, psychomotor retardation, loss of libido, marked anhedonia.
- **Severity** – mild, moderate, **severe without/ with psychotic symptoms** (**mood-congruent delusions – guilt, poverty, nihilism**; Cotard).
- **Masked depression** – presents with somatic complaints (common in India – aches, pains).
- **Other forms** – **dysthymia (persistent depressive disorder) – mild depression ≥ 2 years**; **seasonal affective disorder (winter – light therapy)**; **postpartum depression** (2 weeks – 1 yr; vs **postpartum blues – day 3–5, self-limiting**); **atypical depression** (hypersomnia, hyperphagia, mood reactivity – MAOIs); **premenstrual dysphoric disorder**; grief vs depression (grief – self-esteem preserved).

### Treatment
- **Antidepressants** (**SSRIs first-line**), **ECT** (severe, suicidal, psychotic, catatonic, pregnancy), **psychotherapy – CBT, IPT**, rTMS, light therapy; **treat for 6–12 months after remission**.

### Nursing care of depressed patient
- **Safety first – assess suicide risk** (ask directly), **suicide precautions**, close observation (**especially when improving – energy ↑**).
- **Nutrition & fluids** (weigh, small frequent meals, favourite foods), **sleep hygiene**, **elimination (constipation)**, **personal hygiene – assist ADLs**.
- **Spend time with patient**, **accept silence**, **simple, short conversation**, **don't be overly cheerful** ("cheer up" – non-therapeutic).
- **Simple, structured, achievable tasks** → success → self-esteem; **positive reinforcement**; **avoid complex decisions** (impaired concentration).
- **Encourage expression of feelings (anger, guilt)**, cognitive restructuring (challenge negative thoughts), gradually increase activities & social interaction.
- **Medication** – delayed onset (2–4 weeks), compliance, side effects; **dispense limited quantity** (overdose risk).

## 6.2 Mania & Bipolar Disorder

- **Bipolar affective disorder (BPAD)** – recurrent episodes of **mania/ hypomania & depression**; **lifetime ~1 %**, **equal sex**; **onset ~20s**; **strong genetic component**.
- **Bipolar I** – **≥ 1 manic episode** (± depression); **Bipolar II** – **hypomania + major depression** (no full mania); **cyclothymia** – **≥ 2 yrs of mild mood swings** (hypomanic & depressive symptoms); **rapid cycling – ≥ 4 episodes/ year** (valproate).
- **Mania – ≥ 1 week** (any duration if hospitalised); **hypomania – ≥ 4 days**, no psychotic features, no marked impairment.

### Features of mania ("DIG FAST")
- **D**istractibility, **I**ndiscretion/ **I**mpulsivity (reckless spending, sexual indiscretion), **G**randiosity (inflated self-esteem, delusions of grandeur), **F**light of ideas, **A**ctivity ↑ (psychomotor agitation, goal-directed), **S**leep need ↓ (**decreased need for sleep**), **T**alkativeness (**pressure of speech**).
- **Mood** – **elated/ euphoric**, **irritable** (when thwarted), **labile**; **infectious gaiety**; **clang associations, punning**; **colourful/ bizarre dress, heavy make-up**; **poor judgement**, **lack of insight**; exhaustion, dehydration, weight loss.

### Treatment
- **Acute mania** – **lithium/ valproate + antipsychotic** (olanzapine, haloperidol, risperidone), **BZD** (sedation), **ECT** (resistant/ severe).
- **Maintenance (prophylaxis)** – **lithium (gold standard)**, valproate, lamotrigine (bipolar depression), quetiapine.
- **Antidepressant alone** may precipitate mania ("**switch**").

### Nursing care of manic patient
- **Safety** – of patient & others; **↓ environmental stimuli** (**quiet, single room**, dim lights, avoid group/ competitive activities), **remove hazardous objects**.
- **Firm, calm, consistent approach**; **set limits** on behaviour (clearly, consistently by all staff); **avoid arguing/ power struggles**; don't laugh at jokes/ encourage.
- **Short, simple, clear communication**; **redirect** energy into **non-competitive physical activities requiring gross motor movement (walking, cleaning, sweeping)** – **avoid competitive games**.
- **Nutrition** – **high-calorie, high-protein finger foods & fluids "on the go"** (can't sit to eat), monitor weight & I/O (**dehydration, exhaustion**).
- **Rest & sleep** – quiet environment, limit caffeine, sedation as ordered.
- **Hygiene & dress** – supervise.
- **Protect from impulsive acts** (spending, sexual), **manipulation** – consistent limits.
- **Lithium** – monitor levels & toxicity; teach.
- **Psychoeducation** – early warning signs, sleep regularity, compliance, avoid substances.

## 6.3 Suicide

- **Suicide** – intentional self-inflicted death; **attempted suicide/ deliberate self-harm**; **suicidal ideation**.
- **India (NCRB 2022)** – **~1.7 lakh suicides/ year** (rate ~12 per lakh); **hanging – most common method**, followed by **poisoning (pesticides/ insecticides)**; most common causes – **family problems & illness**; **daily wage earners** – largest group; **Maharashtra** highest number.
- **WHO** – > 7 lakh deaths/ yr globally; **4th leading cause of death in 15–29 yrs**.
- **World Suicide Prevention Day – 10 September**; **National Suicide Prevention Strategy (Nov 2022) – reduce suicide mortality by 10 % by 2030**; **Tele-MANAS 14416**; **MHCA 2017 Sec 115 – decriminalises suicide attempt** (presumed severe stress; govt duty to care & rehabilitate) – overrides the old **IPC Section 309** (punishment for attempted suicide).

### Risk factors
- **Previous suicide attempt (strongest predictor)**, **depression (most common associated illness – ~15 % of severely depressed die by suicide)**, **bipolar**, **schizophrenia**, **alcohol/ substance use**, **personality disorder (borderline)**, chronic painful illness, **hopelessness (key psychological factor)**.
- **Male sex (completed suicide 3 ×), female (attempts 3–4 ×)**; elderly & young adults; **single, divorced, widowed**, living alone, **unemployment, financial loss, recent loss/ bereavement**, family history of suicide, access to means (**pesticides, firearms**), **lack of social support**.
- **SAD PERSONS scale** – **S**ex (male), **A**ge (< 19 or > 45), **D**epression, **P**revious attempt, **E**thanol abuse, **R**ational thinking loss (psychosis), **S**ocial support lacking, **O**rganised plan, **N**o spouse, **S**ickness (chronic) – score ≥ 7 → hospitalise.

### Warning signs (clues)
- **Talking about death/ suicide ("I won't be a problem for you much longer")**, **giving away possessions**, **making a will, saying goodbyes**, withdrawal, **sudden calmness/ improved mood after depression** (decision made), hopelessness, collecting pills/ buying rope/ pesticide, increased substance use, reckless behaviour.

### Assessment
- **Ask directly & clearly** – "**Are you thinking of killing yourself?**" – **asking does NOT increase the risk** (myth); explore **plan (method, means, time, place – lethality & specificity)**, intent, previous attempts, protective factors.

### Nursing management (suicide precautions)
- **Ensure safety – highest priority**: **constant one-to-one observation (within arm's length for high risk)**, **room near nursing station**, **remove all potentially harmful objects (sharps, belts, ropes, shoelaces, glass, cords, plastic bags, medications)**, check items brought by visitors, **unbreakable windows**, ligature-free bathroom, **check mouth after medication (hoarding)**, observe **closely during shift change, night & early morning, weekends** (high-risk times), **when mood improves suddenly**.
- **Therapeutic relationship** – listen non-judgementally, **convey hope**, explore feelings & reasons for living, **no-suicide contract (adjunct only, not a substitute for observation)**.
- **Treat underlying illness** (antidepressants, **ECT**), crisis intervention, problem solving, **mobilise support systems**, involve family.
- **Discharge** – safety plan, restricted access to means (pesticide storage locked – **community pesticide storage**), follow-up, helplines.
- **Gatekeeper training** (QPR – Question, Persuade, Refer).
