## 1.1 Definitions

:::def Mental health (WHO)
"A state of **well-being** in which an individual **realises his or her own abilities, can cope with the normal stresses of life, can work productively** and is able to **make a contribution to his or her community**." **Mental illness** – clinically significant disturbance in **cognition, emotion regulation or behaviour** causing **distress or impaired functioning**.
:::

- **Characteristics of mentally healthy person** – positive self-concept, realistic perception, **adaptability/ coping**, autonomy, **self-actualisation (Maslow)**, environmental mastery, integration, good interpersonal relations.
- **Psychiatry** – branch of medicine dealing with mental disorders; **psychiatric (mental health) nursing** – specialised area using theories of human behaviour as its science & **purposeful use of self as its art** (ANA).
- **World Mental Health Day – 10 October**; **NIMHANS – Bengaluru**; **Central Institute of Psychiatry (CIP) – Ranchi (1918)**; **Hospital for Mental Health – Ahmedabad, Vadodara, Jamnagar, Bhuj (Gujarat)**.
- **Prevalence (National Mental Health Survey 2015–16, NIMHANS)** – **~10.6 % adults** have a mental disorder; **treatment gap 70–92 %**; common – **depression, anxiety, substance use (alcohol)**.

## 1.2 History & Pioneers

| Person | Contribution |
|---|---|
| **Hippocrates** | Natural causes of mental illness (humours) – "melancholia" |
| **Philippe Pinel** | **Removed chains** from mentally ill (Bicêtre, Paris, **1793**) – **"moral treatment"**; often called **father of modern psychiatry** |
| **William Tuke** | York Retreat (England) – humane care |
| **Benjamin Rush** | **Father of American psychiatry** |
| **Dorothea Dix** | Reform of asylums (USA) |
| **Emil Kraepelin** | Classified mental illness; **"dementia praecox"** (schizophrenia) & manic-depressive psychosis |
| **Eugen Bleuler** | Coined **"schizophrenia" (1908/1911)**; **4 A's** |
| **Kurt Schneider** | **First-rank symptoms** of schizophrenia |
| **Sigmund Freud** | **Father of psychoanalysis**; id-ego-superego; defence mechanisms; free association, dream analysis |
| **Linda Richards** | **First American psychiatric nurse**; started first training school for psychiatric nurses (1882) |
| **Hildegard Peplau** | **Mother of psychiatric nursing**; **Interpersonal Relations in Nursing (1952)** |
| **Cerletti & Bini** | **ECT (1938)**, Italy |
| **Ladislas Meduna** | Convulsive therapy (camphor/ metrazol, 1934) |
| **Manfred Sakel** | Insulin coma therapy (1933) |
| **Egas Moniz** | **Prefrontal lobotomy** – Nobel 1949 |
| **John Cade** | **Lithium** for mania (1949) |
| **Delay & Deniker** | **Chlorpromazine (1952)** – first antipsychotic |
| **Maxwell Jones** | **Therapeutic community** |
| **Aaron Beck** | **Cognitive therapy (CBT)** |
| **B. F. Skinner / Pavlov** | Operant / classical conditioning |

- **India** – first asylums set up under British rule in the **18th century (Bombay, Calcutta, Madras)**; **Indian Lunacy Act 1912** → **Mental Health Act 1987** → **Mental Healthcare Act 2017**; **NMHP 1982**; **DMHP 1996**; **National Mental Health Policy 2014**; **Tele-MANAS 2022**.

## 1.3 Classification of Mental Disorders

| System | By | Current |
|---|---|---|
| **ICD (International Classification of Diseases)** | **WHO** | **ICD-11 (effective Jan 2022)**; mental disorders – **Chapter 6** (ICD-10 – Chapter V, F00–F99) |
| **DSM (Diagnostic & Statistical Manual)** | **American Psychiatric Association** | **DSM-5 (2013), DSM-5-TR (2022)** |

- **Old classification** – **psychosis** (loss of contact with reality, **no insight** – schizophrenia, mania) vs **neurosis** (contact with reality maintained, **insight present** – anxiety, OCD, phobia).
- **Organic** (identifiable brain cause – delirium, dementia) vs **functional** (no structural cause).

## 1.4 Psychiatric Signs & Symptoms (Very Important)

### Disorders of perception
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Illusion** | **Misinterpretation of a real external stimulus** (rope seen as snake) |
| **Hallucination** | **Perception without external stimulus**; **auditory – most common (schizophrenia)**; **visual – organic (delirium, DT)**; **tactile – formication (cocaine "bugs")**; olfactory/ gustatory (temporal lobe epilepsy) |
| **Hypnagogic / hypnopompic** | Hallucinations while falling asleep / on waking (normal, narcolepsy) |
| **Depersonalisation** | Feeling **oneself** is unreal/ changed |
| **Derealisation** | Feeling **surroundings** are unreal |
| **Déjà vu / Jamais vu** | Familiar feeling for new / unfamiliar feeling for familiar |

### Disorders of thought
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Delusion** | **Fixed, false, unshakable belief**, not in keeping with patient's socio-cultural background; **persecutory – most common**; grandiosity (mania), nihilistic & guilt (severe depression), jealousy (**Othello syndrome – alcohol**), erotomanic (**de Clérambault's**), reference, control |
| **Obsession** | **Recurrent, intrusive, unwanted thought** recognised as **own** & irrational (OCD) |
| **Flight of ideas** | Rapid shift of ideas with **some connection** – **mania** |
| **Loosening of association (derailment)** | No logical connection – **schizophrenia** |
| **Tangentiality** | Never reaches the goal |
| **Circumstantiality** | Reaches goal after many unnecessary details |
| **Thought block** | Sudden stop in thought – **schizophrenia** |
| **Neologism** | **New words** made up |
| **Word salad** | Incoherent mixture of words |
| **Clang association** | Words chosen by **sound (rhyming)** – **mania** |
| **Perseveration** | Persistent repetition of same response – **organic** |
| **Echolalia / echopraxia** | Repeating words / actions of others – **catatonia** |
| **Poverty of speech/ alogia** | Negative symptom |
| **Confabulation** | **Filling memory gaps with imaginary events** – **Korsakoff** |

### Disorders of mood/ affect & behaviour
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Mood** | Sustained, pervasive emotion (**subjective** – "climate") |
| **Affect** | Observable expression of emotion (**objective** – "weather") – **blunted, flat (schizophrenia), labile, inappropriate (incongruent)** |
| **Euphoria / elation / exaltation / ecstasy** | Increasing degrees of happy mood (mania) |
| **Anhedonia** | **Loss of pleasure** – depression, schizophrenia |
| **Ambivalence** | Co-existence of opposing feelings (schizophrenia) |
| **Alexithymia** | Inability to describe emotions |
| **Catatonia** | **Stupor, mutism, negativism, posturing, waxy flexibility (catalepsy), echolalia/ echopraxia, automatic obedience** |
| **Stereotypy / mannerism** | Repetitive purposeless movement / goal-directed odd movement |
| **Psychomotor retardation / agitation** | Depression |

### Disorders of memory & consciousness
- **Amnesia** – **retrograde** (before event), **anterograde** (after event – new learning); **Korsakoff – anterograde + confabulation**.
- **Orientation** – **time lost first, then place, person last** (organic).
- **Insight** – awareness of illness (**grade 1 complete denial → grade 6 true emotional insight**); **psychosis – poor insight**.
- **Judgement** – ability to assess situation & act appropriately.

## 1.5 Personality & Theories

- **Freud's structure of personality** – **Id** (instincts, **pleasure principle**, unconscious), **Ego** (**reality principle**, mediator), **Superego** (**conscience**, morals).
- **Levels of consciousness** – conscious, preconscious, unconscious ("iceberg").
- **Psychosexual stages** – oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital; **fixation**.

@fig defence_mse.svg | Freud's model, common defence mechanisms & MSE components

- **Defence (ego defence) mechanisms** – unconscious strategies to reduce anxiety:

| Mechanism | Example |
|---|---|
| **Denial** | Alcoholic says "I don't have a drinking problem" (**most primitive**) |
| **Projection** | Blaming others for own unacceptable feelings ("He hates me" when I hate him) – **paranoia** |
| **Rationalisation** | Logical-sounding excuse ("I failed because the paper was out of syllabus") |
| **Repression** | **Unconscious** forgetting of painful memory (**basic defence**) |
| **Suppression** | **Conscious** pushing away of thoughts (only conscious one) |
| **Displacement** | Angry at boss → shouts at child |
| **Regression** | Return to earlier developmental behaviour (hospitalised child bedwetting) |
| **Sublimation** | Unacceptable drive → **socially acceptable** (aggression → boxing) – **most mature/ healthy** |
| **Reaction formation** | Shows **opposite** behaviour (dislikes someone but is excessively nice) |
| **Undoing** | Act to cancel a guilty act (gift after quarrel) – **OCD** |
| **Compensation** | Weak in studies → excels in sports |
| **Identification / introjection** | Adopting traits of admired person / incorporating values |
| **Intellectualisation** | Talking about facts to avoid feelings |
| **Conversion** | Psychological conflict → **physical symptom** (paralysis) |
| **Dissociation** | Separation of thoughts/ memory from awareness (amnesia, fugue) |
| **Fantasy** | Day-dreaming |

- **Other theories** – **Erikson** (psychosocial), **Sullivan** (interpersonal), **Maslow** (hierarchy of needs), **behavioural** (Pavlov, Skinner), **cognitive** (Beck), **biological (neurotransmitters)**.
- **Neurotransmitters** – **dopamine ↑ – schizophrenia, mania**; **↓ dopamine – Parkinsonism**; **serotonin ↓ & norepinephrine ↓ – depression**; **GABA ↓ – anxiety**; **acetylcholine ↓ – Alzheimer's**; **glutamate**.

## 1.6 Mental Health Team & Nurse's Role

- **Team** – psychiatrist, **psychiatric nurse**, clinical psychologist, psychiatric social worker, occupational therapist, counsellor, recreational therapist, family.
- **Roles of psychiatric nurse** – **caregiver**, **therapeutic communicator**, **counsellor**, **health educator**, **milieu manager**, **advocate**, **case manager**, **socialising agent**, **surrogate parent**, **medication manager**, crisis interventionist, researcher, liaison/ consultant.
- **Principles of psychiatric nursing** – **accept patient as he/ she is**, **use self therapeutically**, **consistency**, **reassurance**, **avoid physical & verbal force**, **set limits**, **confidentiality**, **observe & record behaviour accurately**, **honesty**, **non-judgemental attitude**, maintain dignity, understand behaviour as meaningful.
