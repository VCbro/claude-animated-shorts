## 4.1 Electroconvulsive Therapy (ECT)

:::def ECT
**Induction of a generalised (grand mal) seizure** by passing a controlled **electric current through the brain** under **general anaesthesia & muscle relaxation (modified ECT)**. Introduced by **Cerletti & Bini (1938)**. Exact mechanism unclear – ↑ neurotransmitter sensitivity (monoamines), neurotrophic effects.
:::

- **Indications** – **severe depression with suicidal risk/ refusal to eat (most common & most effective indication)**, **psychotic depression**, **catatonia (stupor)**, **treatment-resistant depression**, **acute mania** (refractory), **schizophrenia with catatonia/ severe excitement**, **NMS**, depression in **pregnancy** (safer than drugs), postpartum psychosis.
- **Contraindications** – **no absolute contraindication** now; **relative – raised intracranial pressure (brain tumour – most important)**, recent MI (< 3 months), recent stroke, aneurysm, severe HTN, retinal detachment, pheochromocytoma, high anaesthetic risk.
- **Course** – **2–3 times/week**, total **6–12 sessions**.
- **Electrode placement** – **bilateral (bitemporal)** – more effective, more memory loss; **unilateral (non-dominant)** – less cognitive side effects.
- **Adequate seizure** – motor seizure **≥ 20–25 seconds** (EEG seizure longer); monitored by **cuff method** (BP cuff inflated on one limb – observe convulsion in that limb).

### Pre-ECT nursing care
- **Informed written consent** (patient/ nominated representative – **MHCA 2017**).
- **NPO (nil by mouth) for 6–8 hours** (prevents aspiration).
- **Pre-anaesthetic check-up**, investigations (CBC, ECG, X-ray, fundus if needed).
- **Remove dentures, jewellery, hairpins, contact lenses, nail polish/ make-up** (to observe cyanosis); **hair clean & dry, no oil** (better electrode contact).
- **Empty bladder & bowel** (incontinence during seizure).
- **Vital signs** recorded; loose clothing; ID band.
- **Premedication** – **atropine/ glycopyrrolate** (↓ secretions, prevent vagal bradycardia).
- Reassure patient (anxiety).

### During ECT
- **Supine** position, **IV line**; **anaesthesia – thiopentone/ propofol/ methohexital**; **muscle relaxant – succinylcholine (suxamethonium)** → prevents fractures; **100 % O₂ with bag & mask**; **mouth gag/ bite block** (protects teeth & tongue); electrodes with conducting jelly; **ECT shock**; observe & time seizure; ventilate till spontaneous breathing.

### Post-ECT care
- **Recovery position (lateral)**, **airway**, suction, O₂, **vital signs every 15 min** till stable, side rails up.
- **Re-orient** patient (confusion common), stay with patient.
- **Oral fluids/ food** only after full consciousness & **gag reflex returns**.
- Observe for **headache, confusion, memory loss, muscle pain (succinylcholine), nausea**.

### Side effects
- **Memory impairment – most common** (**retrograde & anterograde amnesia**, usually **temporary – recovers in weeks**), **post-ictal confusion**, **headache**, **muscle aches**, nausea; rare – fractures (unmodified), prolonged seizure, cardiac arrhythmia, status epilepticus, death (~ anaesthesia risk).
- **MHCA 2017 (Sec 95)** – **ECT only with anaesthesia & muscle relaxant ("modified")**; **ECT prohibited in minors** (except with permission of Mental Health Review Board under specific conditions).

## 4.2 Other Somatic Therapies

| Therapy | Key points |
|---|---|
| **rTMS (repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation)** | Depression – non-invasive, no anaesthesia |
| **Insulin coma therapy (Sakel)** | Historical (obsolete) |
| **Psychosurgery (lobotomy – Moniz)** | Rare now – refractory OCD (cingulotomy) |
| **Light therapy (phototherapy)** | **Seasonal affective disorder (SAD)** |
| **Sleep deprivation** | Depression (short-term) |
| **Deep brain stimulation, VNS** | Refractory depression/ OCD |

## 4.4 Milieu Therapy & Rehabilitative Activities

- **Milieu therapy (therapeutic community)** – **Maxwell Jones**; **use of the total environment (people, setting, activities) as a therapeutic tool**; **patient participation in ward management (community meetings)**, democratic, open communication, **24-hr living-learning**; **nurse – milieu manager**.
- **Characteristics of therapeutic milieu** – safety, structure, norms, **limit setting**, open communication, balance, **involvement**, validation, acceptance.
- **Occupational therapy** – purposeful activities to improve function (crafts, gardening); **vocational rehabilitation**.
- **Recreational therapy** – games, music, dance; **activity therapy**.
- **Bibliotherapy** (reading), **art therapy**, **music therapy**, **yoga & meditation**.
- **Psychoeducation** – patient & family about illness, medication, relapse signs (**↓ relapse**).
- **Social skills training**, **day care centres**, **half-way homes**, **sheltered workshops**, **long-stay homes**.

## 4.5 Restraint & Seclusion

- **Last resort** – only when **imminent danger to self/ others** and less restrictive measures (**verbal de-escalation, PRN medication**) failed.
- **MHCA 2017 (Sec 97)** – **physical restraint only when necessary to prevent imminent harm**, by order of psychiatrist, **not as punishment**, minimum time, **monitored regularly**, informed to nominated representative, recorded; **seclusion & solitary confinement prohibited**; **chaining prohibited**.
- **Nursing** – adequate staff, explain reason, **check circulation & skin every 15 min**, vital signs, **offer fluids, toilet**, release one limb at a time/ ROM every 2 hrs, document, debrief.
