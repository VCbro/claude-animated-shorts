# Psychiatric Assessment, Therapeutic Communication & Nurse–Patient Relationship

## 2.1 Psychiatric Assessment

- **Psychiatric history** – identifying data, **chief complaints (in patient's own words)**, history of present illness, past psychiatric & medical history, **family history** (mental illness, suicide, substance use), **personal history** (birth, milestones, education, occupation, marital, sexual), **premorbid personality**, substance use, forensic history.
- **Informants** – patient + relatives (reliability & adequacy noted).

### Mental Status Examination (MSE)
| Component | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| **General appearance & behaviour** | Grooming, dress, posture, eye contact, rapport, psychomotor activity, abnormal movements |
| **Speech** | **Rate, rhythm, volume, tone, quantity** (pressure of speech – mania; poverty – depression/ schizophrenia) |
| **Mood** (subjective – "How do you feel?") & **affect** (objective – range, intensity, appropriateness) | Depressed, elated, anxious, irritable; flat/ blunted |
| **Thought** | **Stream/ form** (flight of ideas, loosening), **content** (**delusions, obsessions, suicidal/ homicidal ideas**, phobias) |
| **Perception** | Hallucinations, illusions, depersonalisation |
| **Cognitive functions** | **Consciousness**, **orientation (time, place, person)**, **attention & concentration (serial 7s/ 3s, digit span)**, **memory (immediate – digit span; recent – 24 hrs events; remote)**, intelligence, **abstract thinking (proverbs, similarities)** |
| **Judgement** | Personal, social, **test judgement** ("What will you do if you see a house on fire?") |
| **Insight** | Awareness of illness – **grades 1–6** |

- **Rating scales** – **MMSE (Folstein) – cognition, max 30 (≤ 23/ 24 = impairment)**; **HAM-D (Hamilton depression)**, **BDI (Beck Depression Inventory)**, **PHQ-9 (depression screening)**, **GAD-7**, **YMRS (Young Mania)**, **BPRS (Brief Psychiatric Rating Scale)**, **PANSS (schizophrenia)**, **Y-BOCS (OCD)**, **CIWA-Ar (alcohol withdrawal)**, **CAGE & AUDIT (alcohol)**, **SAD PERSONS (suicide risk)**, **AIMS (tardive dyskinesia)**, **GCS**.
- **Investigations** – CBC, LFT, RFT, TSH, **serum lithium**, drug screen, EEG, CT/ MRI, psychological tests (IQ – **WAIS, Binet**; personality – **MMPI, Rorschach inkblot, TAT**).

## 2.2 Therapeutic Communication

:::def Therapeutic communication
**Purposeful, goal-directed, patient-centred** interaction that helps the patient **express feelings, understand problems & find solutions**; the nurse uses **self as a therapeutic tool**.
:::

| **Therapeutic techniques** | Example |
|---|---|
| **Active listening** | Attentive, eye contact, nodding (**SOLER** – Sit squarely, Open posture, Lean forward, Eye contact, Relaxed) |
| **Broad openings** | "What would you like to talk about today?" |
| **Open-ended questions** | "How have you been feeling?" |
| **Silence** | Gives time to think/ express (therapeutic pause) |
| **Restating** | Repeating main idea – "You say you can't sleep." |
| **Reflecting** | Directing feelings back – "You seem angry." |
| **Clarification** | "I'm not sure I understand. Could you explain?" |
| **Focusing** | Concentrating on one point |
| **Exploring** | "Tell me more about that." |
| **Validation/ consensual validation** | Checking understanding |
| **Giving recognition** | "Good morning, Mr. Shah. You combed your hair." |
| **Offering self** | "I'll sit with you for a while." |
| **Presenting reality** | "I don't hear any voices; you are in the hospital." |
| **Voicing doubt** | "That seems hard to believe." (delusions – gently) |
| **Summarising** | Review of main points at end |
| **Encouraging comparison, formulating a plan** | – |

| **Non-therapeutic (blocks)** | Example |
|---|---|
| **False reassurance** | "Don't worry, everything will be fine." |
| **Giving advice** | "If I were you, I would..." |
| **Approving/ disapproving, judging** | "That's good/ bad." |
| **Asking "Why?"** | Makes patient defensive |
| **Changing the subject** | – |
| **Belittling feelings** | "Everybody feels that way." |
| **Arguing / challenging** | "You can't be the President." |
| **Defending** | "The doctor is very good." |
| **Stereotyped/ cliché responses** | "Keep your chin up." |
| **Probing** | Persistent questioning |
| **Literal responses, interpreting** | – |

- **Johari window** (Joseph Luft & Harry Ingham) – **open (known to self & others), blind (others know, self doesn't), hidden (self knows, others don't), unknown**; self-awareness ↑ by **self-disclosure & feedback** → enlarges open area.
- **Self-awareness** of nurse – essential for therapeutic use of self.
- **Transference** – patient **unconsciously transfers feelings** about significant others onto the nurse; **counter-transference** – nurse's emotional reaction to patient (must be recognised – supervision).
- **Empathy (understanding patient's feelings from his perspective)** vs **sympathy** (sharing feelings – not therapeutic); **empathy – key to therapeutic relationship**.
- **Process recording** – verbatim record of nurse–patient interaction for analysis (student learning).

## 2.3 Nurse–Patient Relationship (Peplau)

@fig maslow_peplau.svg | Maslow's needs pyramid & Peplau's phases of nurse–patient relationship

| Phase | Tasks |
|---|---|
| **Pre-interaction** (added by Travelbee/ others) | Nurse's **self-exploration** of feelings, fears; gather data; plan first meeting |
| **Orientation (introductory)** | **Introductions, establish trust & rapport**, **contract** (time, place, duration, purpose), **confidentiality**, **termination discussed from the start**, identify problems; patient may **test** the nurse |
| **Working phase (identification + exploitation)** | Explore stressors, **promote insight**, develop **coping**, problem solving, overcome resistance; **transference** may appear |
| **Termination (resolution)** | Review progress, **deal with feelings of separation/ loss** (sadness, anger, regression), evaluate goals, **plan for future/ discharge**; **termination should be planned & gradual** |

- **Peplau's nurse roles** – **stranger, resource person, teacher, counsellor, surrogate, leader** (+ technical expert).
- **Characteristics of therapeutic relationship** – **trust, empathy, respect, genuineness, acceptance, non-judgemental, warmth, boundaries, confidentiality**.
- **Boundaries** – avoid personal/ social relationship, gifts, over-involvement; **professional relationship**.
- **Maslow – priority**: physiological → safety → love/ belonging → esteem → self-actualisation; in psychiatry, **safety (suicide, violence)** is a top priority after physiological needs.

## 2.4 Nursing Process in Psychiatry (examples)

| Problem | Nursing diagnosis (NANDA) |
|---|---|
| Suicidal ideation | **Risk for suicide / self-directed violence** |
| Aggression | **Risk for other-directed violence** |
| Hallucinations | **Disturbed sensory perception** |
| Delusions | **Disturbed thought processes** |
| Withdrawal | **Social isolation** |
| Poor self-care | **Self-care deficit** |
| Low self-esteem | **Chronic low self-esteem** |
| Anxiety | **Anxiety (mild/ moderate/ severe/ panic)** |
| Non-compliance | **Ineffective health management** |

- **Levels of anxiety (Peplau)** – **mild** (↑ alertness, learning enhanced), **moderate** (narrowed perception, selective attention), **severe** (very narrow, can't problem-solve), **panic** (disorganised, loss of control – **stay with patient, calm, simple directions, safe environment**).

:::exam PYQ style
- MSE – mood is **subjective**, affect is **objective**.
- Test for attention/ concentration – **serial 7s / digit span**.
- Proverb interpretation tests – **abstract thinking**.
- "What will you do if you find a stamped letter?" – **test judgement**.
- MMSE maximum score – **30**.
- Suicide risk scale – **SAD PERSONS**; alcohol screening – **CAGE**; withdrawal – **CIWA-Ar**; TD – **AIMS**.
- Rorschach inkblot – **personality (projective) test**.
- Most therapeutic response to patient's feelings – **reflection/ open-ended**.
- "Don't worry, you'll be fine" – **false reassurance (non-therapeutic)**.
- Asking "why" is – **non-therapeutic**.
- Hallucinating patient – **present reality ("I don't hear voices")**.
- Johari window – **self-awareness**; quadrants – **open, blind, hidden, unknown**.
- Patient treats nurse like his mother – **transference**.
- Phases (Peplau) – **orientation → identification → exploitation → resolution**.
- Contract & trust established in – **orientation phase**.
- Termination discussed – **from the beginning (orientation)**.
- Panic anxiety – **stay with patient, calm simple directions**.
:::
