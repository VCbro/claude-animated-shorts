## 4.1 Biostatistics – Basics

- **Biostatistics** – application of statistical methods to biological/ medical data. **Father of biostatistics – Francis Galton** (statistics in general – Sir Ronald Fisher is father of modern statistics).
- **Data types** – **qualitative (categorical – nominal: sex, blood group; ordinal: ranked – grades of pain)** & **quantitative (numerical – discrete: number of children; continuous: Hb, height, weight)**.
- **Sources of data** – **census**, **registration of vital events (CRS)**, **SRS (Sample Registration System)**, notification of diseases, hospital records, **NFHS, surveys**, **HMIS**, disease registers.

### Presentation of data
| Method | Use |
|---|---|
| **Tabulation** | Simple/ complex tables |
| **Bar diagram** | **Discrete/ qualitative** data comparison (simple, multiple, component/ proportional) |
| **Histogram** | **Continuous quantitative** data (frequency distribution) – no gaps between bars |

| **Ogive** | Cumulative frequency (median from graph) |

### Central tendency & dispersion
- **Mean** (arithmetic average) – affected by extreme values. **Median** – middle value of arranged data (best when skewed). **Mode** – most frequent value. **In normal distribution – mean = median = mode**.
- **Range** – highest − lowest (simplest); **mean deviation**; **standard deviation (SD) – most commonly used & best measure of dispersion** – √[Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)]; **variance = SD²**; **coefficient of variation = SD/ mean × 100**.
- **Example**: values 2, 4, 4, 5, 10 → mean = 25/5 = **5**, median = **4**, mode = **4**, range = **8**.

@fig normal_curve.svg | Normal distribution & key biostatistics points

- **Normal (Gaussian) curve** – bell-shaped, symmetrical; **mean ± 1 SD = 68.27 %**, **± 2 SD = 95.45 %**, **± 3 SD = 99.73 %**; **mean ± 1.96 SD = 95 %**.
- **Skewness** – positive (tail right; mean > median > mode), negative (tail left).
- **Standard error (SE) = SD/√n**.

### Sampling
- **Probability (random)** – **simple random** (lottery, random number tables), **systematic** (every nth), **stratified** (sub-groups – heterogeneous population), **cluster** (e.g. **30-cluster survey – immunization coverage (WHO)**), multistage.
- **Non-probability** – convenience, purposive, quota, snowball.
- **Sampling error** – reduced by increasing sample size.

### Tests of significance
- **Null hypothesis (H₀)** – no difference; **p < 0.05** → significant (reject H₀).
- **Type I error (α)** – rejecting a true H₀ (false positive); **Type II error (β)** – accepting a false H₀; **power = 1 − β**.
- **Chi-square test** – **qualitative data/ proportions**; **t-test (Student's)** – **difference between two means (small samples < 30)**; paired t-test (before–after); **Z-test** – large samples; **ANOVA** – > 2 means; **correlation coefficient (r)** – −1 to +1.

## 4.2 Demography

- **Demography** – scientific study of human population – **size, composition (age, sex), distribution** & changes (fertility, mortality, migration). **Father of demography – John Graunt**.
- **Demographic cycle (5 stages)**: (1) **High stationary** (high birth & death rates – India till 1920); (2) **Early expanding** (death rate falls, birth high); (3) **Late expanding** (death rate falls further, birth rate starts falling – **India now**); (4) **Low stationary** (both low – Europe); (5) **Declining** (birth < death – Germany, Japan).
- **Population pyramid** – **broad base (high fertility – developing, India's earlier)** vs narrow base/ bulging top (developed, ageing).
- **Dependency ratio** – (population < 15 + > 64) ÷ population 15–64 × 100. **Demographic dividend** – ↑ working-age share.

### Census
- **Census of India** – every **10 years**; **first (non-synchronous) 1872 (Lord Mayo)**; **first synchronous 1881 (Lord Ripon)**; conducted by **Registrar General & Census Commissioner (MHA)**; **Census 2011 – 15th**; next census postponed (COVID) – **Census 2027** (scheduled, includes caste enumeration).

## 4.3 Vital Statistics & Registration

- **Registration of Births & Deaths (RBD) Act 1969** – **compulsory registration**; report **birth & death within 21 days** (late fee after); **still birth** also registered; **Registrar** at village – Panchayat secretary/ (urban – municipal health officer); **RBD Amendment Act 2023** – **digital birth certificate as single document for date of birth**, national database.
- **CRS (Civil Registration System)**; **SRS (Sample Registration System, 1964–65 pilot, full 1969–70)** – **dual record system** (continuous enumeration by part-time enumerator + independent 6-monthly survey by supervisor) – gives reliable **annual birth & death rates, IMR, MMR, TFR**.
- **MCCD (Medical Certification of Cause of Death)** – ICD-10/ ICD-11 coding.

### Fertility & mortality indicators

| Indicator | Formula | India (latest SRS) |
|---|---|---|
| **Crude Birth Rate (CBR)** | Live births ÷ **mid-year population** × 1000 | **~19.3 (2021)** |
| **Crude Death Rate (CDR)** | Deaths ÷ mid-year population × 1000 | **~7.5 (2021)** |
| **General Fertility Rate (GFR)** | Live births ÷ **women 15–49 yrs** × 1000 | – |
| **Total Fertility Rate (TFR)** | Average **number of children a woman would bear** in her reproductive life at current rates | **2.0** (SRS 2021/ NFHS-5) |
| **Gross Reproduction Rate (GRR)** | Average number of **daughters** a woman would have | – |
| **Net Reproduction Rate (NRR)** | Daughters, **adjusting for mortality**; **NRR = 1 ≈ TFR 2.1 (replacement level)** | ~1 |
| **IMR** | Infant deaths (< 1 yr) ÷ **live births** × 1000 | **27 (2021)** |
| **NMR** | Neonatal deaths (< 28 days) ÷ live births × 1000 | **19** |
| **Post-neonatal MR** | 28 days – 1 yr deaths ÷ live births × 1000 | – |
| **Perinatal MR** | (Still births ≥ 28 wks + early neonatal deaths) ÷ **(live births + still births)** × 1000 | – |
| **U5MR** | Under-5 deaths ÷ live births × 1000 | **31** |
| **MMR (ratio)** | Maternal deaths ÷ **live births** × **1,00,000** | **93 (2019–21)** |
| **Maternal mortality rate** | Maternal deaths ÷ **women 15–49** × 1,00,000 | – |
| **Life expectancy at birth** | – | **~70 yrs** |
| **Growth rate** | CBR − CDR (natural increase) | ~1.2 % |
| **Couple Protection Rate (CPR)** | % eligible couples effectively protected by any contraceptive | NFHS-5 modern method use ~56.5 % |

- **Eligible couple** – currently married couple, **wife aged 15–49 yrs**; **eligible couple register** maintained by ANM (updated yearly – April).
- **Kerala** – lowest IMR & MMR; highest life expectancy.
