# Concepts of Health, Disease & Community Health Nursing
<!-- desc: Definitions of health, dimensions, determinants, spectrum, concepts of disease causation, natural history, levels of prevention, community, CHN principles & roles -->

## 1.1 Health

:::def Health (WHO, 1948)
"Health is a state of **complete physical, mental and social well-being** and not merely the absence of disease or infirmity." **Spiritual dimension** suggested later (1980s). **Operational definition** – absence of obvious disease + functioning normally.
:::

- **Dimensions of health** – **physical, mental, social, spiritual**, emotional, vocational, (environmental, philosophical, cultural, socioeconomic, educational, nutritional, curative, preventive).
- **Positive health** – perfect functioning of body & mind (ideal, not achievable fully). **Health continuum / spectrum** – positive health → better health → freedom from sickness → unrecognised sickness → mild → severe sickness → death.
- **Concepts of health** – **biomedical** (absence of disease – germ theory), **ecological** (man–environment equilibrium), **psychosocial**, **holistic** (all sectors – most accepted).
- **Determinants of health** – **biological (heredity)**, **behavioural & socio-cultural (lifestyle)**, **environment**, **socioeconomic** (income, education, occupation, political system), **health services**, ageing, gender, equity, information & communication, aging of population, other sectors (agriculture, food).
- **Indicators of health** – mortality (**IMR – most sensitive**), morbidity, disability (**DALY** – disability-adjusted life year = YLL + YLD; **Sullivan's index** – expectation of life free of disability; **QALY**), nutritional status, health care delivery (doctor–population ratio), utilisation (immunization coverage), social & mental health, environmental, socio-economic, health policy, quality of life (**PQLI – Physical Quality of Life Index = IMR + life expectancy at age 1 + literacy**; 0–100), **HDI (Human Development Index – life expectancy at birth, education – mean & expected years of schooling, GNI per capita)** – by UNDP.
- **Responsibility for health** – individual (self-care), community, state, international.

## 1.2 Disease & Its Causation

- **Disease** – a condition in which body health is impaired; **illness** – subjective (patient's feeling); **sickness** – social role (state of social dysfunction).
- **Theories of disease causation**: **supernatural** → **theory of humours (Hippocrates)** → **miasmatic theory** (bad air) → **germ theory (Louis Pasteur, Robert Koch)** – single cause → **epidemiological triad (agent–host–environment)** → **multifactorial causation** (Pettenkofer) → **web of causation (MacMahon & Pugh) – for chronic diseases**.
- **Koch's postulates** – agent present in every case, isolated in pure culture, reproduces disease in animals, re-isolated.
- **Iceberg phenomenon of disease** – **tip = clinical cases (seen)**; submerged portion = **sub-clinical, carriers, undiagnosed** (larger); e.g. **hypertension, diabetes, anaemia, malnutrition, polio** (large hidden). **Diseases without iceberg** – rabies, tetanus, measles (mostly clinical).

## 1.3 Natural History of Disease

- **Pre-pathogenesis phase** – disease agent not yet entered man; interaction of agent, host, environment (**man in the presence of the disease** – potential).
- **Pathogenesis phase** – entry of agent → **incubation period** → early pathogenesis (subclinical) → clinical disease → **outcome (recovery, disability, carrier state, death)**.

## 1.4 Levels of Prevention (Leavell & Clark)

| Level | Phase | Modes of intervention | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Primordial** | Before risk factors appear | Health education (lifestyle) | Preventing obesity, smoking habit in children |
| **Primary** | Pre-pathogenesis | **Health promotion** (education, nutrition, environment, lifestyle) & **specific protection** | **Immunization, chemoprophylaxis, iodised salt, fluoridation, helmet, protective clothing, condoms** |
| **Secondary** | Early pathogenesis | **Early diagnosis & treatment** | **Screening (Pap smear, BP, sugar), case finding, treatment of TB/ leprosy patients** |
| **Tertiary** | Late pathogenesis | **Disability limitation & rehabilitation** | Physiotherapy in polio, reconstructive surgery in leprosy, CBR |

- **Rehabilitation types** – **medical** (restoration of function), **vocational** (earning capacity), **social** (family/ social relationships), **psychological** (personal dignity, confidence).
- **Disability chain** – **disease → impairment → disability → handicap** (WHO ICIDH); impairment = loss of structure/ function (organ level); disability = restriction of activity (person level); handicap = social disadvantage. **ICF (2001)** – functioning, disability & health.
- **Primary prevention most effective & economical**; **secondary prevention = main approach for chronic diseases without primary prevention**.

## 1.5 Community & Community Health Nursing

- **Community** – a group of people living in a **common geographical area**, sharing common values, interests, culture, needs (WHO). Characteristics – common area, common interests, sense of belonging, social structure.
- **Community health** – health status of members of a community and actions to improve it.
- **Community health nursing (CHN)** – synthesis of **nursing practice and public health practice** applied to promoting & preserving health of populations; **population-focused, comprehensive, continuous, family as unit of service**.
- **Public health (Winslow, 1920)** – "the science & art of **preventing disease, prolonging life and promoting health & efficiency** through organised community effort".
- **Father of public health** – **Cholera John Snow (father of modern epidemiology)**; Father of public health nursing – **Lillian Wald** (coined term "public health nurse", Henry Street Settlement, New York 1893); **William Rathbone – district nursing (Liverpool, 1859)**; **Florence Nightingale – "health nursing"**.
- **Principles of CHN** – based on **needs of community**, **family as unit**, **community participation**, health education as integral part, **continuity of care**, utilise existing resources, **intersectoral coordination**, periodic evaluation, records & reports, work within **national health policy**, respect for cultural values, **preventive & promotive focus**.

### Roles of Community Health Nurse
- **Care provider** (clinic, home), **health educator**, **counsellor**, **advocate**, **case manager/ coordinator**, **collaborator**, **leader/ manager/ supervisor** (of ANMs, ASHAs), **researcher**, **change agent**, **epidemiologist/ case finder** (surveillance), **referral agent**, **trainer** (of health workers, dais), **record keeper**.
- **Qualities** – good communicator, culturally sensitive, observant, resourceful, empathetic.

## 1.6 Health Promotion & Concepts

- **Ottawa Charter (1986)** – first International Conference on Health Promotion; **5 action areas** – build healthy public policy, create supportive environments, strengthen community action, develop personal skills, reorient health services. **Strategies – advocate, enable, mediate**.
- **Health promotion** – process of enabling people to increase control over & improve their health.
- **Concepts of control** – **eradication** (termination of all transmission by extermination of agent – **smallpox (1980)**, globally), **elimination** (interruption of transmission from a large region – **regional/ national**, e.g. polio from India, yaws), **control** (reduction of incidence to a level where it is no longer a public health problem), **monitoring** & **surveillance**.
- **Smallpox – only human disease eradicated globally (WHO declared 8 May 1980)**; India smallpox-free – **1977** (last case Saiban Bibi – 24 May 1975 – Assam/Karimganj); **Yaws** – India declared free 2006 (first country WHO-certified 2016); **Guinea worm** – India certified free 2000; **Polio** – 2014; **Maternal & neonatal tetanus** – 2015; **Trachoma** – WHO validated India as having eliminated trachoma as a public health problem in **2024**.
