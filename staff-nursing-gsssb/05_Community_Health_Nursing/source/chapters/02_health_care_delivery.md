## 2.1 Health Committees (Most Asked)

| Committee | Year | Key recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhore Committee** (Health Survey & Development Committee, chairman **Sir Joseph Bhore**) | **1946** (appointed 1943) | **Integration of curative & preventive**; **PHC concept**; 3-month training in preventive medicine; **"social physician"**; short-term: **1 PHC per 40,000** (long-term **3 Million Plan** – 75-bed PHC per 10,000–20,000) |
| **Mudaliar Committee** (Health Survey & Planning Committee) | **1962** | Strengthen existing PHCs before new; **1 PHC per 40,000**; District hospitals strengthened; **All India Health Service** |
| **Chadah Committee** | **1963** | Malaria maintenance phase – **1 basic health worker per 10,000** population (vigilance); family planning duty to health workers |
| **Mukerji Committee** | **1965 / 1966** | Separate staff for family planning; delinking malaria from FP |
| **Jungalwalla Committee** (Integration) | **1967** | **Integration of health services**, unified cadre, equal pay, no private practice |
| **Kartar Singh Committee** (Multipurpose workers) | **1973** | **Multipurpose workers (MPW)** – ANM → **female health worker**; male MPW; **1 PHC per 50,000** with **16 sub-centres (each ~3,000–3,500)** having 1 male + 1 female worker; **1 health supervisor per 4 health workers** |
| **Shrivastava Committee** (Group on Medical Education & Support Manpower) | **1975** | **Community health workers/ Village health guides**; referral services complex (**ROME – Reorientation of Medical Education**, 1977) |
| **Bajaj Committee** | 1986 | Health manpower planning |

- **Village Health Guide Scheme – 2 Oct 1977**; **ICDS – 1975**; **ROME scheme 1977**.

## 2.2 Alma-Ata Declaration & Primary Health Care

:::def Alma-Ata (Almaty, Kazakhstan; USSR) – 6–12 Sept 1978
WHO-UNICEF International Conference on Primary Health Care; goal **"Health for All by 2000 AD"**; **Primary Health Care** = essential health care based on practical, scientifically sound & socially acceptable methods, **universally accessible** to individuals & families **through their full participation**, at a **cost the community & country can afford**. **Astana Declaration 2018** – renewed commitment.
:::

- **Elements of PHC (8 – "ELEMENTS")**: **E**ducation (health education on prevailing problems), **L**ocally endemic disease control, **E**xpanded immunization, **M**aternal & child health incl. family planning, **E**ssential drugs, **N**utrition (food supply & proper nutrition), **T**reatment of common diseases & injuries, **S**afe water & basic sanitation. (+ mental health, dental, handicapped – added later).
- **Principles of PHC (4 + 1)**: **Equitable distribution**, **community participation**, **intersectoral coordination**, **appropriate technology** (+ focus on prevention).
- India accepted – **National Health Policy 1983**.

## 2.3 Health Care Delivery – Structure

- **Central level** – **Ministry of Health & Family Welfare** (Departments: Health & Family Welfare, Health Research/ ICMR); **DGHS** (Directorate General of Health Services – technical adviser); **Central Council of Health & Family Welfare** (1952, chairman – Union Health Minister).
- **State level** – State Ministry, Directorate of Health Services; **health is a State subject** (Constitution – **7th Schedule, State List**; **Article 47 – duty of state to raise nutrition & public health**).
- **District level** – **Chief District Health Officer (CDHO)/ CMO**, district hospital; **district – principal unit of administration**.
- **Block/ Taluka level** – **CHC**, PHCs; **Taluka Health Officer (THO)/ BMO**.
- **Village level** – **sub-centre (AAM)**, ASHA, AWW, **VHSNC (Village Health, Sanitation & Nutrition Committee)**, **Gram Panchayat**; **Panchayati Raj (3-tier – 73rd Amendment 1992)** – Gram Panchayat, Panchayat Samiti (block), Zila Parishad (district).

| Facility (IPHS 2022) | Plain area | Hilly/ tribal/ difficult | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sub-Centre (SC) – AAM** | **5,000** | **3,000** | **Most peripheral contact point**; 1 ANM (FHW) + 1 MPW (male) + **CHO (Community Health Officer)** at AAM; **6 SCs under 1 PHC** |
| **PHC – AAM** | **30,000** | **20,000** | **Medical Officer** + 14 staff; 4–6 beds; **1st contact with doctor**; referral unit for 6 SCs |
| **CHC (FRU)** | **1,20,000** | **80,000** | **30 beds**, **4 specialists (surgeon, physician, OBG, paediatrician)** + anaesthetist; **referral for 4 PHCs**; **First Referral Unit** (emergency obstetric care, CS, blood storage, newborn care) |
| Sub-district/ district hospital | – | – | Secondary care |
| **Medical colleges, AIIMS** | – | – | Tertiary care |

- **Urban** – **Urban PHC (UPHC) per 50,000** (NUHM); **UCHC per 2.5–5 lakh**; **Urban AAM (per 15,000–20,000)**; **MAS (Mahila Arogya Samiti)** in slums; **ASHA per 1,000–2,500** in urban slums.
- **Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM)** – **ABHA (Ayushman Bharat Health Account) – 14-digit health ID** (Sept 2021).

## 2.4 Ayushman Bharat (2018)

| Pillar | Details |
|---|---|
| **Health & Wellness Centres (HWC) → Ayushman Arogya Mandir (AAM)** (renamed **Nov 2023**) | Launched **14 April 2018 – Jangla, Bijapur (Chhattisgarh)**; SCs & PHCs upgraded to deliver **Comprehensive Primary Health Care (CPHC)** – **12 service packages**; **CHO (mid-level health provider – B.Sc. nursing/ GNM/ BAMS with 6-month Certificate in CPHC)**; free essential drugs & diagnostics; **teleconsultation (eSanjeevani)**; wellness activities (yoga) |
| **PM-JAY (Pradhan Mantri Jan Arogya Yojana)** | Launched **23 Sept 2018 (Ranchi, Jharkhand)**; **₹5 lakh per family per year** for secondary & tertiary hospitalisation; **cashless, paperless**; beneficiaries per **SECC 2011** deprivation criteria; **no cap on family size or age**; **Ayushman card**; **extended to all senior citizens ≥ 70 yrs irrespective of income (Oct 2024 – Ayushman Vay Vandana card)**; **National Health Authority (NHA)** implements |

- **CPHC – 12 service packages**: (1) **care in pregnancy & childbirth**, (2) **neonatal & infant care**, (3) **childhood & adolescent care**, (4) **family planning, contraception & reproductive health**, (5) **management of communicable diseases & national health programmes**, (6) **general OPD – acute simple illnesses & minor ailments**, (7) **screening, prevention & management of NCDs**, (8) **mental health**, (9) **ophthalmic (eye) care**, (10) **ENT care**, (11) **oral health**, (12) **elderly & palliative care** (+ emergency medical services incl. burns & trauma – expanded).
- **Universal screening of NCDs (≥ 30 yrs)** at AAM – hypertension, diabetes, **oral, breast & cervical cancer**.
- **Gujarat** – **PMJAY–MA (Mukhyamantri Amrutum) – cover ₹10 lakh per family** (state top-up); MA scheme started **2012** for BPL.

## 2.5 National Health Mission (NHM)

- **NRHM – launched 12 April 2005** (rural; 18 high-focus states); **NUHM – 1 May 2013**; both under **NHM (2013)**.
- **Key components** – **ASHA** (1 per 1,000 population), **Rogi Kalyan Samiti (RKS)/ Hospital Management Society**, **untied funds** to SCs/ PHCs/ CHCs, **VHSNC**, **IPHS** standards, **JSY**, mobile medical units, **NAMS**, district health action plans, **RMNCH+A** (2013), AYUSH mainstreaming, **National Ambulance Services (108/ 102)**.
- **ASHA (Accredited Social Health Activist)** – **woman, resident of the village, 25–45 yrs (preferably married/ widowed/ divorced), literate – formal education up to 8th class (10th preferred)**, selected by **Gram Sabha/ VHSNC**; **1 per 1,000 population** (tribal/ hilly – per habitation); **performance-based incentives (not salaried)**; **"link worker" / interface between community & health system**; drug kit (ORS, IFA, OCP, condoms, paracetamol, cotrimoxazole, zinc, chloroquine, RDT kit); **ASHA facilitator – 1 per 20 ASHAs**; induction training in modules (Modules 1–7).
- **AWW (Anganwadi Worker)** – under **ICDS (Ministry of Women & Child Development)** – **1 Anganwadi Centre per 400–800 population** (rural/ urban, plain areas).

## 2.6 National Health Policy (NHP)

| Policy | Key points |
|---|---|
| **NHP 1983** | First NHP; **"Health for All by 2000"**, PHC approach, VHGs |
| **NHP 2002** | Increase public health spending to **2 % of GDP**; eradicate polio/ yaws, eliminate leprosy |
| **NHP 2017** | **Universal health coverage; "assured comprehensive care"**; public health expenditure **2.5 % of GDP by 2025**; **HWCs**; targets – **life expectancy 70 by 2025**, **TFR 2.1 by 2025**, **U5MR 23 by 2025**, **MMR 100 by 2020**, **IMR 28 by 2019**, **NMR 16 & still birth rate single digit by 2025**; TB elimination by 2025; leprosy elimination by 2018 |

- **National Population Policy 2000** – immediate: contraception & infrastructure; medium-term: **TFR to replacement level (2.1) by 2010**; long-term: **stable population by 2045**.
- **Health care financing** – out-of-pocket expenditure in India is high (~40–50 %; declining).
