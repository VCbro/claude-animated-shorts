## 12.1 Health Education

:::def Health Education (National Conference on Preventive & Social Medicine, USA)
"A process that **informs, motivates and helps people to adopt & maintain healthy practices and lifestyles**, advocates environmental changes as needed to facilitate this goal and conducts professional training & research to the same end." Health education aims at **changing knowledge → attitude → practice/ behaviour (KAP)**.
:::

- **Aims/ objectives (WHO)** – encourage people to **adopt & sustain health-promoting lifestyles**; make **judicious use of health services**; take own decisions to improve health status (**individual & collective responsibility – self-reliance**).
- **Approaches** – **regulatory (legal)**, **service**, **health education** (most effective in long term), **primary health care** approach.
- **Models** – **medical model**, **motivation model**, **social intervention model**.
- **Contents** – human biology, nutrition, hygiene, family health, disease prevention & control, mental health, prevention of accidents, use of health services.

### Principles of health education
1. **Credibility** – message consistent with scientific knowledge & local culture.
2. **Interest** – people won't learn what they're not interested in (**felt needs**).
3. **Participation** – **active participation – high level of learning** (principle of "**learning by doing**").
4. **Motivation** – awaken desire; **primary** (inborn – hunger, sex) & **secondary** (rewards, praise, love); **incentives**.
5. **Comprehension** – **level of understanding**, local language, avoid jargon.
6. **Reinforcement** – repetition of message at intervals.
7. **Learning by doing** – Chinese proverb "**If I hear, I forget; if I see, I remember; if I do, I know**".
8. **Known to unknown** – start where people are.
9. **Setting an example** – health worker should practise what is taught.
10. **Good human relations**.
11. **Feedback**.
12. **Leaders** – use local leaders (opinion leaders, teachers, religious leaders) as agents of change.

- **Stages of adoption of new idea (Rogers' diffusion of innovation)** – **Awareness → Interest → Evaluation → Trial → Adoption**; adopter categories – innovators (2.5 %), early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards.
- **Behaviour change models** – **Health Belief Model** (perceived susceptibility, severity, benefits, barriers, cues to action, self-efficacy), **Stages of change (Transtheoretical – Prochaska & DiClemente)**: **pre-contemplation → contemplation → preparation → action → maintenance** (± relapse), PRECEDE-PROCEED, social learning theory (Bandura).

## 12.2 Methods of Health Communication

| Category | Methods | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Individual approach** | **Personal contact, home visits, counselling, personal letters, telephone** | **Most effective** for behaviour change; time-consuming |
| **Group approach** | **Lecture/ chalk-talk**, **demonstration**, **group discussion**, **panel discussion**, **symposium**, **workshop**, **seminar**, **conference**, **role play**, **brainstorming**, **Philips 66 (buzz session)**, **case study**, **film shows**, **puppet show**, **exhibition** | Two-way, cost-effective |
| **Mass approach** | **Television, radio, newspapers, posters, health magazines, internet/ social media, mobile (SMS), cinema, folk media** (street plays – nukkad natak, bhavai in Gujarat), **hoardings** | Reaches large numbers; **one-way – limited behaviour change**; creates awareness |

### Group methods – details
| Method | Description |
|---|---|
| **Lecture** | One-way; **group ≤ 30**; **duration 15–20 min**; supplement with AV aids |
| **Demonstration** | **"Show how" – carefully prepared presentation** – e.g. **ORS preparation, hand washing, breastfeeding** – **"seeing is believing"**; **result demonstration** (shows outcome) vs **method demonstration** (shows how) |
| **Group discussion** | **6–12 members**, leader + recorder; circular seating; **each one participates**; free exchange |
| **Panel discussion** | **4–8 qualified persons (experts) discuss a topic in front of a large audience**; chairman; **audience participates at end** |
| **Symposium** | **Series of speeches by experts on a selected subject**; **no discussion among speakers**; audience may ask questions at end |
| **Workshop** | **Series of meetings (4 or more)**, emphasis on **individual work within group (problem solving)**, 10–25 participants |
| **Seminar** | Group of experts/ learners study a topic intensively under guidance |
| **Conference** | Large meeting to share knowledge; many sessions |
| **Role play** | Participants **enact a real situation** – learn attitudes, communication |
| **Brainstorming** | **Free spontaneous ideas** from group, no criticism; then evaluated |
| **Philips 66 / buzz session** | Large group divided into groups of **6, discuss for 6 min** |

## 12.3 Audio-Visual (AV) Aids

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Auditory** | Radio, tape recorder, public address system, podcasts |
| **Visual – projected** | **Slides, filmstrips, overhead projector (OHP), LCD/ PowerPoint, epidiascope** |
| **Visual – non-projected** | **Chalkboard (blackboard – most commonly used)**, **flannel graph**, **flash cards** (series of 10–12 cards, talk with each), **flip charts**, **posters (one idea, few words, bold)**, charts, **models, specimens, exhibits**, pamphlets, leaflets |
| **Combined (audio-visual)** | **Television, films/ videos, cinema**, demonstrations, puppetry |

- **Poster** – **attract attention, single message, simple, large letters**, placed at eye level, changed frequently.
- **Edgar Dale's "Cone of Experience"** – from **most concrete (base) – direct purposeful experiences** → contrived experiences (models) → dramatised → demonstrations → field trips → exhibits → TV/ films → recordings, radio, pictures → visual symbols → **verbal symbols (most abstract – top)**. **Learning retention** – people remember ~10 % of what they read, 20 % hear, 30 % see, 50 % see & hear, 70 % say, **90 % say & do**.

## 12.4 Communication

- **Communication** – process of **sharing information, ideas, feelings** to bring about **understanding**; Latin *communis* (common).
- **Elements** – **Sender (communicator) → Message → Channel (medium) → Receiver (audience) → Feedback** (+ effects); **SMCR model (Berlo)**.
- **Types** – **one-way (didactic – lecture)** vs **two-way (Socratic – discussion)**; **verbal** (oral/ written) vs **non-verbal** (body language, gestures, facial expression, eye contact – conveys ~ 2/3 of message); formal vs informal (**grapevine**); **upward, downward, horizontal**; **visual**, **telecommunication**.
- **Barriers** – **physiological** (deafness, poor vision, aphasia), **psychological** (emotional disturbance, neurosis, prejudice, fear), **environmental** (noise, invisibility, crowding), **cultural** (illiteracy, language, beliefs, customs, attitudes – **most important in India**), semantic (jargon), status differences.
- **Key communication skills** – **active listening**, empathy, clarity, feedback, respect, culturally appropriate.
- **IEC** – Information, Education & Communication; **BCC** – Behaviour Change Communication (targeted to specific audience to change behaviour); **SBCC** – Social & BCC; **IPC** – interpersonal communication (most effective for behaviour change).
- **Health communication strategies** – **advocacy** (to policy makers), **social mobilisation** (community, partners), **BCC** (individuals).

## 12.5 Counselling

- **Counselling** – **two-way, face-to-face** process to **help a person make decisions & solve problems** (helping relationship); based on **trust, confidentiality, empathy, non-judgemental attitude**, client-centred.
- **GATHER approach (family planning counselling)** – **G**reet, **A**sk, **T**ell, **H**elp, **E**xplain, **R**eturn visit.
- **Pre-test & post-test counselling (HIV)** – informed consent, confidentiality (**ICTC**).
- **Steps** – establish rapport, explore problem, identify options, help decision, plan action, follow-up.
- **Counselling vs health education** – counselling individual, decision-oriented; education informs.

## 12.6 Planning a Health Education Programme

1. **Assess needs** (felt needs, community diagnosis) → 2. **Set objectives (SMART)** → 3. **Identify target group & content** → 4. **Select methods & media** → 5. **Pre-test materials** → 6. **Implement** → 7. **Evaluate (process, impact, outcome)**.
