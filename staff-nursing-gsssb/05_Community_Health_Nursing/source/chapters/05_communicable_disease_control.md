## 5.1 Basic Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Infection** | Entry & development/ multiplication of an infectious agent in man/ animal |
| **Contamination** | Presence of agent on body surface/ articles (not multiplication) |
| **Infestation** | Lodgement & development of **arthropods** on body surface/ clothing (lice, scabies) |
| **Source** | Person/ animal/ object from which agent passes **immediately** to host |
| **Reservoir** | Where agent **normally lives & multiplies** (human, animal, non-living – soil) |
| **Carrier** | Infected person who **harbours the agent without clinical disease & serves as source** – **incubatory** (measles, mumps, polio, diphtheria, pertussis), **convalescent** (typhoid, diphtheria, dysentery, cholera), **healthy** (polio, cholera, diphtheria, salmonella), **temporary** (< 6 months) vs **chronic** (> 6 months – **typhoid (gallbladder), hepatitis B**, malaria, gonorrhoea) |
| **Incubation period** | Time from entry of agent to onset of first symptom |
| **Generation time** | Interval from receipt of infection to **maximal infectivity** of host |
| **Serial interval** | Gap between onset of primary & secondary cases |
| **Communicable period** | Time during which agent may be transferred |
| **Herd immunity** | Level of resistance of a community/ group to an infection (immunized majority protects others) – **not applicable to tetanus** |

- **Incubation period** importance – tracing source, **period of surveillance/ quarantine (= max IP)**, immunization of contacts, identify point-source vs propagated.
- **Short IP** – **cholera (1–2 days), food poisoning (hours), influenza (1–3 days)**, diphtheria (2–6 days). **Long IP** – **hepatitis B (45–180 days), leprosy (years), rabies (days–years; usually 1–3 months)**.

## 5.2 Modes of Transmission

| Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|
| **Direct contact** (STIs, leprosy, skin infections, scabies) | **Vehicle-borne** – water, food, milk, blood, fomites... (cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A, polio, hepatitis B via blood) |
| **Droplet infection** (< 1 m – common cold, diphtheria, pertussis, TB, influenza, meningococcal) | **Vector-borne** – **mechanical** (housefly – typhoid, cholera) or **biological** (mosquito – malaria) |
| **Contact with soil** (tetanus, hookworm) | **Airborne** – **droplet nuclei** (TB, measles, chickenpox – long distance) & **dust** |
| **Inoculation into skin/ mucosa** (rabies, hepatitis B) | **Fomite-borne** – articles (diphtheria, typhoid, HAV) |
| **Transplacental (vertical)** – TORCH, HIV, syphilis, HBV | **Unclean hands & fingers** – most common in faeco-oral |

- **Biological transmission types** – **propagative** (multiply, no development – plague in rat flea), **cyclo-developmental** (develop, no multiply – **filaria in mosquito**), **cyclo-propagative** (both – **malaria in Anopheles**).
- **Extrinsic incubation period** – time in vector (malaria 10–14 days, dengue 8–10 days).

## 5.3 Control of Communicable Diseases

### (A) Control of reservoir
- **Early diagnosis**, **notification**, **epidemiological investigation**, **isolation**, **treatment**, **quarantine**, disinfection.
- **Isolation** – **separation of infected persons** for the **period of communicability** (in hospital/ home). Types – strict, respiratory (droplet/ airborne), contact, enteric, blood-body fluid; **standard precautions** for all. **Not useful** where many subclinical cases (polio, hepatitis A).
- **Quarantine** – limitation of freedom of movement of **well (healthy) contacts** exposed to communicable disease for a period **not longer than the longest incubation period**; e.g. **yellow fever – 6 days**; **absolute** (complete), **modified** (selective – e.g. exclude children from school), **segregation**. **International Health Regulations (IHR 2005)**.
- **Surveillance** – continuous scrutiny; **active** (health worker goes house to house – **malaria fever cases, AFP**) vs **passive** (cases come to facility); **sentinel surveillance** (selected sites – HIV); **syndromic**.
- **Notifiable diseases** – reporting to health authority mandatory; international (IHR) – **cholera, plague, yellow fever** (+ SARS, smallpox, new influenza subtype, wild polio).

### (B) Interrupt transmission
- Water & food sanitation, **hand washing (most effective single measure in hospital infection control)**, vector control (environmental, chemical, biological – **Gambusia fish, BTI**), disinfection, safe sex, safe blood.

### (C) Protect susceptible host
- **Active immunization**, **passive immunization (immunoglobulins)**, **chemoprophylaxis** (e.g. INH for TB contacts, rifampicin for meningococcal contacts, doxycycline for leptospirosis, SDR-PEP for leprosy), non-specific measures (nutrition, health education, personal hygiene).

## 5.4 Disinfection

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Disinfection** | Killing infectious agents **outside the body** by chemical/ physical means (**not spores**) |
| **Concurrent disinfection** | **Immediately after discharge** of infectious material from body (stool, urine, sputum, soiled linen) – during illness |
| **Terminal disinfection** | After patient has **left/ died/ isolation ends** – room, bed, articles (largely replaced by **terminal cleaning**) |
| **Precurrent (prophylactic)** | **Before** – chlorination of water, pasteurisation of milk, hand washing |
| **Sterilisation** | Destruction of **all forms including spores** |
| **Antiseptic** | Inhibits/ kills organisms on **living tissue** |
| **Deodorant** | Removes odour |

| Agent | Use |
|---|---|
| **Bleaching powder (33 % chlorine)** | Water, faeces, urine, floors |
| **Sodium hypochlorite 1 %** (0.5 % surfaces) | **Blood spills, BMW, HBV/ HIV contaminated** surfaces |
| **Phenol (carbolic acid 5 %)** | **Standard disinfectant – Rideal-Walker coefficient** (phenol coefficient) |
| **Cresol (Lysol 2–5 %)** | Faeces, sputum, floors |
| **Alcohol 70 %** | Skin, thermometers, hand rub (60–80 %) |
| **Formaldehyde (formalin)** | Fumigation of rooms (OT), preservation |
| **Glutaraldehyde 2 % (Cidex)** | **Endoscopes, heat-sensitive instruments** (high-level disinfectant; sterilant in 10 hrs) |
| **Chlorhexidine** | Skin, hand wash, cord care |
| **Povidone iodine** | Skin preparation |
| **Lime (quick lime)** | Faeces, burial |
| **Burning/ incineration** | Contaminated dressings, anatomical waste |
| **Autoclave 121 °C, 15 lb/in², 15–20 min** | Linen, instruments – **sterilisation** |
| **Hot air oven 160 °C × 2 hrs** | **Glassware, powders, oils** |
| **Boiling (100 °C, 10–30 min)** | Not sterilisation (spores survive) |
| **Pasteurisation** – **holder 63–66 °C × 30 min**; **flash/ HTST 72 °C × 15–20 sec** | Milk |

- **Spaulding classification** – **critical** (enter sterile tissue – sterilise), **semi-critical** (mucous membranes – high-level disinfection), **non-critical** (intact skin – low-level).

## 5.5 Surveillance – IDSP/ IHIP

- **IDSP (Integrated Disease Surveillance Project/ Programme)** – launched **Nov 2004**; **3 levels of case definitions**: **S – Syndromic** (by health worker, **S form**), **P – Presumptive** (by MO, **P form**), **L – Laboratory confirmed** (**L form**); weekly reporting (Monday–Sunday); **Rapid Response Team (RRT)** at district; **IHIP (Integrated Health Information Platform)** – digital, real-time (2021); **call centre 1075**.
- **AFP surveillance** – < 15 yrs; **measles–rubella surveillance (fever + rash)**; **Ni-kshay** (TB); **HMIS**; **IHIP**.
- **Epidemic threshold**; **early warning signals** – clustering of cases, unusual deaths.
