# Occupational Health
<!-- desc: Occupational hazards, pneumoconiosis, lead & other poisonings, occupational cancers & dermatoses, prevention, ESI Act, Factories Act, health of workers, ergonomics -->

## 11.1 Basics

- **Occupational health (ILO/ WHO, 1950)** – promotion & maintenance of the **highest degree of physical, mental & social well-being of workers in all occupations**; **"adaptation of work to man and of each man to his job"** (ergonomics).
- **Father of occupational medicine – Bernardino Ramazzini** (*De Morbis Artificum* – 1700).
- **Ergonomics** – fitting the job to the worker.

| Hazard type | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Physical** | Heat (heat stroke, cramps), cold, light, **noise (NIHL)**, vibration (**Raynaud's – "white fingers"**), radiation, pressure (**caisson disease – decompression sickness**) |
| **Chemical** | Dusts, gases, metals (lead, mercury, chromium), solvents (benzene – leukaemia) |
| **Biological** | **Brucellosis, anthrax (wool sorter's disease), leptospirosis, Q fever**, TB, tetanus, hepatitis B/ HIV (health workers) |
| **Mechanical** | Accidents, injuries |
| **Psychosocial** | Stress, shift work, burnout |
| **Ergonomic** | Back pain, repetitive strain injury, carpal tunnel |

## 11.2 Pneumoconiosis & Lung Diseases

| Disease | Dust | Occupation / key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Silicosis** | **Free silica (SiO₂)** | **Mining, quarrying, stone cutting, sand blasting, pottery, glass**; **most common & most serious pneumoconiosis**; **↑ TB risk (silicotuberculosis)**; **"snowstorm" X-ray, eggshell calcification of hilar nodes**; irreversible; **notifiable** |
| **Anthracosis** | **Coal dust** | **Coal miners** ("black lung") |
| **Asbestosis** | **Asbestos fibres** | Mining, insulation, brake lining; **mesothelioma (pleura), lung cancer**; **asbestos bodies** |
| **Byssinosis** | **Cotton fibre dust** | **Textile mills**; **"Monday fever/ Monday chest tightness"** |
| **Bagassosis** | **Sugarcane fibre (bagasse)** | Paper/ cardboard industry; **thermophilic actinomycetes** |
| **Farmer's lung** | **Mouldy hay/ grain** | **Thermophilic actinomycetes (Micropolyspora faeni)** – hypersensitivity pneumonitis |
| **Siderosis** | Iron oxide | Welders, iron workers (benign) |
| **Berylliosis** | Beryllium | Electronics |
| **Tobacosis** | Tobacco dust | Tobacco industry |

- **Dust particles 0.5–3 µm** (respirable) reach alveoli – most dangerous.

## 11.3 Occupational Poisonings & Cancers

| Agent | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Lead (plumbism)** | **Most common occupational metal poisoning**; battery, paint, printing, smelting; **inhalation – main route**; **Burtonian blue line (gums)**, **abdominal colic**, constipation, anaemia (**basophilic stippling**), wrist drop, encephalopathy; **↑ coproporphyrin in urine (CPU), ↑ ALA, blood lead**; Rx – **chelation (CaNa₂EDTA, DMSA, BAL)** |
| **Mercury** | Tremors, erethism, gingivitis, acrodynia; **Minamata disease (methyl mercury – fish)** |
| **Chromium** | **Nasal septum perforation**, chrome ulcers, lung cancer |
| **Arsenic** | Raindrop pigmentation, keratosis, skin cancer |
| **Benzene** | **Leukaemia, aplastic anaemia** |
| **Carbon monoxide** | Headache, **cherry-red colour**; hyperbaric O₂ |
| **Methyl isocyanate (MIC)** | **Bhopal gas tragedy (2–3 Dec 1984)** – Union Carbide; **National Pollution Control Day – 2 Dec** |
| **Organophosphates** | Farmers – cholinergic crisis; **atropine + PAM** |

| Occupational cancer | Agent |
|---|---|
| **Skin cancer** | **Soot, coal tar, mineral oil** (scrotal cancer in chimney sweeps – **Percival Pott, 1775 – first occupational cancer**), arsenic, UV |
| **Lung cancer** | Asbestos, chromium, nickel, arsenic, radon (uranium miners) |
| **Bladder cancer** | **Aromatic amines (aniline dyes – benzidine, β-naphthylamine)** – dye & rubber industry |
| **Leukaemia** | **Benzene**, ionising radiation |
| **Liver (angiosarcoma)** | **Vinyl chloride** |
| **Mesothelioma** | **Asbestos** |
| **Nasal sinus** | Wood dust, nickel |

- **Occupational dermatoses** – **most common occupational disease overall** (contact dermatitis – cement, chromium, rubber, chemicals).
- **Occupational hazards of health workers** – **needle-stick injuries (HBV most transmissible – 30 %, HCV 3 %, HIV 0.3 %)**, TB, back injuries, radiation, chemicals (glutaraldehyde, cytotoxic), violence, stress.
- **Agricultural workers** – pesticides, snake bite, zoonoses (leptospirosis, brucellosis), heat stroke, farmer's lung, injuries.

## 11.4 Prevention

- **Medical measures** – **pre-placement (pre-employment) examination** (baseline, fit for job), **periodic medical examination** (early detection), medical & health care services, notification, supervision of working environment, **records**, health education, **first aid**.
- **Engineering measures (most effective)** – **substitution** (non-toxic for toxic – e.g. sandblasting with non-silica), **enclosure/ isolation**, **local exhaust ventilation**, **wet methods (dust suppression)**, mechanisation/ automation, design of building, good housekeeping, environmental monitoring (**TLV – threshold limit values**).
- **Personal protective equipment (PPE)** – masks/ respirators, ear plugs/ muffs, goggles, gloves, aprons, helmets, safety shoes – **last line of defence**.
- **Legislation** – see below.
- **Hierarchy of controls** – **elimination > substitution > engineering > administrative > PPE**.

## 11.5 Labour Laws & Social Security

| Act | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Factories Act 1948 (amended 1987)** | Applies to factories with **≥ 10 workers (with power) / ≥ 20 (without power)**; **minimum age 14 yrs**; **working hours ≤ 48 hrs/week, ≤ 9 hrs/day**; adolescents (15–18) ≤ 4.5 hrs/day; **no woman at night (7 PM – 6 AM)** except with safeguards; **canteen (≥ 250 workers)**, **crèche (≥ 30 women workers)**, **first-aid box (1 per 150 workers)**, **ambulance room (≥ 500 workers)**, **welfare officer (≥ 500)**, **safety officer (≥ 1000)**; annual leave with wages (1 day per 20 days worked); **notifiable diseases to Chief Inspector of Factories** |
| **ESI Act 1948 (Employees' State Insurance)** | **First implemented – 24 Feb 1952 (Kanpur & Delhi)**; covers **non-seasonal factories/ establishments with ≥ 10 employees**, **wage ceiling ₹21,000/month (₹25,000 for disabled)**; contribution – **employer 3.25 %, employee 0.75 %** (employees earning < ₹176/day exempt from contribution); administered by **ESIC** (Ministry of Labour) |
| **ESI benefits (7)** | **Medical benefit** (full, for insured person & family), **sickness benefit (70 % of wages, up to 91 days/yr)**, **maternity benefit (100 % wages, 26 weeks)**, **disablement benefit** (temporary – 90 % of wages; permanent – as per loss of earning capacity), **dependants' benefit** (on death from employment injury – 90 % of wages shared), **funeral expenses (₹15,000)**, **unemployment allowance (Rajiv Gandhi Shramik Kalyan Yojana/ ABVKY)**; + rehabilitation, confinement expenses |
| **Employees' Compensation Act 1923** (Workmen's Compensation) | Compensation for injury/ occupational disease/ death arising out of employment |
| **Maternity Benefit Act 1961 (amended 2017)** | 26 weeks leave; crèche (≥ 50 employees) |
| **Mines Act 1952**, **Plantations Labour Act 1951**, **Dock Workers Act** | Sector-specific |
| **Child & Adolescent Labour Act 1986 (amended 2016)** | No child < 14 yrs in any occupation; 14–18 not in hazardous |
| **New Labour Codes (2019–20)** – **Code on Social Security 2020**, **Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions Code 2020** | Subsume older Acts (implemented in phases) |
| **ILO** | **Geneva**, est. **1919** |

- **Institutes** – **National Institute of Occupational Health (NIOH) – Ahmedabad, Gujarat** (ICMR); **Central Labour Institute – Mumbai**.
- **Sickness absenteeism** – index of health of workers.
