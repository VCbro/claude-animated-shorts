# Epidemiology

## 3.1 Basics

:::def Epidemiology (Last, 1988)
"The study of the **distribution and determinants** of health-related states or events in specified populations, and the **application** of this study to the **control of health problems**." Greek: epi = upon, demos = people, logos = study. **Father of epidemiology – Hippocrates**; **father of modern epidemiology – John Snow** (cholera, Broad Street pump, London 1854).
:::

- **Aims** – describe distribution & size of disease problems, identify aetiological factors, provide data for planning, implementation & evaluation of services.
- **Components** – **disease frequency, distribution (time, place, person), determinants**.
- **Epidemiological triad** – **agent, host, environment**.

@fig epi_triad_chain.svg | Agent–host–environment triad & chain of infection

### Terms
| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Endemic** | **Constant presence** of a disease within a given geographic area (e.g. malaria, common cold) |
| **Epidemic** | Occurrence **clearly in excess of normal expectancy** in a community/ region |
| **Pandemic** | Epidemic spread **across countries/ continents** (COVID-19, influenza 1918) |
| **Sporadic** | Scattered, irregular cases (tetanus, polio now) |
| **Outbreak** | Epidemic limited to localised increase (village, hostel) |
| **Exotic** | Imported into country where it doesn't exist (rabies in UK) |
| **Zoonosis** | Transmitted between vertebrate animals & man (rabies, plague, anthrax) |
| **Epizootic** | Epidemic in animals |
| **Nosocomial** | Hospital-acquired infection |
| **Iatrogenic** | Physician/ treatment-induced |
| **Opportunistic** | Infection in immunocompromised host |

## 3.2 Measurements in Epidemiology

| Tool | Definition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Rate** | Event in a population over **time** × multiplier; numerator **is part of denominator** | Death rate, birth rate |
| **Ratio** | **a : b** – numerator **not part of** denominator | Sex ratio, doctor–population ratio, MMR (ratio) |
| **Proportion** | **a / (a + b)** – numerator included in denominator; usually % | % of children immunized |

- **Crude rate** – entire population; **specific rate** – age/ sex/ cause specific; **standardised rate** – adjusted (direct/ indirect) for comparison.

### Incidence & prevalence
- **Incidence** – number of **NEW cases** in a specified period ÷ population at risk × 1000. Measures **risk**; useful for **acute diseases & aetiology** studies.
- **Attack rate** – incidence in an epidemic (**% – cases ÷ population at risk during a limited period**).
- **Secondary attack rate (SAR)** – **(number of exposed persons developing disease within the range of incubation period following exposure to primary case) ÷ (total susceptible contacts) × 100** – **primary case excluded** from numerator & denominator; measures **infectiousness/ communicability**.
- **Prevalence** – **ALL cases (old + new)** at a point/ period ÷ population × 1000; **point prevalence** (one point in time) / **period prevalence** (incidence + point prevalence); useful for **chronic diseases**, planning services.
- **Prevalence = Incidence × Mean duration (P = I × D)**.
- **Case fatality rate (CFR)** – **deaths from a disease ÷ cases of that disease × 100** – measures **killing power/ virulence** (e.g. rabies ~100 %).
- **Proportional mortality rate** – deaths from a specific cause ÷ total deaths × 100.
- **Survival rate**, **morbidity**: frequency (incidence, prevalence) & duration.

## 3.3 Epidemiological Methods (Study Designs)

| Type | Design | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| **Descriptive** | Time, place, person ("**who, when, where**") | **First phase**; generates hypothesis |
| **Analytical – Case-control (retrospective)** | **Effect → cause**; cases (disease) vs controls (no disease); look back for exposure | **Odds ratio**; quick, cheap, **rare diseases**; recall & selection bias |
| **Analytical – Cohort (prospective/ longitudinal/ incidence/ follow-up)** | **Cause → effect**; exposed vs non-exposed followed up | **Incidence, relative risk (RR), attributable risk**; expensive, long, loss to follow-up; Framingham heart study |
| **Cross-sectional** | One point in time | **Prevalence** study |
| **Experimental – RCT (Randomised Controlled Trial)** | Randomisation, intervention vs control; **blinding (single, double, triple)** | **Gold standard/ most powerful** for causality & drug efficacy |
| **Field trials / community trials** | Healthy people / whole communities (vaccine trial) | – |

- **Relative risk (RR)** = incidence among exposed ÷ incidence among non-exposed (**strength of association**). **Attributable risk (AR)** = incidence exposed − non-exposed (÷ exposed × 100). **Odds ratio** – approximate RR in case-control.
- **Hierarchy of evidence** – **systematic review/ meta-analysis > RCT > cohort > case-control > cross-sectional > case series/ reports > expert opinion**.
- **Bias** – selection, information (recall, interviewer), confounding; **randomisation** removes selection bias & confounding; **double blinding** removes observer & subject bias.
- **Criteria for causation (Bradford Hill)** – **temporal association (most important)**, strength, dose–response, consistency, specificity, biological plausibility, coherence.

## 3.4 Screening

- **Screening** – search for unrecognised disease/ defect by rapid tests in **apparently healthy** people (**secondary prevention**). **Mass**, **high-risk/ selective**, **multiphasic (several tests at once)**, **opportunistic (case finding)**.
- **Criteria (Wilson & Jungner, 1968)** – important health problem, recognisable latent stage, natural history known, suitable & acceptable test, effective treatment available, cost-effective, continuous process.

| | Disease present | Disease absent |
|---|---|---|
| **Test +** | **a – True positive** | **b – False positive** |
| **Test −** | **c – False negative** | **d – True negative** |

- **Sensitivity = a/(a + c)** – ability to identify **correctly those who HAVE disease** (true positive rate). High sensitivity → few **false negatives** → **good for screening (rule out – "SnNout")**.
- **Specificity = d/(b + d)** – ability to identify correctly those **WITHOUT disease** (true negative rate). High specificity → few **false positives** → **confirmatory ("SpPin")**.
- **Positive predictive value (PPV) = a/(a + b)**; **NPV = d/(c + d)**. **PPV increases with prevalence**.
- **Validity** (accuracy – sensitivity & specificity), **reliability** (repeatability/ precision), **yield** (amount of previously unrecognised disease detected).

## 3.5 Investigation of an Epidemic (Steps)

1. **Verify the diagnosis** (clinical, lab).
2. **Confirm the existence of an epidemic** (compare with previous data – excess over expected).
3. **Define population at risk** (map, census).
4. **Rapid search for all cases & their characteristics** (case definition, line listing).
5. **Data analysis – time (epidemic curve), place (spot map), person**.
6. **Formulate hypothesis**.
7. **Test hypothesis**.
8. **Evaluate ecological factors**.
9. **Further investigation of population at risk**.
10. **Writing the report** + control measures (**start control measures early**, alongside).

- **Epidemic curve** – **common-source single exposure** (point source – **sharp rise & fall, all cases within one incubation period** – e.g. food poisoning, Bhopal gas tragedy), **common source continuous/ repeated**, **propagated (person-to-person – gradual rise, tails off, over several incubation periods – e.g. hepatitis A, measles)**, slow (modern) epidemics (CHD, cancer).
- **Spot map** – John Snow's cholera map.
