## 4.1 Antihypertensives

| Class | Drugs | Key side effects / points |
|---|---|---|
| **ACE inhibitors ("-pril")** | **Enalapril, captopril, ramipril, lisinopril** | **Dry cough (bradykinin) – most common**, **angioedema**, **hyperkalaemia**, **first-dose hypotension**, ↑ creatinine, **teratogenic (contraindicated in pregnancy)**, bilateral renal artery stenosis; **renoprotective in diabetics**, HF, post-MI |
| **ARBs ("-sartan")** | **Losartan, telmisartan, olmesartan** | Like ACEI but **no cough**; hyperkalaemia; contraindicated in pregnancy |
| **Calcium channel blockers** | **Amlodipine, nifedipine** (dihydropyridines), **verapamil, diltiazem** (non-DHP) | **Ankle oedema, headache, flushing, reflex tachycardia** (DHP); **bradycardia, constipation (verapamil)**, heart block; **grapefruit ↑ levels** |
| **Thiazide diuretics** | **Hydrochlorothiazide, chlorthalidone**, indapamide | First-line HTN; hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia (gout), hypercalcaemia, hyponatraemia |
| **β-blockers** | Atenolol, metoprolol, **labetalol** | See ANS chapter |
| **α1-blockers** | Prazosin | First-dose hypotension |
| **Central α2 agonists** | **Clonidine, methyldopa** | Clonidine – rebound HTN; **methyldopa – safe in pregnancy** |
| **Vasodilators** | **Hydralazine** (pregnancy, lupus-like syndrome), **minoxidil** (hirsutism), **sodium nitroprusside** (**hypertensive emergency – IV; protect from light (wrap in foil); cyanide/ thiocyanate toxicity – max 72 hrs**) | – |

- **Hypertensive emergency** – **IV labetalol, nicardipine, sodium nitroprusside, esmolol**; **reduce MAP by ≤ 25 % in first hour** (avoid precipitous fall → stroke).
- **Pregnancy HTN** – **labetalol, nifedipine, methyldopa (hydralazine IV)**; **avoid ACEI/ ARB**.
- **Nursing** – check **BP before each dose** (lying & standing), **rise slowly (postural hypotension)**, **don't stop abruptly** (clonidine, β-blockers), salt restriction, monitor K⁺ & renal function, weight.

## 4.2 Antianginal Drugs

| Drug | Key points |
|---|---|
| **Nitroglycerin (glyceryl trinitrate, GTN)** | **Venodilator (↓ preload)** + coronary dilator; **SL 0.4–0.5 mg (tablet/ spray) – onset 1–3 min**; **repeat every 5 min up to 3 doses**; if pain persists → **MI – call emergency**; **side effects – throbbing headache (most common), flushing, postural hypotension, reflex tachycardia**; **tolerance** (nitrate-free interval 8–12 hrs, e.g. remove patch at night); **store in original dark glass bottle, tightly closed, away from heat/ moisture**; tingling/ burning on tongue = potent (traditional check); **contraindicated with sildenafil/ tadalafil (PDE-5) – severe hypotension**; IV infusion – glass bottle/ non-PVC sets |
| **Isosorbide dinitrate/ mononitrate** | Oral prophylaxis |
| **β-blockers** | Reduce O₂ demand – stable angina, post-MI |
| **CCBs** | **Prinzmetal (vasospastic) angina – DOC** |
| **Nicorandil, ranolazine, ivabradine, trimetazidine** | Adjuncts |
| **Aspirin + statin** | Secondary prevention |

## 4.3 Heart Failure & Digoxin

- **HF drugs** – **ACEI/ ARB/ ARNI (sacubitril-valsartan)**, **β-blockers (carvedilol, metoprolol succinate, bisoprolol)**, **MRA (spironolactone)**, **SGLT2 inhibitors (dapagliflozin, empagliflozin)** – ↓ mortality; **loop diuretics (symptom relief – oedema)**, digoxin (symptoms/ AF rate), ivabradine, inotropes (dobutamine) in acute.
- **Digoxin (cardiac glycoside; Digitalis lanata)**:
  - **Inhibits Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase → ↑ intracellular Ca²⁺ → ↑ contractility (positive inotrope)**; **↑ vagal tone → ↓ HR & AV conduction (negative chronotrope/ dromotrope)**.
  - Uses – **heart failure with AF (rate control)**, AF/ atrial flutter.
  - **Narrow therapeutic index** – **therapeutic level 0.5–2 ng/ml** (HF: 0.5–0.9); long t½ (~36–40 hrs) – **loading (digitalisation) dose** then maintenance (0.125–0.25 mg/day).
  - **Toxicity** – **GI: anorexia, nausea, vomiting (earliest)**, **visual: blurred, yellow-green vision (xanthopsia), halos**, confusion, fatigue, **arrhythmias – bradycardia, heart block, ventricular bigeminy (most common arrhythmia), VT**.
  - **Factors ↑ toxicity** – **hypokalaemia (most important – diuretics)**, hypomagnesaemia, **hypercalcaemia**, renal impairment, elderly, hypothyroidism, **drugs – amiodarone, verapamil, quinidine**.
  - **Antidote – digoxin-specific antibody Fab fragments (Digibind/ DigiFab)**; correct K⁺, stop drug, atropine for bradycardia.
  - **Nursing** – **check apical pulse for 1 full minute before giving – withhold if < 60/min (adult)** (< 90–110 infant, < 70 child) & report; check K⁺, digoxin level; same time daily; teach toxicity signs & pulse counting; avoid antacids (↓ absorption).

## 4.4 Antiarrhythmics

| Class (Vaughan Williams) | Drugs | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **I – Na⁺ channel blockers** | **Lignocaine (IB – ventricular arrhythmias)**, procainamide, flecainide | Lignocaine toxicity – CNS (seizures) |
| **II – β-blockers** | Propranolol, esmolol, metoprolol | Rate control |
| **III – K⁺ channel blockers** | **Amiodarone**, sotalol | **Amiodarone – broad spectrum (VF/ VT, AF)**; side effects – **pulmonary fibrosis**, **thyroid dysfunction (hypo/ hyper – iodine)**, **corneal microdeposits**, **photosensitivity, blue-grey skin**, hepatotoxicity, ↑ QT, **↑ digoxin & warfarin levels**; very long t½ |
| **IV – Ca²⁺ channel blockers** | **Verapamil, diltiazem** | SVT, AF rate; not in WPW/ VT |
| **Others** | **Adenosine** – **PSVT DOC**: **6 mg rapid IV push followed by rapid 20 ml saline flush (proximal vein), then 12 mg if needed**; **t½ < 10 sec**; transient asystole, flushing, chest discomfort, dyspnoea; **contraindicated in asthma**; **atropine** (bradycardia), **digoxin**, **MgSO₄ (torsades de pointes)** |

## 4.5 Diuretics

| Class | Drugs | Site | Key points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Loop ("high ceiling")** | **Frusemide (furosemide)**, torsemide, bumetanide | **Thick ascending loop of Henle (Na-K-2Cl)** | **Most potent**; **acute pulmonary oedema (IV – also venodilation)**, HF, oedema, hypercalcaemia; **hypokalaemia (most common)**, hyponatraemia, hypovolaemia, hypotension, **ototoxicity (rapid IV – give slowly ≤ 4 mg/min)**, hyperuricaemia, hyperglycaemia, metabolic alkalosis; **sulfa allergy** |
| **Thiazides** | **Hydrochlorothiazide**, chlorthalidone, metolazone | Distal convoluted tubule | HTN first-line; **↓ calcium excretion (hypercalcaemia) – used in calcium kidney stones**, nephrogenic DI; hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia |
| **K⁺-sparing** | **Spironolactone, eplerenone** (aldosterone antagonists), **amiloride, triamterene** | Collecting duct | **Hyperkalaemia** (avoid K supplements, ACEI caution), **gynaecomastia (spironolactone)**; **ascites in cirrhosis (DOC – spironolactone)**, HF, **Conn's syndrome**, hirsutism/ PCOS |
| **Osmotic** | **Mannitol (20 %)** | Proximal tubule/ loop | **↓ ICP (cerebral oedema)**, ↓ IOP (acute glaucoma); **contraindicated in HF/ pulmonary oedema, anuria**; **crystals – warm & use filter** |
| **Carbonic anhydrase inhibitors** | **Acetazolamide** | PCT | **Glaucoma**, **acute mountain sickness**, epilepsy; metabolic acidosis, paraesthesia |

- **Nursing** – **give in the morning** (avoid nocturia), **daily weight (best indicator of fluid loss)**, I/O, BP (postural), **serum K⁺**, **K⁺-rich foods (banana, orange, potato, coconut water) with loop/ thiazides** – **avoid with K-sparing**, watch digoxin toxicity with hypokalaemia.

## 4.6 Anticoagulants, Antiplatelets & Thrombolytics

| Drug | Mechanism/ route | Monitoring | Antidote | Points |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Unfractionated heparin (UFH)** | Activates **antithrombin III** (↓ thrombin, Xa); **IV/ SC (never IM)**; immediate onset | **aPTT (1.5–2.5 × control)** | **Protamine sulphate (1 mg per 100 units)** | **Safe in pregnancy** (doesn't cross placenta); **HIT (heparin-induced thrombocytopenia)** – monitor platelets; SC in abdomen ≥ 5 cm from umbilicus, **don't aspirate/ massage** |
| **LMWH – enoxaparin**, dalteparin | Anti-Xa; **SC once/ twice daily** | Usually none (anti-Xa if needed) | Protamine (partial) | DVT prophylaxis/ treatment, ACS; **don't expel air bubble** from prefilled syringe; less HIT |
| **Warfarin** | **Vitamin K antagonist** (↓ factors **II, VII, IX, X**); **oral**; onset 3–5 days (overlap with heparin) | **PT/ INR – target 2–3** (**2.5–3.5 mechanical valves**) | **Vitamin K (phytonadione)**; FFP/ **PCC** for bleeding | **Teratogenic (fetal warfarin syndrome)**; **many interactions** – ↑ effect: aspirin, NSAIDs, amiodarone, metronidazole, cotrimoxazole, fluconazole, erythromycin; ↓ effect: rifampicin, carbamazepine, vitamin K; **consistent intake of green leafy vegetables (vitamin K)**; avoid alcohol; soft toothbrush, electric razor; report bleeding (gums, urine, black stools) |
| **DOACs – dabigatran** (thrombin inhibitor), **rivaroxaban, apixaban** (Xa inhibitors) | Oral, fixed dose | No routine | **Dabigatran – idarucizumab**; Xa inhibitors – andexanet alfa | AF, DVT/ PE |

| Antiplatelet | Points |
|---|---|
| **Aspirin (low-dose 75–150 mg)** | **Irreversible COX-1 inhibition → ↓ thromboxane A2** (effect lasts platelet life **7–10 days**); MI (**chew 300–325 mg non-enteric**), stroke prevention; GI bleed, **Reye's syndrome in children**, asthma (aspirin-sensitive) |
| **Clopidogrel, prasugrel, ticagrelor** | **P2Y12 (ADP) inhibitors**; with aspirin = **DAPT after stents** |
| **Abciximab, tirofiban, eptifibatide** | GP IIb/IIIa inhibitors (IV) |
| **Dipyridamole, cilostazol** | – |

- **Stop aspirin/ clopidogrel ~5–7 days, warfarin ~5 days before elective surgery** (as per surgeon).

| Thrombolytics (fibrinolytics) | Points |
|---|---|
| **Streptokinase** (antigenic – allergy, can't repeat within 1 yr), **alteplase (tPA)**, **tenecteplase (single IV bolus)**, reteplase, urokinase | **Convert plasminogen → plasmin → dissolves fibrin clot**; **STEMI – within 12 hrs (best within 3 hrs; door-to-needle ≤ 30 min)**; **acute ischaemic stroke – alteplase within 4.5 hrs** (after CT excludes haemorrhage); massive PE; **bleeding – main risk**; contraindications – active bleeding, recent surgery/ trauma, **history of haemorrhagic stroke**, severe uncontrolled HTN, aortic dissection; **antidote – tranexamic acid / aminocaproic acid** (antifibrinolytics) |

- **Tranexamic acid** – antifibrinolytic – PPH (1 g IV within 3 hrs), trauma, menorrhagia, dental bleeding in haemophilia.

## 4.8 Haematinics, IV Fluids, Electrolytes & Blood

### Haematinics
| Drug | Points |
|---|---|
| **Oral iron – ferrous sulphate (200 mg = 60 mg elemental), fumarate, gluconate** | **Black stools (harmless)**, constipation/ diarrhoea, epigastric pain; **best absorbed on empty stomach** (give after food if GI upset); **with vitamin C (orange juice) ↑ absorption**; **avoid tea, coffee, milk, antacids, calcium, tetracyclines** (↓ absorption); **liquid iron – through straw (teeth staining)**; continue **3 months after Hb normal** to replenish stores; **iron poisoning in children – desferrioxamine** |
| **Parenteral iron – iron sucrose (IV), ferric carboxymaltose (IV, up to 1 g), iron dextran (IM Z-track/ IV – test dose, anaphylaxis risk)** | Malabsorption, intolerance, severe anaemia late pregnancy, CKD |
| **Folic acid** | 5 mg/day (megaloblastic); 400 µg (NTD prevention) |
| **Vitamin B12 (cyanocobalamin/ hydroxocobalamin) IM** | **Pernicious anaemia – lifelong injections** (intrinsic factor absent) |
| **Erythropoietin** | **CKD anaemia**; HTN, thrombosis |

### IV fluids
| Fluid | Type | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **0.9 % NaCl (normal saline)** | **Isotonic (308 mOsm/L)** | Shock, resuscitation, **with blood transfusion (only fluid compatible)** |
| **Ringer lactate (Hartmann's)** | Isotonic (273 mOsm/L) | **Burns, trauma, surgery, diarrhoea (closest to plasma)**; avoid in severe liver failure, hyperkalaemia caution |
| **5 % dextrose (D5W)** | Isotonic in bag (252) → **physiologically hypotonic** (free water) | Maintenance, **hypernatraemia**; **avoid in raised ICP/ head injury** |
| **0.45 % NaCl (half NS)** | **Hypotonic** | Cellular dehydration, hypernatraemia |
| **3 % NaCl** | **Hypertonic** | Severe symptomatic hyponatraemia, raised ICP (slow, central line, monitor Na – **correct ≤ 8–10 mEq/L/day – osmotic demyelination**) |
| **10 % / 25 % / 50 % dextrose** | Hypertonic | **Hypoglycaemia** |
| **DNS (5 % dextrose in 0.9 % saline)** | Hypertonic | Maintenance |
| **Colloids – albumin, dextran, gelatin (Haemaccel), HES** | – | Volume expansion; **dextran – anaphylaxis, bleeding** |

- **Potassium chloride** – **never IV push**; dilute; **max 10 mEq/hr (peripheral)**; **K⁺ ≤ 40 mEq/L** peripheral; ensure urine output (≥ 30 ml/hr); cardiac monitor for high rates; **oral KCl – dilute in water/ juice, after food (GI irritation)**. Normal serum K⁺ **3.5–5.0 mEq/L**.
- **10 % calcium gluconate** – **slow IV (over 10 min)** – hypocalcaemia, **hyperkalaemia (cardioprotection)**, **MgSO₄ toxicity antidote**; **don't mix with bicarbonate** (precipitates); **calcium chloride – central line (irritant)**.
- **Sodium bicarbonate (7.5/ 8.4 %)** – severe metabolic acidosis, hyperkalaemia, TCA overdose.
- **Hyperkalaemia treatment** – **calcium gluconate (stabilises myocardium – first)**, **insulin + dextrose**, salbutamol nebulisation, bicarbonate (shift into cells), **resins/ diuretics/ dialysis (remove)**.

### Blood transfusion – nursing points
- **Consent, blood grouping & cross-matching**, **verify at bedside by 2 staff** (patient ID, blood group, bag number, expiry).
- **Start within 30 min of issue; complete 1 unit within 4 hrs**; **18–20 G cannula**; **blood set with filter (170–200 µm)**; **only normal saline** (no dextrose – haemolysis; no RL – clotting with calcium; no drugs in line).
- **Vitals before, 15 min after start, then hourly & at end**; **first 15 min slowly (most reactions)** – stay with patient.
- **Reactions** – **febrile non-haemolytic (most common)**, **allergic (urticaria – antihistamine)**, **acute haemolytic (ABO incompatibility – most dangerous: fever, chills, back/ loin pain, chest tightness, hypotension, haemoglobinuria, DIC)**, **TACO (circulatory overload)**, **TRALI**, anaphylaxis (IgA-deficient), bacterial contamination, delayed haemolytic, **hyperkalaemia, hypocalcaemia (citrate) in massive transfusion**, infection (HIV, HBV, HCV, malaria, syphilis).
- **If reaction – STOP transfusion immediately**, **keep IV line open with NS (new tubing)**, inform doctor & blood bank, vitals, **send bag + set + patient blood & urine samples**, document.
- **Storage** – **packed RBCs/ whole blood 2–6 °C (CPDA-1: 35 days; SAGM: 42 days)**; **platelets 20–24 °C with continuous agitation – 5 days**; **FFP ≤ −18 °C (ideally −30 °C) – 1 yr; thaw at 37 °C, use within 6 hrs**; **cryoprecipitate** – fibrinogen, factor VIII.
- **1 unit packed RBC ↑ Hb by ~1 g/dl** (adult).
