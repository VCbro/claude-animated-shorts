## 2.1 Rights of Medication Administration

- **Classic 5 rights** – **Right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time**.
- **Extended (10 rights)** – + **right documentation, right to refuse, right assessment, right education (patient), right evaluation/ response**; some add right reason, right form.
- **3 checks** – when **taking from shelf/ cabinet**, when **preparing**, and **before administering (at bedside)**.
- **Two identifiers** – name + ID number/ DOB (**never bed number**).
- **Do NOT give a drug prepared by someone else**; **don't leave medicines at bedside**; **document immediately after giving** (never before).
- **Verbal/ telephone orders** – only in emergency; **read back**, get signed within 24 hrs.

## 2.3 Storage of Drugs

- **Cool, dry, dark place**; **original labelled containers**; **check expiry** ("first expiry, first out"); **schedule X/ narcotics in double-lock cupboard** with register & count every shift.
- **Refrigerate (2–8 °C)** – **insulin (unopened), vaccines, oxytocin, methylergometrine, carboprost, anti-D**, **suxamethonium**, some eye drops (as per label); lyophilised ASV can be kept at room temperature.
- **Light-sensitive (protect/ amber)** – **sodium nitroprusside (cover infusion), nitroglycerin (dark glass), frusemide inj, adrenaline, methylergometrine, amphotericin B, vitamin K, chlorpromazine, ciprofloxacin infusion**.
- **Nitroglycerin tablets** – **original dark glass bottle, tightly capped**, replace periodically (loses potency).
- **Multi-dose vials** – date & time of opening; discard as per policy (usually 28 days).

## 2.4 High-Alert Medications (ISMP)

- **Insulin**, **heparin & anticoagulants**, **concentrated KCl / potassium phosphate**, **opioids**, **sedatives (IV midazolam)**, **neuromuscular blockers**, **chemotherapy**, **hypertonic saline (3 %)**, **magnesium sulphate**, **digoxin IV**, **vasopressors (adrenaline, noradrenaline)**, **thrombolytics**, oral methotrexate (weekly).
- **Independent double check by 2 nurses**, standardised concentrations, infusion pumps, separate storage, **"LASA" (look-alike sound-alike)** caution, **tall-man lettering** (e.g. hydrOXYzine vs hydrALAzine).
- **KCl – NEVER IV push/ bolus (cardiac arrest)**; always diluted; max **10 mEq/hr peripheral** (up to 20 mEq/hr central with monitoring); **≤ 40 mEq/L** in peripheral line; check **urine output** before giving.

## 2.5 Dose Calculations

- **Basic formula** – **Dose = (Desired ÷ Have) × Vehicle**.
- **Weight-based** – **Dose (mg) = weight (kg) × mg/kg**.
- **IV flow (drops/min) = Volume (ml) × Drop factor (drops/ml) ÷ Time (min)**.
  - **Macro-drip – 15 or 20 drops/ml** (adult); **micro-drip – 60 drops/ml** (paediatric) → **drops/min = ml/hr**.
  - **ml/hr** (infusion pump) = **total volume ÷ total hours**.
- **Infusion time (hrs)** = volume ÷ ml per hour.
- **Dosage by concentration** – **% solution = g per 100 ml** (e.g. **5 % dextrose = 5 g/100 ml = 50 g/L**; **0.9 % NaCl = 9 g/L**; **10 % calcium gluconate 10 ml = 1 g**); **ratio solutions – 1 : 1000 adrenaline = 1 mg/ml**; **1 : 10,000 = 0.1 mg/ml**.
- **Insulin** – **U-100 = 100 units/ml** (insulin syringe 1 ml = 100 units); **U-40 = 40 units/ml** – use matching syringe.
- **Heparin** – units/ml; **infusion rate (units/hr)**.
- **mcg/kg/min** (vasopressors) – **ml/hr = (dose mcg/kg/min × weight × 60) ÷ concentration (mcg/ml)**.
- **Conversions** – **1 g = 1000 mg; 1 mg = 1000 µg; 1 L = 1000 ml; 1 kg = 2.2 lb; 1 teaspoon = 5 ml; 1 tablespoon = 15 ml; 1 oz = 30 ml; 1 grain ≈ 60–65 mg; °F = (°C × 9/5) + 32**.

### Worked examples
| Problem | Answer |
|---|---|
| 1000 ml NS over 8 hrs, drop factor 20 | 1000 × 20 ÷ 480 = **~42 drops/min** |
| 500 ml over 4 hrs, drop factor 15 | 500 × 15 ÷ 240 = **~31 drops/min** |
| 100 ml antibiotic over 30 min, micro set (60) | 100 × 60 ÷ 30 = **200 drops/min** (or 200 ml/hr pump) |
| Order amoxicillin 250 mg; syrup 125 mg/5 ml | 250/125 × 5 = **10 ml** |
| Order 0.5 g; tablet 250 mg | 500/250 = **2 tablets** |
| Order 75 mg pethidine; vial 100 mg/2 ml | 75/100 × 2 = **1.5 ml** |
| Heparin 5000 U; vial 25,000 U/5 ml | 5000/25,000 × 5 = **1 ml** |
| Gentamicin 5 mg/kg/day for 60 kg | **300 mg/day** |
| 3 L in 24 hrs by pump | 3000/24 = **125 ml/hr** |

### Paediatric dose rules
- **Young's rule** – Child dose = **Age (yrs) ÷ (Age + 12) × adult dose** (1–12 yrs).
- **Clark's rule** – **Weight (lb) ÷ 150 × adult dose**.
- **Fried's rule** – **Age (months) ÷ 150 × adult dose** (infants).
- **BSA rule (most accurate)** – **BSA (m²) ÷ 1.73 × adult dose**; **Mosteller BSA = √(height cm × weight kg / 3600)**.

## 2.6 Special Administration Points

| Route/ form | Points |
|---|---|
| **Oral** | Upright position; **don't crush enteric-coated (EC), sustained-release (SR/ ER/ XL) tablets**; liquid iron via straw; **check swallowing (psychiatric)**; give antacids separately (chelation) |
| **Sublingual** | Under tongue, don't swallow/ chew, no water |
| **IM** | **Ventrogluteal (safest – no major nerves/ vessels)**, vastus lateralis (infants), deltoid (≤ 1 ml), dorsogluteal (**sciatic nerve risk – upper outer quadrant**); **Z-track** for iron dextran, hydroxyzine; aspirate (not needed for vaccines in deltoid/ VL per current guidance); needle 21–23 G |
| **SC** | 45–90°; **don't aspirate or massage after heparin/ insulin** (haematoma) |
| **ID** | 10–15°, bevel up, wheal formed, don't massage |
| **IV** | Check patency, **compatibility**, rate; **flush between drugs**; never mix in blood; watch for **extravasation/ infiltration, phlebitis** |
| **Eye drops** | Lower conjunctival sac, **press nasolacrimal duct (inner canthus) 1–2 min**; drops before ointment; 5 min gap between drops |
| **Ear drops** | Warm to body temp; **adult – pinna up & back**; **child < 3 yrs – down & back** |
| **Nasal drops** | Head tilted back |
| **Inhaler (MDI)** | Shake, exhale, inhale slowly while pressing, **hold breath 10 sec**; spacer; **wait 1 min between puffs**; **bronchodilator before steroid**; **rinse mouth after ICS** |
| **Vaginal** | Lying, after bath, applicator; at bedtime |
| **Rectal suppository** | **Left lateral (Sims')**, insert beyond internal sphincter (~10 cm adult) |
| **Transdermal patch** | Rotate sites, remove old patch, hairless clean skin, don't cut (fentanyl) |

## 2.7 Medication Errors

- Types – wrong patient, drug, dose, route, time, omission, wrong technique, **LASA**, expired drug.
- **If an error occurs** – **assess & monitor patient first**, **inform doctor/ nurse in-charge immediately**, give antidote/ corrective care, **document facts (incident report – not in patient's chart as "incident report")**, disclose, analyse (system issue – **root cause analysis**), non-punitive culture.
- **Prevention** – rights & checks, clear orders, calculation double-check, **barcode**, avoid distractions, patient involvement.
