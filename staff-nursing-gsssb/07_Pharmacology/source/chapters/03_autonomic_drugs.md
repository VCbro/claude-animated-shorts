## 3.1 ANS Basics

@fig ans_drugs.svg | Sympathetic vs parasympathetic effects & key drugs

| | **Sympathetic (adrenergic)** | **Parasympathetic (cholinergic)** |
|---|---|---|
| Neurotransmitter | **Noradrenaline** (post-ganglionic) | **Acetylcholine** |
| Heart | ↑ rate & force (**β1**) | ↓ rate |
| Bronchi | **Dilate (β2)** | Constrict, ↑ secretion |
| Pupil | **Dilate – mydriasis (α1)** | **Constrict – miosis** |
| BP/ vessels | ↑ (α1 vasoconstriction) | – |
| GI | ↓ motility | ↑ motility & secretions |
| Bladder | Retention (relax detrusor) | Contraction (urination) |
| Glucose | ↑ (glycogenolysis) | – |
| Salivary | Thick scanty | Copious watery |

- **Receptors** – **α1** (vasoconstriction, mydriasis), **α2** (↓ NA release – clonidine), **β1** (heart), **β2** (bronchi, uterus relax, glycogenolysis), **β3** (lipolysis); cholinergic – **muscarinic (M)** & **nicotinic (Nn – ganglia, Nm – skeletal muscle)**.

## 3.2 Cholinergic Drugs (Parasympathomimetics)

| Drug | Use |
|---|---|
| **Acetylcholine** | Rarely (eye surgery – rapid miosis) |
| **Bethanechol** | **Post-operative urinary retention**, paralytic ileus |
| **Pilocarpine** | **Glaucoma (miosis ↑ aqueous outflow)**, xerostomia; reverses mydriasis |
| **Neostigmine** (anticholinesterase – quaternary, doesn't cross BBB) | **Myasthenia gravis**, **reversal of non-depolarising neuromuscular block** (with atropine/ glycopyrrolate), post-op ileus/ retention, cobra bite |
| **Pyridostigmine** | **Myasthenia gravis (drug of choice – longer acting)** |
| **Edrophonium** | **Tensilon test** – diagnosis of MG, differentiate myasthenic vs cholinergic crisis |
| **Physostigmine** (crosses BBB) | **Atropine/ anticholinergic poisoning antidote**, glaucoma |
| **Donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine** | **Alzheimer's disease** |

- **Cholinergic side effects/ toxicity ("SLUDGE/ DUMBELS")** – **S**alivation, **L**acrimation, **U**rination, **D**iarrhoea/ **D**efecation, **G**I cramps, **E**mesis; **bradycardia, bronchospasm/ bronchorrhoea, miosis**, sweating, muscle fasciculations, weakness.
- **Myasthenic crisis** (under-medication – weakness improves with edrophonium) vs **cholinergic crisis** (overdose – worsens; Rx atropine).

### Organophosphate (OP) poisoning
- **Irreversible anticholinesterases** – insecticides (**parathion, malathion, chlorpyrifos**), nerve gases; **very common suicidal poisoning in rural India (farmers)**.
- **Features** – **muscarinic (SLUDGE, bradycardia, bronchorrhoea, pin-point pupils)**, **nicotinic (fasciculations, weakness, paralysis – respiratory failure)**, CNS (confusion, seizures, coma); **↓ serum/ RBC cholinesterase**; **kerosene-like smell**.
- **Treatment** – **remove contaminated clothes, wash skin with soap & water** (staff wear gloves), airway, O₂, **ATROPINE (antidote of choice) – 1.8–3 mg IV bolus, double dose every 5 min until "atropinisation"** (**clear chest/ dry secretions (main endpoint), HR > 80, SBP > 80, dry axillae, pupils no longer pin-point**), then infusion; **Pralidoxime (2-PAM – oxime) – cholinesterase reactivator** (give early, before "ageing"); intubation/ ventilation; **intermediate syndrome** (24–96 hrs – proximal & respiratory weakness).
- **Carbamates** (reversible – propoxur, carbaryl) – atropine; oximes not needed generally.

## 3.3 Anticholinergic (Antimuscarinic) Drugs

| Drug | Uses |
|---|---|
| **Atropine** (from **Atropa belladonna**) | **Pre-anaesthetic (↓ secretions, prevent vagal bradycardia)**, **symptomatic sinus bradycardia/ heart block (0.5–1 mg IV; ACLS 1 mg every 3–5 min, max 3 mg)**, **OP poisoning**, mushroom poisoning, mydriatic/ cycloplegic (eye exam – refraction in children), with neostigmine |
| **Glycopyrrolate** (doesn't cross BBB) | Pre-anaesthetic, with neostigmine |
| **Hyoscine (scopolamine)** | **Motion sickness (transdermal patch)**; **hyoscine butylbromide (Buscopan) – abdominal colic** |
| **Ipratropium, tiotropium** (inhaled) | **COPD, asthma** |
| **Tropicamide, cyclopentolate, homatropine** | Mydriatic eye drops (shorter-acting) |
| **Trihexyphenidyl (benzhexol), benztropine** | **Parkinsonism, drug-induced EPS** |
| **Oxybutynin, tolterodine** | **Overactive bladder/ urge incontinence**, enuresis |
| **Dicyclomine** | Abdominal/ menstrual colic, infant colic |

- **Anticholinergic side effects/ toxicity** – **dry mouth (most common), blurred vision (cycloplegia), photophobia, mydriasis, tachycardia, urinary retention, constipation, hot dry flushed skin (hyperthermia – esp. children), confusion/ delirium, hallucinations**.
  - **Mnemonic** – "**Dry as a bone, red as a beet, hot as a hare, blind as a bat, mad as a hatter, full as a flask (bladder)**".
  - **Antidote – physostigmine**; supportive (cooling).
- **Contraindications** – **narrow-angle glaucoma (↑ IOP)**, **BPH/ urinary retention**, paralytic ileus, tachyarrhythmia, myasthenia.
- **Datura poisoning** – anticholinergic (seeds) – same features.

## 3.4 Adrenergic Agonists (Sympathomimetics)

| Drug | Receptor | Key uses & points |
|---|---|---|
| **Adrenaline (epinephrine)** | α, β1, β2 | **Anaphylaxis – DOC (0.5 mg IM 1:1000, thigh)**; **cardiac arrest (1 mg IV 1:10,000 every 3–5 min)**; with local anaesthetics (↓ absorption, prolongs action – **not in fingers, toes, ears, nose, penis – gangrene**); bronchospasm; haemostasis (topical) |
| **Noradrenaline (norepinephrine)** | α1 > β1 | **Vasopressor of choice in septic shock**; **central line preferred**; extravasation → necrosis → **phentolamine** local infiltration |
| **Dopamine** | Dose-dependent: **low (1–5 µg/kg/min) – D1 (renal/ mesenteric dilation)**, **medium (5–10) – β1 (inotropic)**, **high (> 10) – α1 (vasoconstriction)** | Cardiogenic/ septic shock, bradycardia |
| **Dobutamine** | **β1 (inotrope)** | **Cardiogenic shock, acute heart failure**, stress echo |
| **Isoprenaline** | β1 + β2 | Heart block, bradycardia (rare) |
| **Phenylephrine** | α1 | Hypotension during anaesthesia, **nasal decongestant**, mydriatic |
| **Ephedrine** | Mixed (indirect) | Hypotension in spinal anaesthesia; tachyphylaxis |
| **Salbutamol (albuterol), terbutaline** | **β2** | **Asthma (reliever – nebulised/ MDI)**, **tocolytic (terbutaline/ ritodrine)**, **hyperkalaemia** (shifts K into cells); side effects – **tremor (most common), tachycardia, palpitations, hypokalaemia**, hyperglycaemia |
| **Salmeterol, formoterol** | Long-acting β2 (LABA) | **Maintenance** with ICS (never alone in asthma) |
| **Clonidine** | **α2 agonist (central)** | HTN, opioid/ alcohol withdrawal, ADHD; **abrupt stop → rebound hypertension**; sedation, dry mouth |
| **Methyldopa** | α2 (central) | **HTN in pregnancy**; haemolytic anaemia, sedation |
| **Midodrine** | α1 | Orthostatic hypotension |

## 3.5 Adrenergic Blockers

### Alpha blockers
| Drug | Use / point |
|---|---|
| **Prazosin, terazosin, doxazosin** (α1) | HTN, **BPH**, scorpion sting (prazosin); **"first-dose phenomenon" – postural hypotension/ syncope → give first dose at bedtime, lying down** |
| **Tamsulosin** (α1A – prostate selective) | **BPH** (less hypotension); retrograde ejaculation |
| **Phentolamine, phenoxybenzamine** (non-selective) | **Pheochromocytoma**, extravasation of noradrenaline (phentolamine) |

### Beta blockers ("-olol")
| Type | Drugs |
|---|---|
| **Non-selective (β1 + β2)** | **Propranolol** (first; lipid-soluble – CNS), timolol (glaucoma eye drops), nadolol, sotalol |
| **Cardioselective (β1)** | **Atenolol, metoprolol, bisoprolol**, esmolol (ultra-short IV), nebivolol |
| **With α-blocking** | **Labetalol** (pregnancy HTN, hypertensive emergency), **carvedilol** (heart failure) |

- **Uses** – **hypertension, angina, post-MI (↓ mortality), arrhythmias (AF rate control), heart failure (carvedilol, metoprolol, bisoprolol)**, **thyrotoxicosis (symptom control)**, **migraine prophylaxis**, **essential tremor**, **anxiety (performance)**, **glaucoma (timolol)**, portal hypertension (propranolol – varices), hypertrophic cardiomyopathy.
- **Side effects** – **bradycardia, heart block, hypotension**, **bronchospasm (non-selective – contraindicated in asthma)**, **masks hypoglycaemia symptoms (tachycardia, tremor) in diabetics** (sweating persists), fatigue, cold extremities, **sexual dysfunction**, nightmares (propranolol), **worsens Raynaud's**; **abrupt withdrawal → rebound angina/ MI → taper**.
- **Contraindications** – **asthma/ COPD (non-selective)**, **bradycardia (< 50–60)**, **heart block**, decompensated HF, cardiogenic shock.
- **Nursing** – **check apical pulse & BP before giving – hold if HR < 60 or SBP < 90–100** (as per order), teach not to stop abruptly, monitor glucose in diabetics.
- **Beta-blocker overdose antidote – glucagon**.
