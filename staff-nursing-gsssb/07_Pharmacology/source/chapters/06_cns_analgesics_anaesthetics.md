## 6.1 Opioid Analgesics

- **Morphine** (prototype – from **opium poppy**; μ-receptor agonist).
  - Uses – **severe pain (MI, cancer, post-op, trauma)**, **acute pulmonary oedema (LVF)**, palliative dyspnoea, pre-anaesthetic.
  - **Effects/ side effects** – analgesia, euphoria, sedation, **respiratory depression (most dangerous – check RR before giving; withhold if RR < 12/min)**, **pin-point pupils (miosis)**, **constipation (most common – no tolerance develops)**, **nausea, vomiting**, **urinary retention**, **pruritus (histamine release)**, hypotension, bradycardia, ↑ ICP (due to CO₂ retention), cough suppression, **tolerance & dependence**.
  - **Contraindications/ caution** – **head injury (masks signs, ↑ ICP)**, **bronchial asthma/ COPD**, **acute abdomen (masks diagnosis)**, hypothyroidism, **biliary colic (spasm of sphincter of Oddi – pethidine preferred)**, liver disease, infants/ elderly, pregnancy near term (neonatal depression).
  - **Overdose triad – coma, respiratory depression, pin-point pupils** → **naloxone 0.4–2 mg IV** (short-acting – repeat/ infusion).
- **Other opioids** – **pethidine (meperidine)** (labour analgesia, **neonatal respiratory depression if within 2–4 hrs of delivery**, normeperidine seizures; avoid with MAOI), **fentanyl** (100 × morphine; **transdermal patch** – chronic pain; IV anaesthesia), **tramadol** (weak opioid + SNRI – **seizures, serotonin syndrome**), **codeine** (cough, mild pain), **buprenorphine** (partial agonist – OST, SL), **methadone** (OST), **pentazocine** (dysphoria), oxycodone, tapentadol.
- **Naloxone** (antagonist – overdose, **neonatal opioid depression 0.1 mg/kg**), **naltrexone** (oral – alcohol/ opioid dependence).
- **WHO analgesic ladder (cancer pain)** – **Step 1: non-opioid (paracetamol/ NSAID) ± adjuvant** → **Step 2: weak opioid (codeine, tramadol)** + non-opioid → **Step 3: strong opioid (morphine, fentanyl)** + non-opioid; **"by mouth, by the clock (regular), by the ladder, for the individual, attention to detail"**; adjuvants – antidepressants, anticonvulsants, steroids; **prophylactic laxative with opioids**.
- **PCA (patient-controlled analgesia)** – lockout interval prevents overdose; **only patient presses the button**.

## 6.2 Non-Opioid Analgesics

### NSAIDs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs)
- **Mechanism – inhibit cyclo-oxygenase (COX) → ↓ prostaglandins** → **analgesic, antipyretic, anti-inflammatory** (+ antiplatelet – aspirin).
- **Drugs** – **aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid – irreversible COX)**, **ibuprofen, diclofenac, naproxen, ketorolac, indomethacin, mefenamic acid (dysmenorrhoea)**, piroxicam; **selective COX-2 – celecoxib, etoricoxib** (less GI, **↑ CV risk – MI, stroke**).
- **Side effects** – **GI – gastritis, peptic ulcer, GI bleeding (most common)** → **give after food/ with PPI**; **renal (↓ GFR, Na & water retention, AKI – esp. elderly, dehydration)**; **↑ BP, ↓ efficacy of antihypertensives/ diuretics**; **bleeding**; **bronchospasm (aspirin-sensitive asthma)**; hepatotoxicity; **premature closure of ductus arteriosus (3rd trimester – avoid)**.
- **Aspirin special** – **Reye's syndrome (children < 16 yrs with viral illness – avoid)**; **salicylism (high dose) – tinnitus, deafness, vertigo, nausea**; **aspirin poisoning – respiratory alkalosis → metabolic acidosis, hyperthermia** – Rx **alkaline diuresis (sodium bicarbonate)**, haemodialysis; **low dose (75–150 mg) – antiplatelet**; stop 7 days before surgery.
- **Indomethacin** – PDA closure in preterm, gout; **ketorolac** – potent, short-term (≤ 5 days).

### Paracetamol (acetaminophen)
- **Analgesic & antipyretic, weak anti-inflammatory**; **safe in children, pregnancy, peptic ulcer, bleeding disorders, asthma** – **antipyretic of choice in children & dengue**.
- Dose – **adult 500–1000 mg every 4–6 hrs, max 4 g/day** (≤ 2–3 g in alcoholics/ liver disease); **child 10–15 mg/kg/dose**.
- **Toxicity (overdose > 150 mg/kg or > 7.5–10 g adult)** – toxic metabolite **NAPQI** depletes **glutathione → hepatic necrosis** (24–72 hrs); early asymptomatic/ nausea → **↑ liver enzymes, jaundice, liver failure (day 3–4)**; **Rumack-Matthew nomogram** (level at 4 hrs).
- **Antidote – N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** – most effective **within 8–10 hrs** (useful up to 24 hrs+); oral/ IV; **activated charcoal within 1 hr**.

## 6.3 Antiepileptics

| Drug | Use | Key side effects / nursing |
|---|---|---|
| **Phenytoin** | GTCS, focal, status epilepticus (after BZD) | **Gingival hyperplasia (oral hygiene)**, **hirsutism**, coarse facies, **nystagmus, ataxia, diplopia (toxicity)**, megaloblastic anaemia (folate), osteomalacia, **SJS**, teratogenic (fetal hydantoin syndrome); **therapeutic 10–20 µg/ml; zero-order kinetics**; **IV only in NORMAL SALINE (precipitates in dextrose), ≤ 50 mg/min (adult), flush before/ after, cardiac monitoring (hypotension, arrhythmia)**; **purple glove syndrome**; enzyme inducer; **never IM** |
| **Fosphenytoin** | Prodrug – IV/ IM | Safer |
| **Carbamazepine** | **Focal seizures (DOC)**, **trigeminal neuralgia (DOC)**, bipolar | **Agranulocytosis/ aplastic anaemia**, hyponatraemia (SIADH), **SJS (HLA-B*1502 – Asians)**, diplopia, enzyme inducer (OCP failure) |
| **Sodium valproate** | **Broad spectrum – GTCS, absence, myoclonic**, bipolar | **Hepatotoxicity (children < 2 yrs)**, **pancreatitis**, weight gain, **hair loss**, tremor, thrombocytopenia, **NTD (most teratogenic – avoid in pregnancy)**, PCOS; enzyme inhibitor |
| **Ethosuximide** | **Absence seizures (petit mal) DOC** | GI, drowsiness |
| **Phenobarbitone** | **Neonatal seizures (DOC)**, status | **Sedation**, hyperactivity in children, dependence, enzyme inducer |
| **Lamotrigine** | Broad, bipolar depression | **SJS rash – slow titration** |
| **Levetiracetam** | Broad – safe, few interactions; pregnancy | Behavioural changes, irritability |
| **Topiramate** | Broad, migraine | Weight loss, kidney stones, cognitive slowing |
| **Clonazepam, clobazam** | Myoclonic, adjunct | Sedation |
| **Gabapentin, pregabalin** | Neuropathic pain, focal | Dizziness |
| **Vigabatrin / ACTH** | **Infantile spasms** | Visual field defects (vigabatrin) |
| **Magnesium sulphate** | **Eclampsia (DOC)** | Knee jerk, RR, urine output |

- **Status epilepticus** – **IV lorazepam 4 mg (0.1 mg/kg) / diazepam 10 mg / IM/ buccal midazolam** → **phenytoin/ fosphenytoin 20 mg/kg, valproate, levetiracetam** → phenobarbitone → anaesthesia (midazolam/ propofol/ thiopentone infusion).
- **Teaching** – **never stop abruptly (status)**, regular timing, **drug levels**, avoid alcohol, **don't drive** until seizure-free (as per law), medical ID, **contraception (enzyme inducers ↓ OCP effect)**, **folic acid 5 mg before & during pregnancy**, report rash/ sore throat/ bleeding.

## 6.4 Antiparkinson Drugs

- **Parkinson's** – **↓ dopamine (substantia nigra)**, relative ↑ ACh.

| Drug | Points |
|---|---|
| **Levodopa + carbidopa (Syndopa)** | **Most effective**; levodopa – dopamine precursor (crosses BBB); **carbidopa – peripheral decarboxylase inhibitor (↓ dose, ↓ nausea)**; side effects – **nausea, postural hypotension, dyskinesias, "on-off" phenomenon, "wearing off", hallucinations, dark urine/ sweat**; **avoid high-protein meals with dose** (↓ absorption); **pyridoxine (B6) ↓ effect of levodopa alone** (not with carbidopa); **MAOI-A → hypertensive crisis**; don't stop abruptly (NMS-like) |
| **Dopamine agonists** | **Pramipexole, ropinirole, bromocriptine** – impulse control disorders, sleep attacks |
| **MAO-B inhibitors** | **Selegiline, rasagiline** |
| **COMT inhibitors** | Entacapone |
| **Anticholinergics** | **Trihexyphenidyl, benztropine** – tremor, **drug-induced parkinsonism**; avoid elderly (confusion) |
| **Amantadine** | Dyskinesia; also antiviral |

## 6.5 Sedative-Hypnotics & Psychotropics (summary)

- **Benzodiazepines** – **diazepam, lorazepam, alprazolam, midazolam (IV sedation, short-acting), chlordiazepoxide, clonazepam, nitrazepam** – anxiety, insomnia, seizures, alcohol withdrawal, pre-medication, muscle spasm; **sedation, respiratory depression (with alcohol/ opioids), dependence, paradoxical agitation (elderly), falls** – **antidote flumazenil**.
- **Z-drugs (zolpidem, zopiclone)** – insomnia; **barbiturates** – largely obsolete (phenobarbitone, thiopentone).
- **Antipsychotics, antidepressants, lithium** – see Mental Health notes (EPS, NMS, lithium 0.6–1.2 mEq/L, MAOI cheese reaction).

## 6.6 Anaesthetics

### General anaesthesia (GA)
- **Stages of ether anaesthesia (Guedel)** – **I analgesia**, **II excitement/ delirium (dangerous)**, **III surgical anaesthesia (4 planes)**, **IV medullary paralysis (respiratory arrest)**.
- **Components** – unconsciousness, analgesia, amnesia, muscle relaxation, loss of reflexes.

| Agent | Points |
|---|---|
| **Thiopentone sodium** (IV barbiturate) | **Rapid induction (ultra-short – redistribution)**; laryngospasm, hypotension; **intra-arterial injection → gangrene**; extravasation – necrosis; contraindicated – porphyria |
| **Propofol** | **Most common IV induction**; milky white (**soya/ egg lecithin**); rapid smooth recovery, antiemetic; **pain on injection, hypotension**; **propofol infusion syndrome**; ICU sedation |
| **Ketamine** | **Dissociative anaesthesia** (eyes open, analgesia); **↑ BP & HR (good in shock/ hypovolaemia)**, **bronchodilator (asthma)**; **emergence delirium/ hallucinations (give BZD)**, ↑ ICP, ↑ secretions |
| **Etomidate** | Cardiovascular stable; adrenal suppression |
| **Midazolam** | Sedation, amnesia |
| **Nitrous oxide (N₂O – "laughing gas")** | Weak anaesthetic, good analgesic; **Entonox (50 % N₂O + 50 % O₂) – labour**; **diffusion hypoxia** at end (give 100 % O₂); B12 inactivation |
| **Halothane** | **Halothane hepatitis**, arrhythmias (sensitises heart to adrenaline), uterine relaxation |
| **Isoflurane, sevoflurane, desflurane** | **Sevoflurane – inhalational induction in children (pleasant, non-irritant)**; desflurane – rapid recovery, airway irritation |
| **Ether** (historical) | Explosive, irritant – **first public demonstration – William Morton, 16 Oct 1846 (Ether Day)** |

- **Pre-anaesthetic medication** – **anxiolysis (BZD), ↓ secretions (atropine/ glycopyrrolate), analgesia (opioids), antiemetic (ondansetron), ↓ gastric acid (H2 blocker/ PPI, metoclopramide)** – prevents **Mendelson's syndrome (aspiration)**.

### Neuromuscular blockers (muscle relaxants)
| Type | Drugs | Points |
|---|---|---|
| **Depolarising** | **Succinylcholine (suxamethonium)** | **Rapid onset (~1 min), short duration (5–10 min) – rapid sequence intubation, ECT**; **fasciculations, post-op myalgia, hyperkalaemia (burns, crush, paraplegia – avoid)**, **malignant hyperthermia trigger**, ↑ IOP/ ICP; **prolonged apnoea in pseudocholinesterase deficiency**; **not reversed by neostigmine**; **refrigerate** |
| **Non-depolarising (competitive)** | **Atracurium (Hofmann elimination – renal/ hepatic failure), vecuronium, rocuronium**, pancuronium, cisatracurium | **Reversed by neostigmine + glycopyrrolate/ atropine**; **sugammadex – reverses rocuronium/ vecuronium** |
| **Centrally acting muscle relaxants** | **Baclofen, tizanidine, diazepam, chlorzoxazone** | Spasticity, back spasm |
| **Dantrolene** | Direct (ryanodine receptor) | **Malignant hyperthermia (DOC)**, NMS, spasticity |

- **Malignant hyperthermia** – **genetic (autosomal dominant – RYR1)**, triggered by **volatile anaesthetics (halothane) + succinylcholine** → **↑ EtCO₂ (earliest sign), masseter spasm, rigidity, tachycardia, hyperthermia (late), hyperkalaemia, acidosis, rhabdomyolysis** → **stop trigger, 100 % O₂, IV dantrolene 2.5 mg/kg**, cooling, treat hyperkalaemia.

### Local anaesthetics
| Drug | Points |
|---|---|
| **Lignocaine (lidocaine)** | **Most widely used**; surface (2–4 %), infiltration (**1 %**), nerve block, **spinal (5 % heavy)**, antiarrhythmic (IB); **max dose 3 mg/kg (4.5 mg/kg) plain, 7 mg/kg with adrenaline** |
| **Bupivacaine** | **Long-acting**; spinal (0.5 % heavy), epidural; **most cardiotoxic – never IV** |
| **Ropivacaine** | Less cardiotoxic |
| **Prilocaine** | **Methaemoglobinaemia** (Rx methylene blue) |
| **EMLA cream (lidocaine + prilocaine)** | **Topical before venipuncture (apply 60 min, occlusive dressing)** |
| **Cocaine** | Topical (nasal) – vasoconstrictor; abuse |
| **Benzocaine** | Topical |

- **Adrenaline added** – ↓ systemic absorption, prolongs action, ↓ bleeding; **avoid in end-arteries – fingers, toes, ear, nose, penis (gangrene)**.
- **LA systemic toxicity (LAST)** – **circumoral numbness, tingling, metallic taste, tinnitus, slurred speech → seizures → CNS depression, arrhythmia, cardiac arrest** → **stop injection, airway, BZD for seizures, 20 % lipid emulsion (Intralipid)**.
- **Spinal anaesthesia** – complications – **hypotension (most common – preload fluids, vasopressor)**, **post-dural puncture headache (lie flat, hydration, caffeine, epidural blood patch)**, urinary retention, high spinal; **site L3–L4/ L4–L5**.

## 6.7 Other CNS Drugs

- **Migraine** – **acute: paracetamol/ NSAIDs, triptans (sumatriptan – contraindicated in CAD)**, ergotamine (vasospasm); **prophylaxis: propranolol, flunarizine, amitriptyline, topiramate, valproate**.
- **Myasthenia** – pyridostigmine.
- **Vertigo** – betahistine, cinnarizine, prochlorperazine.
- **Dementia** – donepezil, memantine.
- **ADHD** – methylphenidate; **narcolepsy** – modafinil.
- **CNS stimulants** – caffeine (apnoea of prematurity – caffeine citrate), amphetamines.
