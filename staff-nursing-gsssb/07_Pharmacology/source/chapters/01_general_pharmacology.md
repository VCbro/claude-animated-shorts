## 1.1 Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Pharmacology** | Science of drugs – their sources, properties, actions & uses |
| **Drug (WHO)** | Any substance used to **modify or explore physiological systems or pathological states** for the benefit of the recipient |
| **Pharmacokinetics** | **What the body does to the drug** – absorption, distribution, metabolism, excretion (**ADME**) |
| **Pharmacodynamics** | **What the drug does to the body** – mechanism & effects |
| **Pharmacotherapeutics** | Use of drugs in treatment |
| **Pharmacy** | Art & science of preparing & dispensing drugs |
| **Toxicology** | Study of poisons |
| **Pharmacovigilance** | Detection, assessment & prevention of **adverse drug reactions (ADRs)** |
| **Pharmacogenomics** | Genetic basis of variation in drug response |
| **Posology** | Study of **dosage** |
| **Chemotherapy** | Drugs against microbes/ cancer cells |
| **Placebo** | Inert substance (**dummy medication**) – psychological effect |
| **Orphan drug** | Drug for rare disease |
| **Prodrug** | Inactive until metabolised (e.g. **levodopa, enalapril, prednisone**) |

- **Father of pharmacology – Oswald Schmiedeberg**; **father of Indian pharmacology – Col. R. N. Chopra**.
- **Sources** – plants (**morphine – opium poppy, atropine – belladonna, digoxin – foxglove (Digitalis lanata), quinine – cinchona, reserpine – Rauwolfia**), animals (insulin earlier, heparin), minerals (iron, MgSO₄), microbes (**penicillin – Penicillium notatum, Fleming 1928**), synthetic, **biotechnology (recombinant insulin, vaccines)**.
- **Drug names** – **chemical**, **generic/ non-proprietary (official – e.g. paracetamol)**, **brand/ proprietary (trade – e.g. Crocin)**. **Prescribe by generic name** (cheaper – Jan Aushadhi).
- **Official reference** – **Indian Pharmacopoeia (IP)** – first 1955; by **Indian Pharmacopoeia Commission, Ghaziabad**.

## 1.2 Routes of Administration

| Route | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Oral (PO)** | **Safest, most convenient, economical** | Slow, **first-pass metabolism**, GI irritation, not in vomiting/ unconscious |
| **Sublingual (SL)/ buccal** | **Rapid, bypasses first-pass liver metabolism** (nitroglycerin, buprenorphine) | Only small potent drugs |
| **Rectal** | Vomiting/ unconscious patients; partly avoids first-pass | Irregular absorption |
| **Intravenous (IV)** | **Fastest onset, 100 % bioavailability**, large volumes, irritants (diluted) | Most dangerous (no recall), phlebitis, embolism, infection |
| **Intramuscular (IM)** | Depot preparations, moderate volume | Painful, nerve injury, abscess |
| **Subcutaneous (SC)** | Slow sustained absorption (insulin, heparin), self-administration | Small volumes (≤ 1–2 ml) |
| **Intradermal (ID)** | **BCG, Mantoux, sensitivity (test) doses** – 0.1 ml at 10–15° | – |
| **Inhalation** | Rapid local action (asthma), anaesthetics | Technique-dependent |
| **Transdermal** (patches – nitroglycerin, fentanyl, nicotine, hyoscine) | Steady levels, avoids first-pass | Skin irritation |
| **Topical, intrathecal, intra-articular, intraosseous, epidural** | Local/ special | – |

## 1.3 Pharmacokinetics (ADME)

- **Absorption** – movement from site of administration into blood; mostly **passive diffusion**; factors – lipid solubility, ionisation (**weak acids absorbed in stomach – aspirin; weak bases in intestine**), surface area (small intestine – largest), blood flow, food, formulation, **first-pass (presystemic) metabolism** in gut wall & liver (**high in oral nitroglycerin, lignocaine, propranolol, morphine, testosterone**).
- **Bioavailability** – fraction of drug reaching systemic circulation unchanged; **IV = 100 %**.
- **Distribution** – **volume of distribution (Vd)**; **plasma protein binding (albumin – acidic drugs: warfarin, phenytoin, sulfonamides)** – bound drug inactive (reservoir); displacement → toxicity (**sulfonamides displace bilirubin → kernicterus in neonates**); barriers – **blood-brain barrier, placental barrier** (lipid-soluble drugs cross).
- **Metabolism (biotransformation)** – mainly **liver**; **Phase I** (oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis – **cytochrome P450**), **Phase II** (conjugation – glucuronidation, acetylation (**INH – slow & fast acetylators**), sulphation).
  - **Enzyme inducers** (↓ effect of other drugs) – **rifampicin, phenytoin, carbamazepine, phenobarbitone, griseofulvin, chronic alcohol, smoking** (e.g. rifampicin → **OCP failure**).
  - **Enzyme inhibitors** (↑ toxicity) – **erythromycin, clarithromycin, ketoconazole, cimetidine, ciprofloxacin, valproate, isoniazid, grapefruit juice, acute alcohol**.
- **Excretion** – mainly **kidney (urine)**; also bile/ faeces, lungs (anaesthetics, alcohol), sweat, saliva, **breast milk** (relevant in lactation); **alkalinisation of urine ↑ excretion of acidic drugs (aspirin, phenobarbitone poisoning)**.
- **Kinetics** – **first-order** (constant **fraction** eliminated per unit time – most drugs) vs **zero-order (saturation)** (constant **amount** – **phenytoin, alcohol, high-dose aspirin, theophylline**) → small dose ↑ causes big level ↑.
- **Plasma half-life (t½)** – time for plasma concentration to fall to **half**; **steady state reached after ~4–5 half-lives**; drug **eliminated (~97 %) after 4–5 half-lives**; **loading dose** to reach target level quickly (long t½ – digoxin, amiodarone); **maintenance dose** to keep it.
- **Therapeutic drug monitoring (TDM)** – narrow therapeutic index drugs – **digoxin, lithium, phenytoin, theophylline, aminoglycosides, vancomycin, cyclosporine, warfarin (INR)**.

## 1.4 Pharmacodynamics

- **Receptor** – macromolecule to which drug binds to produce effect; types – **G-protein coupled (most common)**, ion channels, enzyme-linked, nuclear (steroids, thyroid).
- **Agonist** (binds & activates – affinity + intrinsic activity), **antagonist** (binds, no activity – blocks agonist; **competitive** – surmountable (atropine), **non-competitive**), **partial agonist** (buprenorphine, aripiprazole), **inverse agonist**.
- **Potency** (dose needed) vs **efficacy** (maximum effect – more important clinically).
- **Therapeutic index (TI) = LD₅₀ / ED₅₀** (or TD₅₀/ED₅₀) – **margin of safety**; **narrow TI → TDM needed**.
- **Synergism** – combined effect > sum (**sulfamethoxazole + trimethoprim**; additive – sum); **potentiation** (one drug ↑ effect of other – **levodopa + carbidopa**, **probenecid + penicillin**); **antagonism** – physical, chemical (**protamine–heparin**, chelators), physiological (**adrenaline vs histamine**), receptor.
- **Tolerance** (↑ dose needed – opioids, nitrates), **tachyphylaxis** (rapid tolerance – ephedrine, nitrates), **cross-tolerance**, **dependence**.

## 1.5 Adverse Drug Reactions (ADR)

| Type | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Side effect** | Unwanted but **expected** pharmacological effect at therapeutic dose | Dry mouth with atropine, sedation with antihistamines |
| **Toxic effect** | Due to overdose/ accumulation | Paracetamol hepatotoxicity, digoxin toxicity |
| **Type A (Augmented)** | **Dose-related, predictable, common (~80 %)** | Hypoglycaemia with insulin, bleeding with warfarin |
| **Type B (Bizarre)** | **Not dose-related, unpredictable** – allergy, idiosyncrasy | Anaphylaxis to penicillin |
| **Idiosyncrasy** | Genetically determined abnormal reaction | **Haemolysis with primaquine in G6PD deficiency**, succinylcholine apnoea (pseudocholinesterase deficiency), **malignant hyperthermia** |
| **Drug allergy (hypersensitivity)** | Immunological | **Type I (anaphylaxis – IgE)**, II (cytotoxic – haemolytic anaemia), III (immune complex – serum sickness), IV (delayed – contact dermatitis) |
| **Iatrogenic** | Physician/ treatment induced | Drug-induced Parkinsonism |
| **Teratogenic** | Fetal malformation | **Thalidomide – phocomelia**, warfarin, isotretinoin, valproate |
| **Carcinogenic/ mutagenic** | Cancer | Anticancer drugs, oestrogens |
| **Withdrawal/ rebound** | On stopping | **Clonidine – rebound HTN**, β-blockers – angina, steroids – adrenal crisis |
| **Drug dependence** | – | Opioids, BZD |
| **Photosensitivity** | – | **Tetracyclines (doxycycline), sulfonamides, chlorpromazine, amiodarone** |
| **Cumulation** | Slow elimination | Digoxin |

- **Anaphylaxis** – most serious type I reaction – **adrenaline 0.5 mg IM (0.5 ml of 1:1000) – drug of choice**, repeat 5–15 min; O₂, IV fluids, antihistamine, hydrocortisone (adjuncts). **Test dose** before penicillin/ ASV (as per protocol); **observe 30 min**.
- **SJS/ TEN** – sulfonamides, carbamazepine, phenytoin, lamotrigine, allopurinol, nevirapine.
- **Pharmacovigilance Programme of India (PvPI) – 2010** (IPC, Ghaziabad) – **"Suspected ADR Reporting Form"** – **any health professional (including nurses) can report**; helpline **1800-180-3024**; **ADR monitoring centres (AMCs)**.

## 1.6 Drug Laws & Schedules

| Law/ Schedule | Key point |
|---|---|
| **Drugs & Cosmetics Act 1940, Rules 1945** | Regulates import, manufacture, sale; **CDSCO** – national regulator; **DCGI** heads |
| **Pharmacy Act 1948** | Pharmacist registration; **Pharmacy Council of India** |
| **NDPS Act 1985** | Narcotic drugs & psychotropic substances |
| **Schedule G** | "Caution – to be taken under medical supervision" (e.g. antihistamines, hypoglycaemics) |
| **Schedule H** | **Prescription-only** drugs ("Rx" – red label) |
| **Schedule H1 (2013)** | **3rd/ 4th-gen antibiotics, anti-TB, habit-forming drugs** – separate **register maintained for 3 years** |
| **Schedule X** | **Narcotics & psychotropics** (e.g. certain sedatives) – **prescription in duplicate**, records kept 2 yrs, special licence |
| **Schedule C/ C1** | Biological products (vaccines, sera, insulin) |
| **Schedule M** | Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP) |
| **Schedule Y** | Clinical trials (now New Drugs & Clinical Trials Rules 2019) |
| **Schedule K** | Exempted drugs |
| **Drugs & Magic Remedies Act 1954** | Prohibits misleading advertisements |
| **Drug Price Control Order (DPCO 2013)/ NPPA** | Price regulation |

- **Essential medicines** – WHO concept (**1977**); **National List of Essential Medicines (NLEM) 2022 – 384 medicines**; **Pradhan Mantri Bhartiya Janaushadhi Pariyojana (PMBJP, 2008)** – generic medicines at Jan Aushadhi Kendras.
- **Over-the-counter (OTC)** – no prescription (paracetamol, ORS, antacids).
