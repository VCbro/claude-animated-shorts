## 7.1 Applied Anatomy

@fig gi_tract.svg | GI tract ke organs aur important landmarks

:::key Digestion – quick facts
- GI tract length ~**9 m** (mouth → anus). Layers: **mucosa → submucosa → muscularis → serosa**.
- **Saliva**: 1–1.5 L/day, **ptyalin (salivary amylase)** – starch digestion starts in mouth.
- **Stomach**: HCl (parietal cells) – also **intrinsic factor** (B12 absorption); **pepsinogen** (chief cells); **gastrin** (G cells). Capacity ~1.5 L. Gastric emptying 2–6 hrs.
- **Small intestine** (~6 m): duodenum (25 cm, C-shaped) → jejunum → ileum. **Main site of digestion & absorption**. **B12 & bile salts absorbed in terminal ileum**. Iron absorbed in **duodenum**.
- **Large intestine** (~1.5 m): caecum, appendix, colon (ascending, transverse, descending, sigmoid), rectum, anus → **water & electrolyte absorption**, Vit K synthesis by bacteria.
- **Pancreas**: exocrine (trypsin, lipase, amylase) + endocrine (insulin, glucagon).
:::

### Abdominal quadrants & regions
| Quadrant | Organs |
|---|---|
| **RUQ** | Liver, gallbladder, duodenum, head of pancreas, right kidney |
| **LUQ** | Stomach, spleen, body/tail of pancreas, left kidney |
| **RLQ** | **Appendix**, caecum, right ovary/tube |
| **LLQ** | Sigmoid colon, left ovary/tube |

- 9 regions: Right/left hypochondriac, **epigastric**, right/left lumbar, **umbilical**, right/left iliac (inguinal), **hypogastric (suprapubic)**.
- Bowel sounds: normal **5–30/min** (5–35); absent = **no sound in 3–5 min** (listen all 4 quadrants) → paralytic ileus/peritonitis; **high-pitched tinkling** = early obstruction.

### GI investigations – nursing points
- **Barium swallow / meal** (upper GI) – NPO 6–8 hrs; after: **fluids & laxative** (barium → constipation, **white chalky stool for 24–72 hrs**).
- **Barium enema** (lower GI) – clear liquid diet + bowel prep; position **left lateral/Sims**.
- **Upper GI endoscopy (OGD)** – NPO 6–8 hrs, local throat spray; after: **NPO till gag reflex returns**, watch for perforation (pain, fever, bleeding).
- **Colonoscopy** – clear liquid diet 1–2 days, **bowel prep (PEG solution)** till clear yellow output; left lateral with knees flexed; after: watch bleeding, perforation; flatus normal.
- **Stool for occult blood (guaiac)** – avoid red meat, iron, vitamin C, NSAIDs 3 days before.
- **Paracentesis** – **empty bladder first** (bladder puncture prevention), sitting upright/high Fowler's; measure abdominal girth & weight before & after; max **4–5 L** at a time (albumin for large volume).
- **Liver biopsy** – check **PT/INR & platelets**, vitamin K before; position **supine with right arm above head**, patient **holds breath after exhalation** during insertion; after: **right lateral position for 2–3 hrs** with pillow under costal margin.

## 7.2 Disorders of Mouth & Oesophagus

- **Stomatitis** – mouth inflammation; soft bland diet, saline rinse, avoid hot/spicy; chemo patients – soft brush.
- **Oral cancer** – most common in India (male), **tobacco chewing** (gutkha), leukoplakia (white patch – premalignant), **erythroplakia (red patch – more malignant)**.
- **Achalasia** – LES fails to relax → **dysphagia to both solids & liquids**, regurgitation; "bird's beak" on barium; Rx: pneumatic dilatation, **Heller's myotomy**.
- **Oesophageal cancer** – progressive **dysphagia (solids → liquids)**, weight loss; risk: smoking, alcohol, Barrett's oesophagus (adenocarcinoma).

### GERD (Gastro-Oesophageal Reflux Disease)
- **LES weakness** → acid reflux into oesophagus. Risk: obesity, pregnancy, smoking, fatty food, caffeine, chocolate, alcohol, hiatus hernia.
- S/S: **heartburn (pyrosis)** – worse after meals & lying down, regurgitation, dysphagia, chronic cough. Complication: **Barrett's oesophagus** (premalignant – squamous → columnar).
- Rx: **PPIs (omeprazole, pantoprazole – 30 min before breakfast)**, H₂ blockers (ranitidine/famotidine), antacids, prokinetics.

:::key GERD / Hiatus hernia – lifestyle teaching
- **Small frequent meals**; avoid eating **2–3 hrs before bedtime**
- **Elevate head of bed 15–20 cm (6–8 inches)** – blocks under head legs
- Avoid fatty food, chocolate, peppermint, caffeine, alcohol, spicy/acidic foods, smoking
- Weight loss; avoid tight clothes & bending/lifting after meals
- Remain **upright 1–2 hrs after meals**
:::

- **Hiatus hernia** – stomach part diaphragm ke upar chest me; **sliding (most common)** vs paraesophageal (rolling). Same management as GERD; surgery – **Nissen fundoplication**.

## 7.3 Gastritis & Peptic Ulcer Disease

- **Gastritis** – acute (NSAIDs, alcohol, stress) / chronic (**H. pylori**, autoimmune – pernicious anaemia).
- **Peptic ulcer** – erosion of mucosa in stomach/duodenum. Most common cause: ***Helicobacter pylori*** (gram –ve, spiral; produces urease); 2nd: **NSAIDs**. Others: Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (gastrin-secreting tumour), stress (**Curling's ulcer – burns**; **Cushing's ulcer – head injury**).

| Feature | **Gastric ulcer** | **Duodenal ulcer** (more common, ~80 %) |
|---|---|---|
| Age | > 50 yrs | 30–60 yrs |
| Pain timing | **½–1 hr after meal**; food **worsens** pain | **2–3 hrs after meal & at night (midnight–3 am)**; food **relieves** pain |
| Acid | Normal / low | **High** |
| Weight | **Weight loss** | Weight gain (eats to relieve pain) |
| Vomiting | Common | Uncommon |
| Malignancy | Possible | Rare |
| Bleeding | Haematemesis more | **Melena** more |

:::mnemo G vs D
**G**astric = **G**ets worse with food (pain soon after eating).
**D**uodenal = **D**ecreases with food (pain when stomach empty / night).
:::

- Diagnosis: **upper GI endoscopy with biopsy** (gold standard), **H. pylori tests** – **urea breath test**, stool antigen, rapid urease (CLO) test, serology.
- **H. pylori triple therapy** (14 days): **PPI + Clarithromycin + Amoxicillin** (or Metronidazole); quadruple – PPI + bismuth + metronidazole + tetracycline.
- Drugs: PPIs, H₂ blockers, **Sucralfate** (coats ulcer – **give 1 hr before meals**, separate from other drugs by 2 hrs), **Misoprostol** (NSAID-induced; **contraindicated in pregnancy**), antacids (1–3 hrs after meals & HS; **Al – constipation, Mg – diarrhoea**).
- Diet: regular meals, avoid foods that cause pain, **avoid alcohol, smoking, caffeine, NSAIDs**; bland diet & milk **no longer recommended** (milk → rebound acid).

### Complications of peptic ulcer
- **Haemorrhage** (most common) – haematemesis (**coffee-ground vomitus**), melena (**black tarry stool** – needs ~60 ml blood in upper GI). Rx: 2 large bore IV, blood, **NG lavage (room temperature saline)**, IV PPI, endoscopic therapy.
- **Perforation** (most serious) – **sudden severe "knife-like" epigastric pain spreading to shoulder (referred)**, **board-like rigid abdomen**, rebound tenderness, absent bowel sounds, shock; X-ray – **air under diaphragm**. Rx: NPO, NG suction, IV fluids, antibiotics, **emergency surgery**.
- **Pyloric (gastric outlet) obstruction** – projectile vomiting of undigested food, visible peristalsis, succussion splash.

### Gastric surgery & Dumping syndrome
- Surgeries: **Billroth I** (gastroduodenostomy), **Billroth II** (gastrojejunostomy), vagotomy, pyloroplasty, total gastrectomy.
- **Dumping syndrome** – food (hypertonic chyme) rapidly enters jejunum.
 - **Early (10–30 min)**: fluid shift → dizziness, sweating, palpitation, tachycardia, cramps, diarrhoea, fainting.
 - **Late (1.5–3 hr)**: reactive **hypoglycaemia**.

:::tip Dumping syndrome diet – "Dry, Lie, Protein-fat high"
- **Small frequent meals**, **high protein, moderate fat, LOW carbohydrate** (no simple sugars)
- **No fluids with meals** – drink **30–60 min before/after** meals
- **Lie down (recumbent/semi-recumbent) 20–30 min after meals**
- Total gastrectomy → lifelong **Vitamin B12 injections** (no intrinsic factor → pernicious anaemia)
:::

- **Gastric cancer** – risk: H. pylori, smoked/salted foods, nitrates; **Virchow's node** (left supraclavicular), **Krukenberg tumour** (ovary metastasis), Sister Mary Joseph nodule (umbilicus).

## 7.4 Upper vs Lower GI Bleeding
| | Upper GI (above ligament of Treitz) | Lower GI |
|---|---|---|
| Causes | Peptic ulcer (most common), **oesophageal varices**, Mallory-Weiss tear (vomiting) | Diverticulosis, haemorrhoids, colorectal cancer, IBD |
| Signs | **Haematemesis, melena** | **Haematochezia** (bright red blood per rectum) |

## 7.5 Appendicitis

- Inflammation of **vermiform appendix** (obstruction by **faecalith** most common); age 10–30 yrs; most common surgical emergency.
- S/S: **periumbilical pain → shifts to RLQ (McBurney's point)** within hours, anorexia (almost always), nausea/vomiting, low-grade fever, ↑WBC.

:::exam Signs of appendicitis
- **McBurney's point tenderness** – 1/3 distance from **ASIS to umbilicus** (from ASIS side; i.e. junction of lateral 1/3 & medial 2/3)
- **Rovsing's sign** – pressure on **LLQ → pain in RLQ**
- **Rebound tenderness (Blumberg's sign)** – peritoneal irritation
- **Psoas sign** – hip extension se pain (retrocaecal)
- **Obturator sign** – internal rotation of flexed hip se pain (pelvic appendix)
- **Sudden relief of pain = possible perforation (danger!)** → peritonitis
:::

:::warn Suspected appendicitis me NEVER
- **No heat (hot pad)** on abdomen → rupture
- **No laxatives / enema** → perforation
- **No pain killers (strong)** before diagnosis (mask symptoms) – as per doctor
- Keep **NPO**, IV fluids, ice pack may be used; position **semi-Fowler's / right side-lying with knees flexed**
:::

- Rx: **Appendectomy** (laparoscopic). Perforation → peritonitis → drain; position **Fowler's** (localize infection in pelvis).

## 7.6 Peritonitis
- Inflammation of peritoneum – most common cause: **perforated appendix / peptic ulcer**; E. coli.
- S/S: severe abdominal pain, **rigid board-like abdomen**, rebound tenderness, **absent bowel sounds**, fever, tachycardia, shock; patient lies **still with knees flexed**.
- Rx: NPO, **NG suction**, IV fluids & antibiotics, analgesics, **semi-Fowler's**, surgery.

## 7.7 Inflammatory Bowel Disease (IBD)

| Feature | **Crohn's disease** | **Ulcerative colitis** |
|---|---|---|
| Area | **Mouth to anus** (any part), mostly **terminal ileum** | **Colon & rectum only** (starts in rectum, goes up) |
| Pattern | **Skip lesions**, transmural (full thickness) | **Continuous**, mucosal only |
| Stool | 3–5/day, **non-bloody**, fatty (steatorrhoea) | **10–20/day, bloody diarrhoea with mucus** |
| Look | **Cobblestone appearance**, fistulas, strictures, abscess | Pseudopolyps, **"lead-pipe" colon** |
| Complications | Fistula, obstruction, malabsorption (B12) | **Toxic megacolon**, haemorrhage, **colon cancer risk ↑** |
| Surgery | Not curative (recurs) | **Total colectomy = curative** |
| Smoking | Worsens | (Protective) |

- Rx: **5-ASA (sulfasalazine/mesalamine)**, corticosteroids, immunosuppressants (azathioprine), biologics (infliximab); **low-residue, high-protein, high-calorie diet** during flare; avoid milk (lactose), caffeine, raw fruits; TPN for bowel rest.
- **Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS)** – functional; alternating diarrhoea/constipation, pain relieved by defecation; no structural damage; stress-related; high-fibre diet.
- **Diverticulosis** – sac-like pouches (sigmoid), low-fibre diet; **Diverticulitis** – inflamed → **LLQ pain**, fever ("left-sided appendicitis"). Acute phase: NPO/clear liquids, antibiotics; later **high-fibre diet**; avoid straining.

## 7.8 Intestinal Obstruction

| | **Mechanical** | **Paralytic (Adynamic) ileus** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Adhesions (most common in small bowel)**, hernia (strangulated), volvulus, intussusception, tumour (**most common large bowel**) | **Post-abdominal surgery (most common)**, hypokalaemia, peritonitis, opioids |
| Bowel sounds | **High-pitched, hyperactive** above obstruction (early), later absent | **Absent / hypoactive** |

- **Small bowel obstruction**: early **profuse vomiting (faecal vomiting later)**, colicky pain, mild distension, dehydration, **metabolic alkalosis**.
- **Large bowel obstruction**: **marked distension**, constipation/**obstipation** (no flatus), vomiting late, cramping lower abdominal pain.
- Rx: **NPO, NG tube decompression (Ryle's)**, IV fluids & electrolytes, **I/O chart**, abdominal girth measurement, surgery if strangulation.
- **Volvulus** – twisting (sigmoid – elderly); **Intussusception** – telescoping (infants – **red currant jelly stool**, sausage-shaped mass).

## 7.9 Hernia

- Protrusion of organ through weak muscle wall.
- **Inguinal** (most common overall) – **Indirect** (most common; through inguinal ring – young males, congenital) / Direct (through posterior wall – elderly); **Femoral** (more in females); **Umbilical**; **Incisional** (surgical scar); Hiatal.
- **Reducible** → **Irreducible (incarcerated)** → **Strangulated** (blood supply cut – **emergency**: severe pain, vomiting, tender, red mass, fever).
- Pre-op: avoid straining, **truss** (reducible only). Post-op herniorrhaphy: **avoid coughing** (deep breathing ok – **splint incision**), **scrotal support & ice bag** (scrotal swelling), avoid heavy lifting **6 weeks**, stool softeners.

## 7.10 Haemorrhoids, Fissure & Fistula
- **Haemorrhoids (piles)** – dilated veins of anal canal; **internal (painless bright red bleeding)** above dentate line; external (painful, thrombosed). Causes: constipation, straining, pregnancy, portal HTN. Rx: **high-fibre diet, fluids, sitz bath (warm 3–4 times/day)**, ointments, banding, **haemorrhoidectomy** (post-op: **pain severe**, first BM painful – stool softener, sitz bath, watch bleeding & urinary retention, side-lying).
- **Anal fissure** – tear, **severe pain on defecation**, streak of blood; sitz bath, high fibre.
- **Fistula-in-ano** – abnormal tract; pus discharge; fistulotomy, **seton**.
- **Pilonidal sinus** – sacrococcygeal, hairy young men ("jeep driver's disease").

## 7.11 Colorectal Cancer
- Most are **adenocarcinoma**; risk: age > 50, **low-fibre high-fat diet**, red meat, polyps, IBD (UC), family history, smoking, obesity.
- S/S: **change in bowel habits (most common)**, rectal bleeding, anaemia (right side – occult blood, fatigue), **ribbon/pencil-like stools** (left/rectal), tenesmus, weight loss.
- Screening: **stool occult blood yearly from 45–50 yrs**, **colonoscopy every 10 yrs**. Marker: **CEA** (monitoring recurrence).
- Surgery: **Abdominoperineal resection (APR)** → permanent colostomy; post-op perineal wound – sitz bath, side-lying.

### Stoma (Colostomy / Ileostomy) Care

:::key Stoma – nursing points
- **Healthy stoma = beefy red/pink, moist, slightly raised**. **Pale = anaemia**; **dark red/purple/black = ischaemia/necrosis → report immediately**.
- Mild oedema first 6 weeks normal; stoma functions in **2–4 days** (colostomy).
- **Ileostomy** – **liquid, continuous, enzyme-rich** output → **skin excoriation risk**, **dehydration & electrolyte loss** → fluids 2–3 L/day; no irrigation.
- **Colostomy** – sigmoid/descending → **formed stool**; **irrigation possible** (sigmoid colostomy) – 500–1000 ml **lukewarm water**, bag hung **45–50 cm (18–20 inch) above stoma**, patient sitting; cone tip; time ~ same daily.
- Transverse colostomy – semi-formed/mushy.
- Pouch **empty when 1/3 – 1/2 full**; change every 3–7 days; opening **1/16–1/8 inch (2–3 mm) larger** than stoma.
- Skin: clean with **warm water**, pat dry; avoid alcohol-based products.
- Odour/gas control: avoid **beans, cabbage, onion, eggs, fish, beer, carbonated drinks**; yogurt, buttermilk, parsley reduce odour.
- Ileostomy – avoid **high-fibre foods (nuts, corn, popcorn, raw vegetables)** → blockage; chew well.
- Body image – encourage patient to **look at & touch stoma**; **Ostomy association**.
:::

## 7.12 Nasogastric (Ryle's) Tube & Enteral Feeding

- Length: **NEX** – **N**ose tip → **E**arlobe → **X**iphoid (+ few cm). Position: **high Fowler's**, head flexed (chin to chest) during insertion; patient swallows (sips of water).
- **Placement check**: **X-ray = gold standard (most accurate)**; bedside – **aspirate gastric content pH ≤ 5 (1–5)**; auscultation (whoosh test) **no longer reliable**.
- Before each feed: check placement & **residual volume** (> 250–500 ml as per policy → hold/inform); return aspirate.
- Feed by **gravity**, syringe held **15–30 cm (6–12 inch) above stomach**; **flush 30 ml water** before & after feed/medicine.
- Patient **semi-Fowler's (30–45°) during feed & 30–60 min after** → aspiration prevention.
- Feed temperature: **room temperature**; continuous feed bag change every 24 hrs (hang time 4–8 hrs for open system).
- **Diarrhoea** – most common complication of tube feeding; **aspiration** – most serious.
- Removal: patient **holds breath**, quick steady pull.
