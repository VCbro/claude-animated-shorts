# Liver, Biliary System & Pancreas

## 8.1 Liver – Applied Physiology

:::key Liver – facts
- **Largest internal organ & largest gland** (~1.5 kg), RUQ; **dual blood supply** – hepatic artery (25 %, O₂) + **portal vein (75 %, nutrients)**. Functional unit = **lobule**; **Kupffer cells** (macrophages).
- Functions: **bile production (500–1000 ml/day)**, glycogen storage, **protein synthesis (albumin, clotting factors II, VII, IX, X – Vit K dependent)**, detoxification (drugs, **ammonia → urea**), bilirubin metabolism, storage of **Vit A, D, B12, iron**.
:::

### Liver Function Tests (LFT)
| Test | Normal |
|---|---|
| Total bilirubin | **0.3 – 1.0 (up to 1.2) mg/dL** |
| Direct (conjugated) | 0.1 – 0.3 mg/dL |
| SGPT / ALT (**most specific for liver**) | 7 – 56 U/L |
| SGOT / AST | 10 – 40 U/L |
| Alkaline phosphatase (ALP) | 40 – 130 U/L (↑ in obstructive jaundice, bone disease) |
| Serum albumin | **3.5 – 5.0 g/dL** |
| Total protein | 6 – 8 g/dL |
| PT | 11 – 13.5 sec (INR 0.8–1.2) |
| Serum ammonia | 15 – 45 µg/dL |

- **Jaundice (icterus)** – clinically visible when bilirubin **> 2–3 mg/dL**; first seen in **sclera** (also under tongue, palms).

| Type | Cause | Features |
|---|---|---|
| **Pre-hepatic (haemolytic)** | Excess RBC breakdown – sickle cell, malaria, transfusion reaction | **Unconjugated ↑**, urine normal colour, dark stool |
| **Hepatic (hepatocellular)** | Hepatitis, cirrhosis, drugs | Both ↑, dark urine |
| **Post-hepatic (obstructive)** | Gallstones in CBD, **carcinoma head of pancreas** | **Conjugated ↑**, **dark (tea-coloured) urine, clay-coloured (pale) stool, pruritus** (bile salts in skin) |

- **Pruritus care**: cool baths, **baking soda / calamine**, short nails, cotton clothes, **cholestyramine**.

## 8.2 Viral Hepatitis

| | **Hep A** | **Hep B** | **Hep C** | **Hep D** | **Hep E** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Virus | RNA (Picorna) | **DNA** (only DNA one) | RNA | RNA (defective – **needs HBV**) | RNA |
| Route | **Faeco-oral** | **Blood, sexual, perinatal** | **Blood** (IV drug, transfusion) | Blood (with B) | **Faeco-oral** (water) |
| Incubation | 15–50 (~30) days | **30–180 (~60–90) days** | 15–160 (~50) days | | 15–64 days |
| Chronicity | No | **Yes** (5–10 % adults, **90 % neonates**) | **Yes – most chronic (75–85 %)** | Yes | No |
| Vaccine | **Yes** | **Yes** (0, 1, 6 months) | **No** | (HBV vaccine protects) | No (available in China) |
| Special | Children; mild | **Carrier state**, HCC risk | Cirrhosis, HCC; curable with DAAs | Superinfection – severe | **Pregnant women – high mortality (~20 %)** |

:::mnemo Vowels hit your Bowels
Hepatitis **A** & **E** (vowels) → **faeco-oral (bowels)**. Baaki (B, C, D) → **blood/body fluids**.
:::

### Hepatitis B markers
| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **HBsAg** | Infection present (acute or chronic) – **first marker** |
| **Anti-HBs** | **Immunity** (recovery or vaccination) |
| **HBeAg** | **High infectivity / active replication** |
| **Anti-HBc IgM** | **Acute / recent infection** |
| Anti-HBc IgG | Past/chronic infection |

- **Phases of hepatitis**: **Pre-icteric (prodromal)** – anorexia (most common), nausea, fatigue, **aversion to smoking/food smell**, RUQ discomfort, flu-like → **Icteric** – jaundice, dark urine, clay stools → **Post-icteric (convalescent)**.
- Management: **rest**, **high-calorie, high-carbohydrate, moderate protein, low-fat diet**, small frequent meals, avoid **alcohol & hepatotoxic drugs (paracetamol)**; antivirals for chronic B (**tenofovir, entecavir**) & C (**sofosbuvir-based DAAs**).
- **Post-exposure (Hep B)**: unvaccinated → **HBIG (within 24 hrs, max 7 days) + vaccine**. **Newborn of HBsAg +ve mother → HBIG + Hep B vaccine within 12 hrs of birth** (different sites).
- Hep B vaccine: **IM deltoid** (adult), anterolateral thigh (infant); **NOT gluteal** (poor immune response).
- **National Viral Hepatitis Control Programme** launched **28 July 2018** – **28 July = World Hepatitis Day** (birthday of Baruch Blumberg, discoverer of HBV).

## 8.3 Liver Cirrhosis

:::def Cirrhosis
Chronic, **irreversible** liver disease – normal tissue **fibrosis & nodules** se replace ho jata hai. Types: **Alcoholic (Laennec's – most common)**, **Post-necrotic** (viral hepatitis B/C), **Biliary** (obstruction), Cardiac (RHF). NAFLD/NASH increasingly common.
:::

### Clinical features (2 mechanisms)
- **Hepatocellular failure**: jaundice, **↓ albumin → oedema & ascites**, **↓ clotting factors → bleeding**, **spider angiomas**, **palmar erythema**, **gynaecomastia** & testicular atrophy (↑ oestrogen), **asterixis**, fetor hepaticus, **hepatic encephalopathy**.
- **Portal hypertension** (> 10–12 mmHg): **oesophageal varices**, **caput medusae**, **splenomegaly** (→ thrombocytopenia), **ascites**, haemorrhoids.

### Ascites management
- **Sodium restriction (≤ 2 g/day)**, fluid restriction if hyponatraemic
- **Diuretics – Spironolactone** (first choice, K-sparing) **+ Furosemide**
- **Daily weight & abdominal girth** (same place – mark the skin, same time)
- **Paracentesis** (large volume → **IV albumin**), TIPS
- Position: **semi/high Fowler's** (breathing easy)
- Monitor for SBP (spontaneous bacterial peritonitis)

### Oesophageal Varices
- Dilated veins at lower oesophagus due to portal HTN → rupture → **massive painless haematemesis** – **medical emergency**, most common cause of death in cirrhosis.
- Rx: **ABC, 2 large-bore IV, blood transfusion**, **IV Octreotide / Vasopressin / Terlipressin** (splanchnic vasoconstriction), **Endoscopic band ligation (EVL – first choice)** / sclerotherapy, antibiotics, **Sengstaken-Blakemore tube** (balloon tamponade – temporary; **scissors at bedside** to cut tube if airway obstruction; check balloon pressure, deflate per order every 8–12 hrs), **TIPS**.
- Prevention of re-bleeding: **non-selective beta-blocker (propranolol)**; avoid straining, alcohol, NSAIDs, hot/rough foods.

### Hepatic Encephalopathy
- **↑ Ammonia (NH₃)** crosses blood-brain barrier (liver ammonia ko urea nahi bana pata).
- Precipitating factors: **GI bleeding (most common)**, high-protein diet, constipation, infection, hypokalaemia, sedatives/opioids, dehydration, alcohol.
- S/S: confusion, sleep reversal, **asterixis ("liver flap" – flapping tremor of outstretched hands)**, **fetor hepaticus** (musty sweet breath), **constructional apraxia** (can't draw star/ handwriting change), → stupor → coma. Grades I–IV (West Haven).
- Rx: **Lactulose** (acidifies colon, NH₃ → NH₄⁺ trapped & excreted; goal **2–3 soft stools/day**), **Rifaximin**/Neomycin (kill ammonia-producing gut bacteria), **avoid sedatives**, treat precipitating cause; protein – **not restricted long-term** (moderate 1–1.5 g/kg; plant/dairy proteins better) – acute stage short restriction.
- Nursing: neuro checks, **handwriting/star drawing daily**, safety (side rails), bleeding precautions, skin care.

:::warn Cirrhosis – bleeding precautions
Soft toothbrush, electric razor, avoid IM injections (or small needle + pressure 5 min), check stool for blood, **Vitamin K** as ordered, avoid aspirin/NSAIDs, monitor PT/INR & platelets.
:::

- **Liver cancer (Hepatocellular carcinoma)** – risk: cirrhosis, HBV, HCV, aflatoxin; marker **α-fetoprotein (AFP)**.
- **Liver transplant** – lifelong immunosuppressants (tacrolimus, cyclosporine); watch rejection & infection.
- **Hepatic/liver abscess** – amoebic (*Entamoeba histolytica*, right lobe, "**anchovy sauce pus**") vs pyogenic.

## 8.4 Gallbladder Disorders

- Gallbladder stores & concentrates bile (**30–50 ml**); **CCK (cholecystokinin)** → contraction after fatty meal.
- **Cholelithiasis (gallstones)** – most are **cholesterol stones**. Risk: **"5 F" – Fat, Female, Forty, Fertile, Fair** (+ family history, rapid weight loss, OCP).
- **Cholecystitis** – inflamed gallbladder (90 % due to stones).

:::key Cholecystitis – S/S
- **RUQ / epigastric pain after fatty meal**, radiating to **right shoulder/scapula** (referred – phrenic nerve)
- **Biliary colic** – severe colicky pain with nausea/vomiting, fever
- **Murphy's sign** – deep inspiration par RUQ palpation se pain → patient **saans rok leta hai**
- Flatulence, dyspepsia, fat intolerance; **CBD stone (choledocholithiasis)** → obstructive jaundice
- Diagnosis: **Ultrasound abdomen (best initial)**, HIDA scan; **ERCP** (diagnostic + stone removal) – watch for **pancreatitis** after ERCP
:::

- Rx: low-fat diet, analgesics (**Morphine was avoided earlier → sphincter of Oddi spasm; now opioids acceptable**, meperidine historic), **ursodeoxycholic acid** (dissolves cholesterol stones), **ESWL**, **Laparoscopic cholecystectomy (gold standard)**.
- **Post-lap chole**: **shoulder pain** (CO₂ gas irritating diaphragm) → early ambulation, **Sims' / left lateral position** with knees bent, heat; discharge usually same/next day.
- **Open cholecystectomy + CBD exploration → T-tube**: bag **below gallbladder level**, **drainage 300–500 ml first 24 hrs**, decreases gradually; clamp before meals as ordered (trial); report > 1000 ml/day; stool colour returns brown = bile flowing to duodenum.
- **Low-fat diet** long-term (4–6 weeks) after surgery; fat tolerance improves.

## 8.5 Pancreatitis

### Acute Pancreatitis
- **Auto-digestion** of pancreas by its own enzymes (trypsin).
- Causes (**I GET SMASHED**): **I**diopathic, **G**allstones (most common overall), **E**thanol (alcohol), **T**rauma, **S**teroids, **M**umps, **A**utoimmune, **S**corpion sting, **H**ypercalcaemia/**H**ypertriglyceridaemia, **E**RCP, **D**rugs.
- S/S: **severe constant epigastric / LUQ pain radiating to the back**, **worse lying supine & after eating; relieved by sitting up, leaning forward or fetal (knee-chest) position**; nausea, vomiting, fever, **abdominal guarding**, shock.
 - **Cullen's sign** – bluish discolouration **around umbilicus** (haemorrhagic)
 - **Grey Turner's sign** – bluish discolouration of **flanks**
 - **Hypocalcaemia** (Chvostek/Trousseau) – calcium binds to fat necrosis → **poor prognosis**; hyperglycaemia
- Diagnosis: **Serum lipase (more specific; stays elevated longer, 7–14 days)**, **serum amylase** (↑ within 2–12 hrs, normal in 3–5 days; normal 30–110 U/L), CT abdomen (best imaging). Severity: **Ranson's criteria**, BISAP.

:::key Acute pancreatitis – management ("rest the pancreas")
- **NPO**, **NG suction** (gastric juice → stimulation ↓), IV fluids (**aggressive – RL preferred**)
- **Pain relief – opioids** (morphine/hydromorphone/fentanyl) – pain control is priority
- Position: **sitting up / knee-chest / side-lying with knees flexed**
- Early **enteral nutrition** (NJ tube) preferred over TPN when oral not possible; resume orally with **low-fat, high-carbohydrate** diet
- Monitor **blood glucose**, calcium, respiratory status (**ARDS**, pleural effusion – left), shock
- Antibiotics only if infected necrosis; avoid alcohol
:::

### Chronic Pancreatitis
- Most common cause **chronic alcoholism**; progressive destruction → **steatorrhoea** (fatty, foul, frothy, floating stools – exocrine loss), **diabetes mellitus** (endocrine loss), chronic pain, weight loss, calcifications on X-ray.
- Rx: **pancreatic enzyme replacement (Pancrelipase) – with meals & snacks** (don't crush/mix with protein foods; wipe lips – skin irritation), **low-fat diet**, insulin, **stop alcohol**, analgesics.

### Pancreatic Cancer
- Mostly **adenocarcinoma, head of pancreas**; risk – **smoking (most important)**, chronic pancreatitis, DM. S/S: **painless progressive obstructive jaundice**, weight loss, **Courvoisier's sign** (palpable non-tender gallbladder), back pain. Marker **CA 19-9**. Surgery: **Whipple's procedure (pancreaticoduodenectomy)**. Very poor prognosis.
