## 9.1 Applied Anatomy & Physiology

@fig nephron.svg | Kidney aur nephron ke parts – kaun kya karta hai

:::key Kidney – facts
- **Retroperitoneal**, T12–L3; right kidney slightly lower (liver). Weight ~150 g each.
- Functional unit = **Nephron** (~1 million/kidney). Kidneys receive **20–25 % of cardiac output (~1200 ml/min)**.
- **GFR ≈ 125 ml/min (180 L/day)**; urine output ~1–1.5 L/day (99 % reabsorbed).
- Functions (**"A WET BED"**): **A**cid-base balance, **W**ater balance, **E**lectrolytes, **T**oxin removal, **B**P control (renin-angiotensin), **E**rythropoietin (RBC production), **D** – Vitamin **D** activation (calcitriol).
- **Renin** – juxtaglomerular cells → Angiotensin II (vasoconstriction) → **Aldosterone** (Na & water retain, K excrete). **ADH** – water reabsorption in collecting duct.
- Bladder capacity: **300–600 ml** (urge to void at ~150–300 ml). Female urethra **3–4 cm** (UTI more), male **18–20 cm**.
:::

### Renal Function Tests & Urine
| Test | Normal |
|---|---|
| **Blood urea** | 15 – 40 mg/dL |
| **BUN** | 7 – 20 mg/dL |
| **Serum creatinine** (best single indicator) | 0.6 – 1.2 mg/dL (M), 0.5 – 1.1 (F) |
| **Creatinine clearance** (≈ GFR) | 90 – 125 ml/min |
| BUN : Creatinine ratio | 10 : 1 – 20 : 1 |
| Uric acid | 3.5 – 7 mg/dL |
| **Urine specific gravity** | **1.005 – 1.030** (1.010–1.025) |
| Urine pH | 4.5 – 8 (avg 6) |
| Urine | Clear, pale yellow (urochrome); no glucose, protein, ketones, blood |

- **24-hr urine collection**: **discard first morning void**, then collect all urine for 24 hrs **including next morning's first void**; keep on ice/preservative. Ek bhi sample miss → restart.
- **Clean-catch midstream** urine – clean meatus front to back, first part discard, midstream collect.
- From catheter – **aspirate from sampling port with sterile syringe** (never from bag).
- **IVP / CT with contrast** – check iodine/shellfish allergy, **creatinine** (contrast nephropathy), metformin hold 48 hrs, hydrate after.
- **Renal biopsy** – check coagulation; after: **prone/supine with sandbag**, bed rest 24 hrs, watch **haematuria & flank pain**, fluids.
- **Cystoscopy** – lithotomy position; after: pink-tinged urine & burning normal; bright red/clots → report; fluids.

### Urinary terms
**Dysuria** (painful), **Frequency**, **Urgency**, **Nocturia**, **Haematuria**, **Pyuria** (pus), **Oliguria** (< 400 ml/day), **Anuria** (< 100 ml/day), **Polyuria** (> 3 L/day), **Enuresis** (bed wetting), **Retention** (can't empty), **Incontinence**, **Proteinuria**, **Glycosuria**.

## 9.2 Urinary Tract Infection (UTI)

- Most common organism: ***E. coli*** (~80 %). Females > males (short urethra). Risk: **catheter (most common cause of HAI/CAUTI)**, pregnancy, DM, BPH, stones, sexual activity, post-menopause.
- **Cystitis** (lower): **dysuria, frequency, urgency**, suprapubic pain, cloudy foul urine, haematuria. Elderly – **confusion**.
- **Pyelonephritis** (upper – kidney): **high fever with chills, flank pain, CVA (costovertebral angle) tenderness**, nausea/vomiting.
- Diagnosis: urine culture & sensitivity (**> 10⁵ CFU/ml** significant), urinalysis (nitrite, leukocyte esterase).

:::tip UTI prevention teaching
- Fluids **2–3 L/day**, **void every 2–3 hrs** (don't hold), **void after intercourse**
- **Wipe front to back** (females), cotton undergarments, showers instead of tub/bubble bath
- Cranberry juice (may help), avoid irritants (caffeine, alcohol, spicy)
- Complete the **full antibiotic course**; **Phenazopyridine** – urine **orange-red** (analgesic, max 2 days)
:::

## 9.3 Urolithiasis (Renal / Urinary Calculi)

- Most common stone: **Calcium oxalate** (~75–80 %); others – calcium phosphate, **uric acid** (gout), **struvite** (infection – Proteus, staghorn), **cystine**.
- Risk: dehydration, hot climate, immobility, hypercalcaemia, high animal protein/salt, gout, UTI.
- S/S: **Renal colic – excruciating, intermittent flank pain radiating to groin/testis/labia ("loin to groin")**, nausea/vomiting, **haematuria**, restlessness (patient can't lie still), frequency if near bladder.
- Diagnosis: **Non-contrast CT KUB (gold standard)**, X-ray KUB, USG, urinalysis; **strain all urine** (stone analysis).
- Rx: **analgesia (NSAIDs – ketorolac/diclofenac; opioids)** – priority; **fluids 3–4 L/day** (output ≥ 2–2.5 L/day); **tamsulosin** (α-blocker – stone passage); stones **< 5 mm usually pass**; **ESWL** (lithotripsy – shock waves; bruising on flank, haematuria few days, strain urine), **PCNL**, **ureteroscopy (URS)**, open surgery.

| Stone | Diet advice |
|---|---|
| **Calcium oxalate** | Avoid **oxalate-rich**: **spinach, tomato (seeds), chocolate, tea, nuts, beetroot**, rhubarb; ↓ salt & animal protein; **don't restrict dietary calcium** (normal Ca intake protects) |
| **Uric acid** | **Low purine** – avoid organ meat, red meat, sardines, alcohol; **allopurinol**; alkalinize urine |
| **Struvite** | Treat infection; acidify urine |
| **Cystine** | High fluids, alkalinize urine |

## 9.4 Acute Kidney Injury (AKI / ARF)

:::def AKI
**Sudden (hours–days)** loss of kidney function → ↑ BUN & creatinine, ↓ urine output. **Potentially reversible.**
:::

| Type | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Pre-renal** (most common, ~60–70 %) | **↓ renal perfusion** – hypovolaemia, haemorrhage, dehydration, shock, HF, burns |
| **Intra-renal (intrinsic)** | Kidney tissue damage – **Acute Tubular Necrosis (ATN – most common intrarenal)**, **nephrotoxic drugs (aminoglycosides – gentamicin, NSAIDs, contrast dye, amphotericin B)**, glomerulonephritis, rhabdomyolysis, transfusion reaction |
| **Post-renal** | **Obstruction** – BPH (most common in men), stones, tumours, blocked catheter |

### Phases of AKI
1. **Initiation (onset)** – injury se oliguria start tak (hours–days)
2. **Oliguric phase** (1–3 weeks) – **urine < 400 ml/day**, **↑ K⁺ (most dangerous)**, ↑ BUN/creatinine, fluid overload, **metabolic acidosis**, ↓ Na, ↑ PO₄, ↓ Ca, uraemic symptoms
3. **Diuretic phase** (1–3 weeks) – urine **3–5 L/day** (kidneys can't concentrate) → **dehydration, hypokalaemia, hyponatraemia** risk
4. **Recovery phase** – up to 12 months; GFR improves

- Rx: treat cause, **fluid restriction = previous day's output + 500–600 ml** (insensible loss), daily weight, I/O, **manage hyperkalaemia**, diet – **high-calorie (carbohydrate), low protein (adequate), low K, low Na, low PO₄**; dialysis if needed (AEIOU).

:::mnemo AEIOU – Indications for urgent dialysis
**A**cidosis (severe metabolic) · **E**lectrolytes (**refractory hyperkalaemia**) · **I**ntoxication (lithium, aspirin, methanol) · **O**verload (fluid – pulmonary oedema) · **U**raemia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding)
:::

## 9.5 Chronic Kidney Disease (CKD)

:::def CKD
Kidney damage or **GFR < 60 ml/min/1.73 m² for ≥ 3 months**; progressive, **irreversible**. **ESRD/ESKD = GFR < 15** (stage 5) → dialysis/transplant.
Most common causes: **1. Diabetes mellitus (diabetic nephropathy)**, **2. Hypertension**, 3. glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease.
:::

| Stage | GFR (ml/min/1.73 m²) |
|---|---|
| 1 | ≥ 90 (kidney damage with normal GFR) |
| 2 | 60 – 89 |
| 3a / 3b | 45 – 59 / 30 – 44 |
| 4 | 15 – 29 |
| **5 (ESRD)** | **< 15** |

### Clinical features – every system (uraemia)
- **Fluid & electrolytes**: oedema, HTN, **hyperkalaemia**, **hyperphosphataemia + hypocalcaemia** → secondary hyperparathyroidism → **renal osteodystrophy**, metabolic acidosis
- **Haematological**: **Anaemia (↓ erythropoietin)** – normocytic normochromic; bleeding tendency
- **Cardiovascular**: HTN, HF, **uraemic pericarditis** (friction rub), CAD (most common cause of death)
- **Skin**: **pruritus**, yellow-grey (sallow) skin, **uraemic frost** (white urea crystals – late)
- **GI**: anorexia, nausea, vomiting, **uraemic fetor (ammonia/urine smell breath)**, metallic taste, stomatitis
- **Neuro**: fatigue, confusion, peripheral neuropathy, **restless legs**, seizures, coma
- **Respiratory**: Kussmaul breathing, pulmonary oedema
- Reproductive: amenorrhoea, impotence

### CKD management
- Control **BP (ACE inhibitor/ARB – kidney protective)**, blood sugar
- **Erythropoietin (epoetin alfa)** SC – monitor Hb (target 10–11.5 g/dL; > 13 avoid – thrombosis, HTN); give **iron** supplements
- **Phosphate binders (calcium carbonate, sevelamer) – WITH meals**; **calcitriol (active Vit D)**
- Hyperkalaemia management, **sodium bicarbonate** for acidosis
- **Avoid**: Mg-containing antacids/laxatives (**milk of magnesia**), **Fleet (phosphate) enema**, NSAIDs, nephrotoxic drugs; adjust drug doses
- Diet: **↓ protein (0.6–0.8 g/kg – high biological value: egg, meat, milk)** before dialysis; **on dialysis – ↑ protein (1.2 g/kg HD, 1.2–1.3 g/kg PD)**; **low Na, low K (avoid banana, orange, potato, tomato, coconut water, dry fruits), low phosphorus (milk products, cola)**, fluid = urine output + 500 ml, adequate calories
- Skin care for pruritus, oral hygiene, daily weight

:::tip Potassium kam karne ki trick (leaching)
Sabziyon (aloo etc.) ko **chhote tukde me kaat ke, garam paani me 2+ ghante bhigo ke, paani phenk ke, naye paani me pakao** – potassium kafi kam ho jata hai.
:::

## 9.6 Dialysis

| | **Haemodialysis (HD)** | **Peritoneal Dialysis (PD)** |
|---|---|---|
| Membrane | Artificial (**dialyser – "artificial kidney"**) | **Peritoneum** (natural semi-permeable) |
| Access | **AV fistula (best – "lifeline")**, AV graft, temporary catheter (subclavian/jugular/femoral) | Tenckhoff catheter in abdomen |
| Schedule | **3 times/week, 3–5 hrs** | Daily – CAPD (4–5 exchanges/day) or APD at night |
| Anticoagulant | **Heparin** needed | No |
| Main complications | **Hypotension (most common)**, muscle cramps, **disequilibrium syndrome** (headache, nausea, confusion, seizures – fast urea removal → cerebral oedema; first sessions slow), bleeding, infection, air embolism | **Peritonitis (most common & serious – cloudy effluent**, abdominal pain, fever), hernia, hyperglycaemia (dextrose), protein loss, back pain, catheter exit-site infection |

:::key AV Fistula care
- **Thrill (palpate – vibration/buzz) & bruit (auscultate – whooshing sound)** = fistula **patent** → check every shift; absent → report.
- Fistula maturity: **4–6 weeks (up to 3 months)** before use; arm exercises (squeezing ball) to mature.
- **NO BP, NO venipuncture, NO IV, NO injections** in fistula arm; **no tight clothes, watch, jewellery, no sleeping/lifting heavy on that arm**.
- "**Fistula arm – No touch arm**" sign on bed.
- Bleeding after dialysis → direct pressure.
:::

- **Before HD**: weigh, vitals, hold antihypertensives (as ordered – hypotension), check access. **After HD**: weigh (wt loss = fluid removed), vitals, bleeding check, avoid invasive procedures 4–6 hrs (heparin).
- **PD**: warm dialysate to **body temperature (dry heat – never microwave)**; strict asepsis; inflow **10–20 min**, dwell **as prescribed (4–6 hrs CAPD)**, outflow **20–30 min** by gravity; **outflow slow → change position, check kinks, constipation**; outflow should be **clear, straw-coloured**; **cloudy = peritonitis**; record balance; if respiratory distress during inflow → **elevate HOB**, slow.

### Kidney Transplant
- Living donor or cadaver; graft placed in **iliac fossa** (own kidneys usually left).
- Post-op: **urine output hourly** (can be very high – diuresis), fluid replacement; lifelong **immunosuppressants** (tacrolimus, cyclosporine, mycophenolate, prednisolone) → **infection risk** – reverse isolation measures, hand hygiene.
- **Rejection**: **Hyperacute** (minutes–hours – remove graft), **Acute** (1 week–2 years; **fever, graft tenderness, ↓ urine output, ↑ creatinine, weight gain, HTN**), **Chronic** (months–years).

## 9.7 Glomerular Diseases

| Feature | **Acute Glomerulonephritis (AGN – Nephritic)** | **Nephrotic Syndrome** |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Post-streptococcal** (group A β-haemolytic strep throat/skin, **1–3 weeks earlier**); children 2–12 yrs | Minimal change disease (children), FSGS, membranous (adults), DM, SLE |
| Key feature | **Haematuria ("cola / smoky / tea-coloured" urine)**, **HTN**, mild proteinuria, oliguria, **periorbital oedema**, ↑ ASO titre | **Massive proteinuria (> 3.5 g/day)**, **hypoalbuminaemia (< 3 g/dL)**, **generalized oedema (anasarca)**, **hyperlipidaemia** (lipiduria), frothy urine, thrombosis risk |
| BP | **↑** | Normal / low |
| Rx | Bed rest (till haematuria/HTN resolve), **penicillin**, **restrict Na, fluid & protein (if azotemia)**, diuretics, antihypertensives | **Corticosteroids (prednisolone)**, diuretics, ACE-I, statins; **↓ Na**, normal protein (**not high**), skin care, daily weight, infection prevention |

- **Polycystic kidney disease** – autosomal dominant (adult); flank pain, haematuria, HTN, enlarged kidneys.
- **Wilms' tumour (nephroblastoma)** – children; abdominal mass – **don't palpate abdomen**.
- **Renal cell carcinoma** – classic triad: **haematuria, flank pain, palpable mass**; smoking risk; nephrectomy (flank incision → pain on breathing – splint).
- **Bladder cancer** – most common sign **painless haematuria**; smoking, aniline dyes; TURBT, **cystectomy with ileal conduit** (urine continuous; mucus threads normal).

## 9.8 Urinary Retention, Incontinence & Catheterization

- **Retention** – acute (painful, distended bladder) – causes: BPH, post-op, anaesthesia, drugs (anticholinergics, opioids). First try: privacy, running water sound, warm water over perineum, normal voiding position; then catheter. **Bladder scan** – PVR > 100–150 ml significant. Drain **not more than 750–1000 ml at once** (as per policy) – hypotension/haematuria risk.
- **Incontinence**: **Stress** (cough/sneeze/laugh – weak pelvic floor; **Kegel exercises**), **Urge** (overactive bladder – bladder training, anticholinergics like oxybutynin), **Overflow** (BPH, neurogenic – catheter), **Functional** (mobility/cognition issue – toileting schedule), **Reflex** (spinal cord injury).

:::key Kegel (pelvic floor) exercise
Muscles jaise urine ka flow beech me rokte ho waise **tighten 10 sec → relax 10 sec**, **10–15 times, 3 times/day**. Results 6–12 weeks. Pet, thigh, buttocks ke muscles use nahi karne.
:::

### Urinary catheterization – key points
- **Sterile technique**; size adult **14–16 Fr** (female 12–14, male 14–16), child 8–10 Fr; balloon **5–10 ml sterile water** (not saline – crystallize; not air – floats).
- Female – insert **5–7.5 cm (2–3 inch)** then 2.5 cm more after urine; Male – penis held **90° (perpendicular)**, insert **17–22.5 cm (7–9 inch)** till urine + 2.5–5 cm (to "Y"); **replace foreskin** (paraphimosis prevention).
- Secure catheter – **female: inner thigh; male: lower abdomen / upper thigh**.
- Bag **always below bladder level**, never on floor, no kinks; **closed drainage system** – don't disconnect.
- **Catheter care with soap & water** daily; fluids 2–3 L.
- **CAUTI prevention** – remove as early as possible (daily review); no routine bladder irrigation.
- **Suprapubic catheter** – long-term; site care.
- **Continuous Bladder Irrigation (CBI)** after **TURP** – **3-way catheter**; normal saline; keep output **light pink/rose**; bright red/clots → ↑ irrigation flow rate; **True urine output = Total output − Irrigation fluid**.

## 9.9 BPH & Prostate

- **Benign Prostatic Hyperplasia** – men > 50 yrs; **enlarged prostate** compresses urethra.
- S/S (LUTS): **hesitancy**, **weak/interrupted stream**, dribbling, **incomplete emptying**, frequency, **nocturia** (often first), urgency, retention, UTI, hydronephrosis.
- Diagnosis: **DRE (digital rectal exam – smooth, firm, rubbery, symmetrically enlarged)**, **PSA** (normal < 4 ng/ml), USG/PVR, uroflowmetry.
- Drugs: **α-blockers – Tamsulosin, Prazosin** (smooth muscle relax – **orthostatic hypotension**, rise slowly); **5-α-reductase inhibitors – Finasteride, Dutasteride** (shrink prostate – **6 months** to effect; pregnant women should **not handle crushed tablets** – teratogenic).
- Avoid: **antihistamines, decongestants, anticholinergics** (retention), alcohol, caffeine at night, large fluid volume at once.
- Surgery: **TURP (gold standard)**, open prostatectomy, laser.

:::warn Post-TURP care
- **CBI with 3-way catheter** (traction on catheter – taped to thigh – for haemostasis; **leg straight**).
- **Bladder spasms** – common (oxybutynin/B&O suppository); avoid pulling catheter.
- **TUR syndrome** – absorption of irrigation fluid (glycine/water) → **dilutional hyponatraemia** → confusion, nausea, bradycardia, HTN, seizures.
- After catheter removal – **dribbling & incontinence** temporary → **Kegel exercises**; avoid straining, heavy lifting, sitting long, intercourse **4–6 weeks**; fluids 2–3 L; **retrograde ejaculation** common.
:::

- **Prostate cancer** – most common cancer in men (western), **adenocarcinoma, posterior lobe** – **hard, nodular** on DRE; **PSA ↑**; bone metastasis (**osteoblastic, back pain**); Rx – radical prostatectomy, radiation, **hormone therapy (orchiectomy, leuprolide)**.
- **Testicular cancer** – age **15–35**; painless lump; **monthly testicular self-exam (TSE) after warm bath**; markers **AFP, β-hCG**; cryptorchidism risk.
- **Hydrocele** (fluid – transillumination +ve), **Varicocele** ("bag of worms", left side, infertility), **Testicular torsion** (sudden severe pain – **surgical emergency within 4–6 hrs**), **Phimosis/Paraphimosis**, **Epididymitis** (scrotal support, ice).
