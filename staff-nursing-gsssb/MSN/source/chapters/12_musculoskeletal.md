# Musculoskeletal System Disorders

## 12.1 Applied Anatomy

:::key Bones & joints – facts
- Adult skeleton = **206 bones**; newborn ~270–300. **Largest bone – Femur**; **smallest – Stapes**; longest = femur.
- Bone cells: **Osteoblasts** (bone **b**uild), **Osteoclasts** (bone **c**rush/resorb), Osteocytes (mature).
- Bone growth – epiphyseal plate; bone remodelling regulated by **PTH, calcitonin, Vit D, oestrogen**.
- Joints: **Synarthrosis** (immovable – skull sutures), **Amphiarthrosis** (slightly – pubic symphysis), **Diarthrosis/synovial** (freely – knee, hip, shoulder). **Ball & socket** – hip, shoulder; **Hinge** – elbow, knee; **Pivot** – atlas-axis.
- Muscle types: skeletal (voluntary), cardiac, smooth.
- **Ligament** = bone to bone; **Tendon** = muscle to bone.
:::

### Common terms
**Sprain** (ligament injury), **Strain** (muscle/tendon – "pulled muscle"), **Dislocation** (complete displacement of joint surfaces), **Subluxation** (partial), **Contracture**, **Ankylosis** (joint fusion), **Crepitus** (grating), **Kyphosis** (hunchback – thoracic), **Lordosis** (sway back – lumbar), **Scoliosis** (lateral curve).

:::mnemo RICE – soft tissue injury (first 24–48 hrs)
**R**est · **I**ce (20 min at a time, cloth wrapped; first 24–48 hrs) · **C**ompression (elastic bandage) · **E**levation (above heart level). After 48 hrs → **heat**.
:::

## 12.2 Fractures

@fig fracture_types.svg | Fractures ke common types

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Closed (simple)** | Skin intact |
| **Open (compound)** | Bone exposed through skin → **infection (osteomyelitis) risk** |
| **Complete / Incomplete** | Full break / partial |
| **Greenstick** | Incomplete – one side breaks, other bends (**children** – soft bones) |
| **Comminuted** | **3 or more fragments** (crushed) |
| **Transverse / Oblique / Spiral** | Straight across / angle / twisting injury (child abuse suspicion in infants) |
| **Impacted** | One fragment driven into another |
| **Compression** | Vertebra crushed (osteoporosis) |
| **Pathological** | Weak bone se minimal trauma – **osteoporosis, cancer metastasis**, osteomyelitis |
| **Avulsion** | Ligament/tendon pulls a bone fragment |
| **Depressed** | Skull – fragment pushed inward |
| **Stress / fatigue** | Repeated stress – athletes, soldiers (march fracture – metatarsal) |
| **Colles' fracture** | **Distal radius**, dorsal displacement – **"dinner fork" deformity**; fall on outstretched hand (elderly women) |
| **Smith's** | Distal radius volar displacement (reverse Colles') |
| **Pott's fracture** | Ankle (bimalleolar) |

- **S/S**: **pain & tenderness**, **deformity**, swelling, ecchymosis, **loss of function**, **crepitus**, abnormal movement, **shortening** (hip – also **external rotation**).
- **Bone healing stages**: Haematoma formation (1–3 days) → Fibrocartilage/granulation (3 days–2 weeks) → **Callus formation** (2–6 weeks) → Ossification (3 weeks–6 months) → Remodelling (up to 1 yr).
- Healing time approx: **Upper limb 6–8 weeks; Lower limb 12–16 weeks**; children faster.

### First aid for fracture
- ABC, control bleeding (open fracture – **cover with sterile dressing; don't push bone back**)
- **Splint "as it lies"** – include **joint above & below** the fracture
- Elevate, ice, check **neurovascular status distal** before & after splinting
- Don't straighten / move unnecessarily

### Treatment
- **Reduction**: **Closed** (manipulation) or **Open reduction internal fixation (ORIF)** – plates, screws, nails, rods.
- **Immobilization**: cast, splint, traction, external fixator.
- **Rehabilitation**: exercises, weight-bearing as ordered.

:::key 5 P's – Neurovascular assessment (also "6 P's")
**P**ain (out of proportion – earliest), **P**allor, **P**ulselessness (late), **P**aresthesia, **P**aralysis (+ **P**oikilothermia – cool).
Check distal to injury/cast **every 1 hr for first 24 hrs**, then every 4 hrs. Compare with other limb. **Capillary refill < 2–3 sec** normal.
:::

### Cast care
- **Plaster of Paris (POP)** – dries in **24–72 hrs** (feels cool & damp while wet; dry = white, shiny, resonant, odourless); **Fibreglass** – dries in 30 min, lightweight, water-resistant.
- Wet cast – handle with **palms (not fingers)** → finger dents → pressure points; place on **pillow**, keep uncovered to dry; turn every 2 hrs; no heater/hair-dryer on hot setting.
- **Elevate above heart level first 24–48 hrs**; ice packs.
- **Never insert anything inside cast to scratch** (pen, stick) → skin injury → infection; use cool air from hair-dryer.
- Petal the edges; keep dry; check **"hot spots"** (warm area on cast = infection), **foul smell, drainage** → report.
- Isometric exercises of muscles under cast; ROM of joints not immobilized.
- **Cast syndrome** (body/hip spica cast) – **superior mesenteric artery compression** → **nausea, vomiting, abdominal distension, pain** → report; small frequent meals.
- Cast removal – **cast cutter vibrates, doesn't cut skin**; skin washed gently, oil/lotion, don't scrub flaky skin.

### Traction
:::def Traction
**Pulling force** applied to a body part to **reduce & immobilize fracture**, decrease muscle spasm, prevent deformity. **Counter-traction** (usually patient's body weight; bed elevation) is needed.
:::

| Type | Example | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Skin traction** (max 2.5–4.5 kg / 5–10 lb; intermittent) | **Buck's extension** (straight pull – **hip fracture pre-op**, knee), **Russell's** (knee sling – femoral), **Bryant's** (children < 2 yrs/ < 14 kg – both legs vertical 90°, **buttocks slightly off bed**), Cervical halter, pelvic belt | Short-term |
| **Skeletal traction** (pin/wire through bone; 7–14 kg / 15–30 lb, continuous) | **Balanced suspension with Thomas splint + Pearson attachment** (femur), Crutchfield/Gardner-Wells tongs (cervical), **Halo** | Long-term; stronger pull |

:::warn Traction – golden rules
- **Weights hang freely** – never on floor/bed; **never remove weights** (skeletal) without order; never lift weights when moving patient.
- Ropes in pulley grooves, knots away from pulleys; **body in alignment, centre of bed**; feet away from footboard.
- **Pin site care** – sterile, as per protocol (chlorhexidine / NS) – watch infection (redness, drainage, pin loosening).
- Neurovascular checks, skin care (pressure ulcers), DVT/constipation/pneumonia prevention, **trapeze bar** to move.
:::

### Complications of fractures
- **Compartment syndrome** – ↑ pressure in a closed muscle compartment (tight cast/bandage, swelling) → ischaemia. **Earliest sign: severe pain unrelieved by opioids, increased on passive stretch** → paresthesia → pallor → **pulselessness (late)**. Rx: **Loosen/bivalve cast, remove tight bandage**, **keep limb at heart level (NOT elevated – ↓ perfusion)**, no ice, **fasciotomy within 4–6 hrs**. Complication → **Volkmann's ischaemic contracture** (forearm – supracondylar fracture).
- **Fat embolism syndrome** – **long bone (femur) / pelvic fractures**, within **24–72 hrs** → **dyspnoea, hypoxaemia (first), confusion/restlessness, petechiae (chest, axilla, conjunctiva)**, fever, tachycardia. Prevention: **immobilize fracture early, minimal movement**. Rx: **High Fowler's, O₂ high-flow**, ventilation, fluids, steroids.
- **DVT/PE**, **shock** (haemorrhage – pelvis/femur large blood loss), **infection/osteomyelitis**, **delayed union, non-union, malunion**, **avascular necrosis** (head of femur), **Complex regional pain syndrome**.

### Hip fracture
- Elderly women, osteoporosis, fall. **Affected leg – shortened, externally rotated, adducted**, pain in hip/groin.
- **Intracapsular** (femoral neck – **AVN risk**; hemiarthroplasty) vs **Extracapsular** (intertrochanteric – ORIF).
- Pre-op: **Buck's traction** (spasm ↓).
- **Total hip replacement (THR/THA) – prevent dislocation**:
 - **Abduction pillow (wedge) between legs** – keep legs apart
 - **No hip flexion > 90°** (no low chairs, raised toilet seat, no bending to tie shoes – use long-handled reacher/ shoe horn)
 - **No adduction (crossing legs)**, **no internal rotation** (toes pointing in)
 - Sit in high firm chair with arms; turn to **unaffected side** with pillow between legs (as per surgeon)
 - Signs of dislocation: **sudden pain, shortening of leg, rotation** → report
- **Total knee replacement (TKR)** – **CPM (continuous passive motion) machine**; goal **90° flexion**; no pillow under knee (flexion contracture).

## 12.3 Bone & Joint Disorders

### Osteoporosis
- **↓ Bone mass/density** → porous fragile bones → **fractures (vertebral compression – most common, hip, wrist)**. "**Silent disease**".
- Risk: **post-menopausal women (↓ oestrogen) – most common**, age, **small thin frame, Caucasian/Asian**, family history, **low Ca/Vit D**, sedentary, **smoking, alcohol, caffeine**, **long-term corticosteroids**, heparin, hyperthyroidism.
- S/S: **loss of height**, **kyphosis (dowager's hump)**, back pain, fractures.
- Diagnosis: **DEXA scan (bone mineral density) – T-score ≤ −2.5 = osteoporosis**; −1 to −2.5 = osteopenia; > −1 normal.
- Rx: **Calcium (1000–1200 mg/day) + Vit D (800–1000 IU)**, **weight-bearing exercise (walking – best)**, stop smoking/alcohol, fall prevention, **Bisphosphonates – Alendronate**, raloxifene, calcitonin, denosumab, teriparatide.

:::warn Alendronate (bisphosphonate) – how to take
**Early morning, empty stomach**, with **full glass (200–240 ml) of plain water**, **remain upright (sitting/standing) for 30 min**, no food/drink/other meds for **30 min** → oesophagitis/ulcer prevention.
:::

- **Osteomalacia** (adults) / **Rickets** (children) – **↓ Vit D** → soft bones, bowing.
- **Paget's disease** – abnormal bone remodelling; enlarged skull (hat size ↑), bowing; ↑ ALP.
- **Osteomyelitis** – bone infection; ***Staph aureus*** most common; S/S – pain, fever, swelling, tenderness; **bone biopsy (definitive)**, **long-term IV antibiotics (4–6 weeks or more)**, surgical debridement (sequestrectomy – dead bone = **sequestrum**; new bone = **involucrum**); immobilize; high protein, Vit C.
- **Bone tumours** – **Osteosarcoma** (most common primary malignant, **10–25 yrs, distal femur / around knee**, sunburst/Codman's triangle), Ewing's sarcoma (onion peel), Multiple myeloma (elderly – punched-out lesions, Bence-Jones protein), **Osteochondroma** (most common benign). **Metastasis to bone** – breast, lung, prostate, kidney, thyroid ("**BLT with a Kosher Pickle**").

### Arthritis – OA vs RA

| Feature | **Osteoarthritis (OA)** – degenerative | **Rheumatoid Arthritis (RA)** – autoimmune |
|---|---|---|
| Nature | **"Wear & tear"** – cartilage degeneration; most common arthritis | **Systemic autoimmune**, synovium inflamed (pannus) |
| Age/Sex | > 50; obese; women slightly more | **20–50 yrs**, **women 3 : 1** |
| Joints | **Weight-bearing – knees, hips**, spine, DIP | **Small joints – hands (MCP, PIP), wrists, feet**; **symmetrical, bilateral** |
| Stiffness | **< 30 min** morning; **pain ↑ with activity, ↓ with rest** | **> 1 hr morning stiffness**; ↑ after rest |
| Nodes | **Heberden's (DIP), Bouchard's (PIP)** | **Rheumatoid nodules**, **swan-neck, boutonnière deformity, ulnar deviation** |
| Systemic | **No** | **Yes** – fatigue, fever, weight loss, anaemia, Sjögren's, pericarditis |
| Lab | Normal ESR | **RA factor, Anti-CCP (most specific), ↑ ESR/CRP** |
| Rx | Weight loss, exercise, **paracetamol**, NSAIDs, intra-articular steroids, **joint replacement** | **DMARDs – Methotrexate (first-line**; folic acid, liver/CBC monitoring, teratogenic), hydroxychloroquine (**eye exam**), sulfasalazine, biologics, NSAIDs, steroids |

:::tip Arthritis – nursing care
- **Heat** for stiffness (warm shower in morning), **cold** for acute inflammation (RA flare)
- Balance rest & exercise; **ROM exercises**; joint protection – **use large joints**, avoid prolonged same position
- Assistive devices – raised toilet seat, grab bars, built-up handles, cane on **unaffected side**
- Weight reduction (OA); energy conservation (RA)
:::

### Gout
- **↑ Uric acid (hyperuricaemia)** → **monosodium urate crystals** in joints → acute inflammatory arthritis; men > 40; **big toe (first MTP joint) – "Podagra"** most common; **tophi** (chronic deposits – ear, fingers).
- Triggers: **high-purine foods – organ meats (liver, kidney), red meat, seafood (sardines, anchovies), alcohol (beer)**, fructose drinks, thiazide diuretics, aspirin (low dose), dehydration, fasting.
- Diagnosis: joint aspiration – **needle-shaped negatively birefringent crystals**; serum uric acid.
- Acute attack: **Colchicine** (s/e – diarrhoea, GI), **NSAIDs (indomethacin)**, steroids; rest, elevate, **ice**, bed cradle (avoid sheet pressure).
- Long-term (prevention): **Allopurinol** (↓ uric acid production – **fluids 2–3 L/day**, rash → stop; **not started during acute attack**), Febuxostat, Probenecid (↑ excretion); **low-purine diet, avoid alcohol, fluids 2–3 L**.

### Other disorders
- **Ankylosing spondylitis** – young men, **HLA-B27**, spine fusion ("**bamboo spine**"), low back pain & stiffness; posture exercises, firm mattress, **no pillow or thin pillow**.
- **SLE** – see immunology chapter.
- **Carpal tunnel syndrome** – **median nerve** compression; numbness thumb–index–middle fingers; **Phalen's test** (wrist flexion 60 sec) & **Tinel's sign** (tap); splint, surgery.
- **Low back pain / Herniated disc (PIVD)** – **L4–L5 / L5–S1** most common; sciatica (radiating to leg), **straight leg raising test positive**; rest (short), NSAIDs, physiotherapy, **proper body mechanics – lift with legs, back straight, object close to body**; laminectomy/discectomy – **log-roll**, check sensation & movement of legs, CSF leak.
- **Dupuytren's contracture** – palmar fascia (ring/little finger).

## 12.4 Amputation

- Causes: **PVD/diabetes (most common)**, trauma, tumours, infection (gangrene).
- **Post-op care**:
 - Watch **haemorrhage** – keep **tourniquet at bedside**
 - **Elevate stump on pillow only first 24 hrs** (oedema ↓) – **after 24–48 hrs, do NOT keep on pillow** (**hip flexion contracture**)
 - **Prone position 20–30 min, 3–4 times/day** (above-knee amputation) → hip flexion contracture prevention
 - **No pillow between thighs** (abduction contracture); don't let stump hang over bed edge
 - **Stump bandaging – figure-of-8 elastic bandage**, **distal to proximal** (cone shape for prosthesis), re-wrap every 4–6 hrs; shrinker sock
 - **Stump toughening** – push stump against soft then harder surface
 - **Phantom limb pain/sensation** – **real**; acknowledge; analgesics, gabapentin, mirror therapy, TENS
 - Body image, rehab, prosthesis fitting
- Exercise – strengthening arm muscles (**triceps**) for crutches; quadriceps setting.

## 12.5 Mobility Aids

:::key Crutches, Cane & Walker – key points
**Crutches**:
- Length: **2–3 finger widths (~5 cm / 1.5–2 inch) below axilla**; handgrip so **elbow flexed 20–30°**; crutch tip **15 cm (6 inch) in front & to the side** of foot.
- **Weight on HANDS – not axilla** (brachial plexus injury – "crutch palsy").
- Gaits: **4-point** (most stable; partial weight both legs – R crutch, L foot, L crutch, R foot), **3-point** (non-weight bearing one leg – both crutches + affected leg together, then good leg), **2-point** (faster 4-point), **Swing-to/Swing-through** (paraplegics).
- **Stairs**: **UP – "Up with the good" (good leg first)**; **DOWN – "Down with the bad" (crutches + bad leg first)**. "**Good goes to heaven, bad goes to hell.**"
- Sitting: back up to chair, both crutches in hand on **affected side**, lower down.

**Cane**: held in **hand OPPOSITE (unaffected/strong side) to affected leg**; top at **greater trochanter** level, elbow 15–30° flexed; move **cane + affected leg together**, then unaffected leg.

**Walker**: lift & move walker **15–20 cm (6–8 inch)** ahead → step **affected leg** into walker → then unaffected leg; elbows flexed 15–30°; **never use walker on stairs**.
:::
