## 17.1 Eye – Applied Anatomy

@fig eye_anatomy.svg | Eye ke parts – kaunsa part kis disease se juda hai

:::key Eye facts
- 3 layers: **Outer – sclera & cornea** · **Middle (uvea) – choroid, ciliary body, iris** · **Inner – retina**.
- **Cornea** – transparent, **avascular** (O₂ from air/aqueous); most refractive; **corneal reflex (CN V sensory, CN VII motor)**.
- **Aqueous humour** – made by **ciliary body** → posterior chamber → pupil → anterior chamber → drained via **trabecular meshwork & canal of Schlemm**. Normal **IOP 10–21 mmHg** (measured by **tonometer**).
- **Lens** – biconvex, avascular; **accommodation** (ciliary muscle).
- **Retina** – **rods (dim light, night vision – Vit A/rhodopsin)**, **cones (colour, bright light)**; **macula/fovea – sharpest central vision** (only cones); **optic disc – blind spot** (no rods/cones).
- Muscles: **6 extraocular muscles** – CN III (most), IV (superior oblique), VI (lateral rectus).
- **Lacrimal gland** – upper outer orbit; tears drain via **puncta → nasolacrimal duct → nose**.
:::

### Eye assessment
- **Snellen chart** – **20 feet (6 m)**; normal **6/6 (20/20)**; top number = distance of patient, bottom = distance a normal eye can read; **legal blindness ≤ 20/200 (6/60)** in better eye with correction.
- **Refractive errors**: **Myopia** (near-sighted; image in front of retina – **concave/minus lens**), **Hypermetropia/Hyperopia** (far-sighted; image behind retina – **convex/plus lens**), **Astigmatism** (irregular cornea – cylindrical lens), **Presbyopia** (age-related loss of accommodation, > 40 yrs – reading glasses/convex).
- **Ishihara chart** – colour vision. **Ophthalmoscope** – fundus. **Slit lamp** – anterior segment. **Fluorescein stain** – corneal abrasion/ulcer (green).
- **Mydriatics** (pupil dilate) – **atropine, tropicamide, phenylephrine** – **contraindicated in angle-closure glaucoma**; **Miotics** (pupil constrict) – **pilocarpine** (glaucoma).
- **National Programme for Control of Blindness & Visual Impairment (NPCB&VI, 1976)** – India first country to launch. Most common cause of blindness in India – **Cataract**; most common cause of visual impairment – **refractive errors**.

## 17.2 Cataract

- **Opacity (clouding) of the lens** – most common cause of blindness (**reversible**).
- Types: **Senile (age-related – most common, > 50)**, congenital (rubella), traumatic, secondary (**diabetes, steroids**), radiation, smoking, UV.
- S/S: **painless, gradual blurring of vision**, **glare/ halos at night**, faded colours, **"second sight"** (temporary improvement in near vision), **white/grey pupil (leukocoria)** in mature cataract, **frequent change of glasses**.
- Rx: **Surgery only** – **ECCE / SICS (manual small-incision – common in India camps)**, **Phacoemulsification (ultrasound – smallest incision)** + **IOL (intraocular lens) implant**; ICCE (old).

:::key Post-cataract surgery care & teaching
- **Eye shield/patch** – especially **at night for 1–4 weeks**; dark glasses in day
- **Lie on back or on unoperated side** (not on operated side)
- **Avoid ↑ IOP**: **no bending (head below waist), lifting heavy objects (> 5 kg), straining, coughing, sneezing, vomiting**, rubbing eye, squeezing eyes; stool softener; antiemetics
- **Eye drops** – antibiotic + steroid as ordered; correct technique; hand hygiene
- **Report immediately**: **sudden severe eye pain** (↑IOP/haemorrhage), **↓ vision**, flashes/floaters, increasing redness, discharge (endophthalmitis)
- No eye make-up, swimming, dust; head bath (avoid water in eye) – 2 weeks
- Reading/TV allowed; spectacles after ~4–6 weeks
:::

## 17.3 Glaucoma

:::def Glaucoma
Group of eye diseases with **↑ intraocular pressure (IOP > 21 mmHg)** → **optic nerve damage (cupping)** → **irreversible peripheral vision loss** → blindness. "**Silent thief of sight**". Most common cause of **irreversible** blindness.
:::

| Feature | **Open-angle (Chronic / POAG)** – most common (~90 % west) | **Angle-closure (Acute / Narrow-angle)** – emergency |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanism | Trabecular meshwork slowly blocked (drainage ↓) | **Iris blocks angle suddenly** (pupil dilation – dark room, mydriatics, stress, anticholinergics) |
| Onset | **Slow, painless, gradual** | **Sudden** |
| Vision | **Gradual loss of peripheral vision → "tunnel vision"** | **Sudden blurred vision, halos (rainbow) around lights** |
| Pain | **Painless** | **Severe eye pain, headache**, nausea/vomiting |
| Eye | Normal looking | **Red eye, fixed mid-dilated pupil, cloudy (steamy) cornea**, rock-hard eye |
| IOP | Moderately ↑ (22–32) | **Very high (> 30–50)** |
| Rx | Lifelong eye drops; laser trabeculoplasty; **trabeculectomy** | **Emergency – IV acetazolamide/ mannitol, pilocarpine**, β-blocker drops → **laser peripheral iridotomy** |

:::warn Glaucoma drugs & teaching
- **Timolol** (β-blocker drop) – ↓ aqueous production; **systemic effects – bradycardia, bronchospasm (avoid in asthma/COPD)**
- **Pilocarpine** (miotic) – ↑ outflow; **poor night vision, blurred vision, brow ache**
- **Latanoprost** (prostaglandin) – ↑ outflow; **iris/eyelash darkening**, longer lashes
- **Acetazolamide** (oral/IV carbonic anhydrase inhibitor) – **hypokalaemia**, paresthesia, kidney stones; sulfa allergy
- **Lifelong treatment** – glaucoma is **controlled, not cured**; lost vision cannot be restored
- **Punctal occlusion** (press inner canthus 1–2 min) after drops → systemic absorption ↓
- **Avoid atropine/ mydriatics & anticholinergics** (antihistamines, decongestants); avoid ↑ IOP activities; yearly eye check after 40 (tonometry)
:::

## 17.4 Other Eye Disorders

- **Retinal detachment** – separation of retina from choroid; S/S: **sudden painless flashes of light (photopsia), floaters ("spots/cobwebs"), "curtain/ shadow/ veil" coming over field of vision** → **emergency**. Rx: **laser photocoagulation, cryopexy, scleral buckling, vitrectomy, pneumatic retinopexy (gas bubble – specific head positioning, e.g. face-down, so bubble presses retina; no air travel/high altitude till gas absorbed)**; bed rest, eye patches, avoid jerky head movements.
- **Macular degeneration (AMD)** – elderly; **loss of central vision** (peripheral intact), **distorted straight lines (Amsler grid)**; **dry (most common)** – antioxidants, **wet** – anti-VEGF injections.
- **Diabetic retinopathy** – microaneurysms, haemorrhages, neovascularization; yearly fundus exam; laser.
- **Conjunctivitis ("pink eye")** – bacterial (purulent discharge, lids stuck in morning), viral (watery, highly contagious – adenovirus), allergic (itching); **don't patch**; hand hygiene, **separate towels**, warm/cold compress, antibiotic drops (bacterial); clean from **inner to outer canthus**.
- **Trachoma** – *Chlamydia trachomatis*; leading infectious cause of blindness; **SAFE strategy** (Surgery, Antibiotics – azithromycin, Facial cleanliness, Environmental improvement); India declared **trachoma-free (2024, WHO)**.
- **Hordeolum (stye)** – painful, acute infection of eyelash gland (external) → **warm compresses**; **Chalazion** – painless chronic granuloma (meibomian).
- **Blepharitis** – lid margin inflammation – lid hygiene.
- **Keratitis/ Corneal ulcer** – pain, photophobia; contact lens users; don't patch (bacterial).
- **Uveitis/ iritis** – painful red eye, photophobia, small pupil.
- **Xerophthalmia (Vitamin A deficiency)** – **night blindness (first symptom)** → conjunctival xerosis → **Bitot's spots** → corneal xerosis → keratomalacia. Prophylaxis: **Vit A 1 lakh IU at 9 months, then 2 lakh IU every 6 months till 5 yrs** (total 9 doses).
- **Strabismus (squint)** – patching good eye (amblyopia); **Ptosis** – CN III, MG.

### Eye injuries – first aid
- **Chemical burn** – **immediate copious irrigation with water/NS for at least 15–30 min** (most important – before anything else), irrigate **from inner to outer canthus**, check pH (7), evert lids.
- **Foreign body** – don't rub; flush; **penetrating object – DON'T remove**, **stabilize object (paper cup over it)**, **patch BOTH eyes** (sympathetic movement ↓), **no pressure**, HOB 30–45°, refer.
- **Hyphaema (blood in anterior chamber)** – bed rest, **HOB 30–45°**, eye shield, no aspirin.
- **Blunt injury** – ice packs (cold compress).

### Eye drops/ointment instillation
- Hand hygiene; patient **head tilted back, looks up**; **pull lower lid down** → instil into **lower conjunctival sac (centre/outer third)** – **never directly on cornea**; dropper 1–2 cm above, don't touch eye.
- **Close eye gently (don't squeeze) & press inner canthus (nasolacrimal duct) 1–2 min**.
- Multiple drops – **wait 3–5 min between different drops**; **drops before ointment** (ointment last – wait 10 min).
- Ointment – thin ribbon from **inner to outer canthus**.
- **Contact lens** – remove before most drops; artificial eye – remove, clean.

## 17.5 Ear

@fig ear_anatomy.svg | Ear ke 3 parts – external, middle aur inner

:::key Ear facts
- **External**: pinna, external auditory canal (2.5 cm, S-shaped; **cerumen/wax**), tympanic membrane (**pearly grey, cone of light at 5 o'clock (right) / 7 o'clock (left)**).
- **Middle**: ossicles – **malleus, incus, stapes** (stapes – smallest bone; **footplate in oval window**), **Eustachian tube** (pressure equalization; children – **short, straight, horizontal** → otitis media).
- **Inner (labyrinth)**: **cochlea (hearing – organ of Corti)**, **vestibule & semicircular canals (balance)**; CN VIII.
- Sound conduction: pinna → canal → TM → ossicles → oval window → cochlea → CN VIII → **temporal lobe**.
:::

### Hearing tests (tuning fork 512 Hz)
| Test | Normal | Conductive loss | Sensorineural loss |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rinne** (mastoid vs in front of ear) | **AC > BC (Rinne positive)** | **BC > AC (negative)** in affected ear | AC > BC (positive) but both reduced |
| **Weber** (vertex/forehead) | Heard equally (midline) | **Lateralizes to affected (bad) ear** | **Lateralizes to normal (good) ear** |

- **Conductive** hearing loss – external/middle ear problem (wax, otitis media, otosclerosis, perforation). **Sensorineural** – inner ear/ CN VIII (presbycusis – age, **noise-induced**, **ototoxic drugs – aminoglycosides (gentamicin, streptomycin), loop diuretics (furosemide), aspirin (high dose – tinnitus), cisplatin, vancomycin, quinine**).
- **Audiometry** – frequency & intensity; **decibels**; **> 85 dB** prolonged → damage.
- **Presbycusis** – age-related, **high-frequency** loss first.

### Communicating with hearing-impaired
Face the patient, **good light on your face**, **speak slowly & clearly in normal/low pitch (don't shout)**, reduce background noise, use gestures/writing, rephrase (don't just repeat), get attention first (touch), check hearing aid battery.

### Ear disorders
- **Impacted cerumen** – **ear irrigation**: **body-temperature water (37 °C)** (cold/hot → **vertigo**), stream directed **towards upper/posterior canal wall** (not at TM), patient sitting, kidney tray under ear, **contraindicated if perforation/ infection**; softening drops (olive oil/ ceruminolytic) 2–3 days before. **No cotton buds** inside canal.
- **Ear drops**: warm to body temperature; **adult/child > 3 yrs – pinna UP & BACK**; **child < 3 yrs – DOWN & BACK**; lie on unaffected side **5 min** after.
- **Otitis externa (swimmer's ear)** – canal infection; **pain on moving pinna/tragus**; drops, keep dry.
- **Acute otitis media (AOM)** – middle ear infection after URI; **children** (Eustachian tube); **ear pain (ear pulling/rubbing in infants)**, fever, **red bulging TM**; rupture → discharge & pain relief. Rx: **amoxicillin**, analgesics; **Myringotomy + grommets (tympanostomy tubes)** for recurrent/ chronic effusion – keep ears dry (ear plugs).
- **Chronic suppurative otitis media (CSOM)** – persistent discharge through perforated TM; **cholesteatoma** (unsafe – erodes bone); **mastoiditis**; Rx – tympanoplasty, mastoidectomy.
- **Mastoiditis** – pain, swelling, redness behind ear, ear pushed forward; IV antibiotics, **mastoidectomy** (watch **facial nerve – CN VII**, vertigo).
- **Otosclerosis** – **stapes fixed (spongy bone)** → **progressive conductive hearing loss**, young women, hereditary; **Paracusis Willisii** (hears better in noise); Rx – **stapedectomy** (post-op: **avoid nose blowing (open mouth sneeze), bending, straining, air travel, head jerks** for weeks; vertigo common – move slowly; keep ear dry; lie flat with operated ear up as per surgeon).
- **Ménière's disease** – **↑ endolymph (endolymphatic hydrops)**; **TRIAD: episodic vertigo + tinnitus + fluctuating sensorineural hearing loss** + **aural fullness**; attacks hours. Rx: **low-sodium diet (< 2 g/day)**, diuretics, avoid **caffeine, alcohol, tobacco, MSG**; **meclizine, antiemetics**, diazepam during attack; **during attack – lie still in quiet dark room, keep eyes focused on fixed point**, side rails up, fall prevention; surgery – labyrinthectomy.
- **BPPV** – brief vertigo with head position change (otoconia); **Epley manoeuvre**.
- **Labyrinthitis** – vertigo + hearing loss after viral infection.
- **Acoustic neuroma (vestibular schwannoma)** – CN VIII benign tumour; unilateral sensorineural loss, tinnitus.

## 17.6 Nose & Throat

- **Epistaxis (nosebleed)** – most common site **anterior septum – Little's area (Kiesselbach's plexus)**; causes – trauma (nose picking), dry air, **hypertension**, anticoagulants, bleeding disorders.

:::key Epistaxis first aid
- **Sit upright & lean FORWARD** (blood mouth me nahi jayega, aspiration/vomiting ↓) – **NOT head back**
- **Pinch soft lower part of nose (both nostrils) firmly for 10–15 min** continuously; breathe through mouth
- **Ice/cold compress** on nose bridge/ forehead; spit blood, don't swallow
- Persistent → **anterior nasal packing** (ribbon gauze with vaseline/ BIPP), cautery (silver nitrate); posterior bleed → posterior pack/ Foley balloon (**monitor airway & SpO₂**)
- After: **avoid nose blowing, picking, strenuous activity, hot drinks, bending, lifting for 1 week**; humidifier; no aspirin
:::

- **Rhinitis** – allergic (sneezing, itching, watery discharge) – antihistamines, nasal steroids; **avoid overuse of decongestant sprays (> 3–5 days → rebound congestion – rhinitis medicamentosa)**.
- **Sinusitis** – facial pain/pressure (worse bending), purulent discharge, fever; **maxillary sinus most common** (adults); steam inhalation, decongestants, antibiotics, **FESS** (functional endoscopic sinus surgery).
- **Deviated nasal septum** – **septoplasty/ SMR** → nasal packing 24–48 hrs (mouth breathing – oral care, humidifier), **semi-Fowler's**, ice to nose, watch **frequent swallowing (posterior bleeding)**, **no nose blowing** 1–2 weeks, sneeze with mouth open.
- **Nasal polyps** – aspirin sensitivity (Samter's triad – asthma + polyps + aspirin sensitivity).
- **Nasal fracture** – ice, check septal haematoma, CSF leak.
- **Pharyngitis** – mostly viral; **strep throat (GABHS)** → rheumatic fever/ GN risk → full course penicillin.
- **Tonsillitis** – sore throat, fever, dysphagia, enlarged red tonsils with exudate; Rx antibiotics; **Tonsillectomy** – indications: recurrent (≥ 7/yr in 1 yr, ≥ 5/yr for 2 yrs, ≥ 3/yr for 3 yrs), **peritonsillar abscess (quinsy)**, obstructive sleep apnoea.

:::warn Post-tonsillectomy care
- Position: **prone/ side-lying (semi-prone) with head turned to side** until fully awake (drainage of secretions); then **semi-Fowler's**
- **Haemorrhage – most important complication** (first 24 hrs – primary/reactionary; **5–10 days – secondary, when scab/ slough separates**)
- **Earliest sign of bleeding = frequent swallowing** (also clearing throat, restlessness, ↑ pulse, vomiting bright red/ dark blood)
- **Ice collar**, cold fluids, **ice chips/ popsicles**, soft cool foods (ice cream, custard, jelly)
- **Avoid**: **red/brown coloured fluids** (confuse with blood), **citrus juices** (irritate), **hot/spicy/rough foods**, **milk products initially** (thicken mucus – controversial), **straws** (suction → clot dislodge), **gargling, vigorous coughing, clearing throat, nose blowing**
- Analgesics – **paracetamol (NO aspirin/ NSAIDs – bleeding)**
- Throat pain worse around day 4–8; **referred ear pain** common; white membrane/ scab normal; halitosis
:::

- **Peritonsillar abscess (quinsy)** – **trismus, "hot potato voice"**, uvula deviation; drainage.
- **Laryngitis** – voice rest; **Acute epiglottitis** – *H. influenzae b*, children – **drooling, dysphagia, distressed, tripod position, high fever** → **don't examine throat with tongue depressor** (laryngospasm), keep calm, airway equipment ready (emergency intubation/ tracheostomy).
- **Obstructive sleep apnoea** – snoring, daytime sleepiness, obesity → **CPAP**, weight loss.
- **Laryngeal cancer & laryngectomy** – see oncology chapter.
- **Foreign body airway obstruction (choking)** – universal sign (hands at throat); conscious adult – **abdominal thrusts (Heimlich manoeuvre)**; pregnant/ obese – **chest thrusts**; **infant – 5 back blows + 5 chest thrusts** (no abdominal thrusts); unconscious – CPR (look in mouth before breaths; no blind finger sweep).
