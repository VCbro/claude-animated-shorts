# Fluid, Electrolyte & Acid-Base Balance
<!-- desc: Body fluids, IV fluids, Na, K, Ca, Mg imbalances, ABG analysis, I/O chart -->

## 2.1 Body Fluid Compartments

:::key Body water – yaad rakho
- Adult body weight ka **~60 %** paani (newborn ~75–80 %, elderly ~45–50 %; female me fat zyada → paani kam).
- **ICF (Intracellular fluid)** = 2/3 (40 % body wt) → main ion **K⁺** (and PO₄³⁻, Mg²⁺)
- **ECF (Extracellular fluid)** = 1/3 (20 % body wt) → main ion **Na⁺** (and Cl⁻, HCO₃⁻)
    - ECF = Interstitial (15 %) + Intravascular/plasma (5 %) + Transcellular (CSF, pleural, synovial)
- Normal **daily intake ≈ 2500 ml** = Output (Urine 1500 ml + insensible loss via skin & lungs ~900 ml + faeces 100 ml).
- Normal urine output = **0.5–1 ml/kg/hr** (minimum **30 ml/hr**). <400 ml/day = **Oliguria**; <100 ml/day = **Anuria**; >3000 ml/day = **Polyuria**.
:::

### Fluid movement mechanisms
| Process | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Diffusion** | Solute high → low concentration (no energy) |
| **Osmosis** | **Water** low solute → high solute concentration (semi-permeable membrane) |
| **Filtration** | Hydrostatic pressure se fluid ka movement (capillary, glomerulus) |
| **Active transport** | Energy (ATP) use, low → high; e.g. **Na⁺-K⁺ pump** (3 Na out, 2 K in) |

- Normal **serum osmolality = 275–295 mOsm/kg**.
- **Oncotic (colloid osmotic) pressure** – mainly **albumin** se; albumin kam → oedema (cirrhosis, nephrotic syndrome).

## 2.2 IV Fluids

@fig iv_fluid_tonicity.svg | Isotonic, Hypotonic, Hypertonic fluid ka RBC par asar

| Type | Examples | Use | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isotonic** (≈ 290 mOsm) | 0.9 % NaCl (NS), Ringer's Lactate (RL), D5W* | Hypovolaemia, shock, blood transfusion (**only NS**), burns (RL) | Fluid overload in HF/renal failure |
| **Hypotonic** | 0.45 % NaCl, 0.33 % NaCl, 2.5 % dextrose | Cellular dehydration, hypernatraemia, DKA (later) | **↑ICP me contraindicated** (cerebral oedema), burns, trauma |
| **Hypertonic** | 3 % / 5 % NaCl, D10W, D5NS, D5RL, D5½NS | Severe hyponatraemia, cerebral oedema (3 % NS) | Fluid overload, pulmonary oedema; 3 % NS **slowly** & in ICU |
| **Colloids** | Albumin 5 %/25 %, Dextran, Hetastarch, Plasma | Plasma volume expander | Allergy, bleeding (dextran) |

:::tip D5W ka confusion
D5W bag me **isotonic** hota hai, lekin body me dextrose metabolise ho jata hai aur sirf free water bachta hai – isliye **physiologically hypotonic** ki tarah behave karta hai. Isliye ↑ICP / head injury me D5W nahi dete.
:::

:::warn Blood transfusion ke saath
Blood ke saath **sirf 0.9 % Normal Saline** chalao. Dextrose → RBC haemolysis & clumping; RL ka calcium → clotting.
:::

### IV flow rate calculation
- **Drops/min** = (Volume in ml × Drop factor) ÷ Time in minutes
- Macro-drip set = **15 or 20 drops/ml**; Micro-drip (paediatric) = **60 microdrops/ml** (micro drops/min = ml/hr)
- **ml/hr** = Total volume ÷ hours
- Example: 1000 ml in 8 hrs, drop factor 20 → (1000 × 20) ÷ 480 = **41.6 ≈ 42 drops/min**

:::warn IV therapy complications
| Complication | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Infiltration** | Cool, pale, swollen site, flow slow | Stop, remove cannula, **elevate limb**, warm/cold compress as per fluid |
| **Extravasation** | Vesicant drug (e.g. chemo, dopamine) leak → tissue necrosis | Stop, **aspirate**, antidote (e.g. phentolamine for dopamine/norepinephrine) |
| **Phlebitis** | Red, warm, tender along vein | Remove, **warm compress**, re-site in other limb |
| **Air embolism** | Sudden dyspnoea, chest pain, hypotension | Clamp, **LEFT side + Trendelenburg** (Durant's position), O₂ |
| **Fluid overload** | Crackles, distended neck veins, ↑BP, dyspnoea | Slow IV, **High Fowler's**, diuretic, inform |
:::

## 2.3 Sodium (Na⁺) – Normal 135–145 mEq/L

:::two
<div markdown="1">

### Hyponatraemia (< 135)
**Causes:** Vomiting, diarrhoea, diuretics, **SIADH**, excess water, adrenal insufficiency, heart failure.
**S/S:** Headache, confusion, nausea, muscle cramps, **seizures**, coma (brain oedema).
**Management:**
- Fluid restriction (SIADH)
- Hypertonic saline (3 % NS) in severe cases – **slow correction** (max 8–12 mEq/L in 24 hr)
- Seizure precautions
</div>
<div markdown="1">

### Hypernatraemia (> 145)
**Causes:** Dehydration, **Diabetes insipidus**, excess Na intake, hyperventilation.
**S/S:** **Thirst**, dry sticky mucosa, restlessness, fever, agitation, seizures.
**Management:**
- Hypotonic fluids slowly (0.45 % NS / D5W)
- Treat cause (DI → Desmopressin)
- Daily weight, I/O chart
</div>
:::

:::warn Fast correction ka khatra
Hyponatraemia ko tezi se theek karna → **Osmotic demyelination syndrome** (Central pontine myelinolysis). Hypernatraemia ko tezi se theek karna → **cerebral oedema**.
:::

## 2.4 Potassium (K⁺) – Normal 3.5–5.0 mEq/L

:::two
<div markdown="1">

### Hypokalaemia (< 3.5)
**Causes:** Vomiting, NG suction, **loop/thiazide diuretics** (furosemide), diarrhoea, insulin therapy, alkalosis.
**S/S:** Muscle weakness, leg cramps, **flat T wave, prominent U wave**, ST depression, constipation/paralytic ileus, ↑risk of **digoxin toxicity**.
**Management:** K-rich diet (banana, orange, potato, coconut water, spinach); oral KCl; IV KCl.
</div>
<div markdown="1">

### Hyperkalaemia (> 5.0)
**Causes:** **Renal failure**, K-sparing diuretics (spironolactone), ACE inhibitors, burns/crush injury, **acidosis**, Addison's disease, haemolysed sample.
**S/S:** **Tall peaked T wave**, wide QRS, bradycardia, VF/cardiac arrest, muscle weakness, paresthesia.
**Management:** Calcium gluconate 10 % IV (**heart protect**), **insulin + dextrose**, salbutamol neb, sodium bicarbonate, **Kayexalate** (sodium polystyrene sulfonate), **dialysis**.
</div>
:::

:::warn IV Potassium – Golden rules
- **NEVER IV push / bolus** → cardiac arrest!
- Always **diluted** in IV fluid; peripheral line me max **10 mEq/hr** (ICU monitored up to 20 mEq/hr).
- Check **urine output** first (≥ 30 ml/hr) – "**No pee, no K**".
- Infusion pump use karo; cardiac monitoring.
:::

## 2.5 Calcium (Ca²⁺) – Normal 8.5–10.5 mg/dL (ionised 4.5–5.5)

| | Hypocalcaemia (< 8.5) | Hypercalcaemia (> 10.5) |
|---|---|---|
| **Causes** | **Post-thyroidectomy / parathyroidectomy**, hypoparathyroidism, Vit D deficiency, pancreatitis, massive blood transfusion (citrate), CKD | Hyperparathyroidism, bone **malignancy**, prolonged immobilization, thiazides, excess Vit D |
| **S/S** | **Tetany**, numbness/tingling around mouth, **Chvostek's sign**, **Trousseau's sign**, laryngospasm, seizures, prolonged QT | Muscle weakness, **"Bones, stones, groans, moans"** – bone pain, renal stones, abdominal pain, confusion; shortened QT |
| **Management** | **IV Calcium gluconate 10 %** (slowly), oral Ca + Vit D, seizure precautions | **Fluids (NS)** + loop diuretic, calcitonin, bisphosphonates, mobilize patient |

:::key Chvostek vs Trousseau
- **C**hvostek = **C**heek tap (facial nerve, in front of ear) → facial muscle twitch
- **T**rousseau = BP cuff inflate (above SBP for 3 min) → **carpal spasm** (hand)
Dono = **hypocalcaemia** (and hypomagnesaemia).
:::

## 2.6 Magnesium (Mg²⁺) – Normal 1.5–2.5 mEq/L

- **Hypomagnesaemia**: alcoholism, malnutrition, diarrhoea → tremors, tetany, seizures, arrhythmia (Torsades de pointes). Rx: **Magnesium sulphate** IV.
- **Hypermagnesaemia**: renal failure, excess MgSO₄ (e.g. pre-eclampsia treatment) → **loss of deep tendon reflexes**, respiratory depression, hypotension. Antidote: **Calcium gluconate**.

:::exam MgSO₄ toxicity – monitoring (very common question)
Before each dose check: **Patellar (knee) reflex present**, **RR ≥ 12–16/min**, **Urine output ≥ 30 ml/hr**. First sign of toxicity = **loss of patellar reflex**. Antidote = **10 % Calcium gluconate 10 ml IV**.
:::

### Other values
- **Chloride** 96–106 mEq/L · **Phosphate** 2.5–4.5 mg/dL (Ca ke ulta chalta hai) · **Bicarbonate** 22–26 mEq/L

## 2.7 Fluid Volume Deficit vs Excess

:::vs
<div markdown="1">

**Fluid Volume DEFICIT (Dehydration / Hypovolaemia)**
- Thirst, dry mucosa, **poor skin turgor**
- **Tachycardia, weak thready pulse, hypotension**, postural hypotension
- ↓ urine output, **concentrated urine (↑ specific gravity > 1.030)**
- ↑ Hct, ↑ BUN
- Flat neck veins, weight loss
- Rx: Oral/IV isotonic fluids, I/O, daily weight
</div>
<div markdown="1">

**Fluid Volume EXCESS (Hypervolaemia)**
- **Oedema**, weight gain (1 kg = 1 L fluid)
- **Bounding pulse, hypertension**
- **Distended neck veins (JVD)**, **crackles** in lungs, dyspnoea
- Dilute urine, ↓ Hct, ↓ BUN
- S3 heart sound
- Rx: Fluid & Na restriction, diuretics, **Fowler's**, daily weight
</div>
:::

:::tip Daily weight – best indicator
Fluid status ka **sabse accurate indicator = daily weight** (same time, same scale, same clothes, preferably morning after voiding). **1 kg weight gain ≈ 1 litre fluid retention.**
:::

## 2.8 Acid-Base Balance & ABG

### Normal ABG values
| Parameter | Normal |
|---|---|
| **pH** | **7.35 – 7.45** |
| **PaCO₂** (respiratory component, acid) | **35 – 45 mmHg** |
| **HCO₃⁻** (metabolic component, base) | **22 – 26 mEq/L** |
| **PaO₂** | 80 – 100 mmHg |
| **SaO₂** | 95 – 100 % |
| Base excess | −2 to +2 |

- Body ke 3 buffer systems: (1) **Chemical buffers** (bicarbonate-carbonic acid – fastest, seconds), (2) **Lungs** (minutes – CO₂ exhale), (3) **Kidneys** (hours–days, slowest but most powerful – HCO₃⁻ retain / H⁺ excrete).
- Normal ratio **HCO₃⁻ : H₂CO₃ = 20 : 1**.

:::mnemo ROME – ABG ka trick
**R**espiratory = **O**pposite (pH ↑ to PaCO₂ ↓, pH ↓ to PaCO₂ ↑)
**M**etabolic = **E**qual (pH aur HCO₃⁻ same direction me)
:::

| Disorder | pH | Primary change | Compensation | Common causes | Key signs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Respiratory Acidosis** | ↓ | **PaCO₂ ↑** | Kidney ↑HCO₃⁻ | **COPD**, hypoventilation, opioid/sedative overdose, pneumonia, chest injury | Headache, drowsiness, confusion |
| **Respiratory Alkalosis** | ↑ | **PaCO₂ ↓** | Kidney ↓HCO₃⁻ | **Hyperventilation, anxiety/panic**, high altitude, fever, early salicylate | Light-headed, tingling, **tetany** |
| **Metabolic Acidosis** | ↓ | **HCO₃⁻ ↓** | Lungs ↓PaCO₂ (**Kussmaul**) | **DKA**, renal failure, **diarrhoea**, lactic acidosis (shock), aspirin poisoning | Kussmaul breathing, hyperkalaemia |
| **Metabolic Alkalosis** | ↑ | **HCO₃⁻ ↑** | Lungs ↑PaCO₂ (slow breathing) | **Vomiting, NG suction**, diuretics, antacid excess | Hypokalaemia, muscle cramps |

### ABG interpretation – 3 steps
1. **pH dekho** → < 7.35 Acidosis, > 7.45 Alkalosis
2. **PaCO₂ dekho** → kya pH ke hisaab se *opposite* gaya? → Respiratory cause
3. **HCO₃⁻ dekho** → kya pH ke saath *same direction* me hai? → Metabolic cause
4. **Compensation**: dusra value bhi abnormal ho aur pH normal range me aa jaye = **fully compensated**; pH abhi bhi abnormal = **partially compensated**; dusra value normal = **uncompensated**.

:::exam Solved example
pH 7.30, PaCO₂ 55, HCO₃⁻ 25 → pH ↓ (acidosis), PaCO₂ ↑ (opposite = respiratory), HCO₃⁻ normal → **Uncompensated Respiratory Acidosis** (e.g. COPD exacerbation).
pH 7.48, PaCO₂ 40, HCO₃⁻ 32 → **Uncompensated Metabolic Alkalosis** (e.g. repeated vomiting).
:::

:::tip Diarrhoea vs Vomiting
**Vomiting** → stomach ka acid (HCl) jata hai → **Metabolic Alkalosis**.
**Diarrhoea** → intestine ka bicarbonate jata hai → **Metabolic Acidosis**.
:::

- **Allen's test** – ABG ke liye radial artery puncture se pehle **ulnar artery ki patency** check karne ke liye. After ABG: **5 min pressure** (anticoagulant par 10 min), sample **ice me** aur 15–30 min me lab bhejo, air bubbles hatao.
