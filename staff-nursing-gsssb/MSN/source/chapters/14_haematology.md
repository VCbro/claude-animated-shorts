## 14.1 Blood – Applied Physiology

:::key Blood facts
- Blood volume ≈ **5–6 L** (**7–8 % body weight**, ~70 ml/kg); pH **7.35–7.45**.
- **Plasma 55 %** (90 % water; albumin, globulins, fibrinogen) + **Formed elements 45 %** (RBC, WBC, platelets).
- Haematopoiesis: adult – **red bone marrow** (flat bones, vertebrae, sternum, pelvis); fetus – liver, spleen.
- **RBC lifespan 120 days**; **platelets 7–10 days**; neutrophils ~6 hrs in blood. Old RBCs destroyed in **spleen** ("graveyard of RBCs").
- **Erythropoietin** (kidney) → RBC production; needs **iron, B12, folic acid**, protein.
- **Universal donor = O negative**; **Universal recipient = AB positive**. Plasma: AB = universal plasma donor.
:::

### Normal values (adult)
| Parameter | Normal |
|---|---|
| **Haemoglobin** | **Male 13–17 g/dL; Female 12–15 g/dL** |
| **RBC count** | M 4.5–5.5; F 4.0–5.0 million/mm³ |
| **Haematocrit (PCV)** | M 40–50 %; F 36–46 % (≈ 3 × Hb) |
| **WBC (TLC)** | **4,000 – 11,000 /mm³** |
| Differential | **Neutrophils 40–75 %**, **Lymphocytes 20–40 %**, Monocytes 2–8 %, **Eosinophils 1–6 %** (allergy, worms), Basophils 0–1 % |
| **Platelets** | **1.5 – 4.5 lakh /mm³ (150,000–450,000)** |
| **MCV** | 80 – 100 fL (micro < 80, macro > 100) |
| **Reticulocytes** | 0.5 – 2.5 % |
| **ESR** (Westergren) | M 0–15; F 0–20 mm/hr |
| **Bleeding time** | 2 – 7 min (Ivy) |
| **Clotting time** | 4 – 10 min |
| **PT** | 11 – 13.5 sec; **INR 0.8 – 1.2** |
| **aPTT** | 25 – 40 sec |
| Serum iron | 60 – 170 µg/dL; **Ferritin** 20–250 (M) / 10–120 (F) ng/ml |
| Fibrinogen | 200 – 400 mg/dL |

- **Neutropenia** = ANC **< 1500** (severe < 500) → **neutropenic precautions**.
- **Thrombocytopenia** < 1.5 lakh; **spontaneous bleeding risk < 20,000**; < 50,000 → bleeding precautions.

"**N**ever **L**et **M**onkeys **E**at **B**ananas" → **N**eutrophils > **L**ymphocytes > **M**onocytes > **E**osinophils > **B**asophils
:::

## 14.2 Anaemia

:::def Anaemia
Haemoglobin below normal (**WHO: < 13 g/dL men, < 12 g/dL non-pregnant women, < 11 g/dL pregnant women & children 6–59 months**). **Severe anaemia (WHO)**: Hb **< 7 g/dL** in pregnant women & children; **< 8 g/dL** in non-pregnant adults.
General S/S: **fatigue (most common)**, weakness, **pallor** (conjunctiva, palms, nail beds), dyspnoea on exertion, tachycardia, palpitation, dizziness, headache, cold intolerance; severe → HF.
:::

| Type | Cause / Mechanism | Key features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Iron deficiency** (**most common** worldwide & India) | **Blood loss** (menstruation, GI – hookworm, ulcer, cancer), poor intake, pregnancy, malabsorption | **Microcytic hypochromic**, ↓ ferritin (best test), ↑ TIBC; **koilonychia (spoon nails)**, **pica** (eating mud/ice), angular stomatitis, glossitis, brittle hair | **Oral iron (ferrous sulphate)**, IV iron (iron sucrose, ferric carboxymaltose) / IM (**Z-track**), iron-rich diet |
| **Megaloblastic – Vit B12 deficiency** | Strict vegetarian, **lack of intrinsic factor (pernicious anaemia – autoimmune)**, gastrectomy, ileal resection | **Macrocytic**; **smooth beefy red sore tongue (glossitis)**, **neurological signs (paresthesia, ataxia, subacute combined degeneration)**, mild jaundice; **Schilling test** | **Vit B12 IM (cyanocobalamin) – lifelong in pernicious anaemia** |
| **Megaloblastic – Folic acid deficiency** | Poor diet, **alcoholism**, pregnancy, drugs (methotrexate, phenytoin) | Macrocytic, **no neurological signs** | **Folic acid** oral; green leafy vegetables |
| **Aplastic anaemia** | **Bone marrow failure** – drugs (**chloramphenicol**, chemo), radiation, benzene, viral, idiopathic | **Pancytopenia** (↓ RBC + WBC + platelets) → anaemia + infection + bleeding | Remove cause, **bone marrow transplant**, immunosuppression, transfusion; neutropenic & bleeding precautions |
| **Haemolytic** | RBC destruction – sickle cell, thalassaemia, G6PD deficiency, autoimmune, malaria, transfusion reaction | **Jaundice (unconjugated)**, splenomegaly, ↑ reticulocytes, dark urine | Treat cause, folic acid, splenectomy |
| **Anaemia of chronic disease / CKD** | Chronic infection, inflammation, CKD (↓ EPO) | Normocytic | Treat cause, **erythropoietin** |

:::warn Iron therapy – teaching
- **Oral iron**: take on **empty stomach (1 hr before meals)** for best absorption – if GI upset, take with food; **with Vitamin C / orange juice** (↑ absorption); **avoid with milk, tea/coffee, antacids, calcium, eggs** (↓ absorption); **liquid iron through straw** (teeth staining); **black/dark green stools (normal)**; constipation (fibre, fluids); continue **2–3 months (up to 6)** after Hb normal to replenish stores.
- **IM iron dextran**: **Z-track technique**, deep IM upper outer quadrant gluteal, **separate needle** for drawing & injecting, **don't massage**; test dose (anaphylaxis).
- Iron poisoning antidote: **Deferoxamine (Desferrioxamine)**.
- **Anaemia Mukt Bharat (2018)** – **6×6×6 strategy**; IFA supplementation: pregnant/lactating women **60 mg elemental iron + 500 µg folic acid daily for 180 days** (red tablet).
:::

### Sickle Cell Anaemia
- **Autosomal recessive**; abnormal **HbS** (valine replaces glutamic acid at position 6 of β chain); in India – tribal populations (Gujarat, MP, Maharashtra, Chhattisgarh, Odisha). **National Sickle Cell Anaemia Elimination Mission – launched 1 July 2023** (Shahdol, MP) – target **2047**.
- RBCs **sickle under hypoxia, dehydration, cold, infection, high altitude, stress, acidosis** → **vaso-occlusive crisis** (severe pain – bones, joints, abdomen, chest), **acute chest syndrome**, stroke, **splenic sequestration**, aplastic crisis, priapism, leg ulcers, **auto-splenectomy** (infections – pneumococcal), **hand-foot syndrome (dactylitis)** – infants.
- Diagnosis: **sickling test**, **Hb electrophoresis** (confirmatory), HPLC.
- **Crisis management**: **H**ydration (IV fluids), **O**xygen, **P**ain relief (opioids – morphine; no meperidine), rest, warmth (**no cold compress** – vasoconstriction), antibiotics, transfusion; **Hydroxyurea** (↑ HbF – long-term), folic acid, vaccines (pneumococcal, Hib, meningococcal), penicillin prophylaxis in children.
- Avoid: dehydration, high altitude, unpressurized flights, strenuous exercise, cold, smoking, tight clothing.

### Thalassaemia
- **Autosomal recessive** – ↓ synthesis of globin chains (**β-thalassaemia major – Cooley's anaemia**, most common severe in India – Sindhi, Punjabi, Gujarati, Bengali communities).
- Starts at **6 months** age (HbF → HbA switch); **severe microcytic anaemia**, **hepatosplenomegaly**, **chipmunk face (frontal bossing, maxillary prominence)**, growth retardation, "hair-on-end" skull X-ray; **↑ HbF** on electrophoresis.
- Rx: **regular blood transfusion (every 2–4 weeks, keep Hb > 9–10)** → **iron overload (haemosiderosis)** → **iron chelation – Deferoxamine (SC/IV), Deferasirox, Deferiprone (oral)**; folic acid; splenectomy; **bone marrow (stem cell) transplant – only cure**; genetic counselling & **premarital/antenatal screening (HbA2 > 3.5 % = trait)**.
- **8 May = World Thalassaemia Day**.

### Polycythaemia vera
- Myeloproliferative – **↑ RBC, WBC, platelets**; ↑ Hct, hyperviscosity → thrombosis; **ruddy (plethoric) face**, **pruritus after hot bath**, headache, splenomegaly. Rx: **phlebotomy** (Hct < 45 %), hydroxyurea, aspirin; fluids; avoid iron. *Secondary polycythaemia* – COPD, high altitude, smoking.

## 14.3 White Blood Cell Disorders

### Leukaemia
- Malignant proliferation of WBCs in bone marrow → **↓ normal cells** → **anaemia (fatigue), infection (fever – most common cause of death), bleeding (thrombocytopenia)** + bone pain, hepatosplenomegaly, lymphadenopathy, weight loss.
- **ALL** – most common **childhood** cancer (2–5 yrs), best prognosis (CNS involvement – intrathecal chemo).
- **AML** – adults; **Auer rods**.
- **CLL** – most common in **elderly** (> 60) in western; slow; often asymptomatic.
- **CML** – adults 40–60; **Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22)** – BCR-ABL → **Imatinib** (targeted therapy).
- Diagnosis: **bone marrow aspiration & biopsy** (definitive) – site: **posterior superior iliac crest** (adults; sternum alternative), **tibia in infants**; after: **pressure 5–10 min**, lie on site/supine 30–60 min, watch bleeding.
- Rx: chemotherapy (induction → consolidation → maintenance), **bone marrow / stem cell transplant**, targeted therapy, supportive.

:::key Neutropenic (protective/reverse) precautions
- **Private room** (positive pressure/HEPA if available), **strict hand hygiene** (most important)
- **No fresh flowers/plants**, no standing water; **no fresh fruits/vegetables (raw), undercooked meat, unpasteurized dairy** – **low-microbial diet**
- No visitors with infection, no children recently vaccinated with live vaccines
- Avoid **rectal temperature, suppositories, enemas, IM injections** (as possible)
- Monitor **temperature (fever ≥ 38 °C = emergency – febrile neutropenia)** – signs of infection may be absent (no pus)
- Mask for patient outside room; dedicated equipment; daily bath; oral care with soft brush
- **G-CSF (filgrastim)** – ↑ neutrophil production (bone pain s/e)
:::

:::key Bleeding (thrombocytopenia) precautions
- **Soft toothbrush/ toothette**, **electric razor**; no dental floss
- **Avoid IM/SC injections**, rectal temperature, enemas, suppositories; apply **pressure 5–10 min** after venipuncture
- **No aspirin / NSAIDs**, alcohol; stool softeners (no straining)
- Avoid **blowing nose forcefully**, contact sports; padded side rails
- Check urine, stool, emesis for blood; petechiae, gum bleeding; neuro checks (intracranial bleed)
- **Platelet transfusion** if < 10,000–20,000 or bleeding
:::

### Lymphoma
| | **Hodgkin's lymphoma** | **Non-Hodgkin's lymphoma (NHL)** |
|---|---|---|
| Hallmark | **Reed-Sternberg cells** | No RS cells; B-cell mostly |
| Age | **Bimodal – 15–35 & > 55 yrs** | Older; more common overall |
| Spread | **Contiguous (orderly) – cervical lymph node first** (painless, rubbery) | Non-contiguous, extranodal |
| Features | **"B symptoms"** – fever, **night sweats**, **weight loss > 10 %**; **pruritus**; **pain in nodes after alcohol** | Painless lymphadenopathy |
| Prognosis | **Good (curable – ABVD chemo)** | Variable |

- Staging: **Ann Arbor (I–IV)**; EBV associated.
- **Multiple myeloma** – plasma cell malignancy, elderly; **CRAB – hyperCalcaemia, Renal failure, Anaemia, Bone lesions (punched-out)**; **Bence-Jones protein** in urine; M-spike; fluids 3–4 L/day, fracture precautions.

## 14.4 Bleeding Disorders

| Disorder | Defect | Features | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Haemophilia A** (80 %) | **Factor VIII deficiency**; **X-linked recessive** – males affected, females carriers | **Haemarthrosis (bleeding into joints – knees) – most common**, prolonged bleeding after injury/circumcision/dental, deep muscle haematoma, **↑ aPTT**, normal PT & platelets & BT | **Factor VIII concentrate**, **desmopressin (DDAVP)** – mild; **RICE for joint bleed**, no IM, no aspirin; soft toothbrush; non-contact sports (swimming) |
| **Haemophilia B (Christmas disease)** | **Factor IX deficiency**, X-linked | Same | Factor IX concentrate |
| **Von Willebrand disease** | vWF deficiency (most common inherited bleeding disorder; autosomal dominant) | Mucosal bleeding, menorrhagia, ↑ BT | Desmopressin, vWF concentrate |
| **ITP (Immune thrombocytopenic purpura)** | **Autoimmune destruction of platelets** (children – after viral infection, acute; adults – chronic, women) | Petechiae, purpura, epistaxis, gum bleeding; ↓ platelets, normal PT/aPTT | **Corticosteroids**, IVIG, **splenectomy**, platelet transfusion (life-threatening bleed only) |
| **DIC (Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation)** | **Secondary** – sepsis (most common), trauma, obstetric (abruptio placentae, amniotic embolism, IUD), cancer, burns; **simultaneous widespread clotting & bleeding** (factors consumed) | **Bleeding from all sites – IV sites, wounds, mucosa, oozing**, petechiae, organ ischaemia; **↓ platelets, ↓ fibrinogen, ↑ PT/aPTT, ↑ D-dimer/FDP** (most specific) | **Treat underlying cause (most important)**, FFP, cryoprecipitate (fibrinogen), platelets, heparin (selected) |
| **Vitamin K deficiency** | ↓ factors II, VII, IX, X | Newborn (haemorrhagic disease of newborn), malabsorption, warfarin | **Vitamin K** (newborn – 1 mg IM at birth) |

## 14.5 Blood Transfusion

### Blood components
| Component | Storage | Shelf-life | Use / Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Whole blood** | **2–6 °C** | **35 days (CPDA-1)**; 21 days (CPD/ACD) | Acute massive blood loss |
| **Packed RBCs (PRBC)** | 2–6 °C | 35–42 days (SAGM 42) | **Anaemia**; 1 unit ↑ **Hb by ~1 g/dL** (Hct by 3 %) |
| **Platelet concentrate** | **20–24 °C (room temp) with continuous gentle agitation** | **5 days** | Thrombocytopenia; 1 unit ↑ ~5,000–10,000; **don't refrigerate**; infuse fast (~30 min) |
| **Fresh frozen plasma (FFP)** | **≤ −18 °C / −30 °C** | **1 year** | Clotting factors – DIC, warfarin reversal, liver disease; **thaw at 30–37 °C**, use within 6 hrs (24 hr if refrigerated); ABO compatible |
| **Cryoprecipitate** | ≤ −18 °C | 1 year | **Fibrinogen, Factor VIII, vWF, XIII** – DIC, haemophilia A, vWD |
| **Albumin** | Room temp | 3–5 years | Hypovolaemia, hypoalbuminaemia, burns |

:::key Transfusion – nursing procedure
- **Informed consent**; verify **doctor's order**; check **blood group, cross-match, Rh**, unit number, expiry, patient ID – **2 nurses at bedside** (double check)
- **Baseline vitals** before transfusion; start within **30 min of receiving from blood bank**; **complete one unit within 4 hrs** (max)
- **Needle/cannula 18–20 G** (adult; 22–24 child)
- **Blood administration set with filter (170–260 micron)**, prime with **0.9 % NS only**
- **No medications** in the blood line; **don't warm** in microwave/hot water (blood warmer only for massive/rapid transfusion)
- **Start slowly – 2 ml/min (~15 drops/min) for first 15 min, stay with patient** (most reactions occur in first 15 min / 50 ml) → then increase rate
- **Vitals: before, after 15 min, then every 30 min–1 hr, and after completion**
- Don't refrigerate blood in ward fridge; return unused within 30 min
:::

### Transfusion reactions
| Reaction | Cause / Timing | Signs | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Acute haemolytic** (most dangerous) | **ABO incompatibility** – **clerical error (most common)**; within **minutes (first 15 min)** | **Fever, chills, flank / low back pain**, chest pain, dyspnoea, hypotension, tachycardia, **haemoglobinuria (dark/red urine)**, burning along vein, DIC, AKI, shock | **STOP transfusion immediately** |
| **Febrile non-haemolytic** (most common) | Antibodies against donor WBC | Fever, chills | Stop, antipyretic; **leukocyte-reduced** blood next time |
| **Allergic (mild)** | Plasma proteins | **Urticaria, itching**, flushing | Stop/slow, **antihistamine**, may restart if mild (as ordered) |
| **Anaphylactic** | IgA deficient recipient | Bronchospasm, hypotension, shock | Stop, **adrenaline**, airway |
| **Circulatory overload (TACO)** | Too fast/too much – elderly, HF | Dyspnoea, **crackles**, cough, ↑ BP, **JVD** | Slow/stop, **upright position**, diuretic, O₂ |
| **TRALI** | Antibodies in donor plasma; within 6 hrs | Acute respiratory distress, hypoxaemia, **non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema**, hypotension | Stop, respiratory support |
| **Bacterial (septic)** | Contaminated unit (platelets – room temp) | High fever, rigors, hypotension, shock | Stop, cultures, antibiotics |
| **Massive transfusion complications** | | **Hypocalcaemia (citrate)**, hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, dilutional coagulopathy | Calcium gluconate, blood warmer |
| **Delayed haemolytic** | 2–14 days | Fever, jaundice, ↓ Hb | |

:::warn Transfusion reaction – nursing steps
1. **STOP the transfusion immediately**
2. **Keep IV line open with 0.9 % NS** using **new tubing** (don't flush remaining blood into patient)
3. **Check vitals**, stay with patient, notify doctor & blood bank
4. **Recheck identification** (patient & blood bag)
5. **Send blood bag + tubing + fresh blood sample (EDTA & clotted) + first voided urine** to blood bank/lab
6. Treat symptoms (antihistamine, antipyretic, O₂, adrenaline, diuretic), monitor urine output
7. **Document** & incident report
:::

- **Rh factor**: Rh –ve mother with Rh +ve fetus → **Anti-D immunoglobulin (RhoGAM) 300 µg IM within 72 hrs** of delivery/abortion & at **28 weeks** (antenatal).
- Blood donation: age **18–65 yrs**, weight **≥ 45 kg (350 ml) / ≥ 55 kg (450 ml)**, Hb **≥ 12.5 g/dL**, interval **3 months (men) / 4 months (women)**. **14 June = World Blood Donor Day** (Karl Landsteiner's birthday – discovered ABO groups, 1901). **1 October = National Voluntary Blood Donation Day**.
