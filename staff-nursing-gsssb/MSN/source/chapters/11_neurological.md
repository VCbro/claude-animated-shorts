## 11.1 Applied Anatomy

@fig brain_lobes.svg | Brain ke lobes aur unke kaam

:::key Nervous system – facts
- **CNS** = brain + spinal cord; **PNS** = 12 cranial + **31 pairs spinal nerves**.
- Brain weight ~**1.4 kg**; receives **15–20 % cardiac output**, **20 % of body O₂**; glucose = main fuel.
- Meninges: **Dura mater** (outer) → **Arachnoid** → **Pia mater** (inner). **CSF in subarachnoid space**.
- **CSF**: produced by **choroid plexus** (~500 ml/day), total volume **~150 ml**, normal pressure **60–180 mmH₂O (5–15 mmHg)**, **clear, colourless**; protein 15–45 mg/dL; glucose 50–80 mg/dL (~2/3 of blood glucose).
- Spinal cord ends at **L1–L2** (adult) → **lumbar puncture at L3–L4 / L4–L5** (safe).
- **Circle of Willis** – arterial ring at brain base; **Middle cerebral artery** – most common stroke site.
- Dominant hemisphere (speech) = **left** in most people.
:::

## 11.2 Neurological Assessment

### Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) – Teasdale & Jennett (1974)
| Eye opening (E) | | Verbal response (V) | | Motor response (M) | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Spontaneous | **4** | Oriented | **5** | Obeys commands | **6** |
| To voice/speech | 3 | Confused | 4 | Localizes pain | 5 |
| To pain | 2 | Inappropriate words | 3 | Withdraws from pain | 4 |
| None | 1 | Incomprehensible sounds | 2 | **Abnormal flexion (decorticate)** | 3 |
| | | None | 1 | **Extension (decerebrate)** | 2 |
| | | | | None | 1 |

:::exam GCS – must remember
- Total **3 – 15**. **15 = normal**; **≤ 8 = severe injury / coma → "8 = intubate"**; 9–12 moderate; 13–14 mild.
- Lowest score **3** (not 0). Intubated patient – verbal "T" (e.g. 10T).
- **Decorticate** (flexion – arms flexed to chest, "**to the core**") = cerebral hemisphere damage. **Decerebrate** (extension – arms straight, wrists pronated) = **brainstem damage – worse prognosis**.
- **Most sensitive & earliest indicator of neuro deterioration = change in level of consciousness (LOC)**.
:::

- **Pupils** – **PERRLA** (Pupils Equal, Round, Reactive to Light & Accommodation); normal 2–6 mm. **Unilateral fixed dilated pupil** = **uncal herniation (CN III compression – same side)**; pinpoint = pons lesion/opioids; bilateral fixed dilated = severe hypoxia/brain death.
- **Babinski sign** – sole stroke → **toes fanning & big toe dorsiflexion = positive (abnormal in adults)**, normal in infants < 1–2 yrs → UMN lesion.
- **Romberg test** – feet together, eyes closed → sway = positive (proprioception/cerebellar).
- **Kernig's & Brudzinski's** signs → meningeal irritation.
- **Reflex grading**: 0 absent, 1+ diminished, **2+ normal**, 3+ brisk, 4+ hyperactive with clonus.

### Diagnostic procedures
- **Lumbar puncture**: empty bladder; position – **lateral recumbent, fetal position (knees to chest, chin to chest)** or sitting leaning forward; **L3–L4/L4–L5**; **contraindicated in ↑ICP** (herniation) – check fundus/CT first. After: **lie flat (supine) 4–12 hrs** (post-LP headache prevention), ↑ fluids, check site & legs sensation.
- **CT scan** (first choice in acute stroke/head injury – bleeding rule out), **MRI** (remove metal; pacemaker contraindicated; claustrophobia), **EEG** (seizures – hair wash, **no caffeine** 24–48 hr before, sleep deprivation may be ordered; continue/hold antiepileptics as ordered).
- **Cerebral angiography** – iodine allergy check; after: puncture site & distal pulses, leg straight.

## 11.3 Increased Intracranial Pressure (↑ICP)

- Normal ICP **5–15 mmHg** (0–15); **> 20 mmHg sustained = treat**.
- **Monro-Kellie doctrine**: skull is rigid box with **brain (80 %), blood (10 %), CSF (10 %)** – one ↑ → others must ↓, otherwise ICP ↑.
- **CPP (Cerebral perfusion pressure) = MAP − ICP**; normal **60–100 mmHg** (keep **≥ 60–70**).
- Causes: head injury, haemorrhage, tumour, cerebral oedema, hydrocephalus, meningitis.

:::key Signs of ↑ICP
- **Earliest sign: change in level of consciousness** (restlessness, confusion, drowsiness)
- **Headache** (worse in morning), **projectile vomiting** (without nausea), **papilloedema**
- Pupillary changes (ipsilateral dilated, sluggish), motor weakness, seizures
- **Late sign – Cushing's triad**: **↑ Systolic BP (widened pulse pressure) + Bradycardia + Irregular respiration (Cheyne-Stokes/ bradypnoea)**
- Infants: **bulging fontanelle**, ↑ head circumference, high-pitched cry, **sunset eyes**
:::

:::warn ↑ICP – nursing management
- **HOB 30°**, head in **neutral midline** (no neck flexion/rotation – jugular drainage)
- Avoid **hip flexion > 90°**, **Valsalva (straining, coughing, sneezing)**, **isometric exercise** – stool softeners
- Avoid clustering of nursing activities; **suction < 10 sec** (only when needed), hyperoxygenate
- Quiet dark environment, limit visitors
- **Osmotic diuretic – Mannitol 20 %** (use **filter**; crystals – warm; monitor serum osmolality < 320, I/O), hypertonic saline 3 %
- Maintain **PaCO₂ 35–40** (hyperventilation briefly only in herniation), normothermia (treat fever – ↑ metabolism), seizure prophylaxis
- **Fluid** – **isotonic NS**; avoid hypotonic (D5W) fluids
- Corticosteroids (**dexamethasone**) – for **tumour oedema** (not head injury)
- **No opioids/sedatives that mask LOC** (unless ventilated), avoid restraints (struggling ↑ICP)
:::

## 11.4 Head Injury

- **Scalp** laceration – bleeds profusely. **Skull fracture**: linear, depressed, basilar.
- **Basilar skull fracture**: **Battle's sign** (bruising **behind ear – mastoid**), **Raccoon eyes** (periorbital bruising), **CSF rhinorrhoea/otorrhoea** – **halo/ring sign** on gauze (blood centre, yellow ring), **glucose positive** (dextrostix). **Do NOT** put NG tube (use orogastric), no nasal suction, no packing nose/ear, don't let patient blow nose.
- **Concussion** – temporary loss of consciousness, no structural damage, amnesia; observe 24 hrs.
- **Contusion** – bruise of brain tissue.

| | **Epidural (Extradural) haematoma** | **Subdural haematoma** |
|---|---|---|
| Bleed | **Arterial – middle meningeal artery** (temporal bone fracture) | **Venous – bridging veins** |
| Course | **Brief loss of consciousness → "Lucid interval" → rapid deterioration** | Acute (24–48 hr), subacute, **chronic (weeks – elderly, alcoholics, anticoagulants)** |
| CT | **Biconvex (lens-shaped)** | **Crescent-shaped** |
| Rx | **Emergency craniotomy / burr hole** | Evacuation |

- **Subarachnoid haemorrhage** – ruptured **berry aneurysm** → "**worst headache of my life**" (thunderclap), neck stiffness, photophobia; nimodipine (vasospasm prevention).
- Head injury first priority: **ABC with cervical spine immobilization** (assume C-spine injury in every head injury).

## 11.5 Stroke (CVA – Cerebrovascular Accident / Brain Attack)

:::def Stroke
Sudden loss of brain function due to **disruption of blood supply**. **Ischaemic (~85 %)** – thrombotic (most common; during sleep/rest, atherosclerosis) or embolic (**AF** – most common source; sudden, during activity); **Haemorrhagic (~15 %)** – intracerebral (**hypertension** – most common cause) or subarachnoid.
**TIA (Transient Ischaemic Attack)** – symptoms resolve **within 24 hrs (usually < 1 hr)** without infarction – **warning sign of stroke**.
:::

:::mnemo BE FAST – stroke recognition
**B**alance loss · **E**yes (vision loss) · **F**ace drooping · **A**rm weakness · **S**peech difficulty · **T**ime to call **108** – note the **time of onset / last known well**
:::

### Clinical features (depends on site) – **left vs right hemisphere**
| Left hemisphere stroke | Right hemisphere stroke |
|---|---|
| **Right-sided hemiplegia** | **Left-sided hemiplegia** |
| **Aphasia** (speech problems) | **Spatial-perceptual deficits**, **left neglect** |
| Slow, cautious behaviour | **Impulsive**, poor judgement, denies deficits |
| Depression, anxiety | Short attention span; **safety risk ↑** |

- **Expressive (Broca's) aphasia** – frontal lobe; patient **understands but can't speak** (frustrated). **Receptive (Wernicke's)** – temporal; **fluent but meaningless speech**, can't understand. **Global** – both.
- **Dysphagia** (aspiration risk), **dysarthria** (slurred – motor), **homonymous hemianopia** (loss of same half of visual field both eyes → teach to **turn head (scan) toward affected side**, place items on **unaffected side**), **agnosia** (can't recognize objects), **apraxia** (can't perform learned movements).
- Hemiplegia → **flaccid initially → spastic** later.

### Management
- **CT scan first (non-contrast)** – rule out haemorrhage **before thrombolytics**.
- **Thrombolysis (tPA – Alteplase 0.9 mg/kg, max 90 mg; 10 % bolus)** – within **3–4.5 hrs** of symptom onset; BP must be **< 185/110**; no anticoagulants/IM injections/ invasive lines for 24 hrs; watch **bleeding & neuro status**. **Mechanical thrombectomy** – up to 24 hrs (large vessel).
- Ischaemic: aspirin (after 24 hrs if tPA given), antiplatelets, statins; BP permissive (don't lower aggressively unless > 220/120). Haemorrhagic: BP control, reverse anticoagulants, surgery.
- **HOB 30°** (haemorrhagic/↑ICP), ischaemic – flat or ≤ 30° as ordered.

:::key Stroke – nursing care
- **Swallow screen (bedside) before any oral intake** – NPO until cleared; **thickened liquids**, upright **90° for meals**, chin tuck, food on **unaffected side** of mouth, check pocketing.
- **Positioning**: prevent contractures – turn every 2 hrs, **hand roll / splint**, **trochanter roll** (external rotation of hip ↓), **footboard/ high-top sneakers** (footdrop), arm on pillow (shoulder subluxation – **don't pull affected arm**).
- **ROM exercises** within 24–48 hrs, early mobilization.
- Transfer/ambulation – **stand on/ assist from affected side**; cane in **unaffected (strong) hand**.
- Dressing: **put clothes on affected side first, remove from unaffected side first**.
- Communication: simple short sentences, picture boards, give time, one question at a time.
- Bladder training, bowel program, skin care, emotional lability support.
:::

## 11.6 Seizures & Epilepsy

- **Seizure** = abnormal, excessive, sudden discharge of neurons. **Epilepsy** = **≥ 2 unprovoked seizures > 24 hrs apart**.
- **Generalized tonic-clonic (Grand mal)**: aura (in focal onset) → **cry**, loss of consciousness → **tonic phase (stiff, 10–20 sec)** → **clonic phase (jerking, 30–60 sec)** → **post-ictal** (confusion, sleep, headache), **incontinence, tongue bite**.
- **Absence (Petit mal)** – children, **brief staring (5–10 sec)**, eye blinking; many times/day.
- **Myoclonic** – brief jerks; **Atonic (drop attacks)** – loss of muscle tone → fall; **Focal (partial)** – simple (conscious) / complex (impaired awareness, automatisms – lip smacking).
- **Status epilepticus** – seizure **> 5 min** or repeated seizures without regaining consciousness → **medical emergency** → **IV Lorazepam (drug of choice)** / Diazepam (IV/rectal), Midazolam (IM/buccal) → then **Phenytoin/Fosphenytoin**, levetiracetam, valproate.

:::warn During a seizure – Do's & Don'ts
**DO**: stay with patient, **note time & characteristics**, protect head (pillow), **loosen tight clothing**, move dangerous objects, **turn to side (lateral) after/ during** to drain saliva, maintain airway, O₂, suction ready, side rails padded.
**DON'T**: **restrain**, **put anything in mouth** (no tongue blade/spoon/finger), give oral fluids, leave patient.
After: recovery position, reorient, check injuries, document.
:::

### Antiepileptic drugs
| Drug | Key side effects / points |
|---|---|
| **Phenytoin** | **Gingival hyperplasia** (oral hygiene, soft brush), **hirsutism**, ataxia, nystagmus, **teratogenic**; therapeutic level **10–20 µg/ml**; IV only with **NS** (precipitates in dextrose), **≤ 50 mg/min** (hypotension, arrhythmia); purple glove syndrome |
| **Carbamazepine** | Trigeminal neuralgia drug of choice, **agranulocytosis/aplastic anaemia**, SJS, hyponatraemia |
| **Valproate (Sodium valproate)** | **Hepatotoxicity**, **pancreatitis**, weight gain, hair loss, **neural tube defects** (avoid in pregnancy) |
| **Phenobarbital** | Sedation, neonatal seizures drug of choice |
| **Levetiracetam** | Behaviour changes, safer |
| **Ethosuximide** | **Absence seizures** drug of choice |

- Teaching: **never stop suddenly** (status epilepticus), regular dose, avoid alcohol & sleep deprivation, flashing lights, **no driving / swimming alone / heights** till seizure-free (as per law), medical ID, contraception interactions.

## 11.7 Infections of CNS

### Meningitis
- Inflammation of meninges. **Bacterial** (most serious): *N. meningitidis* (meningococcal – **petechial/purpuric rash**, epidemics in crowded places), *S. pneumoniae* (adults – most common), *H. influenzae* (children – vaccine). Viral (aseptic) – milder. TB meningitis.
- S/S: **severe headache, fever, nuchal (neck) rigidity**, **photophobia**, vomiting, altered consciousness, seizures; infants – bulging fontanelle, poor feeding, high-pitched cry.
- **Kernig's sign** – hip & knee flexed 90° → **knee extension painful/resisted**. **Brudzinski's sign** – **neck flexion → involuntary hip & knee flexion**.
- Diagnosis: **Lumbar puncture – CSF**:

| CSF | Bacterial | Viral | TB |
|---|---|---|---|
| Appearance | **Turbid/cloudy** | Clear | Clear/cobweb |
| Cells | **↑ Neutrophils** | ↑ Lymphocytes | ↑ Lymphocytes |
| Protein | **↑↑** | Normal/slight ↑ | **↑↑** |
| Glucose | **↓↓** | **Normal** | ↓ |

- Rx: **droplet isolation for first 24 hrs of antibiotics** (meningococcal), **IV antibiotics ASAP** (after cultures – don't delay), dexamethasone, quiet **dark room**, HOB 30°, seizure & ↑ICP precautions, fluids; **close contacts prophylaxis – Rifampicin / Ciprofloxacin**.
- **Encephalitis** – brain tissue inflammation; viral (HSV – **acyclovir**; **Japanese encephalitis** – Culex mosquito, vaccine in UIP).
- **Brain abscess**, **Tetanus** (see communicable chapter).

## 11.8 Spinal Cord Injury (SCI)

- Common cause: RTA, falls, diving; most common sites **C5–C7, T12–L1**; young males.
- **Complete** (no function below) vs **incomplete** – central cord, Brown-Séquard, anterior cord syndromes.
- **Level**: **C1–C4 → quadriplegia + ventilator dependent** (C3-4-5 diaphragm); **C5–C8 → tetraplegia** with some arm function; **T1 & below → paraplegia**.
- **Spinal shock** – temporary loss of all reflexes below injury (**flaccid paralysis, areflexia**), lasts days–weeks; return of reflexes = end.
- **Neurogenic shock** – injury above **T6** → **hypotension + bradycardia + warm dry skin** (loss of sympathetic tone) → fluids, vasopressors, atropine.

:::warn Autonomic Dysreflexia (Hyperreflexia) – EMERGENCY
Injury **at/above T6**; triggered by noxious stimulus below injury – **full bladder (most common)**, constipation/faecal impaction, tight clothes, pressure ulcer.
S/S: **Sudden severe hypertension (up to 300 mmHg)**, **pounding headache**, **bradycardia**, flushing & sweating **above** injury, cold pale skin below, nasal stuffiness, blurred vision.
Rx: **Sit patient upright immediately (HOB 90°, legs down)** → loosen clothes → **find & remove cause – check bladder (catheter kinked? catheterize)** → bowel (with anaesthetic gel) → skin; **antihypertensive (nifedipine/nitrate)** if BP stays high. Risk: stroke, seizure.
:::

- First aid & care: **immobilize spine** (cervical collar, spine board), **log rolling** (3–4 persons), **jaw thrust** for airway; **methylprednisolone – no longer routinely recommended**.
- **Traction/stabilization**: Crutchfield tongs, **Halo vest** (keep **wrench taped to vest** for emergency CPR; pin site care; never lift by halo frame).
- Long-term: **intermittent self-catheterization**, bowel program (suppository, digital stimulation), **skin care – turn every 2 hrs**, pressure relief, DVT prevention, contracture prevention, respiratory care, sexuality counselling, rehab.

## 11.9 Degenerative & Neuromuscular Disorders

| Disease | Key pathology | Key features | Treatment / Nursing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Parkinson's disease** | **↓ Dopamine** in substantia nigra (basal ganglia) | **TRAP**: **T**remor (**resting, "pill-rolling"** – first sign), **R**igidity (cogwheel), **A**kinesia/**Bradykinesia**, **P**ostural instability; **shuffling/festinating gait**, **mask-like face**, micrographia, drooling, soft monotone voice | **Levodopa-Carbidopa** (avoid **Vit B6/pyridoxine & high-protein meals** with dose; s/e – dyskinesia, orthostatic hypotension, dark urine/sweat), dopamine agonists, MAO-B inhibitors, anticholinergics (tremor); **fall prevention**, raised toilet seat, walk with wide base, swing arms, march to music, thickened liquids |
| **Myasthenia gravis** | **Autoimmune – antibodies against acetylcholine receptors** at neuromuscular junction; thymus abnormality | **Muscle weakness that ↑ with activity & ↓ with rest**; **ptosis & diplopia** (first signs), dysphagia, weak voice, respiratory weakness | **Anticholinesterases – Pyridostigmine (Mestinon)** – give **on time, 30–60 min before meals**; **Tensilon (edrophonium) test**; thymectomy, plasmapheresis, IVIG, steroids; **plan activities in peak energy (morning)** |
| **Guillain-Barré syndrome (GBS)** | **Autoimmune demyelination of peripheral nerves** – after viral/**Campylobacter** infection, vaccine | **Ascending symmetrical flaccid paralysis** (legs → upward), areflexia, paresthesia, **respiratory failure** (major risk), autonomic instability; **CSF – ↑ protein, normal cells (albuminocytological dissociation)** | **Monitor respiration – vital capacity** (< 15–20 ml/kg → ventilate), **plasmapheresis / IVIG**, DVT prevention, pain mx; most recover |
| **Multiple sclerosis** | **Autoimmune demyelination of CNS** (young women 20–40) | **Relapsing-remitting**; visual problems (**optic neuritis, diplopia**), fatigue, numbness, spasticity, **Charcot's triad (nystagmus, intention tremor, scanning speech)**, bladder problems; **Uhthoff's** (heat worsens) | Steroids for relapse, interferon-β, disease-modifying drugs; **avoid heat (hot baths)**, stress, infection; energy conservation |
| **Alzheimer's disease** | Neurofibrillary tangles, **amyloid plaques**, ↓ acetylcholine | Progressive memory loss (**recent memory first**), confusion, wandering, sundowning, aphasia | **Donepezil**, rivastigmine (cholinesterase inhibitors), memantine; safe environment, routine, reality orientation (early), validation therapy (late), **ID bracelet** |
| **Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS)** | Degeneration of **upper & lower motor neurons** | Progressive weakness, fasciculations, dysphagia, respiratory failure; **cognition intact** | Riluzole, edaravone; supportive |
| **Huntington's disease** | Autosomal dominant, chorea | Choreiform movements, dementia | Supportive, genetic counselling |

:::exam Myasthenic vs Cholinergic crisis
| | Myasthenic crisis | Cholinergic crisis |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | **Too little** medication / infection / stress | **Too much** anticholinesterase |
| Tensilon (edrophonium) test | **Improves (strength ↑)** | **Worsens** |
| Features | Weakness, respiratory failure, ↑ HR, ↑ BP | **SLUDGE** (Salivation, Lacrimation, Urination, Diarrhoea, GI cramps, Emesis), bradycardia, miosis, fasciculations |
| Rx | More pyridostigmine, ventilate, IVIG/plasmapheresis | **Atropine** (antidote), stop drug, ventilate |
:::

- **Bell's palsy** – CN VII, sudden unilateral facial paralysis (can't close eye, drooling) → **protect eye (patch, artificial tears)**, steroids, facial exercises; most recover in weeks–months.
- **Trigeminal neuralgia (Tic douloureux)** – CN V, **severe paroxysmal stabbing facial pain** triggered by touch, chewing, cold air, brushing → **carbamazepine**; room temperature food, chew on unaffected side, avoid triggers.
- **Brain tumours** – most common primary **glioma (glioblastoma multiforme – most malignant)**, meningioma (benign); S/S of ↑ICP, seizures, focal deficits.
- **Craniotomy post-op**: **Supratentorial → HOB 30–45°**; **Infratentorial (posterior fossa) → flat, side-lying** (avoid neck flexion), NPO initially (gag reflex); watch ↑ICP, CSF leak, DI/SIADH, seizures.
