## 13.1 Skin – Applied Anatomy

:::key Skin facts
- **Largest organ** of body (~16 % body weight, ~1.5–2 m²).
- Layers: **Epidermis** (avascular; **stratum basale** – new cells, **melanocytes**; stratum corneum – outer dead keratin) → **Dermis** (blood vessels, nerves, hair follicles, **sweat & sebaceous glands**, collagen) → **Subcutaneous tissue (hypodermis)** – fat.
- Functions: protection, **temperature regulation**, sensation, **Vitamin D synthesis**, excretion, fluid balance.
- Epidermis renews every **~28 days**.
:::

### Skin lesions
| Primary lesion | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Macule** | Flat, colour change, **< 1 cm** | Freckle, measles rash |
| **Patch** | Flat, > 1 cm | Vitiligo |
| **Papule** | Raised solid, < 1 cm | Wart, mole |
| **Plaque** | Raised flat-topped, > 1 cm | **Psoriasis** |
| **Nodule** | Raised solid, 1–2 cm, deeper | Lipoma |
| **Vesicle** | Fluid-filled, < 1 cm | **Chickenpox, herpes** |
| **Bulla** | Fluid-filled, > 1 cm | **2nd degree burn blister**, pemphigus |
| **Pustule** | Pus-filled | Acne, impetigo |
| **Wheal** | Transient oedematous raised | **Urticaria**, insect bite, **TB test** |
| Secondary | **Crust, scale, erosion, ulcer, fissure, scar, keloid, lichenification** | |

- **Petechiae** (pinpoint < 3 mm – platelet ↓), **Purpura** (3 mm–1 cm), **Ecchymosis** (bruise > 1 cm), **Spider angioma** (liver), **Cyanosis**, **Jaundice**, **Erythema**.

## 13.2 Pressure Ulcer (Pressure Injury / Bed Sore / Decubitus Ulcer)

@fig pressure_ulcer_stages.svg | Pressure ulcer ke 4 stages – depth ke hisaab se

- Localized damage over **bony prominences** due to **pressure** (± shear & friction). Most common site: **Sacrum / coccyx** (supine), then **heels**, ischial tuberosity (sitting), greater trochanter (side-lying), occiput, elbows, scapula.
- Risk: **immobility (most important)**, malnutrition (↓ protein, albumin < 3.5), incontinence (moisture), ↓ sensation, elderly, anaemia, ↓ LOC, **shear** (sliding down in bed when HOB > 30°), friction.
- **Assessment tool: Braden scale** – 6 parameters (**Sensory perception, Moisture, Activity, Mobility, Nutrition, Friction & shear**); score **6–23**; **≤ 18 = at risk**, **≤ 9 = very high risk**. (Lower score = higher risk.) Norton scale – 5–20; ≤ 14 at risk.
- **Deep tissue injury** – purple/maroon intact skin; **Unstageable** – base covered by slough/eschar (stage can't be determined till debrided).

:::key Prevention & care
- **Reposition every 2 hrs** (chair – every 1 hr; shift weight every 15 min); turning schedule (clock)
- **30° lateral tilt**; **HOB ≤ 30°** (shear ↓); **lift sheet** to move – don't drag
- **Pressure-relieving mattress** (alternating air, water bed), **heels off bed** (pillow under calves)
- **Don't massage reddened bony prominences**; **no donut/ring cushions** (↓ circulation)
- Keep skin **clean & dry**, barrier cream for incontinence
- Nutrition: **high protein, high calorie, Vit C, zinc**, fluids
- Wound: **moist wound healing**; NS cleansing; debridement of necrotic tissue (**except stable dry heel eschar**); appropriate dressings (hydrocolloid, foam, alginate for exudate)
:::

## 13.3 Common Skin Conditions

| Condition | Cause / Key feature | Treatment / Nursing |
|---|---|---|
| **Impetigo** | Staph/Strep; **honey-coloured crusts** around mouth/nose; children; contagious | Topical mupirocin, wash crusts, separate towels |
| **Cellulitis** | Deep skin & subcutaneous infection (Strep/Staph) – red, warm, swollen, tender, spreading | Antibiotics, **elevate**, warm compress, mark borders |
| **Herpes zoster (Shingles)** | **Reactivation of varicella-zoster** in dorsal root ganglion; **unilateral, dermatomal painful vesicles** (don't cross midline); elderly/immunocompromised | **Acyclovir** (within 72 hrs), analgesics; **post-herpetic neuralgia**; contagious to non-immune (chickenpox) – contact/airborne if disseminated |
| **Herpes simplex** | HSV-1 (cold sores, lips), HSV-2 (genital) | Acyclovir |
| **Scabies** | ***Sarcoptes scabiei*** mite; **intense itching worse at night**, burrows in **finger webs, wrists, axilla, genitals** | **Permethrin 5 % cream – neck to toes**, leave 8–14 hrs, repeat after 1 week; **treat all family members simultaneously**; wash clothes/linen in hot water; contact precautions |
| **Pediculosis (lice)** | Head/body/pubic lice; nits on hair shaft | Permethrin 1 %, fine-tooth comb; wash linen hot |
| **Tinea (Ringworm)** | **Fungal (dermatophyte)**; ring-shaped with central clearing; capitis, corporis, cruris (groin), **pedis (athlete's foot)** | Antifungals (clotrimazole, terbinafine, griseofulvin for scalp); keep dry |
| **Candidiasis** | *Candida albicans*; white patches (oral thrush – **wipes off leaving red base**), intertrigo; diabetics, antibiotics, steroids | Nystatin, fluconazole |
| **Psoriasis** | **Chronic autoimmune**, **rapid epidermal turnover (3–4 days)**; **silvery-white scaly plaques** on **elbows, knees, scalp**; **Auspitz sign** (pinpoint bleeding on removing scale), **Koebner phenomenon** (new lesions at trauma site); nail pitting; not contagious | Topical steroids, **coal tar**, salicylic acid, vitamin D analogues (calcipotriol), **PUVA / UVB phototherapy** (eye protection), methotrexate, biologics; stress ↓ |
| **Atopic dermatitis / Eczema** | Allergic; itchy, dry; flexures | Emollients, steroids, avoid triggers, short nails |
| **Contact dermatitis** | Allergen/irritant (nickel, latex) | Avoid cause, steroids |
| **Acne vulgaris** | Sebaceous glands; adolescents | Benzoyl peroxide, retinoids (**isotretinoin – teratogenic**), antibiotics |
| **Urticaria** | Wheals, itching – allergy | Antihistamines |
| **Stevens-Johnson syndrome / TEN** | Severe drug reaction (sulfa, anticonvulsants, allopurinol, nevirapine) – skin & mucosa sloughing | Stop drug, **burns-unit-like care** |
| **Pemphigus vulgaris** | Autoimmune; **flaccid bullae**, **Nikolsky sign +** (skin peels with rubbing) | Steroids, burn-like care |
| **Vitiligo** | Autoimmune loss of melanocytes – white patches | Steroids, PUVA, sun protection |
| **Leprosy** | *M. leprae*; **hypopigmented anaesthetic patches**, thickened nerves | MDT (see communicable chapter) |

### Skin cancer
- **Basal cell carcinoma** – most common, **pearly papule with rolled edges**, rarely metastasizes; sun-exposed areas.
- **Squamous cell carcinoma** – scaly, can metastasize.
- **Malignant melanoma** – most dangerous; **ABCDE**: **A**symmetry, **B**order irregular, **C**olour variation, **D**iameter > 6 mm, **E**volving.
- Prevention: avoid sun 10 am–4 pm, **sunscreen SPF ≥ 30**, protective clothing.

## 13.4 Burns

:::def Burns
Tissue injury caused by **thermal (most common), chemical, electrical, radiation** sources. In India, burns common in women (kitchen, kerosene stoves), children (scalds).
:::

### Depth of burns
| Depth | Old name | Layers | Appearance | Pain | Healing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Superficial** | 1st degree | **Epidermis only** | **Red, dry, no blisters**, blanches (sunburn) | **Painful** | 3–6 days, no scar |
| **Superficial partial thickness** | 2nd degree | Epidermis + upper dermis | **Moist, red, blisters (bullae)**, blanches | **Very painful** | 10–21 days, minimal scar |
| **Deep partial thickness** | 2nd degree (deep) | Epidermis + deep dermis | Red/white, mottled, less moist, slow blanch | Painful (pressure) | 3–6 weeks, scar; may need graft |
| **Full thickness** | **3rd degree** | **Epidermis + dermis + subcutaneous** | **White, leathery, charred, dry, eschar**, no blanching | **Painless (nerve endings destroyed)** | **Skin graft required**, contractures |
| **Deep full thickness** | **4th degree** | **Muscle, tendon, bone** | Black, charred | Painless | Amputation/ grafting |

### Extent – TBSA (Total Body Surface Area)

@fig rule_of_nines.svg | Rule of Nines (Wallace) – adult TBSA calculation

- **Rule of Nines** (adults): Head & neck 9 %, each arm 9 %, front trunk 18 %, back trunk 18 %, each leg 18 %, perineum 1 %.
- **Lund & Browder chart** – **most accurate** (age-adjusted; children – head larger).
- **Palm method** – patient's palm (with fingers) ≈ **1 %** – scattered small burns.
- **Superficial (1st degree) burns are NOT counted** in TBSA for fluid calculation.
- **Major burns**: > 20–25 % TBSA (adult), > 10 % (children/elderly), any full thickness > 10 %, **burns of face, hands, feet, perineum, major joints**, **inhalation**, electrical, chemical burns → **burn centre**.

### Phases of burn management
| Phase | Duration | Key problems |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Emergent / Resuscitative** | **Injury → first 24–72 hrs** (till fluid mobilization & diuresis) | **Fluid shift (plasma → interstitial) → hypovolaemic "burn shock"**, oedema, **hyperkalaemia** (cell destruction), hyponatraemia, **↑ Hct (haemoconcentration)**, metabolic acidosis, ↓ urine, airway oedema |
| **2. Acute** | 48–72 hrs → wound closure | **Fluid remobilization (diuresis)** – **hypokalaemia**, fluid overload risk; **infection (sepsis – most common cause of death after 72 hrs)**, wound care, grafting, nutrition, pain, **Curling's ulcer** |
| **3. Rehabilitation** | Wound closure → return to function | **Contractures, hypertrophic scars, keloids**, body image, pressure garments (worn **23 hrs/day for 12–24 months**) |

### Emergent phase – management

:::key First aid (at scene)
- **STOP the burning** – "**Stop, Drop & Roll**" (clothes on fire); remove from source
- **Cool the burn with cool running water (tap water ~15–25 °C) for 20 min** – **NO ice** (vasoconstriction, hypothermia), **no butter, toothpaste, oil, ghee**
- **Remove jewellery, watches, belts & non-adherent clothing** (before swelling); don't pull stuck clothing
- **Cover with clean dry sheet / cling film**; keep patient **warm** (hypothermia)
- **Don't break blisters**
- **Chemical burns**: brush off dry powder first, then **flush with copious water 20–30 min** (eyes – 15–30 min irrigation)
- **Electrical burns**: **switch off power source first** (don't touch victim); look for **entry & exit wounds**; **ECG monitoring (arrhythmias – VF)**; **myoglobinuria** (rhabdomyolysis → AKI) – urine output 75–100 ml/hr; cervical spine immobilization (fall)
:::

- **Airway – first priority**. **Inhalation injury** suspected if: **facial burns, singed nasal hair/eyebrows, soot in mouth/sputum (carbonaceous)**, **hoarseness, stridor**, burns in enclosed space, cough → **early intubation** (before oedema), **100 % O₂** (CO poisoning – **SpO₂ falsely normal**; check **carboxyhaemoglobin**; cherry-red skin).
- **2 large-bore IV lines** (through unburned skin if possible), **Foley catheter** (hourly urine output), NG tube (paralytic ileus common if > 20 % TBSA), tetanus prophylaxis, IV opioids for pain (**not IM** – poor absorption).

### Fluid resuscitation – Parkland (Baxter) Formula

:::exam Parkland formula – must know
**Total fluid in first 24 hrs = 4 ml × body weight (kg) × % TBSA burned** (**Ringer's Lactate**)
- **½ (50 %) in first 8 hrs** – counted **from the time of burn** (not from arrival)
- **¼ (25 %) in next 8 hrs**, **¼ (25 %) in next 8 hrs**
- **Example**: 60 kg, 40 % TBSA → 4 × 60 × 40 = **9600 ml** → first 8 hrs **4800 ml (600 ml/hr)**, next 16 hrs 4800 ml (300 ml/hr).
- **Best guide of adequate resuscitation = urine output**: **adult 0.5 ml/kg/hr (30–50 ml/hr)**; **child 1 ml/kg/hr**; electrical burns 1–1.5 ml/kg/hr (75–100 ml/hr).
- Colloids (albumin) – usually **after 24 hrs** (capillaries leaky in first 24 hrs). *(Modified Brooke formula: 2 ml × kg × % TBSA.)*
:::

### Acute phase – wound care
- **Strict asepsis**, **protective (reverse) isolation**, no plants/flowers, sterile linen; **bed cradle**; room **warm (~ 30 °C)** – heat loss.
- **Escharotomy** – circumferential full-thickness burns (limbs/chest) → constriction → compartment syndrome/breathing restriction.
- Topical antimicrobials:
 - **Silver sulfadiazine 1 % (SSD)** – most common; s/e **transient leukopenia**; avoid in sulfa allergy, pregnancy near term, neonates; **not on face**.
 - **Mafenide acetate** – penetrates eschar (**ear cartilage**), **painful**, **metabolic acidosis** (carbonic anhydrase inhibitor).
 - **Silver nitrate 0.5 %** – **stains black**, electrolyte loss (hyponatraemia, hypokalaemia).
 - Silver-impregnated dressings, mupirocin, bacitracin (face).
- **Hydrotherapy**/wound cleansing – **premedicate with analgesic 30 min before** dressing.
- **Debridement**: natural, mechanical, **enzymatic (collagenase)**, **surgical (tangential excision)**.
- **Skin grafts**: **Autograft (own skin – permanent; best)**, Allograft/homograft (cadaver – temporary), Xenograft/heterograft (pig – temporary), synthetic (Biobrane, Integra). **Split-thickness** (epidermis + part dermis) – most common; full-thickness (face, hands). **Graft site – immobilize 3–5 days**, elevate, no pressure; **donor site – more painful** (keep dressing undisturbed, moist/dry as per protocol).
- **Nutrition**: **hypermetabolic state** → **high calorie (up to 3000–5000 kcal), high protein (1.5–2 g/kg)**, Vit C, zinc, **early enteral feeding (within 24 hrs)**; **daily weight**.

### Rehabilitation – contracture prevention
- **Position of comfort = position of contracture** → avoid. **Neck – extension (no pillow / small roll under shoulders)**, **shoulders – abducted 90°**, elbows extended, hands – functional/ splinted, hips – neutral extended, knees – extended, **ankles – dorsiflexed 90°** (footboard/splint).
- Early **ROM exercises**, splints, ambulation; **pressure garments** (scar ↓), massage with lotion after healing, sun protection 1 year, body image & psychosocial support.
