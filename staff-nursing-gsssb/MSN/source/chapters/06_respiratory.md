## 6.1 Applied Anatomy & Physiology

@fig respiratory_system.svg | Respiratory tract – trachea se alveoli tak

:::key Quick facts
- Upper tract: nose, pharynx, larynx · Lower tract: trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli.
- **Trachea** ~10–12 cm, **C-shaped cartilage rings**, bifurcates at **carina (T4–T5 / angle of Louis)**.
- **Right main bronchus** – shorter, wider, more vertical → **foreign body aspiration & ET tube misplacement** right side me.
- Right lung **3 lobes**, left lung **2 lobes** (+ lingula, cardiac notch).
- **Alveoli** ~300 million; **Type I** cells – gas exchange; **Type II** cells – **surfactant** (surface tension ↓, alveoli collapse nahi hote).
- **Main muscle of respiration = Diaphragm** (phrenic nerve, **C3-C4-C5** – "C3, 4, 5 keeps the diaphragm alive").
- Respiratory centre: **Medulla & Pons**. Main stimulus to breathe = **↑ CO₂ (H⁺)**; COPD me **hypoxic drive (↓ O₂)**.
- **Tidal volume** ~500 ml · **Vital capacity** ~4800 ml · **Residual volume** ~1200 ml · **Total lung capacity** ~6000 ml · Dead space ~150 ml.
:::

### Respiratory assessment
- **Breath sounds**: Vesicular (normal, peripheral), Bronchial (over trachea).
- **Adventitious sounds**: **Crackles/rales** (fluid – pneumonia, HF; inspiration), **Wheeze** (narrow airway – asthma; expiration), **Rhonchi** (secretions in large airways – clears on coughing), **Stridor** (upper airway obstruction – **emergency**), **Pleural friction rub** (grating – pleurisy).
- **Clubbing** – chronic hypoxia (COPD, bronchiectasis, lung cancer, CHD); angle > 180°, **Schamroth window test**.
- **Barrel chest** (AP : lateral = 1:1) → emphysema. Normal AP : lateral = **1 : 2**.
- **Cyanosis**: central (lips, tongue – reduced Hb > 5 g/dL) vs peripheral (extremities).
- **Pulse oximetry** – normal 95–100 %; inaccurate with nail polish, cold extremities, anaemia, CO poisoning (falsely high).

### Diagnostic tests – nursing points
- **Sputum culture** – **early morning**, deep cough, before antibiotics, rinse mouth with water (no mouthwash/toothpaste); **AFB for TB – 2 samples** (spot + early morning).
- **Bronchoscopy** – NPO 6–8 hrs; after: **NPO till gag reflex returns** (2 hrs), watch for bleeding, laryngospasm.
- **Thoracentesis** – position: **sitting upright, leaning forward over over-bed table**; max **1000–1500 ml** at a time; after: chest X-ray (pneumothorax check), lie on **unaffected side**.
- **PFT/Spirometry** – **FEV₁/FVC ratio < 0.7 (70 %)** = obstructive disease (COPD, asthma).
- **Mantoux test (TST)** – **0.1 ml (5 TU) PPD intradermal**, forearm; read at **48–72 hrs**; **induration** (not redness) measure: **≥ 10 mm positive** (≥ 5 mm in HIV/immunocompromised).

## 6.2 Oxygen Therapy

| Device | Flow | FiO₂ | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Nasal cannula** | **1–6 L/min** | **24–44 %** (each L ≈ +4 %) | Most common; > 4 L → humidify |
| **Simple face mask** | **5–10 L/min** (min 5 to flush CO₂) | 40–60 % | |
| **Partial rebreather** | 6–11 L/min | 60–75 % | Bag 1/3 – 1/2 full |
| **Non-rebreather mask** | **10–15 L/min** | **up to 90–100 %** (≈ 80–95 %) | Bag **never fully deflate**; highest O₂ without intubation |
| **Venturi mask** | 4–12 L/min | **Precise 24–60 %** | **Best for COPD** (exact FiO₂) |
| **High-flow nasal cannula** | up to 60 L/min | 21–100 % | Heated humidified |

:::warn Oxygen safety
- **COPD patient** → **low-flow O₂ (1–2 L/min, SpO₂ target 88–92 %)** – high O₂ se **hypoxic drive** khatam → CO₂ narcosis/apnoea.
- O₂ supports combustion → **"No Smoking"** sign, no candles/matches, cotton clothes (static), no oil/grease on equipment.
- O₂ toxicity: FiO₂ > 50 % for > 24–48 hrs.
- Cylinder colour (India): **O₂ – black body, white shoulder**; **N₂O – blue**; CO₂ – grey; Air – grey with black/white shoulder.
:::

## 6.3 COPD (Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease)

:::def COPD
Progressive, **irreversible** airflow limitation. Includes **Chronic bronchitis** + **Emphysema**. Most common cause = **cigarette smoking** (also biomass/chulha smoke, **α₁-antitrypsin deficiency** – young emphysema).
:::

| | **Chronic Bronchitis – "Blue Bloater"** | **Emphysema – "Pink Puffer"** |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | **Productive cough ≥ 3 months/year for 2 consecutive years** | Permanent **alveolar wall destruction**, air trapping |
| Look | Cyanosed, obese, oedema (cor pulmonale) | Thin, pink, **barrel chest**, **pursed-lip breathing** |
| Main symptom | Cough + **copious sputum** | **Dyspnoea**, minimal cough |
| ABG | Hypoxaemia, hypercapnia early | Near-normal till late |

### Management
- **Stop smoking** – most important intervention.
- **Bronchodilators**: SABA (salbutamol), LABA (salmeterol, formoterol), anticholinergic (**ipratropium, tiotropium**), theophylline (level 10–20 µg/ml; toxicity – tachycardia, seizures).
- Inhaled corticosteroids (budesonide) – **rinse mouth after** (oral candidiasis).
- **Low-flow O₂** (88–92 %), pulmonary rehab, vaccines (**influenza yearly, pneumococcal**).
- Breathing: **Pursed-lip breathing** (inhale nose 2 count, exhale lips 4 count → prevents airway collapse, CO₂ out) & **diaphragmatic/abdominal breathing**.
- Position: **Tripod / Orthopneic** (sit, lean forward on table).
- Diet: **high-calorie, high-protein, small frequent meals**; avoid gas-forming foods; fluids 2–3 L/day (unless HF) – secretions thin.
- **Chest physiotherapy**: postural drainage (before meals or 1–2 hr after), percussion, vibration.

## 6.4 Bronchial Asthma

- Chronic **reversible** airway inflammation → bronchospasm + mucus + oedema.
- Triggers: allergens (dust, pollen, mites), cold air, exercise, smoke, **aspirin/NSAIDs**, **beta-blockers**, stress, infection.
- S/S: **expiratory wheeze**, dyspnoea, chest tightness, cough (worse at night/early morning).
- **Status asthmaticus** – severe attack not responding to usual therapy → **"silent chest" (no wheeze) = ominous sign** (very little air movement) → respiratory failure.

:::key Asthma drugs
- **Reliever (rescue)**: **SABA – Salbutamol (Albuterol)** – drug of choice for acute attack.
- **Controller**: Inhaled corticosteroids (most effective long-term), LABA, **Montelukast** (leukotriene antagonist), cromolyn.
- Status asthmaticus: O₂, nebulized salbutamol + ipratropium, **IV hydrocortisone/methylprednisolone**, **IV magnesium sulphate**, aminophylline.
:::

:::tip MDI (Inhaler) technique
Shake → **exhale fully** → mouthpiece in mouth → press & **inhale slowly & deeply** → **hold breath 10 sec** → wait **1 min** before second puff. **Bronchodilator pehle, steroid baad me** (steroid deep jaye), steroid ke baad **mouth rinse**. Spacer use better.
**Peak flow meter**: Green 80–100 % (OK), Yellow 50–80 % (caution), Red < 50 % (emergency).
:::

## 6.5 Pneumonia

- Lung parenchyma ki **inflammation/infection** – alveoli me exudate (consolidation).
- Types: **Community-acquired (CAP)** – most common organism **Streptococcus pneumoniae**; **Hospital-acquired (HAP)** – ≥ 48 hrs after admission (Pseudomonas, Klebsiella, MRSA); **VAP** (ventilator, ≥ 48 hrs after intubation); **Aspiration pneumonia** – right lower lobe, stroke/unconscious patients.
- S/S: high fever with chills, **productive cough (rusty sputum – pneumococcal)**, **pleuritic chest pain**, tachypnoea, crackles, **bronchial breath sounds**, ↑ tactile fremitus, **dull percussion** over consolidation. Elderly → **confusion** may be first sign.
- Diagnosis: chest X-ray (consolidation), sputum culture, CBC.
- Rx: antibiotics, O₂, fluids **2.5–3 L/day**, antipyretics, **semi/high Fowler's**, deep breathing, incentive spirometry, turn every 2 hrs, ambulation.
- Prevention of VAP: **HOB 30–45°**, oral care with chlorhexidine, daily sedation vacation, hand hygiene.

## 6.6 Pulmonary Tuberculosis

- Organism: ***Mycobacterium tuberculosis*** (acid-fast bacillus, **aerobic** – lung apex me), discovered by **Robert Koch (1882)** – **24 March = World TB Day**. Transmission: **airborne droplet nuclei**.
- S/S: **cough > 2 weeks** (presumptive TB), **evening rise of temperature**, **night sweats**, **weight loss**, anorexia, haemoptysis, fatigue.
- Diagnosis: **Sputum smear (AFB, Ziehl-Neelsen stain)**, **CBNAAT/GeneXpert** (detects TB + **rifampicin resistance in 2 hrs**) / TrueNat, chest X-ray, culture (LJ medium – gold standard, 6–8 weeks), Mantoux, IGRA.
- **NTEP (National TB Elimination Programme)** – earlier RNTCP; India goal: **End TB by 2025** (SDG 2030). **Nikshay Poshan Yojana** – ₹500/month → **₹1000/month (from Nov 2024)** nutrition support. DOTS = Directly Observed Treatment Short-course.

| Drug (HRZE) | Key side effect | Nursing tip |
|---|---|---|
| **Isoniazid (H)** | **Peripheral neuropathy**, hepatotoxicity | Give **Pyridoxine (Vit B6)** |
| **Rifampicin (R)** | **Orange-red urine, sweat, tears** (harmless), hepatotoxicity, ↓ OCP effect | Soft contact lenses stain; use other contraception |
| **Pyrazinamide (Z)** | **Hyperuricaemia (gout)**, hepatotoxicity | Fluids |
| **Ethambutol (E)** | **Optic neuritis – red-green colour blindness**, ↓ visual acuity | Monthly eye check; avoid in young children |
| **Streptomycin (S)** | **Ototoxicity (CN VIII)**, nephrotoxicity | Hearing check |

- Regimen (drug-sensitive, new): **Intensive phase 2 months HRZE + Continuation phase 4 months HRE** (daily, weight-band FDCs) = 6 months.
- **MDR-TB** = resistant to **INH + Rifampicin**; XDR-TB = MDR + fluoroquinolone + Bedaquiline/linezolid resistance. Shorter oral **BPaLM** regimen (Bedaquiline, Pretomanid, Linezolid, Moxifloxacin) – 6 months.
- Nursing: **airborne isolation, N95** for staff, surgical mask for patient during transport; cover mouth while coughing; sputum disposal (burn / disinfect); **treatment adherence** (most important); nutritious diet; non-infectious after ~**2–3 weeks** of effective treatment.
- **BCG vaccine** – at birth, 0.05 ml (infant < 1 month) / 0.1 ml, **intradermal, left upper arm** – protects mainly against severe childhood TB (TB meningitis, miliary).

## 6.7 Pleural Disorders & Chest Injuries

- **Pleural effusion** – pleural space me fluid (> 15 ml normal). Transudate (HF, cirrhosis) vs Exudate (TB, cancer, pneumonia). S/S: dyspnoea, ↓ breath sounds, **stony dull percussion**. Rx: **Thoracentesis**, treat cause, pleurodesis.
- **Empyema** – pus in pleural space → chest tube drainage.
- **Pneumothorax** – air in pleural space → lung collapse. S/S: sudden **sharp chest pain**, dyspnoea, **absent breath sounds** on affected side, **hyper-resonant** percussion.
 - **Spontaneous** – tall thin young males, smokers.
 - **Tension pneumothorax** – air enters but can't exit → **mediastinal & tracheal shift to OPPOSITE side**, severe hypotension, distended neck veins → **EMERGENCY: needle decompression at 2nd ICS mid-clavicular line** (or 4th/5th ICS anterior-axillary) then chest tube.
 - **Open pneumothorax / sucking chest wound** → cover with **3-sided occlusive dressing** (air bahar jaa sake, andar na aaye).
- **Haemothorax** – blood in pleural space.
- **Flail chest** – **2 or more adjacent ribs fractured at 2 or more places** → **paradoxical chest movement** (inspiration me andar, expiration me bahar). Rx: O₂, analgesia, mechanical ventilation (internal splinting).
- **Rib fracture** – avoid tight strapping (atelectasis); analgesics, deep breathing.

### Chest Tube & Underwater Seal Drainage (UWSD)

:::key Chest tube – sabse important points
- Site: **Air (pneumothorax) → 2nd ICS mid-clavicular** (air upar jati hai); **Fluid/blood → 5th–6th ICS mid-axillary** (fluid neeche).
- **3 chambers**: (1) **Collection chamber**, (2) **Water-seal chamber** (2 cm water – one-way valve), (3) **Suction control chamber** (usually 20 cm H₂O).
- **Tidaling (fluctuation)** in water-seal chamber – inspiration par upar, expiration par neeche (spontaneous breathing) = **normal, tube patent**. Tidaling band = lung re-expanded OR tube blocked/kinked.
- **Continuous bubbling** in water-seal = **air leak** → check connections. Intermittent bubbling (cough/exhale) in pneumothorax = normal. **Gentle continuous bubbling in suction chamber = normal.**
- Drainage bottle **always below chest level** (60–90 cm); never lift above chest.
- **No routine clamping** (tension pneumothorax risk). Clamp only briefly to locate leak / change bottle / as ordered.
- **Tube accidentally pulls out** → **cover site immediately with sterile occlusive (petroleum) gauze** → inform.
- **Tube disconnects from bottle** → **put the end in sterile water (2 cm below surface)** → reconnect new system.
- Drainage > **100–200 ml/hr** or bright red → inform. Milking/stripping – only if ordered.
- Removal: patient does **Valsalva / holds breath at end of full inspiration**; occlusive dressing; chest X-ray after.
- Keep **2 haemostats (clamps)** & sterile water/vaseline gauze at bedside.
:::

## 6.8 Other Important Conditions

- **ARDS** – acute non-cardiogenic pulmonary oedema; causes: sepsis (most common), trauma, aspiration, pancreatitis. Hallmark: **refractory hypoxaemia** (not responding to ↑ O₂); **"white-out" / ground-glass** on X-ray; PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤ 300. Rx: mechanical ventilation with **low tidal volume (6 ml/kg)**, **PEEP**, **prone positioning**, treat cause.
- **Pulmonary embolism** – mostly from **DVT of legs**. **Sudden dyspnoea (most common), pleuritic chest pain, tachycardia, anxiety, haemoptysis**, ↓SpO₂. Diagnosis: **CT pulmonary angiography** (gold standard now), D-dimer, V/Q scan. Rx: **High Fowler's, O₂**, heparin, thrombolysis (massive), IVC filter.
- **Bronchiectasis** – permanent dilation of bronchi; **copious foul-smelling sputum (3-layered)**, haemoptysis, clubbing → **postural drainage**.
- **Lung abscess** – foul sputum; aspiration; antibiotics, postural drainage.
- **Occupational lung diseases**: **Silicosis** (stone/quarry), **Asbestosis** (asbestos – mesothelioma), **Anthracosis/Black lung** (coal), **Byssinosis** (cotton – Monday fever), **Bagassosis** (sugarcane), Farmer's lung (mouldy hay).
- **Lung cancer** – most common cause **smoking**; types: **Non-small cell (85 %)** – adenocarcinoma (most common, non-smokers & women), squamous cell (central, smokers); **Small cell (oat cell)** – most aggressive, central, **SIADH/paraneoplastic**. S/S: **persistent cough / change in cough** (most common), haemoptysis, weight loss, hoarseness. **Pancoast tumour** (apex) → **Horner's syndrome** (ptosis, miosis, anhidrosis).
- **Pneumonectomy** → position **supine or operated side down** (healthy lung expand kare); **no chest tube usually** (space fluid se bhar jata hai). **Lobectomy** → chest tube present; position on **unaffected side** or back as ordered.

## 6.9 Tracheostomy & Suctioning

- Tracheostomy – opening in trachea (**2nd–3rd tracheal ring**). Indications: prolonged ventilation, upper airway obstruction, secretion removal.
- **Obturator** & spare tube (same + one size smaller) **bedside** rakho.
- First tube change by surgeon **after 5–7 days** (tract mature).
- **Accidental decannulation** (first 72 hrs) → **call for help, use obturator / hold stoma open with tracheal dilator**, O₂; if fresh tract – bag-mask.
- Cuff pressure **20–25 mmHg (25–30 cmH₂O)** – zyada se tracheal necrosis.
- Tie change: 2 persons; **one finger** space under ties.

:::warn Suctioning rules
- **Hyperoxygenate (100 % O₂) before & after** – 30–60 sec.
- **Sterile technique** (trach/ET); oral suction (Yankauer) – clean.
- Suction **only while withdrawing**, **intermittent with rotation**.
- Each pass **max 10–15 seconds**; max **3 passes**; rest 20–30 sec between.
- Pressure: adult **100–150 mmHg** (80–120 common); child 80–100; infant 60–80.
- Catheter size ≤ **half the internal diameter** of tube.
- Insert **without suction**; stop if bradycardia/desaturation.
:::
