## 5.1 Applied Anatomy & Physiology

@fig heart_anatomy.svg | Heart ke 4 chambers, 4 valves aur blood flow ka rasta

:::key Heart – quick facts
- Weight ~**300 g**, size = apni **closed fist** jitna; **mediastinum** me, 2/3 left side.
- Layers: **Pericardium** (outer sac, 10–50 ml fluid) → **Myocardium** (muscle) → **Endocardium** (inner).
- Valves: **AV valves** – Tricuspid (right), **Mitral/Bicuspid** (left); **Semilunar** – Pulmonary, Aortic.
- **S1 "Lub"** = AV valves close (start of systole); **S2 "Dub"** = semilunar valves close. **S3** = heart failure (ventricular gallop); **S4** = stiff ventricle (HTN, MI).
- Coronary arteries aorta ki base se nikalti hain; **LAD (Left anterior descending) = "widow maker"**. Coronary filling **diastole** me hoti hai.
- **Cardiac output (CO) = Heart rate × Stroke volume** = ~**5 L/min** (SV ~70 ml). **Cardiac index** 2.5–4 L/min/m².
- **Preload** = ventricle me end-diastolic volume (stretch) · **Afterload** = resistance jiske against ventricle pump karta hai.
- **Frank-Starling law**: zyada stretch → zyada force (ek limit tak).
:::

:::mnemo Blood flow trick
**"Try Pulling My Aorta"** → **T**ricuspid → **P**ulmonary → **M**itral → **A**ortic (valve sequence).
Body → SVC/IVC → RA → RV → Pulmonary artery → **Lungs** → Pulmonary veins → LA → LV → Aorta → Body.
*Pulmonary artery = only artery carrying impure blood; Pulmonary vein = only vein carrying pure blood.*
:::

@fig ecg_conduction.svg | Conduction system aur normal ECG waves

### ECG basics
- **12-lead ECG**: 6 limb leads (I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF) + 6 chest leads (V1–V6).
- Chest lead placement: **V1** – 4th ICS right sternal border; **V2** – 4th ICS left sternal border; **V3** – between V2 & V4; **V4** – 5th ICS mid-clavicular; **V5** – anterior axillary line; **V6** – mid-axillary line (same level as V4).
- Limb lead colour (AHA): **RA – White, LA – Black, RL – Green, LL – Red** ("**White on right, smoke (black) over fire (red), green is ground**").
- **Heart rate** from ECG = **300 ÷ number of big boxes** between two R waves (or 1500 ÷ small boxes).

### Common arrhythmias
| Arrhythmia | ECG / Feature | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Sinus tachycardia** | > 100, normal P | Treat cause (fever, pain, hypovolaemia) |
| **Sinus bradycardia** | < 60 | **Atropine** if symptomatic, pacing |
| **Atrial fibrillation** (most common chronic arrhythmia) | **No P waves, irregularly irregular**, fibrillatory waves | Rate control (beta-blocker, diltiazem, digoxin), **anticoagulant (stroke risk)**, cardioversion |
| **Atrial flutter** | **Saw-tooth** F waves | Cardioversion, rate control |
| **PVC** | Wide bizarre QRS, no P | Treat cause (hypokalaemia, hypoxia) |
| **Ventricular tachycardia (VT)** | Wide QRS, rate > 100 | With pulse: amiodarone/synchronized cardioversion; **pulseless → defibrillation** |
| **Ventricular fibrillation (VF)** | Chaotic, no QRS – **no pulse, no cardiac output** | **Immediate defibrillation** + CPR |
| **Asystole** | Flat line | CPR + **adrenaline**; **NOT shockable** |
| **Heart block (3rd degree / complete)** | P & QRS independent | **Pacemaker** |

:::exam Shockable vs non-shockable
**Shockable**: VF, pulseless VT → Defibrillation (**biphasic 120–200 J**, monophasic 360 J).
**Non-shockable**: Asystole, PEA → CPR + Adrenaline 1 mg IV every 3–5 min.
**Synchronized cardioversion** – AF, flutter, unstable VT with pulse (R wave par shock).
:::

## 5.2 Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) & Atherosclerosis

- **Atherosclerosis** = arteries me **fatty plaque** (cholesterol) jamna → narrowing → ischaemia.
- **Risk factors** – *Non-modifiable*: age (M > 45, F > 55), male gender, family history, race. *Modifiable*: **smoking (most important modifiable)**, hypertension, **hyperlipidaemia**, diabetes, obesity, sedentary life, stress.
- Lipid targets: Total cholesterol **< 200 mg/dL**, **LDL < 100** ("bad"), **HDL > 40 (M) / > 50 (F)** ("good"), Triglycerides **< 150**.

### Angina Pectoris
:::def Angina
Myocardium ko O₂ demand > supply → **temporary ischaemia** → chest pain. **No permanent damage (no necrosis).**
:::

| Type | Feature |
|---|---|
| **Stable angina** | Exertion se, **rest / nitroglycerin se 3–5 min me relief** |
| **Unstable angina** (pre-infarction) | Rest me bhi, increasing frequency – **emergency (ACS)** |
| **Variant / Prinzmetal** | **Coronary spasm**, rest me, early morning; ST elevation transient – calcium channel blockers |

**Management:** Rest, **Nitroglycerin (NTG) sublingual**, O₂ if SpO₂ < 90 %, aspirin, beta-blockers, statins; PCI/CABG.

:::warn Nitroglycerin (sublingual) – teaching
- **1 tablet SL every 5 min, max 3 doses**. 1st dose ke 5 min baad relief nahi → **call emergency (108)**.
- Side effect: **headache** (most common), hypotension, flushing, dizziness → **sit/lie down** ke lena.
- Store in **dark/amber glass bottle**, tightly closed; replace every 6 months (or as per label); tongue par **burning/tingling** = potent.
- **Sildenafil (Viagra)** ke saath **contraindicated** (severe hypotension).
- NTG patch: **hairless area**, rotate sites, **remove at night for 10–12 hrs** (tolerance rokne ke liye); wear gloves while applying.
:::

## 5.3 Myocardial Infarction (MI)

:::def MI = Heart Attack
Coronary artery ka **complete blockage** (thrombus on plaque) → myocardium ki **necrosis (death)**. Most common artery = **LAD**. Most common time = **early morning**.
:::

### Clinical features
- **Severe crushing/squeezing chest pain**, retrosternal, **> 20–30 min**, radiates to **left arm, jaw, neck, back**; **NOT relieved by rest or NTG**.
- Sweating (diaphoresis), nausea/vomiting, dyspnoea, anxiety, **fear of death**, pallor.
- **Silent MI** – diabetics & elderly & women (atypical: fatigue, indigestion).

### Diagnosis
| Marker | Rises | Peaks | Returns normal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Troponin I / T** (most specific & sensitive) | 3–6 hr | 24 hr | **7–14 days** |
| **CK-MB** | 3–6 hr | 12–24 hr | 2–3 days (re-infarction detect) |
| **Myoglobin** (earliest) | 1–2 hr | 6–8 hr | 24 hr |

- **ECG**: Hyperacute T → **ST elevation** (injury) → **pathological Q wave** (necrosis – permanent) → T inversion (ischaemia).
- **STEMI** vs **NSTEMI** (ST elevation nahi, troponin positive).

:::mnemo MONA – Initial MI management
**M**orphine (pain & anxiety; preload ↓) · **O**xygen (if SpO₂ < 90 %) · **N**itroglycerin · **A**spirin (**325 mg chewable**, non-enteric coated)
*Correct order of giving*: **Oxygen → Aspirin → Nitrate → Morphine** ("**OANM**")
:::

### Management
- **Thrombolytics** (Streptokinase, Alteplase/tPA, Tenecteplase) – **within 12 hrs** (best < 3 hrs; "**door-to-needle < 30 min**"). Main risk = **bleeding**; contraindicated: recent stroke, active bleeding, recent surgery.
- **PCI (angioplasty + stent)** – "**door-to-balloon < 90 min**" – preferred.
- **CABG** – multi-vessel disease (graft: saphenous vein, internal mammary artery).
- Drugs: **antiplatelets** (aspirin + clopidogrel), **heparin**, **beta-blockers** (O₂ demand ↓), **ACE inhibitors** (remodelling ↓), **statins**.
- **Nursing**: complete **bed rest first 12–24 hrs**, semi-Fowler's, cardiac monitoring, IV access, **stool softener (avoid Valsalva)**, low-fat low-salt diet, small meals, anxiety ↓, cardiac rehabilitation.
- Sexual activity: usually after **4–6 weeks** / when patient can **climb 2 flights of stairs** without symptoms.

:::warn MI complications
**Arrhythmias** (most common – **VF most common cause of death in first hours**), cardiogenic shock, heart failure, pericarditis, **Dressler syndrome** (pericarditis 2–10 weeks later), ventricular aneurysm/rupture, papillary muscle rupture, thromboembolism.
:::

## 5.4 Heart Failure (HF / CCF)

:::def Heart Failure
Heart body ki need ke hisaab se **sufficient blood pump nahi kar pata**. Most common cause = **CAD/MI** & **hypertension**.
:::

:::vs
<div markdown="1">

**LEFT-sided failure → LUNGS**
(blood backs up into lungs)
- **Dyspnoea**, **orthopnoea** (lying down breathless – pillows count), **PND** (paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnoea)
- **Crackles/rales**, cough with **pink frothy sputum** (pulmonary oedema)
- Fatigue, restlessness, confusion
- S3 gallop, tachycardia, ↓ urine output
- Cyanosis
</div>
<div markdown="1">

**RIGHT-sided failure → BODY (systemic)**
(blood backs up into venous system)
- **Pedal / dependent oedema** (pitting), sacral oedema in bed-bound
- **Jugular vein distension (JVD)**, ↑ CVP
- **Hepatomegaly**, **ascites**, RUQ pain
- **Weight gain**, anorexia, nausea
- Nocturia
- Cause: **Left HF (most common)**, **cor pulmonale (COPD)**
</div>
:::

:::mnemo L = Lungs, R = Rest of body
**L**eft failure → **L**ungs (dyspnoea, crackles). **R**ight failure → **R**est of body (oedema, JVD, liver).
:::

- **Diagnosis**: **BNP (B-type natriuretic peptide) ↑** (> 100 pg/ml), echo (**ejection fraction**; normal **55–70 %**; HFrEF ≤ 40 %), chest X-ray (cardiomegaly).
- **NYHA classification**: Class I (no limitation) → II (slight, ordinary activity se symptoms) → III (marked, less than ordinary activity) → IV (symptoms **at rest**).

### Management
- **Position: High Fowler's** with legs dependent (venous return ↓) – acute pulmonary oedema me.
- O₂, **Diuretics** (**Furosemide** – loop), ACE inhibitors/ARB/ARNI, **beta-blockers** (carvedilol, metoprolol), spironolactone, **Digoxin**, vasodilators.
- **Sodium restriction (< 2 g/day)**, fluid restriction (1.5–2 L), **daily weight** (gain **> 1–1.5 kg/day or 2 kg/week → report**), I/O chart.
- Rest + gradual activity, small frequent meals.

:::warn Digoxin – very important
- Action: **+ve inotrope** (force ↑), **–ve chronotrope** (HR ↓).
- **Apical pulse 1 full minute** before giving – **< 60/min (adult)** ya **< 70 (child)**, **< 90–110 (infant)** → **hold** & inform.
- Therapeutic level: **0.5–2.0 ng/ml**.
- **Toxicity signs**: **anorexia, nausea, vomiting (earliest)**, **visual disturbances – yellow-green halos**, blurred vision, bradycardia, arrhythmias, confusion.
- **Hypokalaemia** → digoxin toxicity ↑ (furosemide ke saath K⁺ monitor).
- **Antidote: Digibind (Digoxin immune Fab)**.
:::

## 5.5 Hypertension

| Category (ACC/AHA 2017) | Systolic | | Diastolic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | < 120 | and | < 80 |
| Elevated | 120–129 | and | < 80 |
| Stage 1 HTN | 130–139 | or | 80–89 |
| Stage 2 HTN | ≥ 140 | or | ≥ 90 |
| **Hypertensive crisis** | **> 180** | and/or | **> 120** |

*(JNC-7: HTN = ≥ 140/90; Prehypertension 120–139/80–89 – Indian exams me dono pooche jaate hain.)*

- **Primary/Essential HTN** (90–95 %) – no known cause. **Secondary** – renal disease, pheochromocytoma, Cushing's, coarctation of aorta, pregnancy, drugs (OCPs, steroids).
- **"Silent killer"** – usually asymptomatic; occipital headache (morning), epistaxis, dizziness.
- Target organ damage: **Heart** (LVH, HF, MI), **Brain** (stroke), **Kidney** (CKD), **Eyes** (retinopathy).
- **Hypertensive emergency** (with organ damage) → IV **labetalol, nitroprusside, nicardipine**; BP ko **first hour me 25 % se zyada nahi** girana.

:::key Lifestyle – DASH diet
- **Salt < 5 g/day** (WHO) / Na < 2.3 g · DASH diet (fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy) · weight loss · **exercise 30 min/day, 5 days/week** · limit alcohol · quit smoking · stress reduction.
:::

### Antihypertensive drugs
| Group | Example | Key side effect / nursing point |
|---|---|---|
| **Diuretics** – Thiazide | Hydrochlorothiazide | Hypokalaemia, hyperglycaemia, hyperuricaemia |
| Loop | Furosemide | **Hypokalaemia**, ototoxicity, give **morning** |
| K-sparing | Spironolactone | **Hyperkalaemia**, gynaecomastia |
| **ACE inhibitors (-pril)** | Enalapril, Lisinopril, Captopril | **Dry cough** (most common), **angioedema**, hyperkalaemia, first-dose hypotension; **contraindicated in pregnancy** |
| **ARBs (-sartan)** | Losartan, Telmisartan | No cough; hyperkalaemia |
| **Beta-blockers (-olol)** | Atenolol, Metoprolol, Propranolol | **Bradycardia**, bronchospasm (**avoid in asthma** – non-selective), masks hypoglycaemia, don't stop suddenly |
| **Calcium channel blockers** | Amlodipine, Nifedipine, Diltiazem | **Ankle oedema**, headache, flushing, gingival hyperplasia; avoid grapefruit |
| **Alpha-blockers (-zosin)** | Prazosin | **First-dose orthostatic hypotension** – bedtime dose |
| **Central alpha agonist** | Clonidine, **Methyldopa (pregnancy)** | Rebound HTN if stopped suddenly |
| **Vasodilator** | Hydralazine, **Sodium nitroprusside** | Nitroprusside – **light sensitive (cover bottle)**, **cyanide toxicity** |

:::tip Orthostatic hypotension ki teaching
Antihypertensive lene wale patient ko bolo – bed se **dheere-dheere utho** (lying → sitting → standing), pehle pair latka ke baitho. Hot bath, alcohol, long standing avoid.
:::

## 5.6 Valvular & Inflammatory Heart Diseases

### Rheumatic Fever & Rheumatic Heart Disease (RHD)
- Cause: **Group A β-haemolytic Streptococcus** throat infection (pharyngitis) ke **2–3 weeks baad** autoimmune reaction; age **5–15 yrs**.
- Most commonly affected valve: **Mitral** (then aortic).
- **Jones criteria** – *Major* (**J♥NES**): **J**oints (migratory polyarthritis – most common), **♥** Carditis, **N**odules (subcutaneous), **E**rythema marginatum, **S**ydenham's chorea. *Minor*: fever, arthralgia, ↑ESR/CRP, prolonged PR interval. Diagnosis = evidence of strep infection (**ASO titre ↑**) + **2 major** or **1 major + 2 minor**.
- Treatment: Penicillin, aspirin (arthritis), bed rest; **Secondary prophylaxis: Benzathine penicillin 1.2 million units IM every 3–4 weeks** (≥ 5 yrs/till age 21–40 depending on carditis).

| Valve disease | Key features |
|---|---|
| **Mitral stenosis** (most common RHD lesion) | Dyspnoea, **haemoptysis**, **AF**, **mid-diastolic rumbling murmur**, malar flush, thrombo-embolism |
| **Mitral regurgitation** | Pan-systolic murmur, fatigue, dyspnoea |
| **Mitral valve prolapse** | Mid-systolic click, mostly young women, asymptomatic |
| **Aortic stenosis** | **Triad: Angina, Syncope, Heart failure (dyspnoea)**; ejection systolic murmur; avoid strenuous exercise |
| **Aortic regurgitation** | Wide pulse pressure, **water-hammer (Corrigan) pulse**, head bobbing (de Musset sign) |

- Valve replacement: **Mechanical valve** – lifelong **warfarin** (INR 2.5–3.5); **Biological/tissue valve** – lasts 10–15 yrs, no long-term anticoagulation.

### Infective Endocarditis
- Cause: **Streptococcus viridans** (sub-acute; after dental procedure), **Staph aureus** (acute; IV drug users – tricuspid valve).
- Signs: fever, new murmur, **Osler's nodes** (painful, fingers), **Janeway lesions** (painless, palms/soles), **splinter haemorrhages** (nails), **Roth spots** (retina), petechiae, clubbing, emboli.
- Diagnosis: **blood cultures (3 sets)**, echo (**vegetations**). Rx: IV antibiotics **4–6 weeks**; **antibiotic prophylaxis before dental procedures** in high-risk patients.

### Pericarditis & Cardiac Tamponade
- **Pericarditis**: **sharp chest pain ↑ on lying down/deep breath, relieved by sitting up & leaning forward**; **pericardial friction rub** (hallmark); ECG – diffuse ST elevation. Rx: NSAIDs, colchicine.
- **Cardiac tamponade** – pericardial sac me fluid/blood → heart compress. **Beck's triad**: **Hypotension + Muffled/distant heart sounds + JVD**; **pulsus paradoxus** (SBP falls > 10 mmHg on inspiration). Rx: **Pericardiocentesis** (emergency).

### Cardiomyopathy
**Dilated** (most common; alcohol, viral), **Hypertrophic** (genetic; sudden death in young athletes; **avoid dehydration & strenuous exercise**, no digoxin/nitrates), **Restrictive** (amyloidosis).

## 5.7 Vascular Disorders

### Peripheral Arterial vs Venous Disease
| Feature | **Arterial (PAD)** | **Venous (CVI)** |
|---|---|---|
| Pain | **Intermittent claudication** (walking se calf pain, rest se relief); rest pain in severe | Aching, heaviness, **elevation se relief** |
| Pulses | **Weak/absent** | Normal |
| Skin | Pale, cool, **shiny, hairless**, thick nails | **Brown pigmentation**, warm, **oedema** |
| Ulcer | **Toes/feet, deep, punched-out**, painful, pale base | **Medial malleolus (ankle), shallow**, irregular, red base |
| Position | **Legs dependent (neeche)** | **Legs elevated (upar)** |
| Rx | Walking program, stop smoking, cilostazol, bypass | Compression stockings, elevation, Unna boot |

:::tip Position trick
**A**rterial → **A**ir me nahi (legs DOWN – gravity se blood pahunche). **V**enous → **V**ertical up (legs ELEVATED).
:::

- **Buerger's disease (Thromboangiitis obliterans)** – young **male smokers**; **absolute smoking cessation** = only effective treatment.
- **Raynaud's phenomenon** – cold/stress se fingers **white → blue → red**; mostly young women; keep warm, avoid caffeine & smoking, CCB.
- **Aortic aneurysm** – abdominal (most common, below renal arteries) – **pulsatile abdominal mass**, bruit; rupture → severe back pain, shock. Avoid straining; BP control.
- **Varicose veins** – dilated tortuous superficial veins (long saphenous); **Trendelenburg test**; Rx: elastic stockings, sclerotherapy, stripping; avoid long standing & crossing legs.

### DVT (Deep Vein Thrombosis)
- **Virchow's triad**: **Venous stasis** + **Vessel wall injury** + **Hypercoagulability**.
- Risk: immobility, post-op (esp. hip/knee surgery), pregnancy, OCPs, obesity, cancer, long flights.
- S/S: unilateral **calf pain, swelling, warmth, redness**; Homan's sign (not reliable). Diagnosis: **Duplex Doppler USG**, D-dimer.
- Rx: **Heparin** (initial) → **Warfarin** (overlap 4–5 days); LMWH (enoxaparin – SC in abdomen, don't rub, don't expel air bubble); bed rest initially with leg elevation.
- **Do NOT massage** the leg (embolism). Complication: **Pulmonary embolism**.

:::key Anticoagulants – Heparin vs Warfarin
| | **Heparin** | **Warfarin** |
|---|---|---|
| Route | IV / SC | Oral |
| Onset | Immediate | 3–5 days |
| Monitoring | **aPTT** (1.5–2.5 × control; normal 30–40 s) | **PT/INR** (target **INR 2–3**; mechanical valve 2.5–3.5) |
| **Antidote** | **Protamine sulphate** | **Vitamin K** (phytonadione) + FFP |
| Pregnancy | **Safe** (doesn't cross placenta) | Teratogenic – avoid |
| Diet | – | **Consistent** intake of green leafy vegetables (Vit K) |
:::

## 5.8 Cardiac Investigations & Procedures – Nursing points
- **Cardiac catheterization/angiography** – femoral or radial; check allergy (**iodine/seafood**), NPO 6–8 hrs, consent. **After**: affected **leg straight 4–6 hrs** (femoral), pressure on site, check **distal pulses**, colour, temperature every 15 min; fluids to flush dye; watch bleeding/haematoma.
- **Echocardiography** – non-invasive, ejection fraction. TEE (transoesophageal) – NPO, gag reflex check after.
- **Stress test (TMT)** – avoid caffeine, smoking, heavy meal before; stop if chest pain/ST changes.
- **Holter monitoring** – 24–48 hr ECG; patient diary maintain kare; no bath.
- **CVP** – normal **2–8 mmHg (3–8 cmH₂O)**; zero point = **phlebostatic axis** (4th ICS, mid-axillary line). ↑CVP = fluid overload/RHF; ↓CVP = hypovolaemia.
- **Pacemaker**: avoid MRI (non-compatible), keep mobile phone > 15 cm, **arm on pacemaker side – no raising above shoulder for few weeks**, check pulse daily, carry ID card.

## 5.9 CPR / BLS (AHA)

:::key Adult BLS – remember numbers
- Sequence: **C-A-B** (Compressions → Airway → Breathing) – (newborn me A-B-C).
- Check responsiveness & breathing + **carotid pulse ≤ 10 seconds**.
- **Compression rate 100–120/min**, **depth 5–6 cm (2–2.4 inch)**, allow **full chest recoil**, minimize interruptions (< 10 s).
- **Ratio 30 : 2** (1 or 2 rescuers, adult); child/infant 2 rescuers = **15 : 2**.
- Hand position: **lower half of sternum** (centre of chest). Infant: 2 fingers / 2-thumb encircling just below nipple line; depth **4 cm** infant, **5 cm** child.
- Airway: **Head tilt–chin lift**; suspected spinal injury → **jaw thrust**.
- With advanced airway: **1 breath every 6 sec (10/min)** + continuous compressions.
- **AED** – as soon as available; switch compressors every **2 min** (5 cycles).
- **Drowning victim / asphyxia** – breathing important; lay rescuer for child alone – **2 min CPR then call**.
:::

- **Chain of survival (IHCA)**: Early recognition & prevention → activate emergency response → high-quality CPR → defibrillation → post-arrest care → recovery.
- ACLS drugs: **Adrenaline 1 mg IV every 3–5 min**; **Amiodarone 300 mg** first dose (then 150 mg) for VF/pVT; Lidocaine alternative.
