## 16.1 Cancer – Basics

:::def Cancer (Neoplasm)
Uncontrolled, abnormal **cell growth** that invades surrounding tissue and can **spread (metastasize)** to distant sites. Study = **Oncology**.
:::

| Benign tumour | Malignant tumour |
|---|---|
| Slow growth, **encapsulated** | Rapid, **not encapsulated**, invasive |
| Well-differentiated (cells look normal) | **Poorly differentiated / anaplastic** |
| **No metastasis** | **Metastasis** (blood, lymph, seeding) |
| Rarely recurs | Often recurs |
| Usually not fatal | Fatal if untreated |

- Naming: **-oma** (benign, e.g. lipoma, fibroma); **Carcinoma** – epithelial (most common); **Sarcoma** – connective tissue (bone, muscle); **Leukaemia**, **Lymphoma**; **Carcinoma in situ** – not invaded basement membrane.
- **Carcinogens**: **Tobacco (most important preventable cause)**, alcohol, **viruses** (HPV – cervical/oral; HBV/HCV – liver; EBV – Burkitt's lymphoma, nasopharyngeal; HTLV-1; H. pylori – stomach), radiation (UV – skin), chemicals (asbestos – mesothelioma; benzene – leukaemia; **aniline dyes – bladder**; vinyl chloride – liver), **aflatoxin – liver**, diet (low fibre, high fat, smoked/salted food), obesity, genetics (**BRCA1/2 – breast & ovary**).
- Common cancers in India: **Men – oral/lip & lung**; **Women – breast (most common), then cervical**. Overall most common cause of cancer death worldwide – **lung cancer**.

:::mnemo CAUTION – 7 warning signs (American Cancer Society)
**C** – Change in bowel or bladder habits
**A** – A sore that does not heal
**U** – Unusual bleeding or discharge
**T** – Thickening or lump in breast or elsewhere
**I** – Indigestion or difficulty in swallowing
**O** – Obvious change in wart or mole
**N** – Nagging cough or hoarseness
(+ **U**nexplained anaemia, **S**udden weight loss → "CAUTION US")
:::

### Grading & Staging
- **Grading** – degree of differentiation (cells kitne normal dikhte hain): **Grade I (well) → IV (undifferentiated/anaplastic)**.
- **Staging – TNM**: **T** = Tumour size (T0–T4, Tis = in situ), **N** = Node involvement (N0–N3), **M** = Metastasis (M0/M1).
- Clinical stage 0 (in situ) → I, II (localized) → III (regional spread) → **IV (distant metastasis)**.

### Tumour markers
| Marker | Cancer |
|---|---|
| **CEA** | Colorectal (also lung, breast) |
| **AFP** | **Liver (HCC)**, germ cell / testicular |
| **CA-125** | **Ovarian** |
| **CA 19-9** | **Pancreatic** |
| **CA 15-3** | **Breast** |
| **PSA** | **Prostate** |
| **β-hCG** | Choriocarcinoma, testicular, molar pregnancy |
| **Calcitonin** | Medullary thyroid |

- **Biopsy = definitive diagnosis** (excisional, incisional, needle – FNAC, core).

### Prevention & Screening
| Cancer | Screening |
|---|---|
| **Breast** | **BSE monthly from age 20** (**5–7 days after menses**; post-menopausal – fixed date each month); **Clinical breast exam (CBE)**; **Mammography yearly/ 2-yearly from 40–50** |
| **Cervix** | **Pap smear** (every 3 yrs from 21 / 25), **VIA (Visual Inspection with Acetic acid – white patch)** – India's low-cost screening (30–65 yrs, every 5 yrs); HPV DNA test; **HPV vaccine (9–14 yrs girls)** – India's indigenous **Cervavac** |
| **Oral** | **OVE (Oral visual examination)** – 30+ yrs (NPCDCS/NP-NCD) |
| **Colorectal** | FOBT yearly, colonoscopy every 10 yrs (from 45–50) |
| **Prostate** | PSA, DRE |
| **Testicular** | Monthly TSE |

- **National Cancer Awareness Day – 7 November** (Marie Curie's birthday); **World Cancer Day – 4 February**. India – population-based screening for **oral, breast, cervical** cancers (≥ 30 yrs) under **NP-NCD**.

## 16.2 Cancer Treatment

### Surgery
Prophylactic, diagnostic (biopsy), **curative**, palliative (relieve symptoms), reconstructive.

### Chemotherapy
- Kills **rapidly dividing cells** – cancer + normal fast-dividing cells (**bone marrow, GI mucosa, hair follicles, gonads**) → hence side effects.
- **Cell-cycle specific** (antimetabolites – methotrexate, 5-FU; vinca alkaloids – vincristine) vs **non-specific** (alkylating – cyclophosphamide, cisplatin; antitumour antibiotics – doxorubicin).

| Side effect | Nursing management |
|---|---|
| **Bone marrow suppression (most serious; dose-limiting)** – **nadir 7–14 days** | **Neutropenia** → infection precautions, report **temp ≥ 38 °C**; **thrombocytopenia** → bleeding precautions; anaemia → rest, transfusion, EPO; check CBC before each cycle |
| **Nausea & vomiting (most common)** | **Antiemetics 30–60 min BEFORE chemo** (**ondansetron**, dexamethasone, aprepitant); small frequent bland meals, cold foods, avoid strong odours; ginger |
| **Stomatitis / mucositis** | Soft brush, **saline / sodium bicarbonate mouthwash**, avoid alcohol mouthwash, hot spicy foods; ice chips during 5-FU |
| **Alopecia** | Begins **2–3 weeks**; **temporary – regrowth after treatment** (may differ colour/texture); scarf/wig, cut hair short before, mild shampoo, sun protection |
| Diarrhoea/constipation (vincristine) | Fluids, diet |
| **Fatigue** | Energy conservation, rest, light exercise |
| **Specific toxicities** | **Doxorubicin – cardiotoxicity**, red urine; **Cisplatin – nephrotoxicity, ototoxicity**; **Cyclophosphamide – haemorrhagic cystitis** (fluids 3 L, mesna); **Vincristine – peripheral neuropathy**, constipation; **Bleomycin – pulmonary fibrosis**; **Methotrexate – leucovorin (folinic acid) rescue**; Paclitaxel – hypersensitivity |
| **Extravasation (vesicants – doxorubicin, vincristine)** | **Stop infusion immediately**, **leave needle & aspirate**, notify, antidote, cold (most) / warm (vinca alkaloids) compress, elevate |
| **Tumour lysis syndrome** | Rapid cell death → **hyperkalaemia, hyperuricaemia, hyperphosphataemia, hypocalcaemia** → AKI; **hydration + allopurinol/rasburicase** |
| Reproductive | Infertility – **sperm banking/egg freezing before**; contraception (teratogenic) |

:::warn Safe handling of chemotherapy (cytotoxic drugs)
- Prepare in **Biological Safety Cabinet (Class II, vertical laminar flow)**
- **PPE: double chemo-tested gloves, long-sleeved impermeable gown, eye protection, N95/mask**
- **Pregnant/ breastfeeding staff should not handle**
- Luer-lock syringes, closed-system transfer devices; **no eating/drinking** in the area
- Disposal in **labelled cytotoxic waste container** (yellow – incineration); spill kit
- Patient's **excreta are contaminated for 48 hrs** → wear gloves, **flush toilet twice with lid closed**; linen separate
:::

### Radiation therapy
- **External beam (teletherapy)** – e.g. Cobalt-60, linear accelerator; patient **NOT radioactive**.
- **Internal – Brachytherapy** (sealed source placed in/near tumour – cervix, prostate) & **unsealed** (I-131 oral/IV) – **patient IS radioactive** (emits radiation).

:::key Radiation safety – Time, Distance, Shielding (ALARA)
- **Time** – minimize time near patient (organize care; **max ~30 min per shift**); limit visitors to **30 min/day**
- **Distance** – keep max distance; visitors **≥ 6 feet (2 m)**; **inverse square law** (2× distance → ¼ exposure)
- **Shielding** – **lead apron/ shield**; private room with lead lining
- **Dosimeter (film badge/ TLD) – worn by staff**
- **No pregnant nurses/visitors, no children < 18 yrs**
- **Dislodged implant** → **pick up with long-handled forceps** (never hands) → place in **lead container** kept in room; notify radiation safety officer
- Cervical implant: **strict bed rest, flat/ HOB ≤ 10–20°**, Foley catheter, low-residue diet (no BM), log-roll only
- Linen & dressings **kept in room** until checked by radiation officer
:::

- **Skin care in external radiation** – **don't wash off ink markings**; wash with **lukewarm water & mild soap, pat dry**; **no lotions, powders, deodorants, perfumes on treated area** (unless prescribed); **avoid sun, heat/ice packs, tight clothes**; loose cotton clothing; electric razor.
- Side effects depend on site: skin reactions (erythema → dry/moist desquamation), **fatigue (most common)**, mucositis, xerostomia (head & neck – **dental check before**), diarrhoea (abdomen/pelvis), cystitis, bone marrow suppression, alopecia (only in treated area – may be permanent).

### Other therapies
**Hormone therapy** (Tamoxifen – breast, ER+ ; s/e hot flashes, **endometrial cancer, DVT**; letrozole), **Immunotherapy** (checkpoint inhibitors), **Targeted therapy** (Imatinib – CML; Trastuzumab – HER2 breast – cardiotoxic), **Bone marrow / stem cell transplant**, **Biological response modifiers** (interferon).

## 16.3 Important Cancers – Nursing Points

### Breast cancer
- Most common cancer in Indian women; **upper outer quadrant (most common site)** – tail of Spence; **painless, hard, irregular, fixed lump**; **peau d'orange** (orange-peel skin – lymphatic block), **nipple retraction**, bloody discharge, axillary nodes.
- Risk: **female, age > 50**, BRCA, family history, **early menarche (< 12), late menopause (> 55), nulliparity/first child after 30**, no breastfeeding, HRT, obesity, alcohol.
- Surgery: **Lumpectomy (breast conserving)** + radiation, **Modified radical mastectomy (MRM)**, sentinel lymph node biopsy.

:::key Post-mastectomy care
- Position: **semi-Fowler's**, **affected arm elevated on pillow** (above heart level) – lymphoedema prevention
- **Drains (Hemovac/JP)** – empty & measure, 1–2 weeks
- Exercises: **start fingers/hand/elbow (squeeze ball) day 1**; shoulder exercises gradually (**wall climbing, rope turning, pendulum swing**) after drain removal / as per surgeon
- **Lymphoedema prevention (lifelong)** on affected side: **NO BP, injections, IV, blood draws**; avoid cuts, burns, insect bites; **gloves for gardening**, oven mitts; **no tight jewellery/sleeves**; electric razor; don't carry heavy bags; report swelling
- Body image – breast prosthesis (after healing ~6 weeks), reconstruction; support groups
:::

### Cervical cancer
- **HPV 16 & 18** (most important – ~70 %); risk – early sexual activity, multiple partners, **multiparity**, smoking, HIV, low socioeconomic, OCPs long-term.
- **Post-coital bleeding (most common early symptom)**, intermenstrual bleeding, foul watery discharge; late – pain, fistula.
- Screening: **Pap smear / VIA / HPV DNA**; CIN (pre-cancer) → cryotherapy/ LEEP; Rx – Wertheim's (radical) hysterectomy, radiation (brachytherapy), chemo.
- **HPV vaccine** – India: **Cervavac (Serum Institute)**; girls 9–14 yrs.

### Oral cancer
- **Most common cancer in Indian men**; **tobacco chewing (gutkha, khaini)**, smoking, betel quid, alcohol, poor oral hygiene, HPV.
- Premalignant: **leukoplakia**, **erythroplakia (highest malignant potential)**, **oral submucous fibrosis** (areca nut – restricted mouth opening).
- Most common site in India – **buccal mucosa / gingivo-buccal sulcus** (where tobacco is kept); tongue (lateral border).
- Rx: surgery (commando operation), radiation, chemo; **post-op – airway (tracheostomy), suction, communication board, nutrition (NG/ PEG)**.
- **Laryngeal cancer** – **persistent hoarseness (> 2 weeks) – earliest sign**; **total laryngectomy → permanent tracheostomy (stoma), loss of voice** – speech rehab (oesophageal speech, electrolarynx, TEP); protect stoma (no swimming, cover during shower), humidification.

## 16.4 Oncological Emergencies
- **Superior vena cava syndrome** – lung cancer/lymphoma; **facial/neck/arm oedema**, distended chest veins, dyspnoea → **semi-Fowler's**, O₂, radiation, steroids; avoid IV/BP in arms.
- **Spinal cord compression** – back pain (first), weakness, bladder/bowel dysfunction → **dexamethasone**, radiation.
- **Hypercalcaemia** (bone metastasis) – fluids, bisphosphonates.
- **Tumour lysis syndrome**, **SIADH** (small cell lung), **cardiac tamponade**, **neutropenic sepsis**, **DIC**.

## 16.5 Palliative & End-of-Life Care

- **Palliative care** (WHO) – improves **quality of life** of patients & families facing life-threatening illness – **relief of pain & suffering** (physical, psychological, social, spiritual); **neither hastens nor postpones death**; begins early, along with curative care. **Hospice** – end-of-life (last ~6 months).
- **Kubler-Ross – 5 stages of grief/dying (1969)**: **D**enial → **A**nger → **B**argaining → **D**epression → **A**cceptance ("**DABDA**"). Not always in order.
- Common symptoms: pain (WHO ladder – Chapter 3), dyspnoea (opioids, fan, O₂, position), nausea, constipation (opioids – laxatives prophylactically), anorexia/cachexia, mouth dryness, delirium, **"death rattle"** (secretions – **side position, glycopyrrolate/hyoscine**; deep suction not useful).
- **Signs of approaching death**: ↓ consciousness, **Cheyne-Stokes breathing**, mottled cold extremities (knees/feet first), ↓ urine output, weak rapid pulse, ↓ BP, **hearing is the last sense to be lost** (keep talking to patient).
- **Signs of death**: no pulse, no respiration, fixed dilated pupils; **Algor mortis** (cooling ~1 °C/hr), **Rigor mortis** (stiffness – starts **2–4 hrs** after death, jaw first, full ~12 hrs, disappears 24–48+ hrs), **Livor mortis** (purple discolouration in dependent parts – 20 min–2 hrs).
- **Last offices (after-death care)**: confirm death by doctor, respect religious rituals, **place body supine, pillow under head (livor in face ↓), close eyes & mouth (rolled towel under chin), dentures in place**, remove tubes (unless **medico-legal case – don't remove, inform police**), clean & dress, ID tags (wrist, ankle, shroud), valuables to family with documentation, family time.
