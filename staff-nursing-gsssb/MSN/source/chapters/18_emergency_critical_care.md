## 18.1 Emergency Nursing – Principles

### Triage
:::def Triage
French word **"trier" = to sort**. Patients ko **severity/ urgency** ke hisaab se priority dena – "**greatest good for the greatest number**" (mass casualty).
:::

| Colour (START / mass casualty) | Priority | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **RED** – Immediate (Priority I) | Life-threatening but **salvageable** – treat first | **Airway obstruction, tension pneumothorax, shock, severe bleeding**, RR > 30, cap refill > 2 sec, can't follow commands |
| **YELLOW** – Delayed/ Urgent (Priority II) | Serious but stable, can wait (hours) | Open fractures, stable abdominal injuries, moderate burns |
| **GREEN** – Minor ("walking wounded") (Priority III) | Can wait long | Sprains, minor cuts, minor fractures |
| **BLACK** – Expectant/ Dead (Priority IV) | Dead or **unsurvivable** injuries | No breathing after airway opened, massive head injury |

- **START triage** (Simple Triage And Rapid Treatment) – **RPM**: **R**espiration (> 30 → red), **P**erfusion (cap refill > 2 sec/ no radial pulse → red), **M**ental status (can't follow simple commands → red); walking → green. Pediatric – **JumpSTART**.
- Hospital ED triage: **ESI (Emergency Severity Index) – 5 levels**; ESI 1 = resuscitation (immediate).

### Primary & Secondary survey (ATLS)
- **Primary survey – ABCDE**:
 - **A** – **Airway with cervical spine protection** (jaw thrust)
 - **B** – Breathing & ventilation (tension pneumothorax, flail chest, open pneumothorax)
 - **C** – Circulation & **haemorrhage control** (direct pressure; tourniquet for limb), 2 large-bore IVs
 - **D** – Disability (neuro – **AVPU**: Alert, Voice, Pain, Unresponsive; GCS; pupils)
 - **E** – **Exposure & Environment** (undress fully, then **prevent hypothermia**)
- **Secondary survey** – head-to-toe exam, **SAMPLE history** (**S**igns/symptoms, **A**llergies, **M**edications, **P**ast history, **L**ast meal, **E**vents).
- **Golden hour** – first 60 min after trauma (maximum survival if definitive care). **Platinum 10 minutes** (on-scene).
- **Trauma deaths – trimodal**: (1) seconds–minutes (immediate), (2) **minutes–hours (golden hour – preventable)**, (3) days–weeks (sepsis, MODS).
- **Medico-legal cases (MLC)** – RTA, burns, poisoning, assault, suicide attempt, unknown/unconscious → inform police; **preserve evidence** (clothes in paper bag, gastric lavage sample), document accurately; **treatment first, formalities later** (Supreme Court – Parmanand Katara case 1989).
- **108** – ambulance (GVK EMRI in Gujarat, launched 2007); **112** – national emergency; **1098** – childline; **181** – women helpline (Abhayam in Gujarat).

## 18.2 Poisoning

### General management
1. **ABC** & vital signs first (treat patient, not the poison)
2. **Identify poison** – container, history, smell, symptoms (toxidrome)
3. **Decontamination**:
 - **Skin** – remove clothes, wash with soap & water (organophosphates – staff wear gloves)
 - **Eyes** – irrigate 15–20 min
 - **GI – Activated charcoal 1 g/kg (adult 50 g)** within **1 hr** (not for **acids/alkalis, alcohols, hydrocarbons/kerosene, iron, lithium, heavy metals**)
 - **Gastric lavage** – within 1 hr of life-threatening ingestion; position **left lateral with head low (left lateral, 15–20° head down)**; tube size **36–40 Fr** (adult); **200–300 ml warm water/NS** each cycle; save first sample for **forensic analysis**; **contraindicated in corrosives (acid/alkali), hydrocarbons (kerosene – aspiration pneumonitis), unprotected airway, convulsions**
 - **Induced vomiting (ipecac) – NOT recommended**
4. **Enhance elimination** – urinary alkalinization (salicylates), **haemodialysis** (methanol, ethylene glycol, lithium, salicylate)
5. **Antidote** if available; supportive care; **psychiatric evaluation** (suicidal)

### Common poisons & antidotes

| Poison / Drug | Antidote |
|---|---|
| **Organophosphorus (OP) insecticides** (most common poisoning in rural India), carbamates | **Atropine** (till atropinization – **dry secretions/ clear chest**) + **Pralidoxime (2-PAM)** (OP only; within 24–48 hrs) |
| **Paracetamol (acetaminophen)** | **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** – within 8–10 hrs best (hepatotoxicity) |
| **Opioids (morphine, heroin)** | **Naloxone** |
| **Benzodiazepines** | **Flumazenil** |
| **Heparin** | **Protamine sulphate** |
| **Warfarin** | **Vitamin K** (+ FFP / PCC) |
| **Digoxin** | **Digibind (Digoxin-specific Fab)** |
| **Iron** | **Deferoxamine (Desferrioxamine)** |
| **Lead** | **EDTA (Calcium disodium), Dimercaprol (BAL), Succimer (DMSA)**, Penicillamine |
| **Arsenic, Mercury, Gold** | **Dimercaprol (BAL)** |
| **Copper (Wilson's)** | **Penicillamine** |
| **Methanol, Ethylene glycol** | **Fomepizole / Ethanol** + dialysis |
| **Cyanide** | **Hydroxocobalamin**; amyl nitrite + sodium nitrite + sodium thiosulfate |
| **Carbon monoxide (CO)** | **100 % O₂** / **Hyperbaric O₂** |
| **Atropine / anticholinergics** | **Physostigmine** |
| **Beta-blockers** | **Glucagon** |
| **Calcium channel blockers** | **Calcium gluconate/ chloride**, insulin-glucose |
| **Magnesium sulphate** | **Calcium gluconate** |
| **Insulin/ sulfonylurea (hypoglycaemia)** | **Dextrose**, glucagon; **Octreotide** (sulfonylurea) |
| **Methotrexate** | **Leucovorin (folinic acid)** |
| **Isoniazid (INH)** | **Pyridoxine (Vit B6)** |
| **Snake bite (neurotoxic/ haemotoxic)** | **Anti-snake venom (ASV – polyvalent)** |
| **Scorpion sting** | **Prazosin**, scorpion antivenom |
| **Tricyclic antidepressants** | **Sodium bicarbonate** |
| **Neuromuscular blockers (non-depolarizing)** | **Neostigmine** (+ glycopyrrolate), Sugammadex (rocuronium) |
| **Malignant hyperthermia** | **Dantrolene** |
| **Aluminium phosphide (celphos – wheat pill)** | **No specific antidote**; supportive, MgSO₄, no water/ lavage with water; KMnO₄ (1:10,000) lavage – debated |
| **Paraquat** | **No antidote**; **avoid O₂** unless hypoxic (↑ toxicity), Fuller's earth |
| **Kerosene** | No lavage, no emesis; O₂, observe for pneumonitis |

:::exam Organophosphorus poisoning – DUMBELS/ SLUDGE
**Muscarinic**: **D**iarrhoea, **U**rination, **M**iosis (**pin-point pupils**), **B**radycardia/ **B**ronchorrhoea/ **B**ronchospasm, **E**mesis, **L**acrimation, **S**alivation/ **S**weating – plus **garlic/kerosene smell**.
**Nicotinic**: muscle fasciculations, weakness, paralysis (respiratory failure), tachycardia, HTN.
**Rx**: decontamination (remove clothes, wash skin), airway & O₂, **Atropine 1.8–3 mg IV bolus, double dose every 5 min until atropinization** (clear chest on auscultation, HR > 80, SBP > 80, dry axillae, pupils no longer pin-point), then infusion; **Pralidoxime**; watch **intermediate syndrome** (24–96 hrs – proximal muscle weakness, respiratory failure).
:::

- **Corrosive (acid/alkali)** – burns of mouth, drooling, dysphagia, stridor → **no emesis, no lavage, no neutralization, no charcoal**; small sips of water/milk only if no perforation & within minutes (controversial); endoscopy within 12–24 hrs; NPO, airway.
- **Alcohol (ethanol) intoxication** – hypoglycaemia, aspiration risk; thiamine before glucose (Wernicke's).
- **Datura** (anticholinergic) – "**dry as a bone, red as a beet, blind as a bat, hot as a hare, mad as a hatter**" → physostigmine.

## 18.3 Bites & Stings

### Snake bite
- India – **"Big Four"**: **Common cobra, Common krait** (neurotoxic), **Russell's viper, Saw-scaled viper** (haemotoxic/ vasculotoxic).
- **Neurotoxic (cobra, krait)**: **ptosis (first sign)**, diplopia, dysphagia, slurred speech, descending paralysis, **respiratory failure**; krait – painless bite at night, abdominal pain.
- **Haemotoxic (vipers)**: local pain, swelling, blistering, **bleeding (gums, bite site), haematuria**, **20WBCT (20-minute whole blood clotting test) – blood doesn't clot = coagulopathy → give ASV**, AKI (Russell's viper).

:::key Snake bite first aid – "Do It R.I.G.H.T."
**R** – **Reassure** the patient (70 % bites non-venomous; fear ↑ heart rate)
**I** – **Immobilize** the bitten limb like a fracture (splint), keep at/below heart level; patient lies still
**G.H.** – **Get to Hospital** immediately (108)
**T** – **Tell** the doctor about symptoms
**DON'T**: **tourniquet/ tight bandage**, **cut, suck**, ice, electric shock, herbal/ traditional remedies, alcohol, painkillers like aspirin; remove rings/ tight items (swelling).
**ASV** – **polyvalent (covers Big Four)**, given **IV** (10 vials initial – 100 ml; no test dose as per national protocol) **only if signs of envenomation**; watch **anaphylaxis (keep adrenaline ready)**; neurotoxic – **neostigmine + atropine**, ventilation.
:::

- **Scorpion sting** – red scorpion (Mesobuthus tamulus) – **autonomic storm**: severe pain, sweating, salivation, priapism, **hypertension, pulmonary oedema, myocarditis** → **Prazosin** (drug of choice), antivenom, local anaesthetic infiltration.
- **Dog bite** – see Rabies (Ch 15).
- **Bee sting** – remove sting by **scraping** (don't squeeze), ice; anaphylaxis watch.

## 18.4 Environmental Emergencies

| Condition | Features | Management |
|---|---|---|
| **Heat cramps** | Painful muscle spasms, sweating | Rest in cool place, **oral salt-water/ ORS** |
| **Heat exhaustion** | Heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, temp **< 40 °C**, normal mental status, **cool clammy skin** | Cool place, lie down, legs up, remove extra clothes, **oral fluids/ ORS**, IV NS |
| **Heat stroke** – **EMERGENCY** | **Core temp > 40 °C (104 °F)**, **hot, dry, flushed skin (sweating may stop)**, **CNS dysfunction – confusion, seizures, coma**, tachycardia, hypotension | **Rapid cooling** (cold water immersion – exertional; **evaporative cooling – spray tepid water + fan**; ice packs to **neck, axilla, groin**), cool IV fluids; **stop cooling at ~38.5–39 °C** (overshoot); **antipyretics (paracetamol) NOT effective**; watch rhabdomyolysis, DIC, AKI |
| **Hypothermia** | Core temp **< 35 °C**; shivering (stops < 32 °C), confusion, bradycardia, arrhythmias (VF) | **Remove wet clothes**, **passive rewarming (blankets)** mild; **active external (warm air)**, **active internal (warm IV fluids 40–42 °C, warm humidified O₂)** severe; handle gently (VF); "**not dead until warm and dead**" |
| **Frostbite** | Freezing of tissue – white, hard, numb | **Rapid rewarming in warm water (37–40 °C) 15–30 min**; **don't rub/ massage**, no dry heat; analgesia (very painful); elevate; don't rupture blisters |
| **Drowning** | Hypoxia – main problem | Remove from water safely, **start with 5 rescue breaths** then CPR (A-B-C emphasis), cervical spine care only if diving injury, O₂, **all near-drowning admit for observation 24 hrs** (secondary drowning – pulmonary oedema), warm |
| **Electric shock** | Entry/exit burns, arrhythmias (VF – AC current), muscle damage, fractures | **Switch off power**, CPR, ECG monitor, fluids (myoglobinuria), C-spine |
| **Lightning** | Asystole/ respiratory arrest | **Reverse triage** – treat "apparently dead" first (they may recover with CPR) |

## 18.5 Critical Care (ICU) Nursing

### ICU basics
- **Nurse : patient ratio – 1 : 1** (ventilated/ critical), 1 : 2 (step-down).
- **Glasgow coma scale**, **RASS** (Richmond Agitation-Sedation Scale, −5 to +4; target 0 to −2), **CAM-ICU** (delirium), **APACHE II** (severity), **SOFA** (organ failure).
- **Invasive monitoring**: **Arterial line** (radial – **Allen's test**; continuous BP & ABG; transducer at **phlebostatic axis**), **CVP**, pulmonary artery (Swan-Ganz) catheter – **PCWP (pulmonary capillary wedge pressure) normal 6–12 mmHg** (↑ in LV failure).
- **ICU psychosis/ delirium** – sensory overload/ deprivation, sleep deprivation; orientation, clock, day-night cycle, family, minimize noise.
- **Sepsis** – life-threatening organ dysfunction due to dysregulated host response to infection; **qSOFA (≥ 2)**: **RR ≥ 22, altered mentation (GCS < 15), SBP ≤ 100**. **Septic shock** – vasopressors needed to keep **MAP ≥ 65** + **lactate > 2 mmol/L** despite fluids.
- **Hour-1 sepsis bundle**: measure **lactate**, **blood cultures before antibiotics**, **broad-spectrum antibiotics**, **30 ml/kg crystalloid** for hypotension or lactate ≥ 4, **vasopressors (noradrenaline)** to keep MAP ≥ 65.
- **MODS** – multiple organ dysfunction syndrome.

### Mechanical ventilation

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Tidal volume (VT)** | **6–8 ml/kg ideal body weight** (lung-protective; ARDS 6 ml/kg) |
| **RR (f)** | 12–20 breaths/min |
| **FiO₂** | 21–100 %; keep lowest to maintain SpO₂ 92–96 % (avoid O₂ toxicity > 60 % long) |
| **PEEP** | **Positive End-Expiratory Pressure** – **5–10 cmH₂O** (alveoli open, oxygenation ↑); high PEEP → **↓ venous return → hypotension**, barotrauma |
| **I : E ratio** | Normally **1 : 2** |
| **Modes** | **Controlled (CMV)**, **Assist-Control (AC)**, **SIMV** (weaning), **Pressure support (PSV)**, **CPAP** (spontaneous, weaning), **BiPAP** (non-invasive – COPD exacerbation) |

:::key Ventilator alarms – causes
| **HIGH pressure alarm** ("**obstruction**") | **LOW pressure/ volume alarm** ("**leak/ disconnection**") |
|---|---|
| **Secretions (suction)**, coughing, **patient biting tube**, kinked tubing, water in tubing, bronchospasm, **patient fighting ventilator**, pneumothorax, ↓ compliance (ARDS, pulmonary oedema) | **Disconnection (most common)**, **cuff leak** (low cuff pressure), extubation/ tube displaced, leak in circuit/ chest tube |
**Rule**: if cause can't be found quickly → **disconnect patient & manually ventilate with Ambu bag (100 % O₂)** & call for help. **Never turn off alarms.**
:::

### VAP prevention bundle
**HOB 30–45°** · **daily sedation interruption ("sedation vacation") & spontaneous breathing trial** · **oral care with chlorhexidine (0.12 %)** · **subglottic secretion drainage** · **cuff pressure 20–30 cmH₂O** · DVT prophylaxis · **peptic ulcer (stress ulcer) prophylaxis** · hand hygiene · avoid unnecessary circuit changes · drain condensate away from patient.

- **ET tube**: placement check – **end-tidal CO₂ (capnography – most reliable)**, bilateral chest rise & breath sounds, **chest X-ray (tip 2–4 cm above carina)**; note **cm mark at lips/ teeth** (21 cm women, 23 cm men approx.); secure; prevent self-extubation.
- **Weaning criteria**: underlying cause resolved, **FiO₂ ≤ 40–50 %, PEEP ≤ 5–8**, SpO₂ > 90 %, hemodynamically stable, alert, **RSBI (RR/VT in L) < 105**, adequate cough; trials of **T-piece/ CPAP/ PSV**. Extubate – **high Fowler's**, suction, deflate cuff, remove at peak inspiration, O₂, watch **stridor** (laryngeal oedema).

### Shock, cardiac & other ICU drugs
| Drug | Use | Point |
|---|---|---|
| **Noradrenaline** | Septic shock (first-line vasopressor) | **Central line** preferred; extravasation → **phentolamine** |
| **Dopamine** | Low dose renal (old), β (cardiac), α (vasopressor) | Extravasation – phentolamine |
| **Dobutamine** | Cardiogenic shock, HF (inotrope) | Tachycardia |
| **Adrenaline** | Cardiac arrest, anaphylaxis | |
| **Atropine** | Symptomatic bradycardia (1 mg IV every 3–5 min, max 3 mg), OP poisoning | |
| **Amiodarone** | VF/pVT, AF | Pulmonary fibrosis, thyroid, hypotension |
| **Adenosine** | **SVT** (6 mg rapid IV push + flush, then 12 mg) | Brief asystole – warn patient; **rapid push near heart (antecubital)** |
| **Sodium bicarbonate** | Severe acidosis, hyperkalaemia, TCA overdose | Don't mix with calcium |
| **Mannitol** | ↑ ICP | Filter needle |
| **Nitroglycerin IV** | MI, hypertensive emergency | Glass bottle/ non-PVC tubing |

## 18.6 Disaster Nursing

:::def Disaster (WHO)
"Any occurrence that causes **damage, ecological disruption, loss of human life, deterioration of health & health services on a scale sufficient to warrant an extraordinary response** from outside the affected community or area."
:::

- Types: **Natural** (earthquake – **Bhuj, Gujarat 26 Jan 2001**, cyclone, flood, drought, tsunami 2004, pandemic) & **Man-made** (**Bhopal gas tragedy 2–3 Dec 1984 – Methyl isocyanate (MIC)**, industrial, war, terrorism, fire, RTA, stampede).
- **Disaster cycle**: **Pre-disaster** (prevention/ **mitigation**, **preparedness**) → **Impact/ Response** (rescue, triage, first aid, evacuation) → **Post-disaster** (**recovery – rehabilitation & reconstruction**).
- **Disaster Management Act 2005**; **NDMA** (Chairman – **Prime Minister**); **SDMA** (Chief Minister); **DDMA** (District Collector); **NDRF** (National Disaster Response Force, 2006). **GSDMA** – Gujarat State Disaster Management Authority (2001, after Bhuj earthquake).
- **International Day for Disaster Risk Reduction – 13 October**.
- **Post-disaster health problems**: injuries, **communicable diseases (water-borne – cholera, typhoid, hepatitis A/E; vector-borne – malaria, dengue; measles in camps)**, malnutrition, **PTSD** & mental health issues; dead body management (identification, respectful handling – **dead bodies of disaster victims rarely cause epidemics**).
- Nurse's role: triage, first aid, emergency care, **shelter health (water chlorination, sanitation, immunization – measles, vitamin A)**, psychological first aid, record keeping, health education, coordination.
- **Mass casualty – hospital disaster plan**, incident command system, **Code Red (fire)**, **Code Blue (cardiac arrest)**, Code Pink (infant abduction).
- **Fire safety – RACE**: **R**escue → **A**larm → **C**ontain (close doors) → **E**xtinguish/ Evacuate. **Fire extinguisher – PASS**: **P**ull pin → **A**im at base of fire → **S**queeze handle → **S**weep side to side. Fire classes: **A** – ordinary combustibles (water), **B** – flammable liquids (foam/ CO₂/ dry powder), **C** – electrical (CO₂/ dry powder – **never water**), D – metals.
