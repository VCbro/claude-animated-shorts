# A short history of the internet: brief

- **Spec:** 9:16, 1080×1920, 24 fps, 47.5 s, voice-led. Style: pixel. Hero: the pixel critter, recoloured for each era's palette. Composed chiptune score at −12 dB under the voice.
- **Voice:** Kokoro `af_heart` (local); the last line is slowed to 0.82. Word times come from faster-whisper (79 of 91 words heard).
- **Format:** F2 chronology as a morph chain: 8 eras, 7 shape morphs, 0 hard cuts.
- **Device:** a year counter rolls forward through every gap (1971 → 1991 passes 1976, 1981…). Each era is drawn at a finer resolution: 20 → 15 → 12 → 10 → 8 → 6 → 4 → 3 px per pixel.
- **The arc:** 1969's "LO" crash pays off today, when the phone sends the whole "LOGIN" after a held silence.

## Claims and sources
- 29 Oct 1969: UCLA tried to send "LOGIN" to SRI at Stanford; the system crashed after "LO". Source: [History.com](https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/first-electronic-message-sent-arpanet-internet-precursor).
- 1971: Ray Tomlinson sent network email between two machines and chose @ to separate the user from the host. Source: [InfoWorld](https://www.infoworld.com/article/2245643/ray-tomlinson-email-inventor-who-picked-the-sign-for-addresses-dies-4.html).
- 6 Aug 1991: Tim Berners-Lee put the first website (info.cern.ch) online. Source: [CERN](https://home.cern/fr/science/computing/birth-web/short-history-web/).
- 1998: the ITU agreed V.90, the 56k modem standard. Source: [ITU](https://www.itu.int/newsarchive/press_releases/1998/04.html).
- 1999: 802.11b was branded "Wi-Fi" (the 802.11 standard itself dates from 1997). Source: [Cisco](https://heritage.cisco.com/tag/internet-event/page/2/).
- 2005: 53% of US home internet users had high-speed access (Pew, May 2005). On screen this is "most home users have high speed". Source: [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2005/09/21/findings/).
- 2007: a multi-touch smartphone was announced on 9 Jan 2007; it is drawn generic, with no brand. Source: [Apple newsroom](https://apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/).
- Today: about 91% of US adults own a smartphone (Pew, 2025). Source: [Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/mobile/).
