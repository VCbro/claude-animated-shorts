# One Line, For Life — treatment

Style: **Watercolor Brush** (`styles/watercolor/STYLE.md`). 45 s, vertical 1080×1920, 24 fps.
Voice: English, warm male narrator (Kokoro `am_` voice), English subtitles burned in + `.srt`.
Topic: the real craft of a tattoo, from first line on paper to healed skin. Original design (a swallow carrying a fern sprig), no studio, no brand.

## 1. Three candidate structures

1. **A process recipe on one tall sheet.** The steps of a tattoo painted top to bottom on one long journal page (sketch → stencil → needle → linework → shading → healing); the camera tilts down the page. Clear, but a plain list: no turn.
2. **A healing journal.** Locked view of one forearm, a page per day from Day 0 to Day 30, each painted over the last. Calm and true, but the craft (the artist's hands, the needle) happens off-screen before the film starts.
3. **A transect through skin.** One tall painted world: the paper sketch at the top, the forearm in the middle, a cross-section of skin below. The film travels down it like a textbook transect, from idea → surface → 1.5 mm under the surface, where the real work of a tattoo happens, and back up to the healed arm.

**Chosen: 3, with the steps of 1 as its first half.** The vertical frame is a tall sheet, so a vertical tilt *is* the story: going deeper is understanding more. The turn is the cross-section (a tattoo is not on the skin, it is in it); the ending pulls back up to the first page. The demo's long lateral journey and its map are avoided: our signature move is a **vertical** tilt into a **cut-away specimen**.

- Opening image (from the topic, not the style's cliché): a single wet line on blank paper that becomes the swallow's wing — the first stroke of a tattoo.
- Ending: the healed arm and the original paper sketch side by side; the brush lifts mid-stroke on a last fern leaf (the page left unfinished: the tattoo will outlive the page).
- Score shape: sparse → builds with the machine → drops to near-silence for the cross-section → warm full return → a single last note.

## 2. Logline and arc

*A tattoo is a line drawn on paper that the skin agrees to keep — and the craft is putting it at exactly the right depth.*

- **Setup** (0–13 s): the line on paper, redrawn until right; the stencil carries it onto skin.
- **Turn** (13–30 s): the machine starts; we tilt down through the skin: the ink sits about 1.5 mm deep, in the dermis. Too shallow fades, too deep blurs. The body's own cells hold the pigment.
- **Ending** (30–45 s): back up: linework, shading, colour burst; two weeks of healing on a locked view; pull back to the first page beside the healed arm.

## 3. Benchmark

- **19th-century dermatology / anatomy watercolour plates and naturalist field journals** (grammar only).
  Learn: one specimen per view, labelled with fine leader lines and small measured values; cut-away cross-sections painted in transparent layers; generous paper margins.
  Don't take: any specific plate, figure or layout.
- **Tattoo flash sheets** (grammar only).
  Learn: a design shown as a clean specimen on a sheet, bold outline + limited palette.
  Don't take: any existing flash design, any artist's work. Our swallow-and-fern is drawn from scratch.

## 9. Facts (spoken or written)

- Tattoo ink is deposited in the dermis, roughly 1–2 mm below the surface; the epidermis above is about 0.1 mm thick and renews itself, which is why ink in it fades.
- Tattoo machines puncture the skin roughly 50 to 3,000 times a minute.
- Too shallow = the ink is lost as the epidermis renews (fading, patchy); too deep = ink spreads into the fat layer (a "blowout": blurred lines).
- Macrophages (immune cells) take up the pigment and hold it; when they die, new macrophages take up the released pigment (Baranska et al., J. Exp. Med., 2018).
- The surface of a new tattoo typically heals in about two to three weeks.
