# Facts used in the film

- Tattoo ink sits in the dermis, roughly 1–2 mm below the surface; the epidermis above it is about 0.1 mm thick on most body skin and renews itself every few weeks, which is why ink placed only in the epidermis fades. (General dermatology references, e.g. Cleveland Clinic, "Skin layers"; DermNet NZ, "Tattoos".) The film says "about 1.5 millimeters".
- Tattoo machines puncture the skin roughly 50 to 3,000 times a minute (commonly cited range, e.g. US FDA consumer pages and Britannica, "Tattoo"). The film says "up to 3,000".
- Ink placed too deep spreads into the fatty layer below the dermis and blurs ("blowout"); too shallow and it is lost as the epidermis renews (patchy, faded).
- Macrophages take up tattoo pigment and hold it; when they die, new macrophages capture the released pigment: Baranska A. et al., "Unveiling skin macrophage dynamics explains both tattoo persistence and strenuous removal", J. Exp. Med. 215(4):1115–1133, 2018.
- The surface of a new tattoo usually heals in about two to three weeks; deeper healing takes longer. The film says "about two weeks". The day 1 / 7 / 14 stages (tender, flaking, healed) are typical, not universal.
- The ink-depth drawing is schematic: layers are not to exact scale except the ruler (1 mm = 300 px).
