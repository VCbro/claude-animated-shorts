# Letter Sounds, Syllables & Spelling Basics
<!-- level: Foundation -->
<!-- desc: Common consonant & vowel sounds, short and long vowels, magic e, digraphs (ch, sh, th, ph, ck), silent letters, syllables, basic spelling rules (-s/-es, doubling, y→i, drop e) -->

:::goals Learning Goals – after this chapter you can
- read common letter **sounds** and letter groups (*sh, ch, th, ph, ck*);
- tell **short** vowel sounds (*cap*) from **long** vowel sounds (*cape*);
- recognise common **silent letters** (*know, write, hour, lamb*);
- break words into **syllables** and spell longer words in parts;
- use four basic **spelling rules** for *-s/-es, -ing* and *-ed*.
:::

:::warm Warm-up
Say these pairs aloud. Listen to the difference: **hat – hate · pin – pine · hop – hope · cut – cute**.
What changed in the spelling? → Only an **e** was added at the end, but the **vowel sound** became long!
:::

## 2.1 Common consonant sounds

Most consonants have one main sound. A few letters have **two sounds**, and some sounds are spelt with **two letters**.

:::rule RULE – Letters with two sounds
| Letter | Hard sound | Soft sound (before **e, i, y**) |
|---|---|---|
| **C** | "k" – **c**at, **c**up, **c**ome | "s" – **c**ity, **c**entre, ri**c**e, bi**c**ycle |
| **G** | "g" – **g**o, **g**ame, ba**g** | "j" – **g**ym, pa**g**e, oran**g**e, **g**iant |
| **S** | "s" – **s**un, bu**s** | "z" – i**s**, ha**s**, rose, music |
| **X** | "ks" – bo**x**, si**x** | "gz" – e**x**am, e**x**act |
:::

:::note NOTE
The soft **g** rule has common exceptions: **g**et, **g**ive, **g**irl, be**g**in – here g is hard although e or i follows. Learn these by heart.
:::

### Two letters, one sound (digraphs)
| Letters | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **sh** | "sh" (श) | **sh**op, fi**sh**, wa**sh** |
| **ch** | "ch" (च) | **ch**air, lun**ch**, tea**ch**er |
| **tch** | "ch" after a short vowel | ma**tch**, wa**tch**, kit**ch**en |
| **th** | soft "th" (थ जैसा, जीभ दाँतों के बीच) | **th**ink, **th**ree, ba**th** |
| **th** | voiced "th" (द जैसा) | **th**is, **th**at, mo**th**er |
| **ph** | "f" | **ph**one, **ph**oto, ele**ph**ant |
| **ck** | "k" after a short vowel | ba**ck**, clo**ck**, ti**ck**et |
| **wh** | "w" | **wh**at, **wh**en, **wh**ite |
| **ng** | nasal "ng" | si**ng**, lo**ng**, spri**ng** |
| **qu** | "kw" | **qu**een, **qu**ick, **qu**estion |

:::hindi Hindi Help – आसान भाषा में
*th* की दो आवाज़ें हैं: **think** में जीभ हल्की-सी दाँतों के बीच रखकर हवा निकालें (न "ट", न "थ" ज़ोर से), और **this** में वही जगह, पर आवाज़ के साथ ("द" जैसा)। **ph** हमेशा "फ़" बोला जाता है: *phone* = "फ़ोन"।
:::

## 2.2 Short and long vowel sounds

:::rule RULE – Short vs long vowels and the "magic e"
- A **short vowel** usually comes in a **consonant–vowel–consonant** word: c**a**p, p**e**t, p**i**n, h**o**p, c**u**t.
- Adding an **e** at the end (consonant–vowel–consonant–**e**) makes the vowel **long** – it says its **letter name** – and the **e** is silent: c**a**p**e**, P**e**t**e**, p**i**n**e**, h**o**p**e**, c**u**t**e**.

| Vowel | Short (as in) | Long (says its name, as in) |
|---|---|---|
| a | c**a**t, m**a**p | c**a**ke, n**a**me |
| e | b**e**d, t**e**n | th**e**se, compl**e**te |
| i | s**i**t, b**i**g | b**i**ke, t**i**me |
| o | h**o**t, d**o**g | h**o**me, n**o**se |
| u | c**u**p, s**u**n | c**u**be, t**u**ne |
:::

### Vowel teams (two vowels, one long sound)
| Team | Sound | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **ai / ay** | long a | r**ai**n, tr**ai**n, d**ay**, pl**ay** |
| **ee / ea** | long e | s**ee**, tr**ee**, t**ea**, r**ea**d |
| **oa / ow** | long o | b**oa**t, r**oa**d, sn**ow**, sl**ow** |
| **igh / ie** | long i | n**igh**t, l**igh**t, p**ie**, t**ie** |
| **oo** | "oo" (long) / "u" (short) | f**oo**d, m**oo**n / b**oo**k, l**oo**k |
| **ou / ow** | "ow" | h**ou**se, **ou**t, c**ow**, n**ow** |

:::note NOTE – one spelling, different sounds
**ow** can sound like *snow* (long o) or *cow* ("ow"). **ea** can sound like *tea* or *bread* ("e"). English spelling is not 100 % regular – read the word and **listen** to it (dictionary or text-to-speech).
:::

## 2.3 Silent letters

:::rule RULE – Common silent letters (written but not spoken)
| Silent letter | Pattern | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **k** | kn- at the start | **k**now, **k**nife, **k**nee, **k**nock |
| **w** | wr- at the start | **w**rite, **w**rong, **w**rist; also ans**w**er, s**w**ord |
| **b** | -mb at the end; before t | lam**b**, clim**b**, com**b**, thum**b**, dou**b**t, de**b**t |
| **h** | some words | **h**our, **h**onest, **h**onour, w**h**at (in most accents) |
| **gh** | -igh, -ough | ni**gh**t, li**gh**t, dau**gh**ter, thou**gh** |
| **l** | -alk, -ould | wa**l**k, ta**l**k, cou**l**d, shou**l**d, ha**l**f |
| **t** | -sten, -stle | lis**t**en, fas**t**en, cas**t**le, whis**t**le |
| **e** | at the end (magic e) | mak**e**, hop**e**, tim**e** |
:::

:::mistake COMMON MISTAKES – pronunciation
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "k-nife" | "naif" | k is silent before n at the start |
| "wa-l-k" | "wawk" | l is silent in -alk |
| "ve-ge-ta-ble" (4 full beats) | "**VEJ**-tuh-bl" (3 beats) | the second e is usually not pronounced |
| "fone" spelt *fone* | **phone** | "f" sound is spelt **ph** in many words |
| "hour" said "ha-war" | "aw-er" | h is silent |
:::

## 2.4 Syllables – reading and spelling long words

:::rule RULE – What is a syllable?
A **syllable** is a **beat** of a word with **one vowel sound**. Clap as you say the word.

| Word | Syllables | Count |
|---|---|---|
| pen | pen | 1 |
| water | wa · ter | 2 |
| computer | com · pu · ter | 3 |
| information | in · for · ma · tion | 4 |
| responsibility | re · spon · si · bi · li · ty | 6 |

**Tip:** Count the **vowel sounds**, not the vowel letters: *cake* has 2 vowel letters but **1** vowel sound → 1 syllable.
:::

:::hindi Hindi Help – आसान भाषा में
Syllable = word का एक "ताल" (beat)। लंबे word को **हिस्सों** में बोलें और लिखें: *in-for-ma-tion*। हर हिस्से में एक **vowel की आवाज़** होती है। *cake* में दो vowel letters (a, e) हैं, पर आवाज़ एक है, इसलिए 1 syllable।
:::

## 2.5 Four basic spelling rules

:::rule RULE 1 – Adding -s or -es (plurals and he/she/it verbs)
- Most words: add **-s** → book**s**, play**s**, run**s**.
- Words ending in **s, ss, sh, ch, x, z, o**: add **-es** → bus**es**, class**es**, wish**es**, watch**es**, box**es**, go**es**, potato**es**.
- **Consonant + y** → change **y to i + es** → baby → bab**ies**, study → stud**ies**, try → tr**ies**.
- **Vowel + y** → just **-s** → boy**s**, day**s**, play**s**, key**s**.
:::

:::rule RULE 2 – Drop the silent e before -ing / -ed / -er
make → mak**ing**, write → writ**ing**, dance → danc**ed**, use → us**er**
**But** keep e before a consonant suffix: hope → hope**ful**, care → care**less**, move → move**ment**.
:::

:::rule RULE 3 – Double the last consonant (short word, short vowel)
One syllable, **one short vowel + one consonant** → double the consonant before **-ing / -ed / -er / -est**:
run → ru**nn**ing, stop → sto**pp**ed, big → bi**gg**er, hot → ho**tt**est, sit → si**tt**ing
**Don't double** after two vowels or two consonants: eat → eating, rain → rained, help → helping.
:::

:::rule RULE 4 – Y to I
**Consonant + y** → change y to **i** before most endings: happy → happ**i**ness, carry → carr**i**ed, easy → eas**i**er.
**Keep y before -ing**: carry → carr**y**ing, study → stud**y**ing (otherwise you would get "ii").
:::

:::note NOTE – British/Indian vs American spelling of doubled l
travel → **travelled, travelling** (Indian/British) · **traveled, traveling** (American). cancel → **cancelled** / **canceled**. Both systems are correct; use one consistently.
:::

:::mistake COMMON MISTAKES – spelling
| ✗ Wrong | ✓ Right | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| writting | **writing** | write → drop e, no doubling (long vowel) |
| runing | **running** | short vowel + one consonant → double |
| studing | **studying** | keep y before -ing |
| babys | **babies** | consonant + y → ies |
| boies | **boys** | vowel + y → just s |
| watchs | **watches** | ch → -es |
| hopeing | **hoping** | drop e before -ing |
| carefull | **careful** | the suffix is -ful (one l) |
:::

:::compare COMPARE & UNDERSTAND – one letter changes the meaning
| Short vowel | Long vowel (magic e) | |
|---|---|---|
| **hoping** = hope + ing (आशा करना) | **hopping** = hop + ing (कूदना) | *I am **hoping** for a job.* / *The rabbit is **hopping**.* |
| **taping** = tape + ing (टेप लगाना) | **tapping** = tap + ing (थपथपाना) | *She is **taping** the box.* / *He is **tapping** the table.* |
| **planed** (made smooth with a plane) | **planned** = plan + ed (योजना बनाई) | *We **planned** a trip.* |
:::

:::revision QUICK REVISION
- **c, g** are soft (s, j) before **e, i, y**: city, gym. Exceptions: get, give, girl.
- Digraphs: **sh, ch, th, ph (= f), ck (= k), wh, ng, qu (= kw)**.
- Magic **e** makes the vowel long and is silent: *hop → hope*.
- Silent letters: **k**now, **w**rite, lam**b**, **h**our, ni**gh**t, wa**l**k, lis**t**en.
- Syllable = one vowel **sound** = one beat.
- Spelling: -es after s/sh/ch/x/o · y→ies (consonant + y) · drop e before -ing · double after a short vowel.
:::
