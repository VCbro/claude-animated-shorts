# Plane ki khidki gol kyun hoti hai? (Comet 1954, metal fatigue)

## Facts + sources
- The de Havilland Comet was the world's first jet airliner. In 1954 two Comets broke up in the air: BOAC Flight 781 near Elba (10 Jan 1954, 35 killed) and South African Airways Flight 201 (8 Apr 1954, 21 killed), 56 in total. https://aviation-safety.net/wikibase/335195 · https://migflug.com/afterburner/de-havilland-comet-first-jet-airliner-metal-fatigue-square-windows/
- Cause: metal fatigue from repeated cabin pressurisation cycles. The crack started at the corner of a near-square cut-out in the fuselage (a window in the roof, the ADF window), where stress concentrates at tight corners. https://aviation-safety.net/wikibase/335195 · NLR-TP-2002-521 (quoted in search results)
- Rounded windows spread the stress around the opening instead of concentrating it at a corner, and this became standard after the Comet. https://time.com/4728767/why-are-airplane-windows-round · https://mentalfloss.com/article/84603/why-are-airplane-windows-round
- Wording: say "chaukor jaisi khidki / tez kone" (they were rectangles with tight corners), not "bilkul chaukor". No other numbers claimed.
